# 1. Journey Purpose

This journey begins with a gardener who has an established bush rose and wants help deciding whether and how to prune it.

It tests whether Pip can turn uncertainty into understanding by interpreting photographs, showing approved comparisons, asking the gardener to confirm the physical rose, preserving the interaction as plant history and continuing from that history at follow-up.

Marie and Sarah's Rose illustrate the experience.

# 2. The Behavioural Loop

1. Marie needs help pruning Sarah's Rose.
2. She supplies context and photographs.
3. Pip proposes visible features and shows approved comparisons.
4. Marie examines the rose and confirms or corrects each observation.
5. Marie chooses what to cut, leave, decide later or refer for experienced local help.
6. The interaction creates the rose's history automatically.
7. Marie returns to observe the response.
8. Pip compares the new evidence with the earlier session and helps her learn from what happened.

# 3. Beginning With a Real Need

Marie opens Ask Pip and chooses:

> **Help me prune my bush rose**

Pip explains:

> “We'll look at your rose together before you decide what to cut. I can suggest features that may be visible in your photographs and show trusted examples. You'll check the actual rose and confirm what is there. You can leave, decide later or get experienced local help at any point.”

Marie creates a minimal Bush Rose Profile with the rose's name, known rose information, location and planting context, and optional personal meaning.

Pip requests a clear overview and relevant close-ups, explains their immediate purpose and checks image quality. If the images are inadequate, Pip asks for a better angle or continues only where approved rules and gardener-confirmed observations support doing so safely.

# 4. Is Pruning Appropriate Now?

Using approved operational knowledge, Pip checks:

- supported bush-rose scope;
- location and seasonal context;
- dormancy or active growth;
- recent planting;
- visible stress, damage or disease;
- clean and sharp secateurs;
- suitable personal protection;
- safe access and working conditions; and
- adequate photographs and confidence in the observations Marie can confirm.

Exact questions, acceptable answers and stopping thresholds come from approved horticultural and safety knowledge.

If the situation is unsupported or uncertain, Pip requests better evidence, recommends leaving the rose for now, proposes a locally appropriate return point or recommends experienced local help.

# 5. Learning to See the Rose

For each supported observation, Pip:

1. examines Marie's overview and close-up photographs through an authorised vision capability;
2. proposes a possible feature and states its uncertainty;
3. retrieves an approved beginner-appropriate comparison;
4. clearly distinguishes Marie's image, Pip's interpretation and the approved reference;
5. highlights or labels the relevant feature and explains common lookalikes and limitations;
6. asks Marie to examine and trace the relevant structure on the actual rose; and
7. records whether Marie confirms, corrects or cannot resolve the proposal.

The supported observations are:

- dead versus living wood;
- damaged growth;
- crossing or rubbing stems;
- inward-growing stems;
- weak or congested growth; and
- the main framework to retain.

Each approved comparison records its viewpoint, context, source, verification, version and approval status. The session retains a reference to the exact comparison used.

# 6. Deciding Before Cutting

Pip creates a proposed pruning plan from Marie's confirmed observations and approved knowledge.

Each item identifies the relevant structure, confirmed observation, possible choice, reason, supported alternatives and remaining uncertainty.

Marie always has four choices:

- **Cut**
- **Leave**
- **Decide later**
- **Get experienced local help**

Before every cut, Marie confirms the intended stem on the physical rose. If she cannot trace it, the interpretation remains uncertain, safety is inadequate or approved knowledge does not cover the situation, Pip defers the affected choice.

Pip does not diagnose the rose or authorise a cut from an image alone.

# 7. Recording What Happened

The session history separately preserves:

- Marie's original photographs;
- Pip's proposed interpretations and uncertainty;
- the exact approved comparisons used;
- Marie's confirmations, corrections or unresolved observations;
- the options and explanations presented;
- planned, completed, left and deferred choices;
- before-and-after photographs; and
- anything to observe next.

Marie can correct the summary before it becomes part of Sarah's Rose's history.

# 8. One Meaningful Return

Pip offers an optional event-based follow-up window determined from the pruning date, location, season, weather context and approved rules. Marie may accept, change or decline it.

At follow-up, Pip requests comparable overview and close-up photographs and helps Marie assess:

- new shoots or buds;
- apparent dieback or damage;
- overall structure and response;
- unexpected symptoms; and
- whether her earlier expectations were met.

Pip compares the new photographs with the saved originals and approved references. Interpretations remain uncertain proposals where appropriate and require gardener confirmation.

Pip asks:

1. Did the pruning journey help you understand your rose better?
2. Did comparing photographs and examples help you recognise what you were seeing?
3. Was it useful that Pip remembered the earlier session and photographs?
4. Would you return with this rose, another plant or another gardening question?

The follow-up becomes part of the same rose history.

# 9. Prototype Validation

The journey records:

- completion;
- confirmed, corrected and unresolved photo proposals;
- ability to identify relevant structures;
- understanding of reasons;
- appropriate use of all four choices;
- safety deferrals and requests for better evidence;
- usefulness of comparison images;
- usefulness of automatic history and remembered context;
- willingness to return; and
- preference compared with written guidance or a general AI conversation.

Experts shall define unacceptable horticultural and safety errors before gardener testing. Early moderated testing establishes baseline results before numerical thresholds are set. Every unsafe recommendation, unsupported certainty or failure to defer is reviewed individually. Photo interpretation performance is measured separately from the gardener-confirmed decision.

# 10. What the Journey Must Demonstrate

The journey must demonstrate that a beginner can move from photographs and contextual questions to confirmed physical observations, understand the available choices, remain in control of every cut, create a truthful plant history automatically and return for a meaningful comparison supported by Pip's memory.

# 11. Outside This First Journey

The first journey excludes autonomous image diagnosis, cultivar identification from appearance, image-only cut authorisation, black-spot care, multiple plant types, a complete Garden Journal, general gardening questions, automated cut overlays, collective learning from user records and complex reminder infrastructure.

# 12. Knowledge Dependency and Alignment

Before testing, approved operational knowledge must cover bush-rose pruning, all supported observations, local timing, plant-condition deferrals, tool and personal safety, approved comparisons and lookalikes, uncertainty and experienced-help conditions.

This journey implements the Ask Pip vision by helping Marie understand Sarah's Rose, preserving its story and using that story when she returns. It shares the product boundary defined in the Bush Rose V1 Architecture.
