# **Chapter 1 — The Rose Sarah Gave Her**

Marie had never planned to grow roses.

One spring afternoon, Sarah arrived carrying an Iceberg rose, its seven white buds just beginning to open.

"I thought this would look lovely in your garden."

Together, they chose a sunny place beside the path, where Marie could see it from the kitchen window. Sarah steadied the plant while Marie filled the hole around it.

Marie cared for the rose through its first growing season and the one that followed. She watered it, cleared fallen leaves from beneath it and cut away the faded blooms. It had mattered to her from the afternoon Sarah brought it, although much of what she did came from hope rather than understanding.

By its second winter, the rose had settled into Marie's garden and grown strongly. She stood in front of it with a pair of secateurs and could not make the first cut.

Without its leaves and flowers, the rose looked unfamiliar. Its stems crossed and disappeared into one another. Marie knew that established bush roses were often pruned, but she did not know whether this was the right time, whether her rose was suitable, or how to begin without damaging something important.

A friend suggested she try Ask Pip.

"A gardening companion that helps you understand your plants."

Marie opened the app.

---

**Ask Pip**

*Growing understanding. Cultivating confidence.*

**Begin**

---

Marie tapped **Begin** and chose:

**Help me prune my bush rose**

Pip invited Marie to take a photograph of the whole rose. She stepped back until she could see it from the base to the tips of its longest stems, then took the picture.

The photograph appeared on the first page of a new plant journal.

Then Pip asked:

*"What would you like to call this rose?"*

Marie typed:

**Sarah's Rose**

Seeing the words on the screen changed something. It was no longer simply the rose Sarah had given her. It had a name of its own, and Pip would know which rose Marie meant when she returned.

When Pip asked what kind of rose it was, Marie remembered the label that had come with it.

**Iceberg**

She recorded the nursery label as the source. Pip added the variety to Sarah's Rose's journal without asking her to prove again what she already knew.

Pip then asked permission to use the phone's location so it could interpret the season where Sarah's Rose was growing. Marie allowed it, checked the town and region shown on the screen and confirmed they were correct.

One final question was optional.

*"Would you like to record why this rose is special to you?"*

Marie wrote:

*"My sister gave it to me. I'd like to look after it well and enjoy it for many years."*

The first photograph, the name, the variety, the location and Marie's words now formed the beginning of Sarah's Rose's journal.

# **Chapter 2 — Before the First Cut**

Before they looked at individual stems, Pip asked Marie a few questions about the rose and the day she had chosen.

Using the location Marie had confirmed, Pip placed the winter timing in its local context. It checked that Sarah's Rose was an established bush rose and asked whether it was dormant or actively growing. It also asked whether the rose had been planted or moved recently and whether Marie had noticed serious stress, major damage or significant disease.

Marie walked around Sarah's Rose as she answered. Its leaves had fallen for winter, and she had not noticed serious damage, disease or unusual stress.

It also asked about Marie's secateurs, gloves, eye protection, the ground around the rose and whether she could reach it safely.

Marie discovered that one blade of her secateurs was catching. Ask Pip explained why clean, sharp tools mattered, so she cleaned and tested them before continuing.

Then Ask Pip asked Marie to add pruning photographs to the journal: a clear overview from the same position as her first photograph and several close-ups. It explained why each view was needed.

Marie slowly walked around Sarah's Rose, photographing the whole plant and the places where stems crossed or disappeared into the centre.

One image was too dark to support a useful comparison. Ask Pip asked her to take it again from a different angle.

By the time the photographs were clear, Marie had prepared her tools, checked the space around her and looked at the whole rose more carefully than she had before. The images were saved in Sarah's Rose's journal as the record of how it looked before pruning.

The secateurs were resting on the path. There was no need to hurry towards the first cut.

# **Chapter 3 — Understanding Before Pruning**

The first close-up appeared beside a clear comparison of dead and living rose wood.

Ask Pip said:

*"This stem may be dead. It looks darker than the growth beside it, and I can't see a healthy bud in the photograph. Can you find the same stem on Sarah's Rose and look at it closely?"*

Marie crouched beside the rose. She traced the stem from its base to its tip and looked closely. It was dry and brittle, with no healthy-looking buds.

**Yes. I can see those signs on the rose.**

They continued one possible feature at a time.

For a pair of stems that appeared to cross, Pip showed Marie what rubbing growth could look like. Marie followed both stems with her hands and found the point where they touched.

**They are rubbing.**

Another photograph seemed to show a stem growing inward. When Marie examined the rose, she found that the camera angle had been misleading. The stem curved forward and had open space around it.

**It only looks inward from that angle. There's room around it.**

One thick stem near the base was partly hidden. A second photograph improved the view, but Marie still could not trace it with confidence through the congestion.

**I can't confirm this one.**

So they left it unresolved.

As they worked, Marie learned about damaged growth, weak or congested stems and the older canes that gave the rose its shape. Pip offered a possible reading and a useful comparison; Marie found the real stem, examined it and said what she could see.

For the first time, the rose began to make sense. Instead of seeing a tangle of branches, Marie was beginning to understand its living structure and why each feature mattered before she decided whether to cut.

# **Chapter 4 — Making the First Decision**

When they had finished looking, Pip brought Marie's observations together, beginning with the dead stem.

For every item, Marie saw the same four choices:

- **Cut**
- **Leave**
- **Decide later**
- **Get experienced local help**

They began with the stem Marie had found dry and brittle.

Pip explained why removing dead wood could help, and Marie chose **Cut**.

Before she lifted her secateurs, Ask Pip asked:

*"Can you follow that stem all the way to the place where you plan to cut?"*

Marie put her phone down. She held the stem, traced it carefully and checked it again.

**I have the stem.**

Only then did she make the cut.

Her hands were nervous, but the decision was hers and she understood the reason for it.

For the rubbing stems, Ask Pip explained that more than one choice could be horticulturally acceptable depending on which stem Marie wanted to retain as part of the framework.

Marie chose **Leave** for one item after deciding the contact was slight and she wanted to observe it.

For a cluster of weak growth, she chose **Decide later**. She understood the comparison but wanted to see the structure again after the clearer decisions had been completed.

Then they returned to the thick stem Marie had been unable to trace.

Marie tried once more to follow the thick stem through the crossing growth. When she lost it again, she chose **Get experienced local help**.

The choice felt useful. Marie understood the limit of what she could safely decide that day.

Marie worked through the remaining observations one at a time, checking each stem on the rose before deciding what to do.

When Marie finished, Pip asked:

*"Would you like to take a photograph of Sarah's Rose now, from the same place as your before photograph?"*

Marie stepped back to the same point beside the path and framed the rose as closely as she could to the earlier view. Pip placed the two photographs together in the journal so that Marie could return to them and see what changed after pruning.

Pip brought the session together: the dead wood Marie had removed, the rubbing stems she had left, the weaker growth she would reconsider and the hidden stem she wanted someone experienced to examine.

Marie checked the summary and corrected one detail about which rubbing stem she had left.

The before and after photographs, observations and decisions became part of Sarah's Rose's journal: what they had looked at, what Marie had seen, what she had chosen and why.

Before putting away her phone, Marie looked once more at Sarah's Rose.

Only that morning, she had seen a mass of branches. Now she could read parts of the rose's structure, and she knew how to pause where the meaning was not yet clear.

# **Chapter 5 — One Meaningful Return**

Pip suggested a time to look at the rose again as the season changed. Marie adjusted it to suit her garden and agreed.

When she returned, the season had begun to turn.

Tiny buds were swelling along several canes. There was new growth in some places and a section that Marie wanted to examine more closely.

Ask Pip asked her to take overview and close-up photographs from viewpoints comparable with the earlier session.

The earlier and current photographs appeared together. They helped Marie find her way back into the rose and remember the choices she had made.

*"This area may show new bud growth. The photograph suggests a change from the earlier session, but please examine the physical cane. Can you confirm what you see?"*

Marie found the cane and looked closely.

**Yes. The buds are raised and beginning to open.**

Elsewhere, a dark mark in one photograph looked worrying. Outside, Marie turned the stem gently and watched the mark disappear as the light moved. It had been a shadow.

They compared what Marie now saw with what she had expected after pruning. Some changes were clear. Others were too early to understand.

The follow-up became part of the same history.

The earlier photographs, Marie's observations and her decisions were still connected. She could remember not only what she had done, but why she had chosen it and what she had left unresolved.

# **Chapter 6 — Growing Understanding**

Later that spring, Sarah came to visit.

As they walked through the garden, she stopped in front of the rose.

"You've been busy."

Marie laughed.

"Slowly."

Sarah stepped closer and looked through the stems.

"I wouldn't know where to begin."

Marie understood the feeling.

"I used to think pruning meant cutting branches until the plant looked tidy," she said. "But the first question isn't what to cut. It is what you're actually looking at."

She showed Sarah an earlier photograph in which a stem had seemed to grow into the centre. Then she showed her how the angle had been misleading and how she had found the stem curving into open space.

"So you don't just follow what appears on the screen?" Sarah asked.

"No. It helps me know what to look for. Then I check the rose."

Sarah smiled.

"You've become quite the gardener."

Marie looked affectionately at Sarah's Rose.

"I'm still learning. But now I understand more of what I'm looking at—and I'm more comfortable saying when I don't know."

Sarah glanced at the photographs on Marie's phone.

"And you can go back to all of that?"

"Yes. I can see what I noticed, what I chose and what happened when I came back."

Sarah thought for a moment. "It helps the gardener grow."

The two sisters stood quietly for a moment, looking at Sarah's Rose.

Marie remembered the winter morning when the plant had seemed like a confusing tangle. She remembered her nervous first decision, the stem she had left alone and the one she had decided to show an experienced gardener.

Sarah's Rose was still teaching her.

With Pip beside her, Marie had begun to build her own judgement through observation, comparison, honest uncertainty and memory.

One gardener. One rose. One careful decision at a time.

**Growing understanding. Cultivating confidence.**

## **The Founders' Reflection**

"This story reminds every future contributor that Ask Pip exists to help gardeners grow in understanding—not merely to answer questions. Every technical decision, knowledge record and feature should move Ask Pip closer to the experience Marie had with Sarah's Rose."
