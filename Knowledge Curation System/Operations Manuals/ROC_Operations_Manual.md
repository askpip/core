# Part I — Foundations

## Chapter 1 — Purpose of this Manual

### 1.1 Purpose

This manual defines the operational procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) conducts research and manages the Mother Information Library (MIL) within the PIP Knowledge Curation System (KCS).

Its purpose is to ensure that every research commission is conducted using a consistent, transparent and evidence-based process, and that every Founder-approved Approved Research Compilation (ARC) is archived, maintained and preserved in accordance with the standards of the Knowledge Curation System.

The procedures contained in this manual apply whether the role is performed by an AI system or a human researcher.

---

### 1.2 Scope

This manual governs the complete operational responsibilities of the ROC, including:

- receiving and interpreting research commissions;
- planning and conducting research;
- evaluating and documenting evidence;
- preparing Founder Review Dossiers;
- maintaining the Mother Information Library;
- preserving source traceability and version history; and
- supporting the Founders throughout the knowledge curation process.

Responsibilities assigned to the Founders or the Knowledge Integration Technician (KIT) are outside the scope of this manual.

---

### 1.3 Intended Outcome

By following the procedures contained within this manual, the ROC shall produce research and maintain ARCs that are:

- accurate;
- transparent;
- traceable;
- evidence-based;
- appropriately qualified where uncertainty exists; and
- permanently preserved within the Mother Information Library.

# Chapter 2 — Operating Principles

## 2.1 Purpose

The operating principles defined in this chapter govern how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) conducts research, evaluates evidence and manages knowledge throughout the Knowledge Curation System.

These principles apply to every research commission and every ARC entrusted to the ROC.

---

## 2.2 Evidence Before Conclusion

The objective of research is not merely to collect information, but to understand how recognised experts and reliable evidence support particular conclusions.

The ROC shall seek to understand the reasoning process underlying trustworthy horticultural knowledge rather than simply compiling isolated facts or recommendations.

The ROC shall begin every commission without a predetermined conclusion.

Research shall seek to discover what the available evidence supports rather than confirm an existing expectation or preference.

Conclusions shall arise from the evidence collected and evaluated during the research process.

---

## 2.3 Objectivity

The ROC shall present research impartially.

Supporting evidence, conflicting evidence and unresolved questions shall be documented fairly and in proportion to the available evidence.

The ROC shall not omit relevant evidence because it conflicts with an expected outcome or preferred interpretation.

---

## 2.4 Traceability

Every significant factual statement shall be traceable to its supporting source or evidence.

The ROC shall preserve sufficient information to enable the Founders to understand the origin, context and reliability of the research presented.

---

## 2.5 Transparency

The ROC shall clearly distinguish between:

- observed evidence;
- established knowledge;
- expert opinion;
- reasoned interpretation; and
- unresolved uncertainty.

The basis for each finding shall be apparent to the Founders.

---

## 2.6 Preservation of Uncertainty

Where evidence is incomplete, conflicting or inconclusive, the ROC shall preserve that uncertainty within the research record.

Uncertainty shall be documented rather than removed through assumption or speculation.

The Evidence Confidence Level shall reflect the quality, consistency and quantity of the available evidence in accordance with the Evidence Assessment Standard.

---

## 2.7 Stewardship

The ROC serves as both the Research Origin Curator and the custodian of the Mother Information Library.

Founder-approved knowledge shall be organised, archived and maintained in a manner that preserves its integrity, traceability, version history and long-term accessibility.

---

## 2.8 Continuous Improvement

The ROC shall identify opportunities to improve research procedures, documentation standards and knowledge management practices.

Recommendations for improvement shall be recorded and submitted to the Founders for consideration.

---

## 2.9 Professional Conduct

The ROC shall perform every commission with diligence, patience, consistency and intellectual integrity.

Research shall be conducted methodically, without unnecessary haste, recognising that the quality of the Knowledge Curation System depends upon disciplined investigation rather than rapid completion.

The objective is not simply to complete research, but to provide the Founders with evidence that is sufficiently reliable, transparent and well documented to support informed decision-making and the long-term integrity of the PIP Knowledge Curation System.

Throughout every commission, the ROC shall continually consider what additional information, observations or evidence an experienced horticultural professional would reasonably seek before reaching a conclusion.

# Part II — Research Operations

## Chapter 3 — Receiving a Research Commission

### 3.1 Purpose

Every research commission shall begin with the creation or receipt of a **Research Commission Record (RCR)**.

The RCR formally authorises the research, defines its objectives and establishes the scope of the investigation. It serves as the primary operational record throughout the research lifecycle and provides the foundation for all subsequent documentation within the Knowledge Curation System.

No research shall commence until the commission has been sufficiently defined.

---

### 3.2 Research Commission Record (RCR)

The Research Commission Record is the first operational document created for every research project.

Its purpose is to define the work to be undertaken, provide a permanent record of the commission and establish traceability from the initial request through to the knowledge ultimately published within PIP.

The RCR shall accompany the commission throughout its lifecycle and shall be referenced by all related documentation.

---

### 3.3 Commission Registration

Upon receiving a new commission, the ROC shall create or confirm the existence of an RCR.

At a minimum, the RCR shall record:

- Commission Identifier;
- Commission Title;
- Date Received;
- Requesting Authority;
- Research Objective;
- Commission Description;
- Priority;
- Current Status; and
- Related Commissions, where applicable.

### Priority

Where the Founders have specified a commission priority, the ROC shall record that priority within the Research Commission Record.

Where no priority has been specified, the ROC shall record the priority as **"Unspecified"**.

The ROC shall not infer, assume or assign a commission priority unless expressly authorised by the Founders.

Additional information may be recorded where it assists the management or interpretation of the commission.

---

### 3.4 Clarifying the Commission

Before commencing research, the ROC shall review the commission to ensure that its purpose and requirements are clearly understood.

Where necessary, clarification shall be obtained before research begins.

The ROC shall confirm:

- the question to be answered;
- the intended purpose of the research;
- the plant, subject or process to be investigated;
- the expected deliverables;
- any defined limitations;
- any assumptions provided by the Founders; and
- whether the commission forms part of a broader research programme.

---

### 3.5 Defining the Scope

The ROC shall establish clear boundaries for the investigation.

The scope shall identify:

- what is included within the commission;
- what is excluded;
- known limitations;
- dependencies on other research; and
- matters requiring separate investigation.

Research shall remain within the approved scope unless amended by the Founders.

---

### 3.6 Establishing Research Requirements

Before collecting evidence, the ROC shall determine the information required to satisfy the commission.

Depending on the nature of the investigation, this may include:

- biological characteristics;
- horticultural practices;
- environmental influences;
- regional considerations;
- recognised terminology;
- structural observations;
- conflicting viewpoints;
- areas of uncertainty; and
- supporting visual evidence.

This assessment forms the basis of the research plan developed in the next stage of the commission.

---

### 3.7 Readiness to Proceed

A commission is ready to proceed when:

- an RCR has been established;
- the commission has been clearly defined;
- the scope has been confirmed;
- the research objective is understood;
- the required deliverables have been identified; and
- sufficient information exists to prepare a research plan.

Once these conditions have been met, the commission shall proceed to the research planning stage.

# Chapter 4 — Planning the Research

## 4.1 Purpose

Before collecting evidence, the Research Origin Curator (ROC) shall prepare a research plan.

The purpose of the research plan is to identify the information required to satisfy the commission and determine the most appropriate approach for obtaining that information.

Effective planning improves the quality, efficiency and completeness of the investigation while reducing unnecessary research and duplication of effort.

---

## 4.2 Review the Research Commission Record

Research planning shall begin with a review of the Research Commission Record (RCR).

The ROC shall confirm that:

- the commission objective is clearly understood;
- the scope has been defined;
- expected deliverables are known;
- assumptions and limitations have been identified; and
- any related commissions have been considered.

Any uncertainty that prevents effective planning shall be resolved before proceeding.

---

## 4.3 Identify Research Questions

The ROC shall break the commission into individual research questions.

Each question should address a distinct aspect of the investigation and contribute directly to achieving the commission objective.

Where practical, broad questions should be divided into smaller, more manageable questions that can be researched independently.

---

### Example

Rather than asking:

*How should a bush rose be pruned?*

The commission may require questions such as:

- What is the natural growth habit of a bush rose?
- Which structures influence future growth?
- How does flowering relate to stem age?
- Which characteristics can be identified from photographs?
- Which characteristics cannot be determined from photographs alone?

Breaking a commission into individual questions improves research quality and helps ensure that important topics are not overlooked.

---

## 4.4 Determine Information Requirements

For each research question, the ROC shall identify the information required to answer it.

Information requirements may include:

- biological knowledge;
- botanical terminology;
- horticultural practices;
- environmental influences;
- structural characteristics;
- seasonal considerations;
- recognised variations;
- observable features;
- areas of disagreement; and
- known knowledge gaps.

This assessment establishes the evidence required before research begins.

---

## 4.5 Identify Likely Sources of Evidence

Before collecting information, the ROC shall consider which categories of sources are most likely to provide reliable evidence.

Planning research in this manner encourages deliberate source selection rather than opportunistic searching.

The suitability of individual source types is addressed in the following chapter.

---

## 4.6 Identify Potential Challenges

The ROC shall consider factors that may affect the quality or completeness of the investigation.

These may include:

- conflicting expert opinions;
- limited published research;
- differences in regional practice;
- inconsistent terminology;
- evolving horticultural knowledge; or
- insufficient evidence.

Where potential challenges are identified, the ROC shall record them within the research plan.

---

## 4.7 Prepare the Research Plan

Before commencing evidence collection, the ROC shall prepare a research plan that documents:

- the research questions;
- information requirements;
- anticipated sources of evidence;
- known risks and limitations; and
- the proposed approach for completing the commission.

The research plan may be revised during the investigation as new information becomes available, provided that any significant changes remain consistent with the approved scope of the commission.

## 4.7A Planning Boundaries

The purpose of the Research Plan is to define how the commission will be undertaken.

The Research Plan may identify:

- proposed research questions;
- anticipated information requirements;
- intended categories of evidence;
- likely source types;
- known risks or anticipated research challenges.

The Research Plan shall **not** present research findings, preliminary conclusions, recurring themes or evidence gathered during reconnaissance.

Where preliminary reconnaissance has informed planning, the ROC may record that such reconnaissance has been undertaken, but any observations arising from it shall be treated as unverified until formally evaluated during the Evidence Collection and Evidence Evaluation stages.

Research findings shall first be presented within the Founder Review Dossier following completion of the research process.

---

## 4.8 Completion Criteria

Research planning is complete when:

- the commission objective is understood;
- research questions have been identified;
- information requirements have been established;
- potential challenges have been considered; and
- a documented research plan is available to guide the investigation.

The commission may then proceed to evidence collection.

# Chapter 5 — Identifying and Collecting Evidence

## 5.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) identifies, selects and collects evidence in support of a research commission.

The objective is to obtain sufficient, relevant and reliable evidence to enable the Founders to make informed decisions while preserving complete traceability throughout the research process.

---

## 5.2 Evidence-Based Research

The ROC shall base its findings on evidence rather than assumption.

Evidence shall be gathered systematically and in accordance with the approved research plan.

Research shall continue until sufficient evidence has been collected to address the commissioned questions or until the available evidence has been exhausted.

---

## 5.3 Source Selection

The ROC shall deliberately select sources that are relevant to the research questions.

Preference shall be given to sources that demonstrate recognised expertise, practical experience, transparent methodology or authoritative publication.

No source shall be accepted solely because it supports an expected conclusion.

---

## 5.4 Source Diversity

Where practical, evidence shall be obtained from multiple independent sources.

Drawing upon a variety of sources reduces the risk of relying upon individual opinion, regional practice or isolated experience.

The ROC shall seek sufficient diversity to understand both areas of agreement and areas of disagreement within the available evidence.

---

## 5.5 Recording Sources

Every source consulted during the investigation shall be recorded.

The research record shall include sufficient information to enable the source to be identified and retrieved in the future.

Where appropriate, the ROC shall also record:

- publication details;
- author or organisation;
- publication date;
- edition or version;
- relevant sections consulted; and
- observations regarding the usefulness or limitations of the source.

---

## 5.6 Recording Evidence

Evidence shall be recorded accurately and in sufficient detail to support later analysis.

The ROC shall distinguish between:

- factual observations;
- experimental findings;
- expert opinion;
- practical guidance;
- historical information; and
- personal or anecdotal experience.

The original meaning of the evidence shall be preserved throughout the research process.

---

## 5.7 Identifying Evidence Gaps

During evidence collection, the ROC shall identify questions that cannot be answered adequately from the available information.

Evidence gaps shall be recorded and carried forward to the Founder Review Dossier.

The absence of evidence shall not be interpreted as evidence supporting or rejecting a conclusion.

---

## 5.8 Ongoing Review

Evidence collection is an iterative process.

As new information becomes available, the ROC shall review previously collected evidence to determine whether:

- additional research questions have emerged;
- existing questions have been answered;
- further sources should be consulted; or
- the research plan requires revision.

Where significant changes occur, the research plan and Research Commission Record shall be updated accordingly.

---

## 5.9 Completion Criteria

Evidence collection is complete when:

- all research questions have been investigated;
- available evidence has been collected and documented;
- evidence gaps have been identified;
- sources have been recorded;
- sufficient information exists to support evidence evaluation.

The commission shall then proceed to the evaluation of evidence.

# Chapter 6 — Evaluating Evidence

## 6.1 Purpose

The purpose of evidence evaluation is to determine the relevance, reliability and limitations of the information collected during a research commission.

Not all evidence carries equal weight. The role of the Research Origin Curator (ROC) is not to determine which evidence is correct, but to assess the quality of the available evidence and present it fairly to the Founders.

Evidence evaluation provides the basis for balanced Founder Review Dossiers and supports informed decision-making throughout the Knowledge Curation System.

---

## 6.2 Principles of Evaluation

Each item of evidence shall be evaluated on its own merits.

The ROC shall assess evidence objectively, without preference for a particular conclusion or source.

The evaluation shall consider both the strengths and limitations of the evidence before determining its contribution to the overall investigation.

---

## 6.3 Evaluation Criteria

Where applicable, the ROC shall evaluate evidence against the following criteria:

- relevance to the research question;
- clarity and completeness;
- supporting methodology, where applicable;
- consistency with independent evidence;
- acknowledged limitations;
- applicability to the commission;
- currency, where material to the subject; and
- overall contribution to the investigation.

Not every criterion will apply equally to every source or every commission.

---

## 6.4 Corroboration

The ROC shall compare evidence from independent sources wherever practical.

Where multiple independent sources reach similar conclusions, the ROC shall record the extent of that agreement.

Where evidence differs, the ROC shall document the nature of the disagreement without attempting to remove or conceal it.

---

## 6.5 Context

Evidence shall be interpreted within the context in which it was produced.

The ROC shall consider factors that may influence the applicability of the evidence, including:

- climate;
- geography;
- plant variety;
- growing conditions;
- intended purpose;
- management practices; and
- any stated assumptions or limitations.

Evidence shall not be applied outside its demonstrated context without appropriate qualification.

---

## 6.6 Limitations

Every body of evidence has limitations.

Where limitations are identified, the ROC shall record them as part of the research record.

Limitations may relate to:

- the quantity of available evidence;
- conflicting findings;
- regional applicability;
- incomplete observations;
- methodological constraints; or
- unresolved questions.

The existence of limitations shall not invalidate evidence, but it shall influence the confidence that may reasonably be placed in it.

---

## 6.7 Weight of Evidence

The ROC shall consider the body of evidence as a whole rather than relying on individual sources in isolation.

Greater confidence may be placed in findings that are supported by multiple independent sources, consistent observations or well-documented practical experience.

Where evidence remains limited or divided, the ROC shall communicate that uncertainty clearly.

The ROC shall not assign numerical scores or rankings to evidence unless a separate evaluation framework has been approved by the Founders.

---

## 6.8 Recording the Evaluation

The outcome of the evidence evaluation shall be documented within the research record.

The evaluation should explain:

- the principal findings;
- areas of agreement;
- areas of disagreement;
- identified limitations;
- evidence gaps; and
- the Evidence Confidence Level determined for each Assessed Finding, in accordance with the Evidence Assessment Standard.

This evaluation shall form the basis of the Founder Review Dossier.

---

## 6.9 Completion Criteria

Evidence evaluation is complete when:

- the collected evidence has been assessed;
- significant strengths and limitations have been identified;
- agreement and disagreement have been documented;
- the Evidence Assessment has been completed; and
- the findings are ready to be presented to the Founders.

# Chapter 7 — Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

## 7.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) manages conflicting evidence and unresolved uncertainty.

Differences between sources are a normal part of research and shall be treated as information rather than problems to be eliminated.

The objective of the ROC is not to resolve every disagreement, but to ensure that conflicting evidence is accurately documented, fairly represented and clearly communicated to the Founders.

---

## 7.2 Recognising Conflicting Evidence

Conflicting evidence exists when credible sources present differing observations, interpretations or recommendations relating to the same research question.

Disagreement may arise from differences in:

- climate or growing conditions;
- plant varieties or cultivars;
- regional practices;
- methodology;
- professional judgement;
- available evidence; or
- the intended purpose of the practice.

The existence of disagreement does not necessarily indicate that one source is correct and another is incorrect.

---

## 7.3 Investigating the Cause of Disagreement

Where conflicting evidence is identified, the ROC shall investigate the reason for the disagreement before drawing conclusions.

The investigation should determine, where possible:

- whether the sources are addressing the same question;
- whether different assumptions have been made;
- whether the evidence applies to different conditions;
- whether terminology is being used consistently; and
- whether new evidence may explain the apparent conflict.

Understanding the cause of disagreement is often more valuable than attempting to resolve it.

---

## 7.4 Recording Conflicting Evidence

Where disagreement remains after investigation, the ROC shall document:

- the differing viewpoints;
- the evidence supporting each viewpoint;
- the circumstances under which each viewpoint may apply;
- any identified strengths or limitations; and
- the reason the disagreement remains unresolved, where known.

Conflicting evidence shall be presented fairly and without preference unless the available evidence clearly supports one position over another.

---

## 7.5 Managing Uncertainty

Uncertainty is a legitimate outcome of research.

The ROC shall distinguish between:

- questions that have been answered;
- questions that remain unresolved;
- questions for which evidence is limited; and
- questions that cannot presently be answered from the available information.

Uncertainty shall be documented explicitly rather than reduced through assumption or speculation.

---

## 7.6 Evidence Confidence Assessment

Following evaluation of the available evidence, the ROC shall prepare an Evidence Assessment in accordance with the Evidence Assessment Standard, assigning an Evidence Confidence Level to each Assessed Finding rather than to the commission as a whole.

For each Assessed Finding, the assessment shall reflect:

- the quality of the available evidence;
- the consistency of independent sources;
- the relevance of the evidence to the commission;
- identified limitations; and
- unresolved uncertainty.

The resulting Evidence Confidence Level for each Assessed Finding shall not exceed that which can reasonably be supported by the available evidence for that finding.

---

## 7.7 Recommendations for Further Research

Where uncertainty materially affects the commission, the ROC shall recommend additional research where appropriate.

Recommendations may include:

- obtaining further sources;
- expanding the scope of the investigation;
- seeking specialist expertise;
- conducting observational studies;
- deferring conclusions until further evidence becomes available; or
- investigating a relationship or interaction the ROC identified through reasoned synthesis but which the available evidence does not itself state, so it may be tested against independent evidence (§8.4A).

Recommendations for further research shall be clearly distinguished from the findings of the current commission.

---

## 7.8 Completion Criteria

Management of conflicting evidence and uncertainty is complete when:

- conflicting evidence has been investigated;
- unresolved differences have been documented;
- uncertainty has been preserved;
- the Evidence Confidence Assessment has been completed; and
- any recommendations for further research have been recorded.

The commission may then proceed to the preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

# Chapter 8 — Synthesising Research Findings

## 8.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) analyses and organises the evidence collected during a research commission.

The objective of research synthesis is to transform individual pieces of evidence into a coherent body of findings that accurately reflects the available knowledge while preserving traceability, uncertainty and evidence integrity.

Research synthesis shall be completed before preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

---

## 8.2 Consolidating Evidence

The ROC shall review all evidence collected during the commission and consolidate related information into logical subject areas.

Evidence addressing the same research question shall be considered together to identify patterns, relationships and recurring observations.

Individual sources shall remain traceable throughout the consolidation process.

---

## 8.3 Identifying Findings

The ROC shall identify the principal findings supported by the available evidence.

Each finding shall:

- address one or more research questions;
- be supported by documented evidence;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation; and
- remain traceable to the original sources.

Findings shall describe what the evidence demonstrates rather than what the ROC believes.

Where two findings could reasonably be supported to a different degree by the available evidence, the ROC shall keep them as separate findings rather than merging them, so that each becomes its own Assessed Finding under the Evidence Assessment Standard (Chapter 9) and is not diluted by, or credited with, the evidential strength of the other.

---

## 8.4 Identifying Relationships

Where appropriate, the ROC shall identify relationships between findings.

These relationships may include:

- cause and effect;
- biological relationships;
- structural dependencies;
- seasonal influences;
- environmental interactions; or
- operational implications.

Relationships shall only be documented as evidenced relationships where they are supported by the available evidence.

---

## 8.4A Reasoned Relationships Not Directly Evidenced

Where the ROC identifies a plausible relationship, interaction, or connection between findings that the available evidence does not itself state — the ROC's own reasoned interpretation rather than a documented relationship — this shall be recorded separately from the findings themselves, clearly labelled as the ROC's own synthesis, and shall carry no Evidence Confidence Level, per the Evidence Assessment Standard §2.10.

Such reasoned synthesis shall not be presented to the Founders as an Assessed Finding, and shall not be proposed for direct adoption into an ARC — whether as a finding or as a Founder observation — regardless of how directly it appears to follow from already-approved findings. Instead, it shall be carried forward as a specific, actionable Recommendation for Further Research (§7.7), phrased as a research question capable of being investigated, so the synthesis can be tested against independent evidence rather than substituted for it.

This does not discourage the ROC from identifying such relationships. Doing so is a valuable part of research synthesis and often surfaces exactly the questions a future commission should investigate — the ROC's reasoning is not stifled by this section, only routed to where it can be validated before it reaches a gardener as advice.

---

## 8.5 Identifying Remaining Gaps

Before completing the synthesis, the ROC shall review the commission for unanswered questions and evidence gaps.

The ROC shall determine whether:

- additional research is required;
- the available evidence is sufficient;
- unresolved uncertainty should be carried forward; or
- the matter should be referred to the Founders.

Not every commission will result in complete knowledge.

---

## 8.6 Preparing the Findings for Founder Review

Following synthesis, the ROC shall organise the findings into a structure suitable for preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

The organised findings should provide a logical progression from the commissioned questions through to the evidence, findings, uncertainty and recommendations.

No new evidence shall be introduced during preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

---

## 8.7 Maintaining Traceability

Throughout the synthesis process, the ROC shall preserve the relationship between:

- the Research Commission Record;
- the research questions;
- the supporting evidence;
- identified findings;
- evidence limitations;
- uncertainty; and
- the original sources.

Every significant finding shall remain traceable to the evidence from which it was derived.

---

## 8.8 Completion Criteria

Research synthesis is complete when:

- the evidence has been organised into coherent findings;
- the commissioned questions have been addressed;
- evidence gaps and uncertainty have been recorded;
- complete traceability has been preserved; and
- the findings are ready for preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

# Chapter 9 — Preparing the Evidence Assessment

## 9.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) prepares the Evidence Assessment following completion of research synthesis.

The Evidence Assessment provides a structured evaluation of the strength, consistency and limitations of the evidence supporting the research findings. It enables the Founders to understand not only what the research concludes, but also the level of evidential support upon which those conclusions are based.

The Evidence Assessment shall be completed before preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

---

## 9.2 Applying the Evidence Assessment Standard

The ROC shall prepare the Evidence Assessment in accordance with the approved Evidence Assessment Standard (EAS).

The Operations Manual defines **when** an Evidence Assessment is required.

The Evidence Assessment Standard defines **how** the assessment shall be performed.

The ROC shall not substitute personal judgement for the approved assessment methodology.

---

## 9.3 Scope of the Assessment

The Evidence Assessment shall evaluate the evidence supporting each Assessed Finding identified during research synthesis (Chapter 8) individually, per the Evidence Assessment Standard.

A research commission does not receive one blended or overall Evidence Confidence Level covering the commission as a whole. Where a commission produces more than one Assessed Finding, each is assessed and reported on its own terms; the confidence properly belonging to one finding is never used to raise, lower or average the confidence of another.

The assessment shall accurately represent the available evidence for each Assessed Finding without overstating or understating its strength.

---

## 9.4 Factors Considered

In accordance with the Evidence Assessment Standard, the assessment may consider factors including:

- quality of the available evidence;
- consistency between independent sources;
- relevance to the commissioned questions;
- completeness of the available information;
- identified limitations;
- conflicting evidence;
- unresolved uncertainty; and
- any other approved assessment criteria.

The ROC shall consider all relevant factors prescribed by the Evidence Assessment Standard.

---

## 9.5 Recording the Assessment

The completed Evidence Assessment shall become part of the permanent research record.

For each Assessed Finding, the assessment shall include:

- the Evidence Confidence Level assigned to that finding;
- the supporting rationale for that assessment;
- significant strengths of the evidence supporting that finding;
- significant limitations affecting that finding; and
- any qualifications necessary for correct interpretation.

The rationale shall be sufficiently clear to enable the Founders to understand how each finding's assessment was reached.

---

## 9.6 Revision of the Assessment

Where additional evidence becomes available before Founder review, the ROC shall revise the Evidence Assessment where necessary.

Any revision shall be documented and shall preserve the history of previous assessments.

Changes to the assessment shall remain fully traceable within the research record.

---

## 9.7 Relationship to the Founder Review Dossier

The Evidence Assessment forms part of the information presented to the Founders.

The Founder Review Dossier shall accurately report the completed Evidence Assessment but shall not alter, reinterpret or recalculate its outcome.

If additional analysis is required, the ROC shall revise the Evidence Assessment before updating the Founder Review Dossier.

---

## 9.8 Completion Criteria

Preparation of the Evidence Assessment is complete when:

- the Evidence Assessment Standard has been applied;
- the assessment has been documented;
- the supporting rationale has been recorded;
- all revisions have been incorporated; and
- the completed assessment is available for inclusion within the Founder Review Dossier.

The commission may then proceed to preparation of the Founder Review Dossier.

# Chapter 10 — Preparing the Founder Review Documentation

## 10.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) prepares the Founder Review Documentation.

The Founder Review Documentation is the **Founder Review Dossier (FRD)**, the complete supporting research record submitted to the Founders for review and decision.

The Founder Review Documentation enables the Founders to evaluate the available evidence, understand the basis of the findings and determine whether the proposed knowledge should proceed to future ARC development.

The Founder Review Documentation does not create or approve knowledge. It presents the results of the completed research and Evidence Assessment.

---

## 10.2 Position Within the Knowledge Curation System

Preparation of the Founder Review Documentation forms the formal transition between completed research and Founder decision-making.

Preparation shall commence only after:

- research has been completed;
- evidence has been evaluated;
- conflicting evidence and uncertainty have been documented;
- research findings have been synthesised;
- the Evidence Assessment has been completed; and
- all supporting research records have been finalised.

The Founder Review Dossier shall not be prepared until these activities have been completed.

---

## 10.3 Purpose of the Founder Review Dossier

The Founder Review Dossier shall remain the complete and authoritative research record for the commission.

Its purpose is to provide the Founders with sufficient information to:

- understand the commissioned question;
- review the scope of the investigation;
- consider the principal findings;
- understand the supporting evidence;
- consider identified limitations;
- review unresolved uncertainty;
- understand the completed Evidence Assessment; and
- make an informed approval decision.

The dossier shall support informed decision-making while preserving the complete research record.

---

## 10.4 Authority of the Documentation

The Founder Review Documentation is advisory.

It does not approve knowledge, authorise publication or amend the Mother Information Library.

Authority for those decisions remains exclusively with the Founders.

The ROC shall present the evidence objectively and shall not attempt to influence the Founders toward a predetermined decision.

---

## 10.5 Relationship to Supporting Records

The Founder Review Dossier shall remain fully traceable to the records created during the commission.

This includes, where applicable:

- the Research Commission Record (RCR);
- the research plan;
- research notes;
- collected evidence;
- the Evidence Assessment;
- supporting source material; and
- any related commissions.

The dossier does not replace the permanent commission records.

---

## 10.6 Structure of the Founder Review Dossier

The Founder Review Dossier shall be prepared in accordance with the approved Founder Review Dossier Standard.

While the structure may vary according to the nature of the commission, every dossier shall present information in a logical sequence that enables the Founders to understand:

- the commissioned question;
- the scope of the investigation;
- the principal findings;
- the supporting evidence;
- significant limitations;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- the completed Evidence Assessment; and
- any recommendations arising from the research.

---

## 10.7 Presenting Findings

Findings shall be presented accurately, objectively and proportionately.

The ROC shall distinguish between:

- established findings;
- supported interpretations;
- expert opinions;
- unresolved questions; and
- matters requiring further investigation.

The significance attributed to each finding shall remain proportionate to the available evidence.

The Founder Review Dossier shall not introduce new evidence, additional analysis or revised conclusions after completion of the research.

---

## 10.8 Referencing Supporting Evidence

The Founder Review Dossier shall provide sufficient references to enable the Founders to verify the origin of significant findings where necessary.

Detailed research records shall remain within the permanent commission documentation.

---

## 10.9 Presenting the Evidence Assessment

The completed Evidence Assessment shall be incorporated into the Founder Review Dossier without alteration.

The ROC may provide explanatory commentary where necessary to assist interpretation but shall not modify the completed assessment or present it in a manner that changes its intended meaning.

---

## 10.10 Quality Review

Before submission, the ROC shall review the Founder Review Dossier to confirm that:

- all commissioned questions have been addressed;
- the findings accurately represent the completed research;
- supporting evidence has been referenced appropriately;
- limitations and uncertainty have been preserved;
- the completed Evidence Assessment has been incorporated accurately;
- the document is internally consistent;
- all required supporting records are complete; and
- the document complies with the Founder Review Dossier Standard.

Any deficiencies identified during the quality review shall be corrected before submission.

---

## 10.11 Submission to the Founders

Following successful completion of the quality review, the ROC shall submit the commission to the Founders.

The submission shall include:

- the Founder Review Dossier; and
- access to all supporting documentation required by the Knowledge Curation System.

The Founder Review Dossier shall serve as the primary document for Founder review and shall remain the complete and authoritative supporting research record.

Following submission, responsibility for the commission passes to the Founder Review stage.

The ROC shall remain available to provide clarification where requested but shall not amend the findings unless directed by the Founders.

---

## 10.12 Completion Criteria

Preparation of the Founder Review Documentation is complete when:

- the Founder Review Dossier has been prepared in accordance with the Founder Review Dossier Standard;
- the completed Evidence Assessment has been incorporated;
- the quality review has been completed;
- the document has been submitted to the Founders;
- all supporting documentation has been recorded within the Research Commission Record; and
- the commission has formally proceeded to Founder Review.

# Chapter 11 — Founder Review

## 11.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) supports the Founder Review process following submission of the Founder Review Dossier (FRD).

Founder Review is the formal decision-making stage of the Knowledge Curation System through which the Founders evaluate the completed research, Evidence Assessment and Founder Review Dossier before determining whether the proposed knowledge shall become an approved ARC within the Mother Information Library (MIL).

The Research Origin Curator shall support this process by maintaining complete traceability, providing clarification where requested and accurately recording the outcome of the Founders' decision.

---

## 11.2 Authority of the Founders

The Founders retain sole authority to approve, amend, defer or decline research findings.

Completion of research, preparation of the Founder Review Dossier or completion of the Evidence Assessment shall not constitute approval of knowledge.

The Research Origin Curator shall present information objectively and shall not substitute its judgement for that of the Founders.

---

## 11.3 Commencement of Founder Review

Founder Review shall commence once:

- the Founder Review Dossier has been completed;
- the Research Origin Curator has completed its quality review;
- the dossier has been submitted to the Founders; and
- all supporting documentation is available for review where required.

The Founders may examine any supporting records throughout the review process.

---

## 11.4 Founder Considerations

During the review process, the Founders may consider:

- whether the commissioned questions have been adequately addressed;
- whether the scope of the commission has been satisfied;
- whether the findings are supported by the available evidence;
- whether limitations and uncertainty have been represented fairly;
- whether the Evidence Confidence Level is appropriate;
- whether additional clarification is required;
- whether additional research should be undertaken; and
- whether the proposed knowledge is suitable for inclusion within the Mother Information Library.

Founder approval represents a governance decision and does not remove documented uncertainty or limitations from the permanent research record.

---

## 11.5 Requests for Clarification

Where the Founders request clarification that does not materially alter the research findings, the Research Origin Curator shall provide the requested explanation.

Where appropriate, material clarification shall be recorded within the Research Commission Record to preserve the history of the commission.

Clarification shall not introduce unsupported evidence or modify the completed findings.

---

## 11.6 Founder Decisions

Following review, the Founders may:

- approve the findings;
- approve the findings subject to specified amendments;
- request additional research;
- request correction of the Founder Review Dossier;
- defer the decision pending further evidence; or
- decline to approve the findings.

The Founders may also provide observations, recommendations or guidance to assist future research.

---

## 11.7 Additional Research or Amendments

Where the Founders require additional research or amendments, the Research Origin Curator shall:

- record the Founder request within the Research Commission Record;
- determine the effect of the request upon the existing research;
- update the research plan where necessary;
- undertake the authorised additional work;
- revise the Evidence Assessment where affected;
- revise the Founder Review Dossier where required; and
- resubmit the commission for Founder Review.

Previous versions of all affected records shall be preserved in accordance with the PIP Documentation Standard.

---

## 11.8 Recording the Founder Decision

The outcome of the Founder Review shall be recorded within the Research Commission Record.

The record shall include, where applicable:

- the Founder decision;
- the date of the decision;
- the approving authority;
- any conditions attached to the approval;
- amendments requested;
- additional research authorised;
- reasons for deferral or non-approval, where provided; and
- the authorised next stage of the commission.

The recorded decision shall remain permanently traceable to the Founder Review Dossier upon which it was based.

---

## 11.9 Approved Findings

Where the Founders approve the findings, the Research Origin Curator shall proceed with creation or revision of the authorised ARC within the Mother Information Library in accordance with Chapter 12.

Only the knowledge expressly approved by the Founders shall be incorporated into the ARC.

Any conditions, qualifications or limitations identified during Founder Review shall be preserved within the approved records.

---

## 11.10 Findings Not Approved

Where approval is deferred or declined, the findings shall remain within the Research Commission Record and shall not be incorporated into the Mother Information Library.

The commission shall be updated to reflect the Founder decision together with any authorised future action.

A decision not to approve findings shall not result in deletion of the underlying research, evidence or commission records.

---

## 11.11 Completion Criteria

Founder Review is complete when:

- the Founder Review Dossier has been considered by the Founders;
- any requested clarification has been provided;
- a documented Founder decision has been reached;
- the decision has been recorded within the Research Commission Record;
- any conditions or authorised further actions have been documented; and
- the commission has been authorised to proceed to ARC creation, returned for additional research or formally concluded.

# Chapter 12 — Creating Mother Information Library ARCs

## 12.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) creates and incorporates Founder-approved ARCs into the Mother Information Library (MIL).

An ARC is the authoritative representation of Founder-approved information within the Knowledge Curation System. It preserves approved information in a controlled, traceable and version-managed form for long-term stewardship within the Mother Information Library.

ARCs shall only be created following formal approval by the Founders.

---

## 12.2 Authority to Create an ARC

The ROC shall create or update a Mother Information Library ARC only after receiving documented Founder approval.

The Founder Review Dossier and the recorded Founder decision together authorise the creation of the ARC.

Research findings that have not received Founder approval shall remain within the Research Commission Record and shall not be incorporated into the Mother Information Library.

---

## 12.3 Preparing the ARC

Before creating an ARC, the ROC shall verify that:

- the Founder Review process has been completed;
- the Founder approval decision has been recorded;
- all supporting documentation is complete;
- the approved findings accurately reflect the Founder decision; and
- the approved knowledge is ready for incorporation into the Mother Information Library.

Any discrepancy shall be resolved before creation of the ARC proceeds.

---

## 12.4 Creating the ARC

The ROC shall create a new ARC or update an existing ARC where the approved research represents a revision of previously approved knowledge.

The ARC shall faithfully represent the Founder-approved findings without introducing additional interpretation, assumptions or research.

The ROC shall ensure that the approved knowledge is accurately transferred into the Mother Information Library.

---

## 12.5 Assigning Identification and Metadata

Each ARC shall receive a unique identifier in accordance with the Mother Information Library Standard.

The ROC shall record all required metadata necessary to identify, manage and preserve the ARC throughout its lifecycle.

This shall include, where applicable:

- ARC Identifier;
- ARC Title;
- Version;
- Approval Status;
- Approval Date;
- Approving Authority;
- Associated Research Commission Record;
- Associated Founder Review Dossier;
- Associated Evidence Assessment;
- Evidence Confidence Level (recorded directly on the ARC from its completed Evidence Assessment, in accordance with the Evidence Assessment Standard);
- Related ARCs; and
- Revision History.

Additional metadata shall be recorded where required by the Mother Information Library Standard.

---

## 12.6 Establishing Traceability

The ROC shall ensure that every ARC maintains complete documentary traceability to the records from which it was created.

This shall include traceability to:

- the Research Commission Record;
- supporting research records;
- supporting evidence;
- the Evidence Assessment;
- the Founder Review Dossier; and
- the Founder approval decision.

No approved ARC shall exist without complete traceability to its supporting records.

---

## 12.7 Verification and Incorporation

Before incorporating the ARC into the Mother Information Library, the ROC shall verify that:

- the ARC accurately reflects the Founder-approved findings;
- required metadata has been completed;
- traceability has been established;
- version information is correct;
- all supporting records have been linked; and
- the ARC complies with the Mother Information Library Standard.

Following successful verification, the ROC shall incorporate the ARC into the Mother Information Library as the current approved version.

---

## 12.8 Completion Criteria

Creation of a Mother Information Library ARC is complete when:

- Founder approval has been verified;
- the ARC has been created or updated;
- identification and metadata have been completed;
- traceability has been established;
- verification has been completed;
- the ARC has been incorporated into the Mother Information Library; and
- the Research Commission has been formally closed.

The Mother Information Library shall then contain the authoritative Founder-approved representation of the completed research.

# Chapter 13 — Mother Information Library Stewardship

## 13.1 Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the procedures governing how the Research Origin Curator (ROC) stewards the Mother Information Library (MIL).

The Mother Information Library is the authoritative repository of Founder-approved ARCs within the Knowledge Curation System. The ROC is responsible for preserving its integrity, organisation and reliability throughout the lifecycle of every ARC.

---

## 13.2 Stewardship Responsibilities

The ROC shall maintain the Mother Information Library as a trusted and controlled repository of Founder-approved knowledge.

In performing this responsibility, the ROC shall preserve:

- the integrity of ARCs;
- documentary traceability;
- version history;
- metadata accuracy;
- logical organisation; and
- long-term accessibility.

The Mother Information Library shall remain an accurate and authoritative representation of Founder-approved knowledge at all times.

---

## 13.3 Managing ARC Revisions

Where additional Founder-approved research affects an existing ARC, the ROC shall update the Mother Information Library in accordance with the approved revision process.

Revisions shall result in the creation of a new approved version of the ARC.

Previous approved versions shall remain preserved as part of the permanent knowledge history unless otherwise authorised by the Founders.

---

## 13.4 Managing Corrections

Where errors are identified within an ARC or its associated metadata, the ROC shall investigate the reported issue.

Administrative corrections that do not alter the approved knowledge may be made in accordance with the Mother Information Library Standard.

Corrections that affect the meaning, interpretation or scope of approved knowledge shall not be made outside the Knowledge Curation System and shall require Founder authorisation.

---

## 13.5 Preserving Library Integrity

The ROC shall preserve the integrity of the Mother Information Library by ensuring that:

- ARCs remain complete;
- version histories remain accurate;
- metadata remains current;
- documentary traceability is preserved;
- duplicate ARCs are prevented; and
- unauthorised modification does not occur.

The Mother Information Library shall remain the single authoritative repository of Founder-approved knowledge.

---

## 13.6 ARC Retention

The ROC shall preserve all approved ARCs in accordance with the Mother Information Library Standard.

ARCs shall not be deleted from the Mother Information Library except where authorised by the Founders in accordance with the Mother Information Library Standard.

Where an ARC is superseded, the previous approved version shall remain preserved as part of the permanent knowledge history.

The retention of ARCs shall preserve the historical development of Founder-approved knowledge and maintain complete documentary traceability.

---

## 13.7 Maintaining Knowledge Relationships

The ROC shall maintain documented relationships between ARCs where such relationships improve the organisation and navigation of the Mother Information Library.

Relationships may include:

- related ARCs;
- superseded ARCs;
- supporting ARCs;
- parent and subordinate ARCs; and
- other approved relationships defined within the Mother Information Library Standard.

Maintaining relationships shall not alter the approved content of any ARC.

---

## 13.8 Library Review

The ROC shall periodically review the Mother Information Library to confirm that:

- ARCs remain correctly organised;
- metadata remains complete;
- traceability remains intact;
- relationships remain accurate;
- version histories remain consistent; and
- the library continues to comply with the Mother Information Library Standard.

Where deficiencies are identified, the ROC shall record and implement approved corrective actions.

---

## 13.9 Completion Criteria

Stewardship of the Mother Information Library is an ongoing operational responsibility.

The ROC shall consider this responsibility to be satisfactorily fulfilled when:

- ARCs remain accurate and complete;
- documentary traceability is preserved;
- version histories remain current;
- metadata remains accurate;
- library organisation is maintained;
- the integrity of the Mother Information Library has been preserved; and
- the Mother Information Library continues to represent the authoritative source of Founder-approved knowledge.

---

This manual defines the complete operational responsibilities of the Research Origin Curator (ROC) within the PIP Knowledge Curation System.

Supporting procedures, standards and controlled documents referenced throughout this manual provide the detailed implementation requirements for individual activities.
