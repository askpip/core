## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on crossing or rubbing stems on an established, dormant bush rose: recognition and definition (Q1), visible indicators (Q2), what a photograph can and cannot establish (Q3), lookalikes (Q4), pruning choices and which stem to remove (Q5), handling a stem with more than one condition and deferral (Q6), and task-specific safety (Q7).

It supports the **crossing or rubbing stems** supported observation in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 Architecture §5.2, one of the six observations the pruning journey addresses once a rose has passed the recently-planted and dormancy gates (`ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`) and dead wood has been addressed (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`). General cut mechanics (distance above a bud, angle, tools, sealing) remain governed by `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`; this ARC's findings are limited to where the cut is made (AF-6) and which of two crossing canes is chosen (AF-7).

This ARC is the source from which KIT will build the crossing/rubbing-stems Observation PKR.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each Assessed Finding is reproduced here faithfully from the Founder-approved Evidence Assessment in `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-02` §8, without new interpretation, per ROC Operations Manual §12.4.

### AF-1 — Crossing and Rubbing Stems Are Treated as a Related Pair

**Finding:** Where sources address crossing and rubbing stems at all, the great majority treat them as a linked pair — often in the same sentence or instruction — rather than as two fully independent conditions with separate rationale.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Independent institutional sources across UK, US and general-arboriculture publisher types converge on treating the two as linked. Several frequently-cited sources address crossing but never use the word "rubbing," so the pairing is not universal.

**Sources:** RHS; Kansas City Rose Society; Oregon State University Extension / University of Illinois Extension (shared text); Texas A&M AgriLife.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether sources that omit "rubbing" consider it implied, or genuinely do not address stem-to-stem contact, was not tested.

### AF-2 — Rubbing Contact Damages Cane Tissue and Creates a Disease-Entry Point

**Finding:** Where sources give a reason for removing rubbing stems, the reason given is that contact between canes damages the cane tissue, which allows disease to enter.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Two independent institutional statements agree on the same causal mechanism; most sources that instruct removal give no reason at all.

**Sources:** Kansas City Rose Society; Oregon State University Extension / University of Illinois Extension.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** No source describes what the resulting damage actually looks like (§6, U-1). Whether the disease-entry risk has been measured, or is a stated but untested rationale, was not addressed.

### AF-3 — Standard (Tree-Form) Roses: One Source Treats Crossing Canes as Acceptable

**Finding:** For standard (tree-form) roses specifically — outside this ARC's bush-rose scope — one source states that crossing canes in the head are "generally not a problem," because growers are prioritising bloom count over an open structure.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low (single source).

**Rationale:** Stated clearly and with a reason, but by a single source and concerning a growth-form outside this ARC's and `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`'s scope.

**Sources:** Rose Society of South Australia, "Rose Pruning – Main Points."

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Recorded for completeness and future reference; does not affect bush-rose guidance.

### AF-4 — A Single Photograph Has Real Limits for Judging Structural Questions of This Kind

**Finding:** No rose-specific source addresses what a photograph can or cannot establish about crossing or rubbing stems. Drawing on general professional pruning practice and one directly relevant academic study, a single, fixed-viewpoint photograph has real limits for judging whether two stems that appear to intersect are actually in contact — the kind of 3D structural question professional practice addresses by physically viewing a plant from multiple sides before cutting.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low (a ROC synthesis of general, non-rose-specific evidence, extrapolated per EAS §2.10).

**Rationale:** Extension guidance independently and consistently treats multi-angle physical viewing as standard practice; a peer-reviewed urban-forestry study directly tests the underlying principle, though for urban trees, not roses.

**Sources:** NC State Extension; University of Missouri Extension (Master Gardener Core Manual); Berland et al. (2019), *Forests* 10(4):349.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** No source states this principle for crossing/rubbing stems specifically. Whether a second photograph specifically resolves the ambiguity was not tested.

### AF-5 — Once Confirmed, a Crossing or Rubbing Stem Is Removed as Routine Maintenance

**Finding:** With near-unanimous support across the source base, once a crossing or rubbing stem is confirmed, it is removed as ordinary dormant-season pruning — not left, deferred, or referred to a professional.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Rationale:** Broad, independent corroboration across every publisher type relied upon in this project (UK national charity, US national and regional/local societies, US university extension services, a federal forestry agency), unanimous in direction.

**Sources:** RHS; Kansas City Rose Society; Clemson; University of Arkansas; Virginia Cooperative Extension; Texas A&M AgriLife; American Rose Society ("Ten Principles"); USDA Forest Service; University of Missouri Extension; UF/IFAS Leon County; Johnson County K-State Extension.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Standard (tree-form) roses are a stated exception, outside scope (AF-3). This is the best-attested finding produced by this commission.

### AF-6 — Removal Is a Full Cut to the Stem's Point of Origin

**Finding:** Where sources specify where the cut is made, a crossing or rubbing stem is removed entirely — back to the ground, the bud union, or the larger cane it originates from — not merely shortened.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Six independent sources agree, unanimous in direction, including two general-terminology sources that define the cut type explicitly.

**Sources:** Kansas City Rose Society; American Rose Society ("Ten Principles"); Portland Rose Society; University of Missouri Extension; University of Arkansas Extension; USDA Forest Service.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Not every source that instructs removal specifies exactly where the cut is made. Consistent with, and does not duplicate, `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`'s general cut-mechanics findings.

### AF-7 — A Given Tie-Break Rule Favours the Stronger Cane

**Finding:** The great majority of sources that instruct removing a crossing or rubbing cane give no criterion at all for choosing which of the two. Among the small number that do, the criterion is vigour- or size-based — remove the weaker, keep the stronger.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Rationale:** Two independent sources agree on the general direction, corroborating a separately-recorded tie-break rule in `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` (AF-33).

**Sources:** UF/IFAS Leon County Extension; Portland Rose Society.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Only two sources give any explicit rule at all; of roughly sixteen sources addressing crossing-cane removal, thirteen give none.

### AF-8 — Deferral Guidance Concerns General Pruning Timing

**Finding:** Where sources advise waiting or deferring a pruning decision, the reason given is general dormant-season timing (avoiding cold-damage dieback, waiting until buds break enough to distinguish live from dead wood) — not anything specific to a confirmed crossing or rubbing stem.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Three independent university-extension sources agree on the general principle and its reasoning; none ties deferral to crossing or rubbing specifically.

**Sources:** Oregon State University Extension; Iowa State University Extension (Linn County); Colorado State University Extension.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether a gardener should ever defer specifically because they are unsure which of two crossing canes to remove was not addressed by any source.

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Points of Variation

**C-1 — Whether an open, uncrowded bush centre is a universally desirable goal.** The great majority of sources treat an open centre as self-evidently desirable. One source (Rose Society of South Australia) explicitly argues against deliberately opening the centre, on the basis that a healthy plant fills gaps on its own and interior canes provide beneficial shading. Preserved as unresolved; AF-1 records the majority position. Practical default: §6.

**C-2 — Selection criterion for which of two crossing canes to remove.** Of roughly sixteen sources instructing removal, thirteen give no selection criterion at all; two give a vigour-based criterion (AF-7). Preserved as a genuine thinness in the evidence. Practical default: §6.

**C-3 — Standard (tree-form) roses versus bush roses.** A scope boundary, not a live conflict: standard/tree-form roses are outside the bush-rose scope this ARC and `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` both define. Recorded as AF-3 for completeness.

## 6. Approved Practical Defaults and Recommendations Carried Forward

The following ROC-recommended defaults were adopted for questions the evidence left open, and are approved for operational use alongside the Assessed Findings above. Each may be overridden by a Founder at any time.

- **What an actual rubbing wound looks like:** treat visible bark damage, wear, or a flattened contact face where two canes visibly touch as sufficient confirmation of "rubbing"; where stems are close but show no visible contact or wear, treat the condition as "crossing" rather than "rubbing."
- **Beginner lookalikes and confounders:** no specific confounder list is offered; confirming any observation on the physical rose before acting is the primary safeguard.
- **Priority when a stem is both crossing/rubbing and inward-growing:** no default is needed — every source found that addresses either condition recommends the same action (cut).
- **Whether professional/experienced help is ever specifically warranted:** governed by the app's general uncertainty/confidence gates, not by a rule specific to crossing or rubbing.
- **Whether a second photograph from a different angle would help:** where a first photograph leaves confidence low on a structural judgment of this kind (AF-4), request a second photograph from a different angle as reasonable operational practice.
- **Task-specific safety for reaching into congested/tangled growth:** cross-refer to `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`'s approved generic safety content as the primary guidance; additionally, take care not to damage a stem being kept while removing one being discarded from tangled growth, and consider cutting a tangled stem in sections rather than pulling it whole (extrapolated from a non-bush-rose source; Low-confidence by nature).
- **Whether crossing/rubbing and inward-growing stems commonly co-occur:** no source states or tests this, and no default is needed — it does not affect the gate logic for either observation. This is a permanent limitation of the evidence, not a pending research item.

**Not yet actioned:** whether the two remaining undrafted MVP observations (damaged growth; weak or congested growth) should be commissioned next, using this same research approach, is a separate Founder decision not resolved by this ARC.
