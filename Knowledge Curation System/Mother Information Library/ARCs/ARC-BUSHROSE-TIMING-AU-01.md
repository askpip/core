## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative record of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission `BUSHROSE-TIMING-AU`:

- **Q1** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band, and what local signal do they tie it to?
- **Q2** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
- **Q3** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
- **Q4** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
- **Q5** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where a winter spray is described, when; and where winter protection is described, when it goes on and comes off? Product names, brands and rates are not collected as findings.
- **Q6** Which climate zones do Australian rose sources use for timing, how are they bounded, and which zones do sources say suit Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses at all?
- **Q7** Which zone could a beginner place themselves in, using the region they enter in Ask Pip, their GPS location, or by naming their zone?
- **Q8** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band? Do they prefer local signals over fixed months?

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Australian sources only. Every finding records the band or state it applies to. Months from one band are not applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are not converted. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5). The months do not replace the bud-swell Suitability Gate in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.

Q5 as commissioned left products out. Under the standing decisions "Products" and "Exclusions" (Pip Knowledge Rules §7), the types of spray the pages name were recorded at the Build Check; no brand and no mixing amount is.

It is the source from which KIT wrote what Pip says about which months to do each rose job in Australia.

#### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — Zone names and boundaries (AF-1, AF-2, AF-3).** OGO calls Melbourne, Canberra and Hobart "Cold Temperate" and Sydney, Perth and Adelaide "Warm Temperate"; TSC lists the same towns as "Cool" and "Temperate". PEN groups Adelaide with Melbourne, Canberra and Hobart as "Cool Southern Zones". OGO and TSC place Alice Springs, Mildura, Albury-Wodonga and Shepparton in an arid zone; BOM-CZ: "Places like Brisbane and Alice Springs are considered subtropical." The schemes are drawn on different principles (gardening experience against latitude and native vegetation). Unresolved; Founder Decision Point 2.

**C-2 — Can main pruning begin in June (AF-11, AF-13)?** June: RSV-PB and GA-RP08 "June or July"; NFP "June/July"; ROSS-CAL "Pruning may commence in late June"; RSNSW-G "late June to the end of July" near the coast; RSWA prunes under "WINTER (June - July)". July: RSV-M (the same society as RSV-PB) lists "Start pruning roses" under July; SARS-WIN "Pruning in July is recommended and avoid the temptation to prune too early"; GAM and GA-MAG "July is the ideal time to prune roses in most areas". Unresolved.

**C-3 — When cold or frosty areas prune (AF-4, AF-14, AF-15).** "early August" (RSV-PB); "from the beginning to the end of August" (RSNSW-G); "late August" (GA-FAQ); "the end of winter" (GAM); "late July or early August" (HSOC-AUT) and "this month or in early September" (HSOC-AUG, August), both Canberra; "August in frost areas" (RSSA-GG). **C-4 — When spring feeding starts (AF-19, AF-20, AF-21).** Latter half of August (RSNSW-CAL); end of August (RSV-M); early September (RSSA-GG, SARS-CAL); September as the main feed (ROSS-CAL); "as new growth appears" (RSWA); shoots "around 2cms in length" (SARS-SPR); "about three weeks after you've pruned" (GA-RP08); "approximately mid Spring", after "the first blooms" (TRE-CAL). Unresolved.

**C-5 — When the last feed is given (AF-22, AF-23, AF-24).** "mid to late February" (RSNSW-G); February, or March if missed (SARS-CAL, SARS-AUT); "early Autumn. ### 8.1 Source characteristics that limit independence or weight

- **TSC, PEN, TRE-CAL, TRE-T, ROSS-CAL, ROSS-SP and FP** are commercial. Nurseries grow and sell roses and write from that experience, but they are not independent authorities. TRE-CAL and TRE-T are one company; so are ROSS-CAL and ROSS-SP.
- **RSNSW-G and RSNSW-CAL** are one society; **RSV-M and RSV-PB** are one society; **SARS-CAL, SARS-WIN, SARS-AUT, SARS-SPR, RSSA-GG and SARS-POT** are one society; **HSOC-AUG and HSOC-AUT** are one society. Each society counts as one voice.
- **GAM and GA-MAG** contain the same sentence on July pruning and count as one voice for it. **GA-FAQ, GA-RP08, GA-TROP and GA-SUB** are television segments from the same broadcaster by four presenters, reporting presenters' or featured growers' practice.
- **RSV-PB and GA-RP08** contain the same two sentences word for word ("The best time to prune is in June or July. But if you live in a really cold area of Australia, then wait until early August ..."). They count as one voice for that statement.
- **OGO and TSC** list the same towns in each zone under different zone names; they appear to use one scheme and count as one voice for the town lists. OGO is a general gardening magazine and TSC a seed retailer; neither is rose-specific in its zone definitions.
- **GA-TROP** reports one Northern Territory garden (2006). **GA-SUB** reports one rose farm near Brisbane (2018). **NFP** is one public rose garden.
- **OGTR** is a 2009 regulatory biology document, not a gardening guide; its cultivation statements are brief.
- **HSOC-AUG** is undated in its text and was last modified in 2019.
- **BOM-CZ, BOM-FROST and BOM-WIF** are meteorological products, not gardening advice.
- **RSWA** gives seasons with months in brackets rather than per-month entries.
- **TSC** contains a planting-month grid for each zone whose marked months could not be read by the extraction step; the grid is not relied on.
- Found at the Build Check: GAM (the national broadcaster's magazine) and FP (one Sydney garden centre) have the same author, which §4.2 did not record; the two count as one voice. FP is an article for Sydney and the Hunter, and is treated as speaking for Sydney. GA-SUB and the Darwin-area item are television segments on one grower's own practice, which the Pip Knowledge Rules §4 do not count as a reliable source on their own. The Horticultural Society of Canberra's two pages are to be tried again; until one opens, nothing is said for Canberra.
- Noted for the Spraying and Planting records: The published answer on winter sprays (PKR-CGD-000013) names copper for Australia from its own research. In this dossier copper is named only on South Australian pages, so it is said here only in South Australia. The published answer on planting months (PKR-CGD-000016) covers the same ground as AF-31 and AF-32 in older wording; it is to be brought into line when these statements are published.
