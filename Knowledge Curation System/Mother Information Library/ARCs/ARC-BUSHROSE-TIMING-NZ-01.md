## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative record of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission `BUSHROSE-TIMING-NZ`:

- **Q1** Main winter pruning: months in each climate band, and the local signal sources tie it to.
- **Q2** Feeding: months to start in spring and to give the last feed, in each band.
- **Q3** Late-season cut-off: when to stop deadheading and summer pruning, in each band.
- **Q4** Planting and moving: months to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band.
- **Q5** Winter clear-up and protection: when to clear leaves and prunings; when any winter spray is done; when any winter protection goes on and comes off. Products, brands and rates are not findings.
- **Q6** Climate bands: how NZ sources divide the country, and whether an authoritative climate or frost source such as NIWA could support a band.
- **Q7** Placing the gardener: which bands a beginner could be placed in from region, town or GPS.
- **Q8** Year-to-year variation: how sources handle an early or late season or a garden near a band edge.

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting and moving for Q4. Every finding records its band. Months from one band are not applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are not converted. Months are context; they do not replace the bud-swell Suitability Gate in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

It is the source from which KIT wrote what Pip says about which months to do each rose job in New Zealand.

#### Climate areas and placing the gardener (Q6, Q7)

**AF-1 (established; national). As approved:** New Zealand rose sources say to prune later where it is colder: the north earlier than the south, and within Canterbury the milder parts earlier than colder areas. **High** (as submitted: National: NZ rose sources contrast the warmer north with the colder south, and milder with colder or frosty districts, and prune later where it is colder; High).

*Evidence as recorded:* NZ rose sources contrast the warmer north with the colder south, and milder with colder or frosty districts, and prune later where it is colder. NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July while in the colder parts of the South Island, it gets under-way in August." DUN-PT: "The general rule is the colder the climate, the later you prune in winter." PALM-YRC: "In the north, it is perfectly fine to start a bit earlier than this, and in the south a bit later." CHS-JUL: "In milder parts of Canterbury, pruning can begin towards the end of the month; colder areas may prefer to wait until August." CHS-AUG: "In colder inland districts, prune once the worst frosts have passed." Also TUI-PG, ODT-GL. DALT-G dissents (C-2).

*Build Check:* North against south, or against the colder parts of the South Island: NZRS-FAQ and PALM-YRC. The general rule, 'the colder the climate, the later you prune': DUN-PT. Within Canterbury only: CHS-JUL and CHS-AUG. TUI-PG's support is a reply to one Christchurch reader; its main article gives one window for everyone. No page speaks of milder or frosty districts of the whole country. Against it: DALT-G says cooler climates can prune earlier (AF-17, C-2).

**AF-2 (expert opinion, single source; national). As approved:** National: the NZ Rose Society splits spring peak bloom into upper North Island, lower North Island and South Island. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* NZRS-FAQ splits spring peak bloom three ways: "first two weeks in November for the upper North Island, last two weeks for the lower North Island and from the 3rd week in November to mid December in the South Island." This concerns flowering, not pruning or feeding.

*Build Check:* About when to see roses in flower, not about a care task. The page adds that there are regional variations and that each season is slightly different.

**AF-3 (unresolved question; national). As approved:** National: no NZ rose source located draws a fixed boundary for its climate bands. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* No NZ rose source located draws a fixed boundary for its bands. They use "up north" and "the colder parts of the South Island" (NZRS-FAQ), "In the north" and "in the south" (PALM-YRC), "milder parts of Canterbury" and "colder inland districts" (CHS-JUL, CHS-AUG), "frosty locations" (ODT-GL), "frosty areas" and "warm climates" (YAT-PR), "cooler climates" and "warmer climates" (DALT-G), or name a town (TUI-PG, NZH-W, DUN-PT).

**AF-4 (supported interpretation; national). As approved:** NIWA: frosts are common in the cooler months for most of New Zealand, but occur rarely north of Waikato. **Moderate** (as submitted: National (NIWA): frosts are common in the cooler months for most of NZ but rare north of Waikato; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* NIWA-NZ: "Frosts are common in the cooler months for most of the country, however they occur rarely north of Waikato." FIG-FROST (NIWA data, 1991 to 2020) gives mean annual ground-frost days of 0.14 at Kaitaia and 4.23 at Auckland, against 84.75 at Christchurch, 116.82 at Queenstown and 116.62 at Alexandra.

*Build Check:* NIWA-OV says frosts can occur anywhere in New Zealand. FIG-FROST gives annual ground-frost days at selected stations, not months. It shows very few at Kaitaia and Auckland, and also few at some coastal places south of Waikato (Wellington, Whanganui, New Plymouth).

**AF-5 (supported interpretation; national). As approved:** NIWA: frosts are less frequent in coastal and northernmost areas, and most frequent at inland South Island locations such as Alexandra. **Moderate** (as submitted: National (NIWA): frosts are less frequent in coastal and northernmost areas and most frequent inland; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Frosts are less frequent on the coast and in the far north, and most frequent inland. NIWA-NZ: frosts "occur less frequently in coastal and northernmost areas." NIWA-OT: "air frosts occur most frequently at the inland location of Alexandra." FIG-FROST: Dunedin 49.78 ground-frost days a year against Alexandra 116.62.

*Build Check:* 'Most frequent' is said of inland South Island locations, not of inland areas in general. NIWA-OT covers Otago only, and its sentence is about air frosts at Alexandra.

**AF-6 (supported interpretation; national). As approved:** NIWA: frost is local and can vary widely over small areas; flat areas where cold air cannot drain away, valleys and basins are the most likely to get frost. **Moderate** (as submitted: National (NIWA): frost is local and can vary widely over small areas; flat land, valleys and basins are most frost-prone; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Frost is local. NIWA-OT: "Frost is a local phenomenon and both its frequency of occurrence and intensity can vary widely over small areas." NIWA-NZ: "Areas most likely to experience frost are flat areas ... and in valleys and basins." ODT-GL makes the same point for roses (AF-36).

*Build Check:* 'Basins' is in the national document only. The Otago document says flat areas and valleys.

**AF-7 (expert opinion, single source; national). As approved:** National (NIWA): NZ's climate runs from warm subtropical in the far north to cool temperate in the far south. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* NIWA-OV: "New Zealand's climate is complex and varies from warm subtropical in the far north to cool temperate climates in the far south", and mean annual temperatures range "from 10°C in the south to 16°C in the north". It names no boundary.

*Build Check:* NIWA-OV also says the coldest month is usually July, and that frosts can occur anywhere in New Zealand.

#### Main winter pruning (Q1)

**AF-8 (established; national). As approved:** Main winter pruning falls between June and August. Every source that names a month includes July; some name June and July, and others July and August. **High** (as submitted: National: main winter pruning falls between June and August, most often July and August; High).

*Evidence as recorded:* Main winter pruning falls between June and August, most often July and August. GU-P: "July and August are the two main months for pruning roses in New Zealand". NZRS-W: "Most people prune in July and even August." NZRS-DEMO: "roses are usually pruned between late June and mid August depending on where you live". YAT-PR: "July is the month when most roses are pruned." PALM-YRC: "July through to mid-late August." MATT: "June, July and August." SPR-P: "June or July are generally the preferred months but some folk will prune in August". KINGS: "June/July". DALT-G: "June/July". Sources differ within the window (C-1).

*Build Check:* June and July: KINGS and DALT-G; SPR-P prefers June or July and says some prune in August. July and August: GU-P, NZRS-W, PALM-YRC and PALM-G. July alone: YAT-PR. June to August: MATT, and NZRS-DEMO (late June to mid August). 'Most often July and August' is not supported and is removed. CHS-SEP lists finishing winter pruning in September for Canterbury (AF-12). YAT-PR and PALM-YRC except roses that are not bush roses (ramblers, weeping standards and once-flowering old roses).

**AF-9 (supported interpretation; north). As approved:** For the north, the New Zealand Rose Society says pruning starts around early July, and one garden centre says the north can start a bit earlier than the July to mid-late August it gives for most of the country. **Low** (as submitted: North: sources place main pruning from early July to early August; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Sources place main pruning in the north from early July to early August. NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July". PALM-YRC: the north can "start a bit earlier" than July. GF (Auckland): "leave them be until pruning time, in July." TUI-PG (Kerikeri): "the end of July, early August is a good time to prune in Northland." (C-3.)

*Build Check:* No page defines 'up north'. NZRS-FAQ gives a start and no end; 'to early August' is removed. PALM-YRC names no month for the north. The months for Auckland and Northland come from replies to individual readers and one remark in an autumn article, and are now separate findings (AF-39, AF-40). The Northland reply, the end of July to early August, is later than the others (C-3). Lowered from Moderate at the wording check: with the Auckland and Northland months moved out, each half rests on one source, and they say different things.

**AF-10 (supported interpretation; colder South Island and frost-prone inland districts). As approved:** For the colder parts of the South Island, sources name August: the colder parts of the South Island get under way in August (New Zealand Rose Society); in Canterbury, colder areas may prefer to wait until August and colder inland districts prune once the worst frosts have passed (Canterbury Horticultural Society); in Dunedin, frosty locations can mean waiting until mid-August (Dunedin Botanic Garden). **Moderate** (as submitted: Colder South Island and frost-prone inland districts: main pruning in August or later, once the worst frosts have passed; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Main pruning is in August or later, once the worst frosts have passed. NZRS-FAQ: "in the colder parts of the South Island, it gets under-way in August." CHS-JUL: "colder areas may prefer to wait until August." CHS-AUG: "In colder inland districts, prune once the worst frosts have passed." ODT-GL (Dunedin): "frosty locations can mean waiting until mid-August." TUI-PG (Christchurch): "late winter or early spring after the last frost." PALM-YRC: "in the south a bit later." (C-4.)

*Build Check:* No page says 'August or later'; that wording is removed. PALM-YRC says only 'in the south a bit later'. TUI-PG's reply to a Christchurch reader names no month ('late winter or early spring after the last frost'). Not all southern sources name August: Dunedin Botanic Garden prunes in late July (AF-11), TUI-PG told a Dunedin reader June or July, with August if unsure, and the New Zealand Rose Society's 2019 pruning demonstrations for Otago, Southland and Westland were held on 20 and 21 July (AF-45; C-10, added at the check). #### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — Which months nationally (AF-8).** June or July: SPR-P "June or July are generally the preferred months"; KINGS "June/July"; DALT-G "June/July". July and August: GU-P "July and August are the two main months"; NZRS-W "July and even August"; PALM-YRC "July through to mid-late August"; YAT-PR "July is the month". NZRS-DEMO spans both: "between late June and mid August depending on where you live". MATT gives "June, July and August". Unresolved; the overlap is July.

**C-2 — Colder places earlier or later (AF-1, AF-17).** NZRS-FAQ, DUN-PT ("the colder the climate, the later you prune"), CHS-JUL, CHS-AUG, ODT-GL, TUI-PG and PALM-YRC prune later where it is colder. DALT-G: "In cooler climates you can prune earlier once all the leaves have fallen", and in warmer climates delays to "mid-late July". DALT-G ties timing to leaf fall, the others to frost. Unresolved; DALT-G is a single commercial voice.

**C-3 — When the north starts (AF-9).** NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July". PALM-YRC: the north can start "a bit earlier" than July. GF (Auckland): "in July". TUI-PG (Kerikeri): "the end of July, early August is a good time to prune in Northland." Unresolved.

### 8.1 Source characteristics that limit independence or weight

- **TUI-PG, TUI-FEED, TUI-Q, YAT-PR, YAT-NZ, PALM-YRC, PALM-G, PALM-FAQ, KINGS, SPR-P, SPR-F, MATT, DALT-G, DALT-Q, ODER and GF** are commercial. Nurseries and garden companies write from practice, but are not independent authorities.
- Pages from one organisation count as one voice: **DUN-PT, DUN-WM and ODT-GL** (Dunedin Botanic Garden); **CHS-MAY to CHS-SEP** (Canterbury Horticultural Society); **NZRS-FAQ, NZRS-W and NZRS-DEMO**; **NIWA-NZ, NIWA-OT, NIWA-OV and FIG-FROST** (NIWA data); **GU-P and GU-F** (one columnist); and each company's pages.
- **NZRS-W** is a 1996 article by one rosarian.
- **CHS** pages are short monthly checklists written for Canterbury; one or two lines each concern roses.
- **NIWA** and **FIG-FROST** describe frost and climate, not roses. FIG-FROST counts ground frosts at selected stations only.
- **NZH-W** is a newspaper column by a garden-centre manager.
- **TUI-PG**'s regional months are answers to individual readers' questions (Kerikeri, Christchurch, Masterton), not a general table.
- **GU-DH** gives no author and frames stopping deadheading as a choice for decorative hips.
- Found at the Build Check: MATT is a nursery in Whanganui, not Auckland. GU-DH's article does not mention New Zealand. GF's date of 26 April 2018 could not be seen on the page. NIWA-OV now resolves to `https://niwa.co.nz/climate-and-weather/overview-new-zealands-climate`. Figures in the frost tables of the two NIWA PDFs could not be read reliably and none is relied on.
- Noted for the Spraying record: The lime sulphur precautions are AF-47 here. `ARC-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` records only the two weeks from NZRS-W; the three weeks (KINGS) and 'never mix lime sulphur with other sprays' (ODER) were confirmed at this check and are not yet in that record.
