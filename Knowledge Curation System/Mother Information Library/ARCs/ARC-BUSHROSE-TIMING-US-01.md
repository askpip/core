## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative record of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission `BUSHROSE-TIMING-US`:

- **Q1** Main winter pruning months, and the local signal tied to them, in each climate band.
- **Q2** Months to start feeding, and for the last feed before winter, in each band.
- **Q3** When to stop deadheading and summer pruning, in each band.
- **Q4** Months to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band.
- **Q5** When to clear fallen leaves and prunings; when a winter spray is done, where described; when winter protection goes on and comes off.
- **Q6** How US sources tie timing to USDA zones, frost dates or state, and which zone bands are needed.
- **Q7** Whether gardeners can readily find their USDA zone, so that Ask Pip could ask for it.
- **Q8** How sources handle an early or late season, or a garden near a band edge.

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting and moving for Q4. United States, zones roughly 4 to 10. Product names, brands and rates are not collected. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

The dossier's questions Q6 to Q8 asked about USDA zones and bands. The Build Check found that no page gives rose months by zone, so this ARC records place by state, county or city instead (§2).

It is the source from which KIT wrote what Pip says about which months to do each rose job in the United States.

#### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — Last feed in band A (AF-21).** ZLESAK: "Ending especially nitrogen fertilization in late-July." MRS-MN: "Do not apply any nitrogen containing fertilizers after August 15th." ARS-NCD: stopping nitrogen "after August". Three sources in one band span about five weeks. Unresolved.

**C-2 — Last feed and stopping deadheading in band C (AF-23, AF-29).** UGA-COBB (zone 8) still feeds "every other week" in September and says "Stop deadheading" in October. NRS-F (zone 7) says granular feeding is "getting too late" in September but feeds liquids "for a while yet", says "Stop all feeding" in November, and continues to "cut buds and blooms (sometimes we have them until Thanksgiving!)". Unresolved.

**C-3 — When winter protection goes on in band A (AF-52).** ZLESAK begins mounding "in mid to late September". ARS-NCD adds leaves "in early November"; ISU-RC starts "in late fall (mid-November)". Band B sources (AF-53) and UNH, Illinois and Penn State wait for hard or killing frosts. ZLESAK's September date is for beginning the soil mound gradually; mulch goes on "after the soil is frozen". Unresolved.

**C-4 — When to start feeding (AF-16 to AF-20).** Most sources tie the first feed to new growth (ISU-RC, ZLESAK, OCRS, UC-CC, CLE-GR) or to the end of severe freezes (MO-G6601). MARIN starts "after the first round of blooms" in April. Unresolved.

**C-5 — Which zone system (AF-1, AF-4, AF-5).** USDA zones measure winter minimum temperature only. UC-PLACER: Sunset zones are "A much more useful guide for California gardeners". AZ-CZ: USDA zones 9a and 9b occur both in southern Arizona and on the Oregon coast, whose growing conditions differ. Unresolved; Founder Decision Point 2.

**C-6 — When a winter spray is done (AF-48 to AF-51).** ARS-NCD: dormant spray in "mid to late October". UGA-COBB: oil sprays in November and December. NRS-W: dormant oil in February. MARIN and OCRS: dormant spray in January to February, before new growth. Whether Pip gives winter-spray advice at all is pending under `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` Founder Decision Point 4. Unresolved.

**C-7 — Iowa State's fertiliser cut-off (`ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` Source Register, ISU-RC).** The ARC records ISU-RC as saying to stop fertilising after mid-July. Re-opened on 1 October 2026 (page dated June 2025), ISU-RC states "additional fertilizer applications during the first bloom period and in mid to late July" and gives no stop date. Not resolved by this commission; recorded for the Founders (§7, final subsection).

**C-8 — Band overlap (AF-6, AF-10, AF-11).** Iowa is stated to be almost all zone 5 (band A). Illinois and Pennsylvania each run from zone 5a to 7b, and their statewide months are used for band B. Florida runs from zone 8 to 11. A garden in zone 5 could therefore be shown band A or band B months. Unresolved; Founder Decision Point 2.

### 8.1 Source characteristics that limit independence or weight

- **No commercial source is relied on.** All relied-on sources are federal, university extension, state climatologist, rose society or botanic garden pages.
- **NRS-T, NRS-W, NRS-F, NRS-S, NRS-SP and NRS-MAR** are one rose society and count as one voice. NRS-MAR and NRS-SP describe one member's own practice in places.
- **ISU-RC, ISU-HT and ISU-ZONE** are one institution. **ILL-CARE and ILL-PR** are one institution. **PIMA-J and PIMA-F** are one guide series. **CLE-P and CLE-GR** are one institution and one author pair. **CSU-W and CSU-PL**, **MO-G6601 and MO-G6600**, and **UAEX-P and UAEX-T** are each one institution.
- **ZLESAK** names its region only as the "northern Midwest" and states no zone for it. It is placed in band A only because it is hosted by a Minnesota rose club and ARS-NCD states that Minnesota is in zones 3–5. That placement is the weakest in band A.
- **MRS-MN** is placed in band A on ARS-NCD's statement that Minnesota lies in zones 3–5; Minnesota's own zones were not stated in a Minnesota page opened (UMN Extension could not be opened, §8.2).
- **Band B rests on two statewide sources**, Illinois and Pennsylvania, each covering zones 5a to 7b. Their months are given for the whole state, not for a zone.
- **AZ-CZ's** Tucson zone (9b) comes from a table as returned by the extraction step, not a quoted sentence.
- **UGA-COBB** is a county guide written by a Master Gardener and consulting rosarian, not an extension specialist; product and brand names in it were not collected.
- **Several sources predate the 2023 zone map** (AZ-LD 2002; BEXAR 2003; CLE-P and CLE-GR 1999; UGA-COBB 2016; MRS-MN 2013). Their months are written for their area, not for a zone, so the map change does not alter what they say, but it may alter which band their area falls in.
- **BEXAR** describes a twice-yearly pruning (October and mid-February) common in Texas; only the mid-February main pruning is used for Q1.
- Found at the Build Check: Pages of one institution count once: the University of Georgia's statewide and Cobb County guides; Florida's two extension pages; Arizona's low-desert and Pima County guides. A Nashville society member's own pruning dates are one person's practice and do not count on their own (Pip Knowledge Rules §4). The North Central District of the American Rose Society speaks for Minnesota, Wisconsin and the Dakotas. The rose expert's page (ZLESAK) is read as speaking for the two states it names, Minnesota and Wisconsin. Temperatures are recorded in degrees Fahrenheit, as the pages give them.
- Noted for other records: Pip's general winter answer (PKR-CGD-000006), shown to every gardener, says to mound soil once the rose has had several hard frosts; the Minnesota and Wisconsin statement here says who differs (C-3). The general growing-season answer (PKR-CGD-000004) says to stop deadheading in early to mid autumn; several US places give a later or earlier local time, said in their own statements. A Founder decided on 8 October 2026 that the general deadheading advice stays for every gardener.
