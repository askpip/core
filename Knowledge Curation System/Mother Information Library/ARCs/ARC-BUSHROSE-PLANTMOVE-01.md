## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative record of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission `BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE`:

- **Q1** When are bare-root and potted bush roses planted, including the winter bare-root season in New Zealand and Australia and autumn/spring in the UK and US?
- **Q2** What site basics do sources give: sun, drainage, spacing, and soil where roses have grown before?
- **Q3** Soaking bare roots, the planting hole, planting and bud-union depth by climate, and watering in.
- **Q4** Moving an established rose: timing, root ball, pruning before or after, and aftercare.
- **Q5** Age limits or success odds for moving a rose.

**Scope.** Hybrid Tea and Floribunda bush roses, Grandiflora provisionally. New Zealand, Australian, UK and US sources; commercial sources labelled. Every online source recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

It is the source from which KIT wrote what Pip says about planting and moving a rose, replacing the "still learning" entry for "When can I plant or move a rose?" in the app's common questions.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each finding is one claim. For each: the finding as approved; the evidence as recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` §7, reproduced unchanged; and, where the Build Check found something, what it found. **Where a finding was corrected, the line marked "As approved" governs, not the opening sentence of the recorded evidence.** Source codes refer to the Source Register at §8.

#### Planting time (Q1)

**AF-1 (established). As approved:** Bare-root roses are planted while dormant: late autumn to early spring in the UK, winter in New Zealand and Australia. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Bare-root roses are planted while dormant. RHS-PL: "Plant in late autumn at leaf fall, and from late winter to early spring, before growth resumes." DA-BR: "during dormancy, from late autumn to early spring." MU: "planted in a dormant condition if purchased bare rooted". NZRS-FAQ: "Winter is the best time to plant as it allows the plant to establish before summer arrives." RSNSW-G: bare-rooted plants "are only available in winter." Also YAT-NZ, KINGS, PALM-G, RSSA, GA-2014P, GA-MAG.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Three New Zealand sources (NZRS-FAQ, KINGS, PALM-G) say winter of roses in general, not of bare-root roses. RSNSW-G says only that bare-root roses are sold in winter. RHS-PL adds not to plant in mid-winter when the ground is frozen.

**AF-2 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Australian sources name May to July or June to July; New Zealand sources say winter, with stock from June and planting into spring. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* In Australia, GA-MAG: "May, June and July are the months to plant bare-rooted roses in Australia." RSSA: "best done in June or July"; RSNSW-CAL prepares soil "for planting in June/July". In New Zealand, KINGS: "New seasons Roses arrive in store from June", and roses "can be planted right through spring"; PALM-G: "from winter through to spring"; NZRS-FAQ and YAT-NZ say winter without naming months.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* May rests on GA-MAG alone; RSNSW-CAL and RSSA name June and July. PALM-G says winter into spring and names no month; June in New Zealand is KINGS alone. ABG, said of container plants, says planting is best done in autumn or winter.

**AF-3 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Cold-winter US extension sources prefer or require spring for bare-root planting; the RHS gives late autumn or late winter to early spring. **Moderate** (as submitted: Cold-winter US extension sources prefer spring for bare-root planting; the RHS gives late autumn or late winter to early spring; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Cold-winter US sources prefer spring for bare-root roses. CSU: "spring planting is preferred over fall." MU: where winters are colder, "spring planting is preferred". ISU (Iowa): bare-root shrubs "must be planted in spring before growth begins." The RHS gives late autumn or late winter to early spring (C-3).

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Only MU gives cold as the reason. MU prefers spring only where winters fall below -10 °F; elsewhere it says autumn or spring is satisfactory. ISU says bare-root shrubs must be planted in spring.

**AF-4 (established). As approved:** Container (potted) roses can be planted outside the bare-root season: all year in UK and New Zealand sources, through the growing season in two US extension sources. **High** (as submitted: Container (potted) roses can be planted through most of the year, with conditions; High).

*Evidence as recorded:* Container (potted) roses can be planted through most of the year, with conditions. RHS-PL: "Plant all year round, provided the ground is neither frozen, nor very dry." MU: "throughout the growing season". CSU: "any time after the last killing frost." YAT-NZ and PALM-G: throughout the year. NZRS-FAQ: "If planting potted roses during spring and summer, keep them well watered."

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Only RHS-PL attaches conditions to all-year planting (ground neither frozen nor very dry). CSU and MU limit it to the growing season, CSU once the last killing frost has passed. NZRS-FAQ covers spring and summer, with watering. Two New Zealand pages still give a best season: ABG autumn or winter, PALM-G winter through spring. High is kept for the claim that a potted rose has a longer planting season than a bare-root one, on which six sources agree.

**AF-5 (established). As approved:** Bare-root roses are planted soon after they are received, and their roots are not allowed to dry out; three sources describe storing them first, for two to three weeks where a time is given. **Moderate** (as submitted: Bare-root roses are planted promptly, and their roots are not allowed to dry out; High).

*Evidence as recorded:* Bare-root roses are planted promptly, and the roots are not allowed to dry out. YAT-NZ: "they need to go into the ground promptly." GA-2019: "planted almost immediately after purchasing." GA-2014P: "it's vital not to let the roots dry out before planting". Where planting is delayed, DA-BR keeps them "in a cool, dry place, protected from frost, for up to three weeks", and ISU stores them at "around 40°F". NZRS-FAQ: shrivelled or wrinkled canes are "a sign it has dried out."

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* No single recorded quotation carried both halves. Planting soon: YAT-NZ, GA-2019, ISU. Roots not drying out: GA-2014P, ISU. AMORE also says to plant as soon as possible. Storage conditions differ: DA-BR up to three weeks in a cool, dry place; RSNSW-G up to 14 days with the roots kept moist; ISU cool and not freezing, with no time given. The NZRS-FAQ quotation is about choosing a plant and does not support the finding. Two ISU sentences were added to the record at the check.

#### Site (Q2)

**AF-6 (established). As approved:** Roses need a sunny site; stated minimums range from four to six hours of direct sun a day. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Roses need a sunny site; stated minimums are four hours (DA-BR), four to six (GA-MAG), five (PALM-G, "Five hours of sunlight is adequate"), five to six (CSU, RSSA) and six (MU, AMORE). ABG: roses "do best in full sun"; KINGS and YAT-NZ say full sun or a sunny spot.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* ABG, KINGS and YAT-NZ say full sun or a sunny spot and give no hours. PALM-G adds that roses are fine with some afternoon shade.

**AF-7 (established). As approved:** Roses need well-drained soil; for heavy soil, four sources suggest raised beds or mounds and four suggest gypsum, two of them with compost. **High** (as submitted: Roses need well-drained soil; raised beds or mounds are suggested where soil is heavy or drains poorly; High).

*Evidence as recorded:* Roses need well-drained soil, with raised beds or mounds where soil is heavy or drains poorly. ABG: "have good drainage." RSSA: "Soil must be well drained". MU gives a test: water "should drain from the hole in 90 to 120 minutes." PALM-G: "try raised beds (at least 30cm above ground level)"; GA-MAG and KINGS: raised bed or mound in heavy soil. Also CSU, YAT-NZ.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Raised beds or mounds: MU, KINGS, PALM-G, GA-MAG. Gypsum: ABG, RSSA, YAT-NZ, KINGS; ABG and RSSA add compost. ABG's and KINGS's sentences on this are recorded quotations; the others were read at the check. CSU suggests a larger hole for compacted soil. High rests on the need for drainage.

**AF-8 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Avoid competition from the roots of nearby trees and shrubs. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Avoid competition from the roots of trees and shrubs. CSU, MU ("The roots of most roses don't compete well with those of trees and shrubs planted close by"), RSSA.

**AF-9 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Spacing depends on the rose's type and habit; stated distances run from about 50 cm to 1 m, and the one given for hybrid teas is 75 to 90 cm. **Moderate** (as submitted: Spacing depends on the rose's mature size; stated distances for bush roses fall roughly between 50 cm and 1 m; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Spacing depends on the rose's mature size. RHS-PL: "Spacing depends on type and habit. Check catalogue or label details." MU: hybrid teas "2-1/2 to 3 feet apart" (about 75 to 90 cm). AMORE: "60-100cm from other roses". FG-SP: "20 inches to 30 inches apart" (about 50 to 75 cm) for garden roses generally.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* RHS-PL gives no distance. Only MU's 75 to 90 cm is said of a bush rose (hybrid teas, in most of Missouri). AMORE's 60 to 100 cm covers several rose types. FG-SP's 50 to 75 cm is the author's own close planting of garden roses.

**AF-10 (expert opinion, conflicting). As approved:** One source gives air circulation as the reason for spacing; another questions that reason. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* GA-MAG: leave space "so there's good air circulation", especially in humid climates. FG-SP questions "that magic air circulation argument" for disease-resistant roses (C-8).

**AF-11 (supported interpretation). As approved:** New roses planted where roses grew before may establish and grow poorly ("replant disease", "rose sickness"). **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* New roses planted where roses grew before may establish and grow poorly. RHS-RD: "New plantings may struggle to establish, make poor growth and may even die". RHS-FAQ: "replant disorder or rose sickness". ARS-RRD: they "may not grow as well". RSSA: "The new rose will not tolerate any old rose roots remaining in its root zone." RHS-PL and DA-MOVE give precautions. FG-RRD reports mixed views on whether it exists (C-4).

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* RHS-PL only names the risk. FG-RRD reports the poor-growth claim second-hand and says opinion is divided.

**AF-12 (supported interpretation). As approved:** The cause of rose replant disease is not established. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* The cause is not established. RHS-RD: "recognised but poorly understood". ARS-RRD: "Nothing specific has been identified as the cause", listing suspected factors (nutrients, soil structure, nematodes, toxins, microbes).

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* RHS-RD gives a leading explanation, a build-up of soil pests and pathogens. ARS-RRD (2013) reports research pointing to a soil-borne factor.

**AF-13 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Sources give different remedies: replace the soil with soil from another part of the garden (by differing amounts), plant elsewhere, or add compost and mycorrhizal fungi; commercial nurseries are reported to rotate with other plants. **Moderate** (as submitted: Sources give different remedies: replace the soil (by differing amounts), plant elsewhere, rotate with other plants, or add compost and mycorrhizal fungi; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* Sources give different remedies: replace soil (RHS-PL 45 cm deep and wide; RHS-RD about 60 cm across and at least 30 cm deep; RSSA old roots plus "an extra 2 buckets of soil"; DA-MOVE fresh soil); plant in another site (RHS-RD); rotate with other plants (ARS-RRD, of German nurseries); or compost and mycorrhizal fungi instead of replacing soil (FG-RRD) (C-4).

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* ARS-RRD describes rotation as a practice of commercial nurseries in Germany, not advice for gardeners. RHS-RD, read at the check, also lists a cardboard-box lining, high-nitrogen fertiliser, tolerant rootstocks, and mycorrhizal products with organic matter alongside the soil swap; these are not recorded quotations. DA-MOVE says to replace the soil and add well-rotted manure or soil improver. No source compares the remedies (R-3).

**AF-14 (expert opinion, single source). As approved:** One source states replant disease can persist for at least nine years. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* RHS-RD: "The problem can persist for at least nine years following removal of the first planting."

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Said of replant disease generally, not of roses.

#### Planting method (Q3)

**AF-15 (supported interpretation). As approved:** The planting hole is wider than the roots; stated sizes vary. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* The hole is wider than the roots; sizes vary. RHS-PL: "roughly twice the width of the plant's roots and the depth of a spade's blade." ISU: two to two-and-a-half times the root spread. CSU: 18 to 24 inches across, 14 to 18 deep. MU: at least 12 to 16 inches deep and 18 to 20 wide. RSSA: "30cm square by a spade depth." PALM-G: twice the depth and width of the root ball.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* RSSA and CSU give a hole size without saying it is wider than the roots.

**AF-16 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Bare roots are spread over a mound of soil in the bottom of the hole. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Bare roots are spread over a mound of soil in the hole. GA-2014P: "a mound of earth in the centre of the hole, to encourage the roots to grow down over the mound." RSNSW-CAL, RSSA, GA-2019, PALM-G ("a mound 5-7cm high"), and UW-PAL when replanting a moved rose.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* The four pages that fully support it are Australian, and are three voices. PALM-G's mound is for a container-grown rose and UW-PAL's for a rose being replanted after a move.

**AF-17 (established). As approved:** Bare roots are soaked in water before planting. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Bare roots are soaked in water before planting. RSNSW-G, RSNSW-CAL, RSSA, GA-2014P, GA-MAG, YAT-NZ ("to hydrate them and remove traces of soil"), DA-BR, ISU, MU, AMORE. CSU soaks "If bare-root plants appear dry". RHS-PL does not mention soaking.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* CSU soaks only if the plants appear dry when received.

**AF-18 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Stated soaking times range from one hour to 24 hours; two US extension sources set 24 hours as the limit. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Soaking times differ, from one hour (RSSA) to 24 hours (RSNSW-G, AMORE); CSU and MU set 24 hours as the limit, MU: "Soaking for more than 24 hours may damage the root system." (C-2).

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* GA-MAG says 'several hours'. The two Rose Society of NSW pages differ from each other. ISU gives no maximum.

**AF-19 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Damaged or broken roots are trimmed at planting. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Damaged or broken roots are trimmed. ISU: "prune off damaged or broken roots". GA-MAG: "Trim damaged roots". NRS-T, when moving: "Prune any broken roots". GA-2019 goes further: "Prune root system back by around one third."

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* Supported for planting by ISU, GA-MAG and YAT-NZ (added at the check). NRS-T says it of a rose being moved. GA-2019 describes a different practice, cutting the whole root system back by a third, and does not support the finding. MU says not to cut off roots that are too large for the hole.

**AF-20 (established). As approved:** Recommended bud-union depth differs: above the soil, at soil level, or below it. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Recommended bud-union depth differs: above, at or below the soil (C-1). This agrees with `ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` AF-12.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* DA-BR states its below-soil depth only for roses planted in pots. DA-MOVE, also UK, replants a moved rose with the union about 2 inches below soil level.

**AF-21 (supported interpretation). As approved:** New Zealand and Australian sources that state a depth put the bud union at or above soil level. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* New Zealand and Australian sources that state a depth put the union at or above soil level: YAT-NZ ("5cm above"), PALM-G ("2-5cm above"), RSNSW-CAL ("3-5cm above"), GA-2014P ("just above"), GA-2019 ("soil level is no higher than the graft"), RSSA ("at soil level"), AMORE ("up to the crown"). TUI-T, when moving: "ensure the graft is above the soil". No NZ or Australian source located recommends burying it.

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* AMORE speaks of the 'crown', not the bud union, and says a long shank is buried.

**AF-22 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Burying the bud union is linked to cold winters; the RHS advises against burying it because of reported dieback risk. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Burying the union is linked to cold winters. OSU-AE: where winter temperatures "predictably drop below 20 degrees" (Fahrenheit), plant 1 to 2 inches below; "The reasons for planting deeper include avoidance of cold damage and stabilizing against wind rock." MU: slightly below in southeast Missouri, 2 inches below in the colder north. CSU and ISU (Colorado, Iowa) bury 2 to 4 inches. RHS-PL: at soil level, "not below as this is reported to increase the risk of rose dieback."

*Build Check, 5 October 2026:* The finding holds two claims. The cold-winter link rests on US extension sources and is Moderate. The RHS advice against burying rests on one organisation, and Pip uses it only as one side of the UK disagreement. DA-BR and DA-MOVE, both UK, bury the union and give no reason. CSU buries the union and gives no reason. OSU-AE also gives steadying against wind rock as a reason, and disease at the graft as the risk in mild climates. ISU's reason is in a sentence added at the check.

**AF-23 (supported interpretation). As approved:** A rose is planted at the depth it grew before: a potted rose at its container depth, and in one source a bare-root rose to its soil mark. **Moderate** (as submitted: A potted rose is planted at the same depth it grew in its container; Moderate).

*Evidence as recorded:* A potted rose goes in at the depth it grew. ABG: "Always plant at the same depth as they are in their container." GA-MAG: "Ensure the soil mark on the stem aligns with the level of the garden bed." KINGS: roots' top "level with the surrounding ground".

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Points of Variation

These are recorded as they stand. They are not resolved. C-1 to C-9 are reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` §6; C-10 was found at the Build Check.

#### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — Bud-union depth (AF-20, AF-21, AF-22).** Above the soil: YAT-NZ "5cm above", PALM-G "2-5cm above", RSNSW-CAL "3-5cm above", GA-2014P "just above", OSU-AE one inch above in mild Eugene. At soil level: RHS-PL ("not below as this is reported to increase the risk of rose dieback"), RSSA, AMORE ("up to the crown"). Below: CSU and ISU 2 to 4 inches, MU slightly below to 2 inches, DA-BR 5 to 8 cm, OSU-AE two inches in cold Central Oregon. The difference follows climate in the sources that give a reason (OSU-AE, MU). Unresolved.

**C-2 — Soaking time (AF-18).** One hour or more (RSSA), one to two hours (ISU), a couple of hours (DA-BR), at least two hours (RSNSW-CAL), several hours (GA-MAG, MU), overnight (CSU, MU), 24 hours (RSNSW-G, AMORE). CSU and MU set 24 hours as the maximum, MU stating longer "may damage the root system". Unresolved.

**C-3 — Autumn or spring for bare-root roses (AF-1, AF-3).** RHS-PL: late autumn at leaf fall, or late winter to early spring. CSU: "spring planting is preferred over fall"; MU prefers spring where winters are colder; ISU plants bare-root shrubs in spring. The difference reflects winter severity. Unresolved.

**C-4 — Replant disease: how real, and what to do (AF-11, AF-13).** RHS-RD calls it "recognised but poorly understood"; FG-RRD reports "mixed feelings" about whether it exists and says replacing soil is "the old method". Remedies: replace soil 45 cm deep and wide (RHS-PL), about 60 cm across and at least 30 cm deep (RHS-RD), or the old roots plus two extra buckets of soil (RSSA); plant elsewhere (RHS-RD); crop rotation (ARS-RRD, a nursery practice); compost and mycorrhizal fungi instead of soil replacement (FG-RRD). Unresolved.

**C-5 — Pruning before moving (AF-29).** Cut back by a third (KINGS), by half (NRS-T), to at least half in the growing season (TRE-T), hard to 3 to 5 buds (TUI-Q), hard (TUI-T), to three or four canes (UW-PAL), to 10 to 12 inches (HEIR), some top growth (DA-MOVE). AE-T (summer move): "There's no need to prune them back first". RHS-MOVE (shrubs generally): "do not prune hard". Unresolved.

**C-6 — Pruning at planting (AF-25).** GA-2019 cuts top growth by a third to a half and roots by a third; CSU leaves about 5 to 12 inches of cane; MU says packaged plants "rarely need additional pruning"; RHS-PL prunes "in the first winter after planting". Unresolved.

**C-7 — How hard it is to move a rose (AF-34, AF-35).** RHS-MOVE names *Rosa* among plants "resenting root disturbance" that "can be tricky to move"; TRE-T: "The older the rose, the more likely that it will not appreciate being moved". DA-MOVE: "Moving a rose is quite forgiving"; NRS-T reports moving over 100 roses "with great success". Unresolved.

**C-8 — Why roses are spaced (AF-10).** GA-MAG spaces roses for "good air circulation", especially in humid climates; FG-SP questions "that magic air circulation argument". Unresolved.

**C-9 — Timing within the dormant season (AF-26, AF-28).** UAEX: February, "the same time we prune them"; PALM-FAQ: after pruning in July, once frost risk has passed; NRS-T: late winter to early spring; UW-PAL: early spring or late fall; RHS-MOVE: any time late October to mid-March (UK). All are within or at the edges of dormancy. Unresolved.

**C-10 — Soil on the roots when moving (AF-30).** For a dormant move TRE-T shakes the soil off the roots and replants the rose as a bare-root rose, and UW-PAL removes the soil around the plant. KINGS: "dig out as large a root ball as you can manage." For a move in the growing season TRE-T says to keep as much soil around the roots as possible. Unresolved.
