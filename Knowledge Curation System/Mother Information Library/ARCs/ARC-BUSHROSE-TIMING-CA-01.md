## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative record of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission `BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA`:

- **Q1** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band, and what local signal do they tie it to?
- **Q2** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
- **Q3** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
- **Q4** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
- **Q5** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where described, when is a winter spray done; and when does winter protection go on and come off?
- **Q6** How do Canadian sources tie rose timing to Canada's Plant Hardiness Zones, to USDA zones, to local frost dates, or to province? Which bands are needed, and what month windows do sources give for each?
- **Q7** Do Canadian sources say gardeners can readily find their hardiness zone, and whether it is Canadian or USDA? If not, what would a beginner need to be asked?
- **Q8** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band?

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Hardy shrub-rose series bred for Canada (for example Explorer and Parkland) are out of scope. Every finding records which band it applies to. Months from one band are not applied to another. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

The dossier's questions Q6 and Q7 asked about hardiness zones and bands. The Build Check found that the rose pages speak for named places, so this ARC records place by country, province, or town or area instead (§2).

It is the source from which KIT wrote what Pip says about which months to do each rose job in Canada.

#### Climate bands and placing the gardener (Q6, Q7)

**AF-1 (established). As approved:** Canada's official plant hardiness zones (Natural Resources Canada) are calculated from several climate variables; the USDA system uses average extreme minimum temperature only. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Canada's official zones are calculated from several climate variables; the USDA system uses average extreme minimum temperature alone. NRCAN-OD: "The map is based on a formula using seven climate variables that influence plant survival". NRCAN-ST lists "lowest average daily temperature in the region's coldest month; length of frost-free periods; rainfall totals from June to November". SR-MCK: "In the United States, the plant hardiness system involves averaging annual extreme minimum temperatures over a period of interest". LBG-MAP: the USDA approach is "based solely on the average annual minimum temperature."

*Build Check:* NRCAN-ST, as the checker read it, lists the variables and gives no USDA comparison; the USDA half rests on SR-MCK and LBG-MAP. Not about roses.

**AF-2 (supported interpretation, single issuing body). As approved:** Natural Resources Canada also publishes an alternative map based on average extreme minimum temperature alone. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* NRCAN-OD: Natural Resources Canada also shows "an alternative plant hardiness zone approach using just one climate variable: average extreme minimum temperature for the period 1991 to 2020."

*Build Check:* Quotations confirmed; no correction.

**AF-3 (supported interpretation). As approved:** Canadian and USDA zone numbers are not interchangeable; two sources say the Canadian number for a place is about one higher. **Moderate**.

*Evidence as recorded:* Canadian and USDA zone numbers are not interchangeable. PG: the two maps "are not the same, and can not be used interchangeably"; for American material, "just remember to mentally add a number to any zone that is indicated." TIMG (Kingston): "The United States Department of Agriculture Map's zones are different- zone 6 here may be zone 5 there."

*Build Check:* Quotations confirmed; no correction.

**AF-4 (expert opinion, single commercial source). As approved:** One source says many Canadian box stores label plants with USDA zones without saying so. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* PG: "Many Canadian box stores use, without ever stating it, the American hardiness zone system."

*Build Check:* Quotations confirmed; no correction.

**AF-5 (expert opinion, one writer). As approved:** One writer says bush-rose labels commonly give a zone 5 rating that assumes winter protection, while Hybrid Teas and Floribundas are hardy only to about zone 8. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* LBG-WP: "The label on bush roses usually accords them a zone 5 hardiness rating, but that's actually a blatant lie." LBG-HZ: "Their zone 5 label is designed to give you an idea of what the plants can take" if carefully protected, and "Most are hardy only to zone 8." The writer does not state which zone system the zone 8 figure uses.

*Build Check:* LBG-WP gives the zone 5 label; the zone 8 figure is on LBG-HZ only. One writer. Not used: Pip does not use zones.

**AF-6 (established). As approved:** Canada's 2025 zone update raised zones by between half a zone and two full zones. **High**.

*Evidence as recorded:* NRCAN-ST: "many areas shifting by half a zone or more and some by as much as two full zones". SR-MCK: "zone values have increased by between half a zone and two full zones across the country". LBG-MAP: "a widespread increase in hardiness zones across Canada". MOSS (Alberta): "Edmonton and Calgary are both zone 4a on the 2025 hardiness map".

*Build Check:* LBG-MAP and MOSS support a widespread increase only; the half-zone to two-zone range is NRCAN-ST's and SR-MCK's.

**AF-7 (expert opinion, limited). As approved:** A gardener can find their Canadian zone by looking up their municipality on the hardiness zone map. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* LBG-MAP: "Consult the hardiness zone map by municipality to find out which zone you are in." NRCAN-ST refers gardeners to "the plant hardiness zone website" for "choosing plants that will thrive in a particular area" but, as extracted, does not describe the lookup. The NRCan map page itself could not be opened (§4.3).

*Build Check:* NRCAN-ST has no municipality lookup sentence; it says only that the website helps choose plants for an area. Rests on LBG-MAP.

**AF-8 (single source). As approved:** The Canadian Rose Society gives winter protection in three zone bands (2–4, 5–6, 7–9) without saying whether the zones are Canadian or USDA. **Low**.

*Evidence as recorded:* CRS-WP sets out winter protection under the headings "Zones 2-4", "Zones 5-6" and "Zones 7-9", mentions "areas where Chinook winds are common" under Zones 2–4 and "Coastal B.C" under Zones 7–9, and does not state whether the zones are Canadian or USDA.

**AF-10 (established). ## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Points of Variation

These are recorded as they stand. They are not resolved. C-1 to C-7 are reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA-01` §6 as first written; where a finding they cite was corrected, §4 governs. The bands and zone bands they name are not used: each finding now speaks for its own place (§4). C-8 and C-9 were found at the Build Check.

#### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — When is the last feed (AF-26 to AF-29)?** CRS-OC (national): "a third application may be made in mid-July. Later applications are not recommended"; HRS and LO-YR (Ontario) and PV (Quebec) agree on mid-July. TMG-F (Toronto): not "after the end of July"; PAL (Ontario): "early August"; GGC (Ontario) and DNA (Alberta): mid-August; LO-YR also says "fed every week from early spring to late August"; GW (coastal BC): "by late summer"; FVRF (coastal BC): none "from September onward". The range within Ontario alone is mid-July to late August. Unresolved.

**C-2 — When is fall planting done, and where (AF-35, AF-36)?** CRS-PL: "in September for fall planting", but "not recommended across the Prairies Provinces". RBG (Ontario): "spring or late autumn". TIMG (Kingston): bare-root grafted roses "for late fall planting". September and late autumn are different windows; none of these sources explains the difference. Unresolved.

**C-3 — When does protection go on (AF-41, AF-42, AF-43)?** CRS-WP, LO-YR, GGC and LBG-WP: after hard frosts or once the ground is freezing; LO-YR adds "not too early". LP (Quebec, commercial): roses are cut "juste avant les premiers gels" (just before the first frosts) and covered "rapidement" (quickly) with a cone. JD (Quebec, commercial): wait for the cooler temperatures of November. LP's "before the first frosts" conflicts in direction with the others. Unresolved.

**C-4 — How soon does protection come off (AF-46 to AF-48, AF-50)?** CRS-SP: "In late spring, when the soil has thawed and a hard freeze is no longer expected". LBG-WP: "as soon as temperatures start to regularly rise above freezing during the day". JD: as soon as snow and ice allow. LO-TP: "As soon as thawing permits". MOSS and GGC uncover in stages. The sources agree that thaw governs but differ on whether to remove early or wait until hard freezes are past. Unresolved.

**C-5 — What is the spring signal (AF-13, AF-14, AF-20, AF-23)?** Most sources use forsythia flowering. LO-TP: at forsythia, "The buds should be swelling and quite obvious but showing no leaves." TC and GW name bud swell; RBG "just before bud break". CRS-WP (Zones 2–4) removes winter kill "when the bush begins to leaf out again". BCL says late pruning "even after the rose has already leafed out" only delays flowering. The sources describe stages from bud swell to leaf-out. Unresolved; see `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` in §7.

**C-6 — Which zone system is meant (AF-3, AF-5, AF-8, AF-9)?** CRS-WP gives Zones 2–4, 5–6 and 7–9 without naming the system. LBG-WP gives "zone 5" label ratings and "(AgCan zone 6)" in the same article. TIMG and GGC state Canadian zones. PG says Canadian and USDA numbers "can not be used interchangeably". A zone number in a Canadian rose source can therefore mean either system. Unresolved; R-2.

**C-7 — Fall pruning (AF-31, AF-32, AF-33).** CRS-WP cuts canes back in fall in every zone band (to about 70 cm around Thanksgiving in Zones 2–4). LO-YR: "Do not prune at this time, unless the canes are so tall they could whip about". GGC trims the top third of tall canes but leaves major shaping until spring. GW (Okanagan): "Avoid fall pruning". The sources agree that major pruning waits for spring; they differ on how much is cut in fall. Unresolved.

**C-8 — When the last feed is on the coast of British Columbia (AF-26, AF-28).** CRS-OC (national): 'a third application may be made in mid-July. Later applications are not recommended'. GW (South Coastal BC): 'Stop fertilizing by late summer to allow plants to prepare for winter.' FVRF (Fraser Valley): 'Refrain from fertilizing and pruning from September onward.' Two garden companies against the national rose society; the society does not speak of the coast separately. Pip shows both views on the coast, leading with the national rose society, whose finding carries the higher level (rule 2); unresolved.

**C-9 — How far to cut canes back before winter in Alberta (AF-45, AF-53).** MOSS, citing the Canadian Rose Society: 'Cut the canes back to about 70 cm and remove the remaining leaves'. DNA (Alberta nursery) cuts roses to about 6 inches before mounding. One nursery against the national rose society; DNA's cut is not shown (Founder's direction, 8 October 2026). Unresolved.

C-6 (which zone system is meant) is on record only: no zones are used. C-3 and C-9 are settled for Pip by the Founder's direction of 8 October 2026: the single company in each is not shown. C-1 is narrowed: the national statement gives mid-July, Toronto and Barrie and Orillia give their own months, and on the coast of British Columbia the disagreement is shown (C-8). The others are told in the statements for the places they concern.

- **GGC, PAL, JD, PV, LP, FVRF, GW, DNA, PG** are commercial. **MOSS** is a business-run guide and is treated as commercial in character. Nurseries and garden centres write from local growing experience, but they are not independent authorities.
- **CRS-WP, CRS-SP, CRS-PL and CRS-OC** are one organisation and count as one voice. None shows an author or date.
- **HRS** follows CRS wording closely (for example on feeding, pruning after removing winter protection, and forsythia) and may derive from it; it is not counted as independent confirmation of CRS where the wording matches (EAS §4.5).
- **MOSS** relays the Canadian Rose Society's Zones 2–4 advice by name; its Thanksgiving statement is not counted as independent of CRS-WP.
- **LO-TP and LO-YR** are one organisation, without bylines, published in 2013. **LO-YR** is internally inconsistent on feeding (three dated applications, and weekly feeding to late August).
- **TMG-P and TMG-F** are one organisation. **LBG-WP and LBG-HZ** are one writer; **LBG-MAP** is a second writer on the same blog.
- **BCL** reports the advice of a rose grower named only as "Brad"; it is a 2009 magazine article. **TC** is a newspaper garden column; the page showed no date.
- **GW**'s Okanagan statements were returned by the extraction step as belonging to its Okanagan and Penticton guidance; the page's layout could not be inspected directly.
- **TMG-F** shows a posting date later than its update date; both are recorded as shown.

- **TG** is a garden writers' blog; it gives one date for "most parts of Canada".
- Most sources do not say whether a zone number is Canadian or USDA; those that do are noted in AF-9.
