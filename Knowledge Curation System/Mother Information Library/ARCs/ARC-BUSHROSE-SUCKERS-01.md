## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on rootstock suckers (`BUSHROSE-SUCKERS`): the features that separate a rootstock sucker from a basal cane of the grafted variety, and which of them exist on a dormant plant (Q1); how reliable those features are, including own-root roses and a buried bud union (Q2); how a confirmed sucker is removed (Q3); what a photograph can establish (Q4, no finding); and when a beginner should leave it or get experienced help (Q5).

It supports sucker handling in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 pruning journey for Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora roses that have passed the rose-type gate (PKR-SGT-000003). It lifts the hold on `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-8, subject to the Founder decisions in §2.

This ARC is the source from which KIT will build the sucker Observation and Decision Logic PKRs.

## 2. Founder Decision Record

Per ROC Operations Manual §11.9/§12.2, this ARC is created following documented Founder approval.

**Decision:** Approved. Findings AF-1 to AF-25 are adopted as stated, with the six preserved points of variation (§5) and the practical defaults at §6. No amendments or exclusions.

**Decided by:** Shaphan, AskPIP Founder Authority, 29 September 2026, in chat (Founder Decision Points 1–7 of `FRD-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01`; the ROC recommendation was chosen at every point).

**Implementation decisions recorded with the approval (for KIT):**

1. **D2 — Placement (b).** Sucker handling is a separate observation item with its own Decision Logic, sitting with the other removal-first observations (before the main framework). PKR-OBS-000006's "new shoots from the base" row points to it.
2. **D3 — Guidance scope (b).** Pip guides removal only when the gardener confirms, on the plant, that the shoot starts below a visible bud union. Otherwise the shoot is left and experienced help is suggested.
3. **D4 — Method (c).** The agreed core is presented: expose where the shoot starts and remove it there, never at soil level (AF-17). Tearing or pulling away is the main method and a clean cut at the origin is the alternative; the disagreement (C-1) is stated.
4. **D5 — Dormant session (a).** In the dormant session Pip uses point of origin only. Leaf, new-growth colour and flower cues are offered only in the growing season, as supporting signs, never alone.
5. **D6 — No visible union (a).** Pip does not call any shoot a sucker when no bud union can be seen, and suggests experienced help if the gardener is concerned. Clearing soil to look for the union is offered as an optional step the gardener may choose.
6. **D7 — Further research.** The recommended defaults for R-1 to R-4 are adopted (§6). Nothing is commissioned. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis (R-5a–e) is not adopted.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` §7 as approved. Source codes refer to the Source Register at §8.

Each finding is one claim. "Dormant?" states whether the feature concerned is described in a form that exists on a leafless rose: **Yes** where it depends on the plant's structure only; **No** where it depends on leaves, new growth or flowers; **Not stated** where sources give no season.

#### Recognising a sucker (Q1)

**AF-1 (established).** The principal feature is where the shoot starts relative to the bud union. Growth from below the union is a sucker; growth from the union, or just above it, is the rose's own basal shoot. TRE-W: "The best way to assess if the growth is a sucker, is to look at where it originates." VSRG: "If below the graft – sucker; conversely, if above the graft – basal shoot." DAVID-A: "The surest way to tell a sucker from a healthy new stem is to look at its point of origin." TAMU-W: basal breaks "arise from the graft union"; suckers "arise from the rootstock below the graft union." ARS-SP: a basal break is "growth originating from the graft union or a few inches above it." Also RHS-SUCK, RHS-FAQ, HARK, YAT-P, UCCE-IM, NZRS-W, EAST, FG-Z. Dormant? **Yes**, where the union can be seen (see AF-2, AF-12).

**AF-2 (established).** A sucker can start below the soil, sometimes some distance from the plant, and sources say to trace the shoot down or clear the soil to see where it starts. RHS-FAQ: suckers "will develop from below ground level". DAVID-A: "You might need to gently ease back the soil to find where it starts." HARK: "Trace the shoot down to where it emerges." EAST: shoots may "pop up at some distance from the main plant". Also RHS-SUCK, YAT-P, ROSS-S, UCCE-VEN, ARS-SP, TRE, FG-Z. Dormant? **Yes**; tracing may require removing soil.

**AF-3 (supported interpretation).** Sucker leaves often look different from the rose's: RHS-FAQ "a different colour (often paler) and/or have a different number of leaflets"; HARK "often smaller, lighter in colour, or with more leaflets"; EAST "seven leaflets and are pale grey green in colour"; ILL-S "totally different from the budded cultivar"; TRE-W "often"; FG-Z (*R. multiflora*) "lighter green foliage". Dormant? **No**.

**AF-4 (supported interpretation).** Leaf features can mislead. EAST: "a lot of roses have seven leaflets as well". TRE-W: "the old rule of 7 leaflets is not always correct", and "some rootstock varieties only have 5". DAVID-A: leaf clues are "not always reliable", as 'Dr. Huey' "often has dark green, glossy leaves not unlike the rose it has been grafted with." ARS-SP and FG-Z say 'Dr. Huey' foliage is harder to tell from Hybrid Tea foliage. Dormant? **No** (concerns leaves).

**AF-5 (expert opinion).** ARS-SP: "Most basal breaks are red in color, in contrast to the light green color of rootstocks", with 'Dr. Huey' an exception. EAST: sucker leaves are pale grey-green, "unlike a lot of rose varieties where the young leaves are often red or bronze". DAVID-A lists "new growth often has a reddish tint" among reasons appearance cues are not reliable. Dormant? **No**.

**AF-6 (supported interpretation).** Sucker flowers differ from the rose's: ILL-S "leaves and flowers that are totally different"; TRE "different foliage and flowers"; UCONN "a stem with red flowers" on a rose bought for a different colour; EAST "small with a single whorl of petals mostly pale pink or off white"; FG-Z 'Dr. Huey' "a small dark red bloom" and *R. multiflora* "sprays of small white, single blooms". TRE-W: the rose's own water shoots "have the same foliage and flowers as the remainder of the plant." Dormant? **No**.

**AF-7 (supported interpretation).** Suckers are described as unusually vigorous: UCCE-IM "extra–vigorous shoots"; EAST "often more vigorous than the variety"; HARK "very vigorously, often straight and tall"; TRE "very strong growth"; GA-2014 "so strong and so big". Dormant? **Not stated.**

**AF-8 (expert opinion, limited).** Prickle and cane character differs between rootstocks. FG-Z: *R. multiflora* "is generally thornless", while 'Dr. Huey' canes "are generally harder to tell apart from the rose variety you purchased." HMF-R: *R. multiflora* forms include "several that are completely thornless"; *R. rugosa* is "extremely thorny". No source gives a prickle or cane-colour rule for recognising a sucker. Dormant? Prickles and canes exist on a dormant plant, but no source describes them for this purpose.

**AF-9 (supported interpretation).** The rootstock used differs by country and nursery. GU-R: "The main rootstock used in rose production in New Zealand is Rosa multiflora or various strains derived from it", with one New Zealand nursery also using *R. laxa*. EAST (UK) uses *R. laxa*. FG-Z: most United States roses use 'Dr. Huey' or *R. multiflora*. ILL-S lists *R. multiflora*, *R. canina* and *R. laxa*. VSRG finds 'Dr. Huey' "the most common form" in its garden in Victoria.

**AF-10 (unresolved question).** No source consulted describes recognising a sucker on a dormant, leafless rose. Of the features sources give, leaf, new-growth colour and flower features (AF-3 to AF-6) are described on foliage or flowers; point of origin (AF-1, AF-2) is described without reference to season.

#### Reliability (Q2)

**AF-11 (established).** On an own-root rose, shoots from the base or roots are the same variety as the rest of the plant. DAVID-A: any such shoot "is part of the original variety and not a sucker in the traditional sense." RHS-FAQ: such shoots "will be identical to the rest of the plant". FG-Z: "They cannot produce a sucker because there is no understock." ROSS-S: with roses "on their own roots", suckers "will not be a problem". The sources differ in terminology (C-3).

**AF-12 (supported interpretation).** Recommended bud-union planting depth differs, so on a given rose the union may be above, at or below the soil. RHS-PL: at soil level, not below. HARK: 2.5 to 5 cm below. FG-B: always buried. EAST: above soil. OSU-AE: depends on climate (C-4).

**AF-13 (supported interpretation).** Sources warn that suckers and the rose's own basal shoots are easily confused. TRE: "They are very easy to mistake with water shoots (basal shoots)". ARS-SP: a basal break is "sometimes confused with a rootstock sucker". TAMU-W: basal breaks "should not be confused with 'suckers'". GA-2014: of a sucker, "Gardeners look at it and they say, 'Oh isn't that a healthy shoot.'"

**AF-14 (expert opinion, single source).** EAST states that the rootstock can produce shoots "from any part of it's stem above the union (where the plant was budded) if it has not been cut off" close enough to the union, and that such shoots are cut off cleanly close to the union. No other source addresses this (C-6).

**AF-15 (expert opinion, limited).** Rootstocks differ in how readily they sucker. GU-R: *R. multiflora* plants "produce very few suckers", and *R. laxa* "throws virtually no suckers". MON (introduction) states that *R. canina* 'Pfänder' and 'Schmid's Ideal' "form a lot of root suckers".

**AF-16 (expert opinion, limited).** Damage to the roots or rootstock is linked with suckering. VSRG: "Damaging the roots may cause the plant to throw up a new cane which is attached to the roots or rootstock." HARK: "Avoid damaging the rootstock during weeding or cultivation."

#### Removing a sucker (Q3)

**AF-17 (established).** A sucker is removed at its point of origin, not at soil level, because a sucker cut at the surface regrows. RHS-FAQ: "dig down to expose its origin on the root, and then pull it away at this point; don't just cut it off at soil level, or it will regrow." UCCE-IM: "Suckers should be removed completely, not just clipped off where they emerge from the soil." TRE: cutting at ground level "multiplies the problem as three may come up where one was cut off." FG-Z: "If you cut above it, then it will simply sprout faster". Also RHS-SUCK, DAVID-A, HARK, EAST, ROSS-S, YAT-P, UCCE-VEN.

**AF-18 (supported interpretation).** Sources disagree on method. Tear, pull, rip, push down or break off: RHS-SUCK, RHS-FAQ, UCCE-IM, DAVID-A, HARK, YAT-P, EAST (below-ground shoots), GA-2014. Cut cleanly at the origin: UCCE-VEN, TRE, FG-Z, ARS-SP, and EAST for shoots above the union. UCONN, in general sucker guidance, says young suckers "can just be rubbed or twisted off" and pencil-thick ones are cut with pruners (C-1).

**AF-19 (supported interpretation).** The stated reason for tearing is that it removes the dormant buds at the sucker's base. RHS-SUCK: tear "so that the majority of dormant basal buds are removed, thus reducing the possibility of regrowth." UCCE-IM: pulling "will remove any adjacent buds as well and prevent the sucker from growing back." HARK: to "remove the dormant bud tissue that causes regrowth." ROSS-S: take "some of the surrounding bark which has dormant eyes". DAVID-A: "A clean cut can often stimulate regrowth". ARS-SP reports the opposite experience (C-1).

**AF-20 (established).** Suckers are best removed early, while young. DAVID-A: "easiest when the shoot is young." YAT-P: "remove suckers as soon as you spot them, they're quite difficult to wrench off once they get established!" ARS-SP: a sucker left to mature means removal results in "even more suckers arising from the same spot". NZRS-W: "eliminated at the earliest opportunity." Also HARK, UCONN. FG-Z differs on waiting for flowers to identify (C-5).

**AF-21 (established).** Suckers are removed because they draw strength from the grafted rose and can take it over. DAVID-A: "a sucker can draw strength away from the grafted rose, weakening the plant above. It may even take over entirely." NZRS-W: suckers "take goodness away from the bush itself." GA-2014: "it will take over and the big mother rose will gradually get weaker and weaker." GA-2020: they "divert energy". Also EAST, TRE, UCONN. This agrees with `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-8.

**AF-22 (expert opinion, limited).** Precautions are each given by a single source: YAT-P "Put on your gloves, support the stem with one hand"; HARK "Refill the soil and firm it gently around the base"; GA-2014's grower, if using a knife, dabs water-based paint on the wound and piles soil back.

**AF-23 (expert opinion, single source).** For repeated suckering after pulling had failed, GA-2020 lifts the whole rose "in winter, when the plant is dormant", washes the roots, and cuts away "a bulbous area way down in the rootball" with a sterilised pruning saw, then prunes damaged roots and the top growth.

#### Leaving it, and getting help (Q5)

**AF-24 (established).** The rose's own basal shoots are valuable and are to be kept. NZRS-W: "Basal shoots are the lifeblood of the plant and are to be protected at all costs". ARS-SP: "NEVER remove basal breaks!" TAMU-W: basal breaks "are the life blood of any rose bush." TRE: water shoots "grow from the bud union and grow into the new stems of the rose." This agrees with `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-10.

**AF-25 (expert opinion, limited).** Sources tie removal to being certain and point to experienced growers when in doubt. DAVID-A: "If you're certain it's a sucker, remove it by hand". VSRG: "If in doubt, call on one of our senior members to determine if it is a sucker." NZRS-D describes free demonstrations where "you can learn what to do from local rose experts" (a pruning context, not sucker-specific).

#### Photographs (Q4)

No finding. No source located addresses what a photograph can establish about a suspected sucker (§4.3). The features sources rely on are point of origin, which may lie below the soil (AF-2, AF-12), and leaves and flowers (AF-3 to AF-6). `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-34 (Low) and AF-41 remain the project's general position on photographs.

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Points of Variation

Reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` §6. These are recorded ambiguity, not resolved.

**C-1 — Tear or pull, versus cut (AF-18).** RHS-SUCK: "Tear, rather than cut away". RHS-FAQ, UCCE-IM, DAVID-A, HARK and YAT-P say pull, tear or rip; EAST says "push downwards" for a shoot from underground; GA-2014's grower breaks suckers off "by stamping on them" or scrapes them out with a knife, and not with secateurs. UCCE-VEN, TRE and FG-Z say cut at the origin, and ARS-SP says cutting flush "is more effective in preventing re-suckering than just yanking it off!" EAST cuts a shoot that arises above the union. Both sides agree that removal is at the origin, not at soil level (AF-17). No comparative evidence was found. Unresolved.

**C-2 — How dependable leaf features are (AF-3, AF-4).** ILL-S says sucker leaves and flowers are "totally different", and RHS-FAQ says leaves "will often differ". DAVID-A, TRE-W, EAST, ARS-SP and FG-Z say leaf features can mislead, most often with the rootstock 'Dr. Huey'. The difference may reflect which rootstock each source has in mind (AF-9). Preserved as two findings.

**C-3 — Whether an own-root rose has "suckers" (AF-11).** FG-Z: own-root roses "cannot produce a sucker because there is no understock." RHS-FAQ: an own-root rose "may still produce suckers from the roots, but these shoots will be identical to the rest of the plant". DAVID-A: such shoots are "not a sucker in the traditional sense." The sources agree in substance that such shoots are the variety; they use the word "sucker" differently. Terminology unresolved.

**C-4 — How deep the bud union is planted (AF-12).** RHS-PL: at soil level, "not below as this is reported to increase the risk of rose dieback". HARK: 2.5 to 5 cm below the soil. FG-B: "Always bury the bud union on a grafted rose." EAST plants with the union above soil so that shoots from below it "can only be suckers". OSU-AE: above the soil in a mild Oregon climate, two inches below in a harsh one. The difference reflects climate and practice. Unresolved.

**C-5 — When to identify and remove (AF-6, AF-20).** DAVID-A, YAT-P, ARS-SP, NZRS-W, HARK and UCONN say remove suckers early or as soon as spotted. FG-Z says "the first spring flowering is the perfect time to identify them", when rootstock flowers show. The positions differ in whether flowers are awaited to confirm identity. Unresolved.

**C-6 — Whether growth at or above the union is always the rose's own (AF-1, AF-14).** VSRG: "if above the graft – basal shoot", and TRE-W says the same of growth at or above the graft. EAST states that the rootstock can shoot from any part of its stem above the union if it was not cut back close enough. Only EAST states this. Unresolved.

**Uncertainty preserved.** Whether a beginner can locate the bud union and judge a shoot's origin on a dormant plant, and what a photograph can establish, are not answered by the evidence (§4.3, §9).

## 6. Approved Practical Defaults Carried Forward

The following ROC-recommended defaults were adopted for questions the evidence left open, and are approved for operational use alongside the Assessed Findings. Each may be overridden by a Founder at any time.

- **R-1 (can a beginner find the union and judge origin when dormant):** Pip treats a shoot as a sucker only when the gardener confirms, on the plant, that it starts below a visible bud union; otherwise the answer is "not sure".
- **R-2 (what a photograph can establish):** a photograph prompts the gardener to check the origin in person; Pip does not confirm a sucker from a photograph alone (consistent with `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-34 and AF-41).
- **R-3 (New Zealand rootstocks):** Pip gives no rootstock-specific appearance cues.
- **R-4 (tear versus cut regrowth):** method as Decision D4 (§2).

The ROC's own reasoned synthesis recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` §9 is not adopted (EAS §2.10; ROC OM §8.4A).
