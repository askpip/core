## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on whether, and how, a recently planted bush rose's youth should restrict the MVP's pruning guidance.

It supports the **"recent planting"** Suitability and Safety Area identified in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 Architecture §5.4 — one of nine areas the journey must address before pruning guidance proceeds. Unlike the six MVP-supported observations (which this ARC does not address), this is a **gate**: it determines whether the journey may proceed to those six observations at all, or must restrict itself to dead-wood removal only, before any diagnostic content is offered.

This ARC is the source from which KIT will build a Suitability Gate PKR, distinct from PKR-SGT-000001 (the dormancy gate), per Architecture §5.4 listing these as separate areas.

## 2. Founder Decision Record

Per ROC Operations Manual §11.9/§12.2, this ARC is created following documented Founder approval.

**Decision:** Approved, adopting a **two-tier gate design** — not the three-tier design AF-4 raised as a possibility.

**Decided by:** Shaphan, AskPIP Founder Authority, 23 August 2026, across a four-part research series the Founder personally drove deeper at each stage, explicitly framing this as safety-critical: "we cannot guide a gardener to prune a rosebush that is not even old enough to prune." Each revision (FRD-01 → -04) was commissioned by direct Founder follow-up questions rather than accepted at the first, thinner pass.

**Scope of approval:**

- **AF-1** (Moderate) — a bush rose's full pruning treatment (the MVP's six supported observations) should generally wait until the rose is established, placed by the evidence at roughly three or more years after planting; dead wood removal specifically may proceed sooner — is approved and **operationalised** into the gate's core rule.
- **AF-2** (Low) — observable signals (active new growth; cane count above a roughly three-cane newly-planted baseline; firmness when gently tested) offer a rough indicator of establishment when planting date is unknown — is approved and **operationalised** as the gate's fallback path, with an explicit instruction that any ambiguous or uncertain signal reading must default to the conservative (not-yet-established) outcome, never to a guessed "established" pass.
- **AF-3** (Moderate for the positive signal only; ambiguous for the negative) — a visible knot or swelling at the base indicates a grafted rose, but its absence does not reliably indicate own-root — is approved as a **documented finding** but **not operationalised** into this gate's logic. It strengthens, rather than weakens, the case for keeping the gate propagation-agnostic (below), since building gate logic on an unreliable negative signal would misclassify buried-graft roses. Whether to add a "can you see a knot near the base" question to the plant profile questionnaire — recorded as data, not wired into this gate — remains open; see §6.
- **AF-4** (Low) — light, mild shaping pruning is generally acceptable after one full growing season, distinct from heavier pruning — is approved as a **documented finding** but explicitly **not operationalised**. The three-tier gate design this finding suggests (dead-wood-only → dead-wood-plus-light-shaping → full set) was considered and deliberately deferred: no source states which of the MVP's six specific observations would count as "light" versus "heavy," and resolving that requires a KIT/Founder judgement call not evidenced here. The Founder chose to ship the simpler, already-fully-evidenced two-tier design now and revisit this later. See §6.

**Propagation type (own-root vs. grafted):** the gate is **propagation-agnostic** for V1. This was decided at FRD-03 (own-root roses establish "a little longer" per Hunt County Master Gardeners, but the difference is unquantified) and reinforced at FRD-04 (AF-3's negative-signal ambiguity means a gate branch on propagation type could not be reliably populated by gardener observation anyway).

**Preserved, not adopted:** RHS's "prune back in the first winter after planting" guidance (FRD-01 Source 3) is preserved as documented, unreconciled minority evidence against a now four-source consensus for the longer restraint period. It was neither disproven nor incorporated — see §5.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each Assessed Finding is reproduced here faithfully from the Founder-approved Evidence Assessments in FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01 through -04, without new interpretation, per ROC Operations Manual §12.4.

### AF-1 — Restraint Period for Full Pruning

**Finding:** A bush rose's full pruning treatment (the MVP's complete six-observation set) should generally wait until the rose is established — placed by the evidence at roughly three or more years after planting, not one season. Dead wood removal specifically may proceed sooner, since removing genuinely dead tissue does not remove the plant's own growth capacity the way framework or crossing-stem pruning does.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Four independent sources converge on a multi-year restraint period: one institutional (University of Illinois Extension, stating "three or more years"), two rosarian/rose-society (Fine Gardening's Zimmerman, "third year or so"; Marin Rose Society's Londeree, exempting only dead wood from the newly-planted restriction), and one general-shrub commercial source (ForeverYard, "2-3 years") converging independently. Not High confidence because even the strongest single source states an approximate range rather than a precise, validated threshold, and one source (RHS) points toward a materially shorter window, unreconciled — see §5.

**Sources:** Cornell Cooperative Extension (Nassau County); Johnson County K-State Research and Extension; University of Illinois Extension; Fine Gardening (Zimmerman); Marin Rose Society (Londeree, "Pruning Roses"). Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01 §4 (Sources 1, 2) and FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-03 §"Additional Evidence Collected" (Sources 11, 12).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** No source gives a precise threshold; "three or more years" and "first few years" are the most specific figures found. RHS's shorter guidance is unreconciled (§5). The own-root/grafted timing difference is flagged (Hunt County Master Gardeners) but unquantified.

### AF-2 — Observable Signals When Planting Date Is Unknown

**Finding:** Where a gardener does not know when their rose was planted, several observable characteristics correlate with establishment: the presence of active new growth; a cane count meaningfully above the roughly three-cane baseline typical of a newly purchased Grade #1 rose; and the base of the plant feeling firmly anchored rather than loose when gently tested.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Rationale:** Three independent sources, none an extension or university publication, converge on plausible but unvalidated signals. No source combines them into one validated composite test, and reliability may vary by propagation type (own-root versus budded), which this project has not separately quantified.

**Sources:** Spring Hill Nursery (grading standard); Marin Rose Society (Londeree, "Before You Buy New Roses"); ForeverYard. Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-02 §"Additional Evidence Collected" (Sources 8, 9, 10).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Not validated in combination; no rose-specific research confirms these signals predict safe pruning readiness specifically, as opposed to general establishment.

---

### AF-3 — Grafted vs. Own-Root Visibility (documented, not operationalised)

**Finding:** A visible knot or swelling at a rose's base reliably indicates a grafted rose when present. Its absence does not reliably indicate an own-root rose — it may simply mean the graft union is planted below the visible soil line, a deliberate, climate-dependent practice (buried for winter protection in colder regions, per an Oregon State University Extension source).

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate for the positive signal (visible knot present → grafted); the negative case does not carry an Evidence Confidence Level — it is an absence of information, not evidence of own-root, per EAS §2.9.

**Rationale:** Two independent sources agree closely on the bud union's appearance, consistent with basic, largely undisputed rose horticultural anatomy. One institutional source (Ask Extension/Oregon State University Extension) establishes the climate-dependent burial practice that makes the negative case ambiguous.

**Sources:** Epic Gardening (Rowe); David Austin Roses; Ask Extension/Oregon State University Extension (Oldfield). Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-04 §"Additional Evidence Collected" (Sources 15, 16, 17).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Not operationalised into this gate (§2). Whether to add this as a plant-profile question (data collection only) is an open product decision, not resolved by this ARC.

---

### AF-4 — Light First-Year Pruning (documented, not operationalised)

**Finding:** Light, mild shaping pruning is generally considered acceptable on a rose that has completed at least one full growing season, distinct from the heavier pruning that AF-1 places further out. What counts as "light" is not defined against this MVP's specific six supported observations by any source found.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Rationale:** Two independent, non-institutional sources agree on the general principle. University of Maryland Extension was checked specifically and is silent rather than corroborating.

**Sources:** Homeguides/SFGate (unattributed); Marin Rose Society (Londeree, "Pruning Roses," the same article as AF-1's Source 12, a distinct passage). Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-04 §"Additional Evidence Collected" (Source 18) and FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-03 §"Additional Evidence Collected" (Source 12).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Not operationalised (§2) — assigning specific MVP observations to a "light" tier is an unresolved design question, not a research gap this ARC attempts to close.

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Evidence

**RHS, "Roses: Planting"** (FRD-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01 §4, Source 3) states normal pruning should begin "in the first winter after planting" — materially sooner than the roughly-three-year consensus AF-1 draws from four other sources. This was investigated across three further revisions and never reconciled. The leading hypothesis — that RHS may describe a lighter, establishment-focused cutback rather than the MVP's full observation set — is ROC's own reasoned synthesis, not evidenced by any source, and per EAS §2.10 is recorded here as a research lead, not adopted. This ARC's approval proceeds on the four-source consensus notwithstanding this unreconciled outlier, per the Founder's decision at FRD-03 Decision Point 3.
