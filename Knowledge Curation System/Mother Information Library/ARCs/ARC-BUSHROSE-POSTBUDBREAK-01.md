## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on removing dead, damaged or diseased wood after bud break (`BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK`): whether such removal is acceptable once growth has begun and how it differs from structural pruning (Q1); differences by cause — frost or winter damage, breakage, canker or disease, borer (Q2); timing limits and their reasons (Q3); how far to cut and whether the healthy-pith check applies (Q4); and what the evidence bears on the dormancy gate's scope (Q5, decided by the Founders, §2).

It resolves the tension recorded in the commission between the frost-damage deferral (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` AF-14, C-2) and the dormancy window (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-1).

This ARC is the source from which KIT will revise PKR-SGT-000001, PKR-DEC-000001 and PKR-DEC-000004.

**Decided by:** Shaphan, AskPIP Founder Authority, 29 September 2026, in chat (Founder Decision Points 1–5 of `FRD-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`; the ROC recommendation was chosen at every point).

**Implementation decisions recorded with the approval (for KIT):**

1. **D2 — Dormancy gate scope (b).** PKR-SGT-000001 governs structural and whole-bush pruning only. Removal of wood confirmed dead, damaged or diseased may proceed after bud break, on these conditions: cut to healthy (white) pith (AF-19); the approved canker margin and hygiene apply (AF-20; `ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` AF-13, AF-15, AF-16); remove only the confirmed wood and keep damage to new growth small (AF-17); healthy canes are not shortened for shape in that session (AF-3).
2. **D3 — Decision Logic (b).** PKR-DEC-000001 (dead wood) and PKR-DEC-000004 (damaged growth) follow D2: their condition becomes "the dormancy gate passes, or the session is limited to removing wood confirmed dead, damaged or diseased under the D2 conditions". PKR-DEC-000004's frost-damage deferral stays as approved (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` AF-14), but the gardener's return leads to damage removal rather than a gate refusal. The late-season caution (AF-15) is shown as advice, with diseased wood exempt (AF-16).
3. **D4/D5 — Further research.** The recommended defaults for R-1 and R-2 are adopted (§6). Nothing is commissioned. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis (R-3a–c) is not adopted.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` §7 as approved. Source codes refer to the Source Register at §8.

#### Q1 — Removal after bud break, and how it differs from structural pruning

**AF-1 (supported interpretation).** Several sources say dead, damaged or diseased wood can be removed at any time, or as soon as it is seen. RHS-DB: "Remove all dead and damaged wood as soon as it is seen." CBG: "Dead, diseased, spindly, or crossing branches can be removed at any time." NYBG-FAQ: "If there is diseased growth on the plant, cut that back at any time, including the fall." PTC (climbing roses): "Dead wood can be removed at any time."

**AF-2 (expert opinion, limited).** Two sources that address a rose already in leaf say dead or damaged wood can still be removed. OSU-LATE (climbing rose, April): "If there are many dead canes that can be removed without damaging the foliage then go ahead and prune them out," adding that this helps "increase air circulation" and "reduce disease problems later in the season". DA-APR (commercial): "The main aim is to remove anything that's dead or damaged and give the plant some shape."

**AF-3 (supported interpretation).** Heavy pruning after leaf-out is described as having a cost. OSU-LATE: "Any type of heavy pruning at this point has the potential to reduce flowering," and it "may also reduce the overall size of the plant for the season." DA-APR: "cutting back at this stage might delay flowering a little." AEX-615970: pruning "only becomes a problem if you have a couple of inches of new growth already leafed out." Sources differ on whether full pruning should still go ahead (C-1).

**AF-4 (established).** General pruning guides place removal of dead, damaged and diseased wood as the first step of the late-winter or early-spring session, and place major pruning at or near bud break (RHS-MB, OSU, ILL, ISU-S, UGA, PUR, UMD, DBG; TAMU-EP "All major pruning should be done in late winter, just as buds break dormancy"; NYBG-LG "before the leaves start to expand"). These guides do not say whether dead, damaged or diseased wood may be removed later in the season.

**AF-5 (supported interpretation).** Removal of dead stems appears in growing-season and summer care guidance. UMD: "Throughout the growing season you should also do some 'dead-head' trimming, removing the spent blossoms and small, dead canes and branches." YAT-S (New Zealand): "remove any dead or thin stems" during summer pruning. YAT-AU: "completely remove any dead or spindly stems". LSU (Louisiana, late August to early September): "First, entirely remove all diseased or dead canes by cutting them back to their point of origin."

#### Q2 — By cause

**Frost or winter damage**

**AF-6 (supported interpretation).** Frost can cause dieback of young shoots after spring growth has begun, and dieback after early pruning can call for a second cut. RHS-DB lists "Browning and dieback of the tips of young shoots in spring (usually the result of frost damage)" and advises removing dead and damaged wood "as soon as it is seen". OSU: "Earlier pruning (before the last hard frost or forecasted cold weather) may cause die-back that necessitates repruning."

**AF-7 (supported interpretation).** Sources differ on whether winter damage is best judged before new foliage emerges (PUR) or after new growth begins (UNL-L, shrub roses; FG-Z). See C-2.

**Canker or disease**

**AF-8 (supported interpretation).** Diseased or infected wood is to be removed promptly, or at any time: NYBG-FAQ ("at any time, including the fall"), AEX-236546 ("Remove and destroy all infected/dead portions of the cane immediately"), RHS-DB ("as soon as it is seen"). UCIPM-CB differs (AF-11, C-3).

**AF-9 (expert opinion).** VT-BOT gives a reason for prompt removal of dead wood where Botryosphaeria is present: "*Botryosphaeria* spp. colonize dead wood and can move from dead to healthy wood." It also lists "heat, drought, freeze injury" among stresses that "can predispose trees and shrubs to infection". The publication covers woody plants generally; rose is in its host list.

**AF-10 (established).** Wounds, including pruning cuts, are an entry route for rose canker fungi. PNW-C: "Both infect cane wounds caused by pruning, harvesting, insects, thorn scars, or scratches." CLE-D: "Wounds are a major way the fungus enters the plant." UCIPM-CB: "The pathogen readily infects wounded or weakened areas of canes." VT-BOT: "Wounded plant tissue is susceptible to infection by *Botryosphaeria* fungi."

**AF-11 (expert opinion).** UCIPM-CB advises: "Prune out infected canes during dry weather during the dormant season." It is the only located source that places removal of infected canes in the dormant season. See C-3.

**Borer**

**AF-12 (expert opinion, limited).** Borer-infested canes are removed when found during spring and summer. MRS: "inspect canes in spring (mid-April – mid June) for egg laying incisions or swelling. If you observe any, remove and destroy infested canes." USU-RSG (written mainly for brambles; roses named as hosts): "Prune out and destroy infested canes in spring through summer to remove larvae."

**AF-13 (supported interpretation).** Borer-damaged canes are cut below the damaged section until the pith is white or the hole is gone. MRS: "Cut each cane below the wilted portion and examine the pith – continue to make short cuts until the pith is white." PTC-CB: "prune the rose cane below the wasp nest area where there may be a slight swelling." AZ1528: "Cut the cane back an inch or two at a time until the hole is no longer visible." USU-RSG: "Prune below the point of insect boring activity, or remove entire canes."

**Mechanical breakage**

**AF-14 (expert opinion, limited).** Only RHS-DB addresses damaged wood outside the pruning session: "Remove all dead and damaged wood as soon as it is seen." No located source addresses broken canes after bud break specifically.

#### Q3 — Timing limits and reasons

**AF-15 (established).** Pruning and deadheading late in the growing season are discouraged because they encourage new growth that winter cold may damage. ISU-S: "Pruning late in the growing season can reduce winter survival rates." NYBG-LG: "tender growth at the end of the season is at greater risk of winter damage." CBG: deadheading "should not be done after Labor Day to avoid encouraging new growth that may be damaged by early cold temperatures". ILL: "Flowers should not be cut after October 1 to allow the plant to begin hardening off for the winter."

**AF-16 (supported interpretation).** Two sources exempt diseased wood from that restraint. NYBG-FAQ: "cut that back at any time, including the fall." ISU-S, in its fall section: "prune out obviously diseased canes". No located source says whether dead (as distinct from diseased) wood is exempt.

**AF-17 (expert opinion, limited).** The two sources that allow removal after leaf-out attach a condition. OSU-LATE: dead canes removed "without damaging the foliage". DA-APR: "If your rose is now in full leaf, it's not too late. Just go a little more gently."

**AF-18 (established absence, limited search).** No located source sets a heat or drought limit on removing dead or damaged wood. ARS-HEAT, on roses in heat stress, advises: "Prune the scorched leaves and blooms off and pray for cooler weather." UCIPM-CB's "dry weather" advice (AF-11) concerns disease spread, not heat.

#### Q4 — How far to cut, and the healthy-pith check

**AF-19 (supported interpretation).** Sources that describe removal outside the dormant session give the same cut-to-healthy-tissue instruction, and none gives a different rule for the growing season. MRS (borer, spring): "continue to make short cuts until the pith is white." VT-BOT: "Prune dead wood back to a healthy area on the branch (white to cream-colored inside when cut)." AZ1528: cut "an inch or two at a time". General guidance: RHS-PG "Prune dieback to healthy white pith"; ARS "white pith core revealed after the cut is made".

**AF-20 (established).** Diseased or cankered wood is cut well below the visibly affected area. CLE-D: "Make all pruning cuts well below the diseased areas." PNW-C: "Cut and destroy infected canes well below the affected area." AEX-236546 uses the same "well below" wording. UNL-L: "at least one inch below any evidence of disease." No common distance is given.

#### Q5 — Bearing on the dormancy gate's scope

No located source discusses an application gate. The evidence bears on Q5 in three ways, stated here without a recommendation:

- The bud-swell window is stated for main pruning (AF-4, agreeing with `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-1). No source applies it to removal of dead, damaged or diseased wood.
- Several sources state that such removal may happen at any time or as soon as seen (AF-1, AF-8, AF-14), and two address a rose already in leaf (AF-2), with a condition (AF-17).
- Sources that discuss pruning after leaf-out treat heavy pruning differently from removal of dead wood, citing reduced or delayed flowering (AF-3).

The decision on the gate's scope is for the Founders (§10).

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Points of Variation

Reproduced from `FRD-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` §6. These are recorded ambiguity, not resolved.

#### 5.1 Conflicts between sources

**C-1 — How acceptable full pruning is once the rose is in leaf (AF-3).** DA-APR: "Even if your rose has leafed up quite a bit, it's still worth pruning" and "It is much better to prune late than not at all." OSU-LATE: "It's a little late in the growing season now so proceed with caution." AEX-615970: "It only becomes a problem if you have a couple of inches of new growth already leafed out." NYBG-LG places pruning "before the leaves start to expand". Unresolved. The sources that permit it agree on a cost to flowering.

**C-2 — When to judge winter damage (AF-7).** PUR: "Roses are best pruned before the new foliage emerges so that you can easily assess winter damage." UNL-L (shrub roses): beginners "may want to delay pruning until new growth begins, which allows them to see exactly where healthy tissue is located". FG-Z describes cutting dead growth back and waiting for regrowth well into summer. Unresolved. This repeats the difference recorded as C-2 in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01`.

**C-3 — When to remove cankered or infected canes (AF-8, AF-11).** UCIPM-CB: "Prune out infected canes during dry weather during the dormant season." NYBG-FAQ: "If there is diseased growth on the plant, cut that back at any time, including the fall." RHS-DB: "Remove all dead and damaged wood as soon as it is seen." AEX-236546: "Remove and destroy all infected/dead portions of the cane immediately", while also stating "Prune live canes in the spring, not fall" (the same wording appears in CLE-D). Unresolved. UCIPM-CB concerns cane blight on caneberries and roses together; the others concern roses.

#### 5.2 Uncertainty preserved

- Whether removing a large share of a bush's canes after leaf-out (for example after severe winterkill) should be treated as removal or as heavy pruning. No source gives a threshold (§9, R-1).
- Whether growing-season wounds carry more canker risk than dormant-season wounds on roses. No rose-specific evidence was located (§9, R-2).
- Whether heat or drought should delay removal. No source addresses it (AF-18).

## 6. Approved Practical Defaults Carried Forward

The following ROC-recommended defaults were adopted for questions the evidence left open, and are approved for operational use alongside the Assessed Findings. Each may be overridden by a Founder at any time.

- **R-1 (when removal after leaf-out becomes heavy pruning):** removal after bud break is limited to wood confirmed dead, damaged or diseased by the pith check; healthy canes are not shortened for shape in the same session.
- **R-2 (canker risk of growing-season cuts):** the approved canker hygiene (cut well below, disinfect tools, bin or burn diseased prunings) applies to any removal after bud break.

The ROC's own reasoned synthesis recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` §9 is not adopted (EAS §2.10; ROC OM §8.4A).
