## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on inward-growing stems on an established, dormant bush rose: definition and purpose (Q1), visible indicators (Q2), what a photograph can and cannot establish (Q3), lookalikes (Q4), pruning choices (Q5), handling a stem with more than one condition and deferral (Q6), and task-specific safety (Q7).

It supports the **inward-growing stems** supported observation in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 Architecture, one of the six observations the pruning journey addresses once a rose has passed the recently-planted and dormancy gates (`ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`) and dead wood has been addressed (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`). This dossier and `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` were researched as one commission but are kept as two separate observations, per Founder confirmation on the Review Form. General cut mechanics remain governed by `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`.

This ARC is the source from which KIT will build the inward-growing-stems Observation PKR.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each Assessed Finding is reproduced here faithfully from the Founder-approved Evidence Assessment in `FRD-BUSHROSE-INWARDGROWTH-02` §8, without new interpretation, per ROC Operations Manual §12.4.

### AF-1 — Inward-Growing or Centre-Directed Growth Is Addressed Mainly as a Shape/Airflow Goal

**Finding:** Most sources address inward or centre-directed growth through a prospective instruction — cut to an outward-facing bud, so new growth points away from the centre — rather than treating an existing inward-growing stem as its own named defect with dedicated recognition criteria, the way crossing and rubbing are treated.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** The pattern (outward-bud cutting as the primary mechanism, rather than inward-stem recognition) is consistent across many independent sources and publisher types.

**Sources:** University of Maryland Extension; Yates New Zealand; South Pacific Roses; Rose Society of Victoria; American Rose Society; Texas A&M AgriLife.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** This is a pattern observed across the source base, not a single stated rule; a small number of sources (AF-2) do treat it as its own recognisable category. Whether sources that only give the outward-bud instruction consider existing inward growth unimportant, or simply address it prospectively rather than diagnostically, was not tested.

### AF-2 — The Stated Indicator Is Growth Direction Toward the Bush's Interior

**Finding:** The small number of sources that do name inward-growing stems as their own category describe the indicator simply as the direction of growth — toward the bush's centre — rather than any more specific visual marker.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low (few sources).

**Rationale:** Three independent sources agree on the basic indicator, though none gives a more specific visual marker than direction itself.

**Sources:** Kansas City Rose Society ("canes will grow toward the center of the bush or are otherwise not growing in a graceful, natural direction"); Dunedin Botanic Garden ("remove inward facing growth"); University of Georgia ("canes that... grow inward").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Kansas City Rose Society's own wording signals this is a soft, subjective judgement. How confidently growth direction can be judged from a photograph is addressed separately at AF-3.

### AF-3 — A Single Photograph Has Real Limits for Judging Structural Questions of This Kind

**Finding:** No rose-specific source addresses what a photograph can or cannot establish about inward-growing stems. Drawing on general professional pruning practice and one directly relevant academic study, a single, fixed-viewpoint photograph has real limits for judging a stem's true growth direction — the kind of 3D structural question professional practice addresses by physically viewing a plant from multiple sides before cutting. This finding is stated identically in `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` (its AF-4).

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low (a ROC synthesis of general, non-rose-specific evidence, extrapolated per EAS §2.10).

**Rationale:** Extension guidance independently and consistently treats multi-angle physical viewing as standard practice; a peer-reviewed urban-forestry study directly tests the underlying principle, though for urban trees, not roses.

**Sources:** NC State Extension; University of Missouri Extension (Master Gardener Core Manual); Berland et al. (2019), *Forests* 10(4):349.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** No source states this principle for inward-growth judgments specifically. Judging a stem's growth direction from a single photograph specifically was not addressed by any source at all — a genuine, undiluted evidentiary gap.

### AF-4 — Once Confirmed, an Inward-Growing Stem Is Removed, on the Same Basis as Crossing/Rubbing Stems

**Finding:** Where sources do name inward-growing stems as their own category, they instruct removing them, on the same footing as crossing or rubbing stems — as ordinary dormant-season maintenance.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low (fewer, but consistent, sources).

**Rationale:** Three independent sources, unanimous in direction (remove). This finding is Low confidence specifically because of thinner coverage than the equivalent crossing/rubbing finding, not because the instruction itself is contested — no source disagrees with removing a confirmed inward-growing stem.

**Sources:** Kansas City Rose Society ("Remove them to the ground or back to the next outward facing bud"); Dunedin Botanic Garden; University of Georgia.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Markedly fewer sources than the equivalent finding for crossing/rubbing stems (sixteen sources), because most sources address inward growth only prospectively (AF-1), rather than as a diagnosis-and-removal category.

### AF-5 — Removal Is a Full Cut to the Stem's Point of Origin

**Finding:** Where sources specify where the cut is made, an inward-growing stem is removed entirely — back to the ground, the bud union, or the larger cane it originates from — not merely shortened, consistent with the equivalent finding for crossing/rubbing stems.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Directly stated for this observation by one source, and consistent with the general structural-removal cut type evidenced at greater depth in `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` (its AF-6).

**Sources:** Kansas City Rose Society ("removed to the ground or back to the next outward facing bud").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Only one source states this for inward-growing stems specifically; the rest of the support is inferred from the general cut-type finding rather than restated per-observation. Consistent with, and does not duplicate, `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`'s general cut-mechanics findings.

### AF-6 — Deferral Guidance Concerns General Pruning Timing

**Finding:** Where sources advise waiting or deferring a pruning decision, the reason given is general dormant-season timing — not anything specific to a confirmed inward-growing stem. This finding is stated identically in `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Three independent university-extension sources agree on the general principle and its reasoning; none ties deferral to inward growth specifically.

**Sources:** Oregon State University Extension; Iowa State University Extension (Linn County); Colorado State University Extension / Ask Extension.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether a gardener should ever defer specifically because they are unsure whether a stem is genuinely inward-growing was not addressed by any source.

## 5. Preserved, Unreconciled Point of Variation

**C-1 — Whether an open, uncrowded bush centre is a universally desirable goal.** The great majority of sources treat an open centre as self-evidently desirable. One source (Rose Society of South Australia) explicitly argues against deliberately opening the centre. The same conflict recorded in `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` C-1, stated here too because it bears equally on inward-growth removal. Preserved as unresolved; AF-1 records the majority position. Practical default: §6.

## 6. Approved Practical Defaults Carried Forward

The following ROC-recommended defaults were adopted for questions the evidence left open, and are approved for operational use alongside the Assessed Findings above. Each may be overridden by a Founder at any time.

- **Beginner lookalikes and confounders (U-1):** no specific confounder list is offered; confirming any observation on the physical rose before acting is the primary safeguard. A phototropism-related confusion (a stem curving toward a light gap could be mistaken for genuine inward growth) is plausible but unsourced — a caution worth bearing in mind, not a rule.
- **Priority when a stem is both inward-growing and crossing/rubbing (U-2):** no default is needed — both classifications lead to the same action (cut), so this is not a live conflict in practice.
- **Whether professional/experienced help is ever specifically warranted (U-3):** governed by the app's general uncertainty/confidence gates, not by a rule specific to inward growth.
- **Task-specific safety for reaching into congested/interior growth (U-4):** cross-refer to `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`'s approved generic safety content and to `ARC-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`'s same default — take care not to damage a stem being kept, and consider cutting a tangled stem in sections.
- **Whether a second photograph from a different angle would help (U-5):** where a first photograph leaves confidence low on growth-direction judgment (AF-3), request a second photograph — ideally one giving a clearer view into the bush's interior — as reasonable operational practice.
- **Whether inward-growing and crossing/rubbing stems commonly co-occur (U-6):** no source states or tests this, and the Founder's own working expectation that they would was tested directly and not supported. No default is needed — it does not affect the gate logic for either observation. This is a permanent limitation of the evidence, not a pending research item.
