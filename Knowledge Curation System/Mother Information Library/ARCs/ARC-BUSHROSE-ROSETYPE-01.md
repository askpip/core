## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on recognising a supported rose type: how a beginner gardener's rose can be qualified as inside or outside the approved Bush Rose scope, first from a label or purchase information and second, where none exists, from features observable on the plant and in a photograph, and what cannot be established without a label or expert help.

It supports the **rose-type scope qualification** step in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 Architecture: confirming a gardener's rose is a Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, or (provisionally) Grandiflora bush rose, as approved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`, before any pruning-observation guidance is offered. It draws the boundary between the three included classes and eight excluded lookalike types (Shrub, Patio, Miniature, Miniflora, Groundcover, Climbing and climbing sports, Standard, Old Garden/species roses), and records how "bush" is used inconsistently at New Zealand retail.

This ARC is the source from which KIT will build the rose-type scope-qualification PKR, and the source the Photo and Comparison-Image Requirements document and the Rose-Type Comparison Image Sourcing Brief / Field Guide (production work, not findings) were built from.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each Assessed Finding is reproduced here faithfully from the Founder-approved Evidence Assessment in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §7–§8, without new interpretation, per ROC Operations Manual §12.4. AF-21 and AF-22 are omitted from this section per §2 above; their full text remains in the FRD.

### Included classes

**AF-1 — Flower Arrangement Separates the Three Included Classes**

**Finding:** Flower arrangement is the feature all consulted class descriptions use first. Hybrid Tea roses bear single or few flowers per stem, Floribundas bear clusters, and Grandifloras bear Hybrid Tea-form flowers in clusters.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** RHS-MB, ARS, RSNSW, ILL-T, LBG (Hybrid Tea/Floribunda); ARS, YAT, HEI, CLE (Grandiflora).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether the features hold for every cultivar (see AF-5).

**AF-2 — Grandiflora Is the Tallest of the Three Included Classes**

**Finding:** Sources that state a height describe Grandiflora as the tallest of the three included classes.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** YAT ("up to 1.8m"), ARS ("6 to 8 feet or more"), HEI ("5 to 7 feet"), ILL-T, CLE.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Actual heights in New Zealand gardens.

**AF-3 — Hybrid Tea and Floribunda Height Ranges Overlap, With No Separating Threshold**

**Finding:** Height ranges for Hybrid Tea and Floribunda overlap across sources, no source gives a height threshold separating them, and one source (OSU) describes Floribunda as the larger plant.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** YAT, LBG, ILL-T, HEI, RHS-MB, OSU.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether any authority gives a threshold that was not found.

**AF-4 — Grandiflora Is Absent From Key New Zealand Classification Sources**

**Finding:** Grandiflora is described in United States and some New Zealand consumer sources, but is not among the options in the New Zealand Rose Society's Type filter, is not among the nine classifications listed by the Rose Society of New South Wales, and does not appear in the category lists of two New Zealand nurseries as extracted.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** ARS, ILL-T, YAT, CLE, HEI (describe it); NZRS-F, RSNSW, TPS, MAT (omit it).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Absence from a category menu or type list does not show absence from New Zealand gardens; actual frequency of Grandiflora labels in New Zealand.

**AF-5 — Individual Cultivars Are Placed in Different Classes by Different Authorities**

**Finding:** Some individual cultivars are placed in different classes by different authorities.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** ILL-T ("the same rose may be placed in different classes by different authorities"); NZRS-F ("some roses may fit into more than one category"); DG (lists 'Queen Elizabeth' as both Hybrid Tea and cluster-flowered, with three different height ranges).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** How often a New Zealand gardener meets a disputed cultivar.

**AF-6 — Grandiflora Pruning Treatment Rests on Thin Evidence**

**Finding:** Grandiflora pruning is treated as the same as, or lighter than, Hybrid Tea pruning across the small number of sources that address it at all; the RHS has no Grandiflora pruning group.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Sources:** OSU (treats "Hybrid Tea-Grandiflora" as one category); WA (one cut height across Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora); HEI (advises lighter cutting for Grandiflora).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether Grandiflora pruning response actually differs from Hybrid Tea. Later use: N — a pruning matter reserved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` §10.

### Excluded types

**AF-7 — Shrub Roses Are Larger, Open, Spreading or Arching Bushes**

**Finding:** Shrub roses are described as larger, open, spreading or arching bushes.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** RHS-SH ("an open and spreading shape, often with arching prickly branches"); ARS ("sprawling habit" growing "5 to 15 feet or more in every direction"); RSNSW ("large bushes, often tall and spreading with many branching canes of a lax arching habit").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Descriptions are typical, not universal; individual shrub cultivars can be compact.

**AF-8 — The Shrub Class Is Heterogeneous and Overlaps Bush Habit**

**Finding:** The shrub class is heterogeneous, and size and habit alone do not separate it from bush roses; it functions as a "catch all" for roses that do not fit elsewhere.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** ILL-T ("catch all" class); YAT (0.5 to 6 m range); DA; WAI-S; MAT.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** "Catch all" comes from one extension page; how often shrub roses sold as bush roses reach the app.

**AF-9 — Patio Roses Are a Separate Category**

**Finding:** Patio roses are a category separate from Hybrid Tea and Floribunda in the Royal Horticultural Society, the New Zealand Rose Society and New Zealand nurseries.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** RHS-SB (groups Patio with Polyantha and Miniature as "small bush roses" of 30–75 cm); RHS-PG (Pruning Group 22); NZRS-F; TPS; MAT; WAI-P; TUI.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** RSNSW lists no patio category; whether every New Zealand seller uses the category.

**AF-10 — Sources Differ on the Nearest Class for a Patio Rose**

**Finding:** Sources differ on the class a patio rose is nearest to: with miniatures and polyanthas, a "down-sized version of a floribunda," "closer to dwarf floribundas" and not miniatures, or a general term for any container rose most commonly floribunda-miniature crosses.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** RHS-SB, RHS-PG; WAI-P; PB; JP.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Sources are largely commercial; where a New Zealand-sold patio rose belongs for pruning purposes.

**AF-11 — Miniature Roses Are Small, Upper Heights About 50–75 cm**

**Finding:** Miniature roses are described as small, with stated upper heights of about 50 to 75 cm.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** RSNSW-M ("no more than 50 cm"); RHS-SB ("often no more than 25cm"); ARS; ILL-T; YAT; LBG.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Figures are given in mixed units and several sources combine miniatures with minifloras.

**AF-12 — Miniflora Is Distinguished by Bloom and Foliage Size**

**Finding:** Miniflora roses are distinguished from miniatures and floribundas by bloom and foliage size, which is not observable on a dormant plant.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** RSNSW-M ("blooms and foliage larger than Miniature roses and smaller than Floribunda roses"); ARS ("intermediate bloom size and foliage").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Any plant-form cue for minifloras, distinct from bloom/foliage size, was not located.

**AF-13 — Groundcover Roses Are a Separate Category Marketed With "Bush" Wording**

**Finding:** Groundcover roses are a separate category, but at least one marketer describes a groundcover cultivar as forming "a low, dense and compact bush."

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** RSNSW ("low spreading plants to 50cm"); YAT; NZRS-A; TPS; NZRS-F; TES (Flower Carpet, "ground cover rose" forming "a low, dense and compact bush").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Appearance evidence rests substantially on one marketer's description.

**AF-14 — Climbing Sports of Bush-Form Roses Exist and Are Catalogued as Climbing Types**

**Finding:** Climbing sports of bush-form roses exist and are catalogued as climbing types, with long canes and flowers on shorter laterals.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** FVR ("climbing sports of bush-form Hybrid Teas that produce long canes (6' to 10')" with flowers "on shorter laterals"); NZRS-F (Type values "Climbing", "Min Climbing", "Pat Climbing", "Tea Climbing"); TPS; WAI.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Whether a climbing sport of an in-scope cultivar ever reverts to bush form was not addressed by any source.

**AF-15 — Standard Roses Are Recognisable by a Tall Bare Grafted Stem**

**Finding:** A standard rose is recognisable by a single tall bare stem carrying a grafted crown.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Sources:** RHS-ST ("a single tall stem, topped with foliage and flowers, like a small tree"); RSNSW; WAI; MAT.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** How an older standard with regrowth below the graft point should be recognised was not addressed.

**AF-16 — Old Garden and Species Roses Are Defined by History and Flowering Habit**

**Finding:** Old Garden Roses and species roses are defined by history, lineage and flowering habit, not by plant form.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** ARS (Old Garden Roses "existed prior to 1867"; species roses "usually single-petaled (4 to 8 petals), once-blooming"); RSNSW; NZRS-F (lists many historic classes: Alba, Bourbon, Damask, Gallica and others).

**Limitations/uncertainty:** No source gives a plant-form rule for this class, only historical/flowering-season facts.

### Labels, names and marketing terms

**AF-17 — New Zealand Retailers Use "Bush" Inconsistently**

**Finding:** New Zealand retailers use "bush" in more than one sense, including for patio, shrub-category and groundcover roses, not only for the included classes.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** TPS (names "Hybrid Tea Bush Roses", "Floribunda Bush Roses" and also a "Rose Bush" category); YAT; TUI; WAI-P (uses "Rose Bush" for a patio rose); WAI-S (uses it for a shrub-category rose); TES.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Commercial sample, not systematic; prevalence across New Zealand retail generally.

**AF-18 — New Zealand Nursery Listings Organise Roses by Type Category**

**Finding:** New Zealand online nursery listings organise roses by type category.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** TPS (Floribunda, Hybrid Tea, Patio, Shrub, David Austin, Groundcover, Climbing, Standard and others); MAT; WAI.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** What is printed on physical plant tags was not established; Palmers gave no labelling information.

**AF-19 — The New Zealand Rose Society Rose Finder Gives a Type for Each Listed Cultivar**

**Finding:** The New Zealand Rose Society Rose Finder is a searchable directory by rose name, breeder and international name, with a Type value for each entry, and states some roses may fit more than one category.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** NZRS-F.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** How many New Zealand-available cultivars it holds was not established.

### Observable features, identification and uncertainty

**AF-20 — Identifying an Unlabelled Rose Is Described as Difficult**

**Finding:** Sources that address identifying an unlabelled rose describe it as difficult and dependent on many plant parts (foliage, stipules, armature, sepals, scent, bloom and growth habit), with bloom and growth habit called the most variable.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** HRA; ROSS ("the foliage is crucial for an accurate identification"); AEX (declined to identify a rose by type, citing "over 30,000 varieties").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Difficulty for modern bush roses specifically, as distinct from older/unnamed roses (HRA's focus).

**AF-23 — RHS Advises Pruning an Unidentified Inherited Rose as a Hybrid Tea**

**Finding:** The Royal Horticultural Society advises pruning an unidentified, overgrown inherited rose as a Hybrid Tea, as a practical default when the type is unknown and waiting for flowering is undesirable.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Sources:** RHS-MBP ("If you inherit an overgrown rose and don't know what type it is ... it is best to prune as for a hybrid tea").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** One source, one country; whether the default is safe if the rose is actually a shrub, climber or standard is not addressed (see R-5b, §6).

**AF-24 — Sources Direct an Unsure Gardener to Experienced People**

**Finding:** Sources point an unsure gardener to a rose society, rosarian or second opinion rather than resolving classification themselves.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Sources:** NZRS-Q ("contact your local rose society or head along to a pruning demonstration"); HRA (recommends a rosarian); ROSS (advises being "prepared to say you don't know" and getting "a second opinion").

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Availability of local society help to a beginner; two of the three sources do not address classification directly.

## 6. Recommendations Carried Forward as Approved Practical Defaults

Founder Decision Point 2 (accept the defaults in R-1 to R-4, or commission any of them) was not separately answered by either Founder on the Review Form. Consistent with the overall "Approve as ARC" outcome and with no commissioning request recorded, the ROC's recommended practical defaults from `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §9 are carried forward as approved operational defaults. Each may be overridden by a Founder at any time.

- **R-1 (dormant-plant features for scope placement):** no source addresses which dormant-plant features a beginner can use to place a rose in scope. Dormant-plant features are not used to establish that a rose is inside scope; a type stated on a label, receipt, listing or by an experienced grower is used where available, and otherwise the type is treated as unknown.
- **R-2 (New Zealand plant tags and pot labels):** a stated type term found on a tag, receipt or listing is accepted as evidence where present; the word "bush" alone is not (AF-17).
- **R-3 (Rose Finder coverage):** the New Zealand Rose Society Rose Finder is treated as one verification route for a named cultivar (AF-19), without relying on it for coverage of all commonly sold cultivars.
- **R-4 (Grandiflora pruning evidence):** retain the provisional inclusion approved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (§5 above) and issue no Grandiflora-specific pruning guidance until stronger evidence exists (AF-6).

**R-5 (ROC's own reasoned synthesis, Not Assigned per EAS §2.10) — preserved, not adopted:** three questions the evidence does not itself answer (whether a gardener's recollection of single-versus-clustered blooms is reliable when dormant, given AF-1; whether pruning an unidentified rose as a Hybrid Tea would harm a shrub/climbing/standard rose, given AF-7/AF-14/AF-15; whether a gardener can distinguish an unpruned groundcover rose from a small shrub when dormant, given AF-13) are recorded for future reference only. None is acted on until independent evidence is found; none was separately raised by either Founder.

**Not yet actioned:** whether to commission expert consultation, an in-store label survey, or a Rose Finder sample check (R-1 to R-3's own "Founder decision needed" lines) remains open and is not resolved by this ARC.
