## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ARC is the authoritative, structured representation of the Founder-approved information produced by the research commission on distinguishing dead wood from living wood on an established, dormant bush rose, before a pruning cut.

It supports the "dead versus living wood" observation identified in the Ask Pip MVP – Bush Rose V1 Architecture §5.2, one of six MVP-supported observations. This ARC covers only that one observation; the other five (damaged growth; crossing or rubbing stems; inward-growing stems; weak or congested growth; the main framework to retain) are not addressed here.

This ARC is the source from which the PIP Knowledge Integration Technician (KIT) will build one or more draft PIP Knowledge Records (PKRs), once KIT's Operations Manual exists and KIT is bootstrapped to operate.

## 4. Approved Findings — Full Content

Each Assessed Finding is reproduced here faithfully from the Founder-approved Evidence Assessment (FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §7–§8), without new interpretation, assumption or research, per ROC Operations Manual §12.4.

### AF-1 — Pith colour

**Finding:** Cutting into a stem: white or pale-green pith indicates living wood; brown, gray, or black pith indicates dead wood. Because a cane can be dead at the tip while still alive further down, the recommended procedure is to cut from the tip downward in small increments, checking the pith at each cut, until living pith is found. Tool condition affects this check: dull tools can crush the cane and obscure the pith reading; sharp, clean tools are recommended.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Rationale:** Seven independent, institutionally reliable, geographically and editorially independent sources corroborate this finding in consistent terms.

**Sources:** Royal Horticultural Society; Oregon State University Extension Service; Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Service; Clemson University Cooperative Extension (HGIC); University of Georgia CAES; Iowa State University Extension and Outreach; American Rose Society. Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 15.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** None material to this finding specifically.

---

### AF-2 — External bark/stem colour

**Finding:** Green stem colour generally indicates living wood; brown, gray, black, or shriveled bark generally indicates dead wood. This indicator carries a specific, sourced false-negative risk: live wood on older canes or bronze-stemmed cultivars can appear brown rather than green. Several sources treat the pith cut (AF-1) as the check that follows an uncertain external read.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Reasonably well corroborated (4–5 independent sources) but carries a documented, sourced false-negative risk that the evidence itself flags rather than resolves.

**Sources:** University of Illinois Extension; Iowa State University Extension and Outreach; American Rose Society; Royal Horticultural Society (indicator); Colorado State University Extension / PlantTalk Colorado (documents the false-negative caveat). Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 1, 3, 4, 10, 15.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** The false-negative risk on older/bronze-cultivar canes; how frequently this occurs in practice is not established by the available evidence.

---

### AF-3 — Dormant buds/eyes

**Finding:** Plump, healthy dormant buds indicate living tissue; a cane with no live buds anywhere along its length is a candidate for full removal.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Three independent, reliable sources agree without contradiction, but this is fewer independent sources than AF-1, and no source addresses bud damage from causes other than cane death (e.g. frost, insects) as a potential confound.

**Sources:** Clemson University Cooperative Extension (HGIC); Iowa State University Extension and Outreach; American Rose Society. Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 7, 10, 15.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Narrower source base than AF-1; reliability where bud damage has a cause other than cane death is not established.

---

### AF-4 — Stem flexibility/brittleness

**Finding:** Living wood bends; dead wood snaps cleanly and feels brittle.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Rationale:** This indicator rests on a single source that is not rose-specific. It should be treated as plausible and physiologically consistent with the other findings, but not as independently confirmed for roses by this research.

**Sources:** LSU AgCenter (general woody-plant guidance, not rose-specific). Full citation at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Source 11.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Single-sourced; source not rose-specific; whether this test performs as reliably on rose canes specifically is not established.

---

### AF-5 — Ambiguous/lookalike conditions

**Finding:** Red/purple/bronze cane colouring can be entirely normal (cultivar characteristic, seasonal pigmentation, cold exposure) or can indicate cane canker, a fungal disease that produces sunken, discoloured, sometimes target-ringed lesions progressively killing tissue beyond the lesion. This can visually resemble simple dead wood at the point of removal but reflects a spreading disease process rather than static dormant-season death.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Low.

**Rationale:** Only two independent sources address this finding — one general descriptive source and one directly on-point real-world case in which an experienced Extension Master Gardener, working from a submitted photo and description, could not confidently distinguish canker from normal pigmentation and recommended continued physical observation rather than a diagnosis. Qualitatively strong (it demonstrates rather than merely asserts the difficulty) but narrow in source count, and only one lookalike condition (canker) was investigated.

**Sources:** University of Maryland Extension; Ask Extension / Colorado Master Gardener Program, Denver County. Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 12, 13.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Narrow source base; other lookalike conditions beyond canker were not investigated within this commission's scope.

---

### AF-6 — Sporotrichosis/thorn-puncture risk

**Finding:** Rose gardeners are a recognised risk group for sporotrichosis, a fungal skin infection entering through thorn punctures. Gloves and long sleeves when handling rose bushes are recommended as mitigation.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Single-sourced, which on source count alone would parallel AF-4's Low assessment. Assessed higher because the single source is a government public-health authority speaking with direct, specific relevance to the exact claim, and because Source Reliability and Relevance are separate Evidence Assessment Factors from Independence — no single factor is decisive alone (EAS §4.8). This is a documented reasoned-judgement call within the range EAS §2.7 anticipates.

**Sources:** New York State Department of Health. Full citation at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Source 14.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Single-sourced; no independent corroboration found despite search effort; frequency of this risk in practice is not quantified by the source.

---

### AF-7 — Cut confirmed dead wood back to living tissue

**Finding:** Once a section of a cane is confirmed dead (per AF-1 through AF-5), it should be removed by cutting back into living tissue — white or pale-green pith, green bark — not merely at the point where dead tissue first becomes visible.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** High.

**Rationale:** Eight independent sources — several of the same institutions that support AF-1 — directly instruct cutting back to healthy tissue rather than stopping at the first sign of dead wood, in consistent, independently-worded terms. This claim was not extracted as its own finding when FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 was originally researched, because the commission's research plan was scoped to diagnosis (telling dead from living) rather than the resulting action; the source material supporting it was already present in the same source log, gathered incidentally while researching AF-1. No new sources were fetched for this addition.

**Sources:** Royal Horticultural Society ("Prune dieback to healthy white pith"); Colorado State University Extension / PlantTalk Colorado ("Prune the cane back one-half-inch into green live wood"); University of Illinois Extension ("Entirely remove all dead or dying canes"); Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Service ("prune the cane back farther, at least one inch below the dead area"); Clemson University Cooperative Extension HGIC; University of Georgia CAES ("continue to cut down the cane until green or white pith is revealed"); University of Missouri Extension ("cutting at least an inch below the dead area"); American Rose Society ("prune cut to good healthy tissue... white pith core revealed after the cut is made"). Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 15.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** None material to the core claim. Exact depth guidance varies slightly across sources (a specific measurement in some, "until healthy tissue" in others) but does not conflict.

---

### AF-8 — Escalating removal where dieback persists

**Finding:** Where a cane continues to show dead or brown pith despite repeated incremental cuts, removal should continue further — potentially to the bud union or the entire cane — rather than stopping at an arbitrary depth.

**Evidence Confidence Level:** Moderate.

**Rationale:** Two sources address this specific escalation directly (UGA CAES, which notes the pith can darken with each successive cut, requiring removal at the bud union; and Clemson HGIC, which recommends removing the entire cane where no live buds remain), with partial procedural support from a third (PlantTalk Colorado's "clip from the top down, checking... with each small portion removed"). This is fewer independent sources than AF-7, and is treated as a separate, weaker finding rather than folded into AF-7, consistent with EAS §3.2.

**Sources:** University of Georgia CAES; Clemson University Cooperative Extension HGIC; partial support from Colorado State University Extension / PlantTalk Colorado. Full citations at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §4, Sources 7, 8, 3.

**Limitations/uncertainty:** Narrow source base; no source states how often this escalation is actually needed in practice.

## 5. Supporting Procedural Content (Not Independently Confidence-Rated)

The following procedural details are drawn from the same source sets as the Assessed Findings above and support their practical application. They are preserved here for completeness but are not themselves separate Assessed Findings, per FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §7.1 and §7.7:

- **Incremental tip-down checking.** Because a cane can be dead at the tip while alive further down, cut from the tip downward in small increments, checking the pith (AF-1) at each cut, until living pith is found. Several sources additionally recommend cutting roughly 1/2 inch to 1 inch past any visible browning.
- **Tool condition and hygiene.** Dull tools crush the cane and can obscure the pith reading (supports AF-1). Sharp, clean tools are recommended generally. Disinfecting shears (e.g. a 70% alcohol dip) between cuts is recommended to avoid spreading disease, relevant to both AF-1 (clean diagnostic cut) and AF-5 (not spreading canker while checking or removing suspect wood).

## 6. Founder Observation — Photograph vs. Physical-Check Boundary

**Asset type:** Founder observation, per the Mother Information Library Standard's Information Assets list. This is **not** an Assessed Finding under the Evidence Assessment Standard — it was not produced by ROC research and does not carry an Evidence Confidence Level. Its provenance is Founder Authority directly, distinct from AF-1 through AF-6 above.

**Approved by:** Shaphan, AskPIP Founder Authority, 22 August 2026.

**Approved text:**

> A photograph is a two-dimensional external image. It cannot show a stem's internal pith colour, which requires cutting into the stem (AF-1), or whether a stem bends or snaps, which requires physically manipulating it (AF-4) — the two most decisive tests this ARC identifies. A photograph can reasonably show external colour (AF-2), visible dormant buds (AF-3), and lesion patterns that may indicate disease (AF-5). Where a photo-based read is uncertain, physical examination remains necessary before a dead/living determination is treated as confirmed — demonstrated concretely by AF-5, where even an experienced Master Gardener couldn't resolve a real canker-vs-pigmentation case from a photo alone.

**Basis for approval:** This text originates from ROC's reasoning at FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §7.5, previously excluded from this ARC (see §2). It was approved directly rather than referred to a new ROC research commission because its content is a logical consequence of what AF-1 and AF-4 already establish (both name a physical action — cutting, bending — that a photograph structurally cannot perform), not a new empirical claim requiring further source corroboration.

**Use:** This is the approved content for the Observation PKR's "What the Photo Cannot Establish" field (PKR Standard §5.1), resolving the gap identified in the KIT dry run of 22 August 2026.

The original ROC interpretation remains fully preserved and traceable in the archived FRD (`Knowledge Curation System/Mother Information Library/Founder Review Dossiers/FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01.md`, §7.5, §8.7) for provenance, distinct from its adoption here as approved Founder-authority content.

## 7. Recommendations Carried Forward for Founder Consideration

Reproduced from FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01 §9–§10, unresolved and unaltered by this ARC's creation:

- Seek at least one additional, ideally rose-specific, source for AF-4 (stem flexibility) before it is presented to gardeners with the same confidence as AF-1.
