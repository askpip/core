# Part I — Foundations

# Chapter 1 — Purpose of this Standard

## 1.1 Purpose

This Standard establishes what a Founder Review Dossier (FRD) is, what it must contain, how it shall be identified and structured, and how it shall be prepared, reviewed and submitted within the PIP Knowledge Curation System (KCS).

Its purpose is to ensure that every research commission reaches the Founders in a complete, accurate and consistently structured form that supports an informed approval decision, without requiring the Founders to reconstruct the research record themselves.

## 1.2 Scope

This Standard applies whenever a Founder Review Dossier is prepared within the Knowledge Curation System.

It defines:

- what the Founder Review Dossier is and is not;
- the required structure and content of a dossier;
- how a dossier shall be identified and remain traceable to its research commission;
- the preparation, quality review and submission procedure; and
- how a dossier is revised or superseded following a Founder decision.

This Standard does not govern:

- how evidence is collected or evaluated — governed by the Research Origin Curator Operations Manual and the Evidence Assessment Standard;
- how a research commission is opened or scoped — governed by the Research Origin Curator Operations Manual;
- how the Founders reach or record their decision — governed by the Research Origin Curator Operations Manual, Chapter 11 (Founder Review);
- what happens to approved research once it becomes an Approved Research Compilation (ARC) — governed by the Mother Information Library Standard.

This Standard governs the dossier itself: the document the Founders read, not the research that produced it.

## 1.3 Intended Outcome

Application of this Standard shall produce Founder Review Dossiers that are:

- complete — covering every element a Founder needs to decide, without requiring separate documents to be consulted for anything material;
- accurate — presenting the completed research and Evidence Assessment without alteration, omission or reinterpretation;
- traceable — identifiable, and clearly linked back to the research commission and supporting records that produced them;
- proportionate — no longer or more elaborate than the commission warrants; and
- consistent — structured the same way regardless of subject, so a Founder's reviewing experience does not depend on which commission they are reading.

# Chapter 2 — What the Founder Review Dossier Is

## 2.1 Definition

The Founder Review Dossier (FRD) is the complete supporting research record prepared for Founder review at the conclusion of a research commission.

It is the sole Founder Review Documentation. It is not summarised by, or paired with, any other document prepared for the same purpose. The Founder Executive Brief, formerly a companion summary document, is retired under SINS-001 v0.5; see §2.4.

## 2.2 What the Dossier Is Not

The dossier does not:

- create, approve or amend knowledge — that authority remains exclusively with the Founders, per the ROC Operations Manual §10.4;
- introduce evidence, analysis or conclusions that were not already reached during the completed research;
- alter, reinterpret or recalculate the completed Evidence Assessment;
- replace the permanent commission records it draws from — the Research Commission Record, research notes, collected evidence and the Evidence Assessment continue to exist independently and remain the underlying source of truth;
- contain anything that is not research about the commissioned topic — see §5.5 for what this excludes and why; or
- treat a comment, expectation or working assumption offered in conversation as if it were evidence or an Assessed Finding. Where such a comment shaped a commission's scope, that belongs in the Research Commission Record (§7, Assumptions Recorded), not in the dossier presented for Founder review, and it is never elevated into a finding's rationale.

## 2.3 Naming

The document name **Founder Review Dossier**, and its acronym **FRD**, are established by the PIP System Identity and Naming Standard (SINS-001) §8.1 and apply consistently across titles, content, metadata, filenames and related references, per SINS-001 §7.

Once a commission is approved, the FRD is archived as a supporting record. The resulting governed record is created and held in the Mother Information Library as an Approved Research Compilation (ARC); it is not renamed from "Dossier" to anything else.

## 2.4 Relationship to the Retired Founder Executive Brief

Before SINS-001 v0.5, the Founder Review Documentation comprised two documents: the FRD and a companion Founder Executive Brief (FEB), intended to give the Founders a shorter summary for routine review at volume.

The FEB is retired. With one Founder reviewing every commission directly and carefully, a second, shorter document duplicated effort rather than saving it. The FRD alone is now the Founder Review Documentation, and this Standard's structure requirements (Chapter 4) are written so that a well-prepared FRD is itself navigable without a separate summary — clear section headings, a stated Evidence Confidence up front, and findings ordered from most to least significant.

Should review volume grow enough that a summary document becomes genuinely useful, that is a future Founder decision, made and documented the same way any retired term may be reinstated — not a default this Standard assumes.

## 2.5 The Dossier Is Only the Research

The Founder Review Dossier exists so a Founder can decide on the researched topic. Every sentence in it shall serve that decision directly: stating the commissioned question, the evidence, the assessment, or the decision the Founder is asked to make. Nothing else belongs, regardless of how it came about or how interesting it may be to record. §5.5 sets out what this excludes in practice.

## 2.6 Application to the Decision Brief and Review Form

The Decision Brief and Review Form prepared alongside a Founder Review Dossier are not governed in structure by this Standard, which addresses the FRD itself. §2.2, §2.5 and §5.5's prohibition on process narrative, reviewer-directed commentary, and non-research content applies equally to them, per Founder direction: a Founder or reviewer reading any of the three documents shall find only the commissioned topic's research and the decision it calls for, never commentary on the documents' own history.

# Part II — Structure and Content

# Chapter 3 — Identification and Traceability

## 3.1 Purpose

Every Founder Review Dossier shall be identifiable on its own and traceable back to the research commission that produced it, without depending on where the file happens to be stored.

## 3.2 Dossier Identifier

Every Founder Review Dossier shall carry a Dossier Identifier of the form:

**FRD-\<subject-scope\>-\<sequence\>**

Where:

- **\<subject-scope\>** is a short, stable label for the commission's subject (for example, `BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD` for a dead-versus-living-wood commission on bush roses);
- **\<sequence\>** is a two-digit number, starting at `01`, identifying this dossier's position among all FRDs sharing the same subject-scope label (for example, a revised dossier following a Founder request for further research becomes `-02`, not a new label).

Example: `FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`.

The Dossier Identifier shall appear in the dossier's Document Metadata and shall be referenced by the Research Commission Record it belongs to.

## 3.3 Traceability

The Founder Review Dossier shall remain fully traceable to the records created during the commission, including, where applicable:

- the Research Commission Record (RCR);
- the research plan;
- research notes;
- collected evidence;
- the Evidence Assessment;
- supporting source material; and
- any related commissions.

Where a dossier is revised or superseded, §9 (Revision and Superseded Versions) governs how that history is preserved.

# Chapter 4 — Required Structure

## 4.1 Purpose

Every Founder Review Dossier shall present the completed research in the sequence set out in this chapter, so that a Founder reads a commission's question, evidence, confidence and recommendations in a consistent, logical order regardless of subject matter.

## 4.2 Required Sections

A Founder Review Dossier shall contain the following sections, in this order:

1. **Document Metadata** — Dossier Identifier (§3.2), title, commission reference, status (Draft or Submitted), preparer, date, and a summary list of each Assessed Finding's Evidence Confidence Level from §8, stated up front, before any other content. Where the commission produced more than one Assessed Finding, every finding's level is listed; none is omitted in favour of an average or a single headline figure.
2. **Research Commission Record (Summary)** — the commissioned question and its scope, drawn from the Research Commission Record without alteration.
3. **Research Plan** — the approach taken to answer the commissioned question, per the ROC Operations Manual, Chapter 5.
4. **Evidence Collected — Source Log** — every source relied upon, sufficient for a Founder to identify and, where necessary, verify its origin, per the ROC Operations Manual, Chapter 6.
5. **Evidence Evaluation** — how the collected evidence was evaluated against the Evidence Assessment Factors, per the ROC Operations Manual, Chapter 6, and the Evidence Assessment Standard, Chapter 4.
6. **Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty** — any disagreement between sources and how it was resolved or preserved as unresolved, per the ROC Operations Manual, Chapter 7.
7. **Principal Findings** — the synthesised findings of the research, presented as one or more Assessed Findings (per the Evidence Assessment Standard §3.2), distinguishing established findings, supported interpretations, expert opinion and unresolved questions, per §5 of this Standard.
8. **Evidence Assessment** — the completed Evidence Assessment, incorporated without alteration, per the Evidence Assessment Standard, stating the Evidence Confidence Level assigned to each Assessed Finding individually.
9. **Recommendations for Further Research** — any further research the ROC identifies as warranted, whether or not the Founders act on it.
10. **Founder Decision Points** — the specific decisions the dossier asks the Founders to make, stated as direct questions (for example: approve as a future ARC; approve with amendment; defer pending further research; decline).

Where a section has no content for a given commission (for example, no conflicting evidence was encountered), the dossier shall state this explicitly rather than omit the section.

## 4.3 Proportionality

The level of detail in each section shall remain proportionate to the complexity of the commission. A narrow, well-established commission need not produce a long dossier; a broad or contested one may require considerably more.

Proportionality shall never be used to omit a limitation, an unresolved uncertainty, or a source that materially affects the Evidence Confidence Level.

# Chapter 5 — Presenting Findings

## 5.1 Accuracy and Proportion

Findings shall be presented accurately, objectively and proportionately. The significance attributed to each finding shall remain proportionate to the available evidence.

## 5.2 Distinguishing Kinds of Finding

The ROC shall distinguish between:

- established findings;
- supported interpretations;
- expert opinions;
- unresolved questions; and
- matters requiring further investigation.

## 5.3 No New Analysis After Completion

The Founder Review Dossier shall not introduce new evidence, additional analysis or revised conclusions after completion of the research. Where additional analysis is required, the ROC shall revise the research and the Evidence Assessment before updating the dossier, per the ROC Operations Manual §9.7.

## 5.4 Referencing Supporting Evidence

The dossier shall provide sufficient references to enable the Founders to verify the origin of significant findings where necessary. Detailed research records shall remain within the permanent commission documentation rather than being reproduced in full within the dossier.

## 5.5 No Process Narrative or Non-Research Content

A Founder Review Dossier contains the research on its commissioned topic and nothing else. The following do not belong in a dossier, whatever section they might otherwise be placed under:

- narration of how many revisions the dossier has been through, or why — beyond the brief, findings-only statement §9.3 requires when a dossier is revised;
- commentary directed at a specific reviewer — whether they have completed their review, what they previously answered, or whether their earlier answer still stands;
- explanation or justification of the dossier's own structure, length, or the number of Founder Decision Points it presents (for example, an account of why a commission that once seemed to require many separate decisions now presents only one);
- the ROC's own reasoning about why one subject was scoped, split from, or connected to another, where that reasoning did not come from a source — this belongs in the Research Commission Record's scope section, stated once, not repeated or re-justified in the dossier; and
- a working comment, expectation or assumption raised in conversation, presented with supporting paragraphs as though it were a research finding. A comment is not evidence merely because it prompted the commission (§2.2).

**Illustration.** A commission on crossing and rubbing stems, scoped separately from inward-growing stems following an offhand comment that the two sometimes occur together, states that comment once, in the Research Commission Record, as the reason the commission exists (§7 of that record, Assumptions Recorded). The dossier itself contains only what recognised sources say about crossing and rubbing stems. It does not explain, justify, or revisit why the two subjects were separated — that is process history, not research, and §2.2 and this section exclude it regardless of how directly it might seem to help a Founder follow along.

Applying this section is not a licence to omit a genuine limitation, an unresolved conflict, or a source that affects an Evidence Confidence Level (§4.3) — those remain required. The distinction is between the research itself, however imperfect or unresolved, and commentary about the dossier's own production.

# Chapter 6 — Presenting the Evidence Assessment

## 6.1 No Alteration

The completed Evidence Assessment shall be incorporated into the dossier without alteration. The ROC may provide explanatory commentary to assist interpretation but shall not modify the completed assessment or present it in a manner that changes its intended meaning, per the ROC Operations Manual §9.6–§9.7.

## 6.2 One Level Per Assessed Finding, Never One Level Per Commission

The dossier does not report one overall Evidence Confidence Level for the commission. Evidence Confidence is assigned to the Assessed Finding (Evidence Assessment Standard §3.2) — a single, discrete claim of fact — and a commission commonly produces more than one.

Every Assessed Finding's own Evidence Confidence Level shall be stated, both in the Document Metadata summary list (§4.2, item 1) and again alongside that finding within the Evidence Assessment section, so a Founder can see at a glance how many findings the commission produced and exactly how confident each one is — never a single blended figure standing in for work of differing evidential strength.

This is required, not merely permitted: a dossier that reports one figure for a commission containing findings of differing strength misrepresents both the well-supported and the poorly-supported finding, consistent with the PIP Knowledge Record Standard §4's prohibition on averaging or inflating Evidence Confidence.

# Part III — Preparation, Review and Submission

# Chapter 7 — Preparation

## 7.1 When Preparation May Begin

Preparation of the Founder Review Dossier shall commence only after:

- research has been completed;
- evidence has been evaluated;
- conflicting evidence and uncertainty have been documented;
- research findings have been synthesised;
- the Evidence Assessment has been completed; and
- all supporting research records have been finalised,

per the ROC Operations Manual §10.2.

## 7.2 Objectivity

The Founder Review Dossier is advisory. It does not approve knowledge, authorise publication or amend the Mother Information Library. Authority for those decisions remains exclusively with the Founders.

The ROC shall present the evidence objectively and shall not attempt to influence the Founders toward a predetermined decision, per the ROC Operations Manual §10.4.

---

# Chapter 8 — Quality Review

## 8.1 Review Before Submission

Before submission, the ROC shall review the Founder Review Dossier to confirm that:

- all commissioned questions have been addressed;
- the findings accurately represent the completed research;
- supporting evidence has been referenced appropriately;
- limitations and uncertainty have been preserved;
- the completed Evidence Assessment has been incorporated accurately;
- every required section (§4.2) is present, including sections stated as not applicable;
- the Dossier Identifier (§3.2) is correctly assigned and referenced by the Research Commission Record;
- the document is internally consistent; and
- all required supporting records are complete.

Any deficiencies identified during the quality review shall be corrected before submission.

---

# Chapter 9 — Submission and Revision

## 9.1 Submission to the Founders

Following successful completion of the quality review, the ROC shall submit the commission to the Founders. The submission shall include:

- the Founder Review Dossier; and
- access to all supporting documentation required by the Knowledge Curation System.

The Founder Review Dossier shall serve as the primary and complete document for Founder review. Following submission, responsibility for the commission passes to the Founder Review stage, per the ROC Operations Manual, Chapter 11.

The ROC shall remain available to provide clarification where requested but shall not amend the findings unless directed by the Founders.

## 9.2 Revision and Superseded Versions

Where the Founders decline to approve a dossier as submitted and instead request further research, amendment or clarification, the ROC shall not silently edit the submitted dossier.

The ROC shall instead:

- carry out the directed further work;
- prepare a revised dossier under the next sequence number for the same subject-scope label (§3.2) — for example, `FRD-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-02` following `-01`;
- state plainly, near the top of the revised dossier, what changed since the prior version, per §9.3; and
- preserve the prior version and the Founders' decision on it as part of the permanent commission record rather than overwriting or deleting it.

This mirrors the terminology-retirement pattern used elsewhere in PIP CORE: prior versions are superseded, not erased, so the record of what the Founders actually reviewed and decided remains intact.

## 9.3 Scope of the Revision Note

The note required by §9.2 identifies, in no more than a few sentences, which findings or sections changed and the one-line evidential reason (new source found, source re-checked, finding strengthened or narrowed). It is not the place for §5.5's excluded content: it does not narrate the review process, address a specific reviewer, or justify the revision's structure. Where a full account of what was researched and found in the revision round is useful to preserve, it belongs in the commission's research notes, not the dossier.

# Chapter 10 — Compliance

## 10.1 Compliance

All Founder Review Dossiers prepared within the PIP Knowledge Curation System shall comply with this Standard. No alternative structure shall be used unless approved by the Founders.

## 10.2 Responsibility

The Research Origin Curator, or other personnel authorised by the Founders, is responsible for preparing Founder Review Dossiers in accordance with this Standard. The Founders retain sole responsibility for reviewing and deciding upon submitted dossiers.

## 10.3 Document Review

This Standard shall be reviewed periodically by the Founders to ensure it continues to support the Knowledge Curation System. Revisions shall be managed in accordance with the PIP Documentation Standard.
