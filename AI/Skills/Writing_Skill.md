# 1. Purpose

Use this Skill to produce clear, natural, professional and purpose-focused writing for PIP.

It governs the expression of established content across chat responses, documents, prompts, correspondence, research summaries, public copy and other written outputs.

# 2. Scope

Apply this Skill whenever artificial intelligence (AI) creates, rewrites, expands, condenses or refines written content for PIP.

Where another approved PIP AI OS document establishes additional requirements for a particular task or output, apply both.

This Skill governs written expression. It does not determine subject matter, authority, document structure, repository procedure or specialist content requirements.

# 3. Instruction, Meaning and Voice

Follow the Founder’s current instruction and preserve the intended meaning, audience, voice, level of formality and required format.

Where wording must be reproduced exactly, preserve it exactly unless alteration is separately authorised.

Improve expression without introducing a new decision, commitment, claim or interpretation.

Do not remove a necessary qualification, distinction or emphasis merely to make writing shorter or smoother.

# 4. Required Writing Characteristics

Completed writing shall:

- communicate the intended meaning accurately;
- serve its stated purpose and intended audience;
- present information clearly and logically;
- use approved terminology consistently;
- remain concise without losing necessary meaning;
- use a natural, professional and context-appropriate voice;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, recommendation, proposal and uncertainty where applicable; and
- remain internally consistent.

Professional writing need not be formal. Use the degree of warmth, personality, directness and technical detail appropriate to the audience and purpose.

# 5. Purpose-Focused Content

Include only content required to fulfil the requested purpose.

Use surrounding conversation when it supplies necessary content, decisions, constraints, tone or context for the intended output. Do not transfer incidental discussion into the writing.

Do not answer objections, questions or discussion points that the requested writing does not need to address.

Keep background, qualifications and boundaries proportionate to the purpose of the writing.

# 6. Positive and Direct Expression

Describe the subject primarily through what it is, what it does and what it provides.

Use negative framing when an exclusion, prohibition, limitation, correction or distinction is necessary to the meaning or function of the writing.

Prefer direct statements over defensive, corrective or anticipatory explanation.

Do not define a subject through unnecessary statements about what it is not.

# 7. Repetition, Emphasis and Economy

Express each distinct idea once unless repetition serves a clear functional purpose.

Retain repetition when it materially improves instruction-following, navigation, safety, recall, persuasion or deliberate rhetorical effect. Otherwise, remove restatements that add emphasis without adding meaning.

Remove filler, decorative wording and explanatory padding that do not improve understanding.

Do not make writing longer merely to make it sound formal, complete or persuasive.

# 8. Professional Boundaries

Completed writing shall avoid:

- conversational material that does not belong in the output;
- unnecessary negative framing;
- defensive or apologetic explanation unrelated to the purpose;
- imagined objections or responses to questions outside scope;
- unsupported promotional, persuasive or exaggerated language;
- speculative elaboration presented as established content;
- repeated statements that serve no functional purpose;
- inconsistent terminology for the same concept;
- generic phrasing that erases an intended voice; and
- content introduced only to make the output appear comprehensive.

These boundaries shall not remove relevant context, useful explanation, appropriate warmth, deliberate voice or necessary caution.

# 9. Clarity and Readability

Use the clearest wording that preserves the required meaning and terminology.

Prefer specific words, direct sentence structures and coherent progression.

Use headings, lists, tables or other structure only when they materially improve comprehension or use.

Avoid jargon where plain language is accurate. Retain technical or governed terminology where replacing it would reduce precision.

Vary sentence length and rhythm where appropriate so prose reads naturally rather than mechanically.

# 10. Apply the Appropriate Form

Match the writing to its intended use.

A controlled document shall be precise, internally consistent and suitable for its governed purpose.

A prompt shall give the receiving system clear, complete and usable instructions.

A chat response shall answer the request directly without unnecessary document-style formality.

Public copy shall be clear, audience-appropriate and consistent with the intended brand voice without unsupported claims or promotional inflation.

Correspondence shall suit its recipient, purpose and level of formality.

A research summary shall preserve evidence, qualifications and uncertainty while making the material efficient to understand.

# 11. Completion

Before presenting or saving written content, ensure that it:

- fulfils the requested purpose;
- follows the required meaning, audience, voice and format;
- contains no unnecessary repetition or incidental conversational material;
- uses positive and direct expression where suitable;
- remains within scope;
- preserves necessary context, distinctions, qualifications and emphasis;
- uses approved terminology consistently;
- reads naturally at the appropriate level of formality; and
- is ready for its intended use.
