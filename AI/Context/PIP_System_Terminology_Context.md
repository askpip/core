# 1. Purpose and Authority

This Context is a compact operational reference for Plant Intelligence Platform (PIP) terminology established by the PIP System Identity and Naming Standard (SINS-001).

SINS-001 remains the authoritative naming Standard. This Context does not replace SINS-001. Where this Context and SINS-001 differ, SINS-001 governs.

# 2. Platform and Public Identities

- **Plant Intelligence Platform (PIP):** the complete parent platform, comprising the systems, technology, knowledge, governance, roles and public interfaces that deliver trusted plant intelligence. PIP is broader than the public application or technical software engine.
- **Ask Pip:** the official public-facing brand and product name. The official displayed spelling is **Ask Pip**. The domain form `askpip.garden` does not replace that spelling.
- **Pip:** the official character, companion, narrator, educator and public face of Ask Pip. Pip has no defined gender. Use **Pip** in normal prose; **PIP** may be used in approved visual branding that intentionally connects Pip with the platform.

# 3. Internal Systems

- **PIP CORE:** the authoritative operational centre and repository of PIP. CORE is a proper system name and is not currently an acronym.
- **Knowledge Curation System (KCS):** the governed system through which knowledge is researched, assessed, reviewed, approved, preserved, integrated and published for operational use. It operates within PIP and may be adapted or used as a standalone knowledge-curation system.
- **KCS Workspace:** the planned digital working environment through which authorised participants may interact with the KCS.
- **PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System (PIP AI OS):** internal platform infrastructure governing artificial intelligence operating anywhere within PIP. It is not part of the public Ask Pip brand.

# 4. KCS Roles

- **Research Origin Curator (ROC):** researches, assesses and organises trustworthy information; prepares Founder Review Documentation; and creates and maintains approved Knowledge Assets in the Mother Information Library (MIL). ROC does not approve knowledge.
- **Knowledge Integration Technician (KIT):** converts Founder-approved knowledge into structured operational intelligence; prepares PIP Knowledge Records (PKRs); and maintains the Live Intelligence Library (LIL). KIT does not create or approve knowledge.

# 5. Knowledge Components

- **Mother Information Library (MIL):** the authoritative home of Founder-approved knowledge, supporting evidence, traceability, metadata and history. ROC maintains the MIL.
- **Live Intelligence Library (LIL):** contains Founder-approved structured operational intelligence prepared for rapid retrieval and use by PIP. KIT maintains the LIL.
- **Knowledge Asset (KA):** a governed body of Founder-approved knowledge concerning a defined plant, subject or knowledge scope. ROC creates and maintains KAs; the Founders approve them; and they are held within the MIL.
- **PIP Knowledge Record (PKR):** a structured operational knowledge unit created and maintained by KIT from Founder-approved knowledge held within the MIL. Draft PKRs require Founder approval before publication to the LIL.

# 6. Research and Review Documents

- **Founder Review Dossier (FRD):** the complete supporting research record prepared for Founder review.
- **Founder Executive Brief (FEB):** the concise summary prepared to support efficient Founder review and decision-making.
- **Evidence Assessment (EA):** records evidence confidence, rationale, limitations and remaining uncertainty.
- **Evidence Assessment Standard (EAS):** governs how EAs are completed and recorded.

# 7. Naming and Acronym Rules

Use approved names consistently across titles, content, metadata, filenames, diagrams, interfaces and related references.

Do not repeat a system or brand prefix where location, metadata or established context already identifies the parent system. Use a prefix where it materially distinguishes the subject. Do not invent an expansion for CORE.

In every standalone document, precede the first substantive use of an acronym with its full term and place the acronym immediately afterward in parentheses. A title or metadata entry satisfies this requirement when it contains both the full term and acronym.

The first-use requirement does not apply to an acronym appearing only within a filename, repository path, URL, domain name, code identifier, configuration value or file extension. Define common technical abbreviations where the intended reader may not reasonably understand them.

Do not create an acronym where the shortened form is unlikely to be reused or would reduce clarity.
