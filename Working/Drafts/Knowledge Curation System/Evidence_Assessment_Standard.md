# Part I — Foundations

# Chapter 1 — Purpose of this Standard

## 1.1 Purpose

This standard establishes the methodology governing how the available evidence shall be evaluated within the PIP Knowledge Curation System.

Its purpose is to ensure that every Evidence Assessment is performed using a consistent, transparent and repeatable methodology that accurately represents the strength, limitations and reliability of the available evidence.

This standard enables the Founders to understand not only the conclusions reached during research, but also the degree of confidence that may reasonably be placed in those conclusions.

---

## 1.2 Scope

This standard applies whenever an Evidence Assessment is prepared within the Knowledge Curation System.

It defines:

- the principles governing Evidence Assessment;
- the factors that shall be considered during evaluation;
- the methodology for determining Evidence Confidence Levels;
- the documentation required to support an Evidence Assessment; and
- the circumstances requiring reassessment.

This standard does not govern:

- research planning;
- evidence collection;
- research synthesis;
- Founder Review; or
- Knowledge Asset management.

These activities are governed by their respective controlled documents.

---

## 1.3 Intended Outcome

Application of this standard shall produce Evidence Assessments that are:

- objective;
- transparent;
- repeatable;
- proportionate to the available evidence;
- fully traceable; and
- clearly understandable by the Founders.

Evidence Assessments shall accurately communicate both the strengths and limitations of the available evidence without overstating or understating the confidence that may reasonably be placed in the research findings.

---

# Chapter 2 — Principles of Evidence Assessment

## 2.1 Purpose

The principles contained within this chapter establish the foundation upon which every Evidence Assessment shall be performed.

These principles ensure that assessments remain consistent regardless of the subject under investigation or whether the assessment is undertaken by an AI system or a human researcher.

---

## 2.2 Evidence Before Opinion

Evidence Confidence shall be determined from the available evidence rather than from personal opinion, assumption, expectation or preference.

Assessments shall reflect what the available evidence supports rather than what the assessor believes to be true.

---

## 2.3 Proportional Confidence

Evidence Confidence shall be proportional to the quality, consistency and applicability of the available evidence.

Greater confidence shall only be assigned where the available evidence reasonably supports it.

Where significant limitations exist, the assigned Evidence Confidence Level shall reflect those limitations.

---

## 2.4 Transparency

Every Evidence Assessment shall provide sufficient explanation to enable the Founders to understand how the assigned Evidence Confidence Level was determined.

Significant strengths, limitations and unresolved uncertainty shall be documented openly.

---

## 2.5 Preservation of Uncertainty

The purpose of an Evidence Assessment is not to eliminate uncertainty but to communicate it accurately.

Where uncertainty remains, it shall be reflected within the assigned Evidence Confidence Level and explained within the supporting rationale.

Evidence Confidence shall never exceed what the available evidence reasonably supports.

---

## 2.6 Consistency

Comparable bodies of evidence shall receive comparable Evidence Confidence Levels when evaluated against this standard.

The methodology shall be applied consistently across all Research Commissions.

---

## 2.7 Repeatability

A competent assessor applying this standard to the same body of available evidence should be capable of reaching substantially the same Evidence Confidence Level and supporting rationale.

Minor differences in **reasoned judgement** may occur; however, the assessment process shall remain consistent, transparent and supported by documented rationale.

---

## 2.8 Traceability

Every Evidence Assessment shall remain fully traceable to:

- the Research Commission;
- the supporting evidence;
- the research findings;
- documented limitations; and
- the rationale supporting the assigned Evidence Confidence Level.

No Evidence Assessment shall exist independently of the research upon which it is based.

**Part II — Evidence Assessment Methodology**

**Chapter 3 — Conducting an Evidence Assessment**

## 3.1 Purpose

An Evidence Assessment shall evaluate the available evidence using the methodology defined within this standard.

The purpose of the assessment is to determine the appropriate Evidence Confidence Level and document the rationale supporting that conclusion.

---

## 3.2 Assessment Process

Every Evidence Assessment shall follow the following process:

1. Review the Research Commission.
2. Review the available evidence.
3. Evaluate each Evidence Assessment Factor.
4. Determine the appropriate Evidence Confidence Level.
5. Record the rationale supporting the assessment.

---

## 3.3 Assessment Requirements

Evidence Assessments shall:

- evaluate the complete body of available evidence;
- consider all Evidence Assessment Factors;
- identify significant limitations and uncertainty;
- assign a single Evidence Confidence Level; and
- record sufficient rationale to support the assigned confidence.    

**Chapter 4 — Evidence Assessment Factors**

## 4.1 Purpose

Evidence Assessments shall evaluate the available evidence against the Evidence Assessment Factors defined within this chapter.

Each factor shall be considered before assigning an Evidence Confidence Level.

---

## 4.2 Relevance

The assessor shall determine whether the available evidence is directly relevant to the Research Commission.

Evidence that does not materially address the research question shall not significantly influence the assessment.

---

## 4.3 Source Reliability

The assessor shall evaluate the reliability of the sources from which the evidence has been obtained.

Consideration shall include:

- recognised expertise;
- demonstrated experience;
- organisational credibility;
- supporting references; and
- transparency of the information presented.

---

## 4.4 Consistency

The assessor shall determine the degree of agreement between independent sources.

Broad agreement between multiple independent sources generally increases Evidence Confidence.

Significant disagreement shall be documented within the assessment.

---

## 4.5 Independence

The assessor shall consider whether the available evidence represents genuinely independent sources or repeated publication of substantially the same information.

Multiple sources repeating the same original material shall not be treated as independent confirmation.

---

## 4.6 Limitations

The assessor shall identify any limitations affecting the available evidence.

Limitations may include incomplete information, restricted applicability, conflicting findings or insufficient supporting evidence.

Significant limitations shall be recorded within the Evidence Assessment.

---

## 4.7 Uncertainty

The assessor shall identify any unresolved uncertainty that may materially affect the research findings.

Where uncertainty remains, it shall be reflected in the assigned Evidence Confidence Level.

---

## 4.8 Overall Evaluation

No single Evidence Assessment Factor shall determine the assigned Evidence Confidence Level.

The Evidence Confidence Level shall represent the overall evaluation of the available evidence after consideration of all relevant factors.            

**Chapter 5 — Evidence Confidence Levels**

## 5.1 Purpose

Evidence Confidence Levels communicate the degree of confidence that may reasonably be placed in the research findings based upon the available evidence.

The assigned Evidence Confidence Level shall reflect the overall evaluation of the available evidence against the Evidence Assessment Factors defined within this standard.

---

## 5.2 Assignment

Every completed Evidence Assessment shall assign a single Evidence Confidence Level where the available evidence has been sufficiently evaluated to support a reasoned conclusion.

Where the available evidence has been sufficiently evaluated within the PIP Knowledge Curation System to support the assignment of a confidence level, the Evidence Confidence shall be recorded as **Not Assigned**, together with the supporting rationale.

---

## 5.3 Evidence Confidence Levels

The following Evidence Confidence Levels may be assigned when supported by the available evidence:

- Very High
- High
- Moderate
- Low
- Very Low

Where an Evidence Confidence Level cannot yet be responsibly assigned, the Evidence Confidence shall be recorded as **Not Assigned**.

No alternative confidence terminology shall be used unless approved by the Founders.

---

## 5.4 Evidence Confidence Level Definitions

### Very High

The available evidence is extensive, reliable and broadly consistent, with minimal unresolved limitations or uncertainty.

The research findings may be relied upon with a very high degree of confidence.

---

### High

The available evidence is reliable and generally consistent.

Minor limitations or uncertainty may exist but do not materially reduce confidence in the research findings.

---

### Moderate

The available evidence reasonably supports the research findings; however, notable limitations, inconsistency or uncertainty remain.

The findings should be applied with appropriate caution.

---

### Low

The available evidence is limited or subject to significant limitations.

The research findings should be applied with considerable caution.

---

### Very Low

The available evidence provides only minimal support for the research findings.

The findings should not be relied upon without substantial additional supporting evidence.

---

### Not Assigned

**Not Assigned** indicates that an Evidence Confidence Level has not been assigned within the PIP Knowledge Curation System.

This status shall be used where the available evidence has not yet been sufficiently evaluated to support the assignment of an Evidence Confidence Level.

The Evidence Assessment shall record the reason why a confidence level has not been assigned and, where appropriate, identify any recommended further research.

---

## 5.5 Reviewing the Assigned Confidence

Before finalising an Evidence Assessment, the assessor shall confirm that:

- the assigned Evidence Confidence accurately reflects the available evidence;
- the assigned confidence is supported by documented rationale;
- identified limitations have been appropriately considered;
- remaining uncertainty has been appropriately considered; and
- the assigned confidence does not exceed what the available evidence reasonably supports.

Where these requirements are not satisfied, the Evidence Assessment shall be reviewed before completion. 

**Chapter 6 — Recording the Evidence Assessment**

## 6.1 Purpose

Every Evidence Assessment shall be recorded in a consistent manner to ensure transparency, traceability and future review.

---

## 6.2 Minimum Record Requirements

Every completed Evidence Assessment shall record:

- Research Commission reference;
- subject of the assessment;
- Evidence Confidence;
- rationale supporting the assigned Evidence Confidence;
- significant limitations;
- remaining uncertainty;
- assessment date; and
- assessor.

---

## 6.3 Supporting Rationale

The supporting rationale shall clearly explain the basis for the assigned Evidence Confidence.

The rationale shall:

- reference the evidence supporting the assessment; 
- identify significant strengths;
- identify significant limitations;
- identify remaining uncertainty; and
- support the assigned Evidence Confidence.

---

## 6.4 Revision

An Evidence Assessment shall be reviewed where:

- significant new evidence becomes available;
- the Research Commission is amended;
- material errors are identified; or
- the Founders request reassessment.

Where revision is required, the Evidence Assessment shall be updated in accordance with this standard.

---

# Chapter 7 — Compliance

## 7.1 Compliance

All Evidence Assessments prepared within the PIP Knowledge Curation System shall comply with this standard.

No alternative methodology shall be used unless approved by the Founders.

---

### 7.2 Responsibility

The Research Origin Curator, or other personnel authorised by the Founders, is responsible for preparing Evidence Assessments in accordance with this standard.

The Founders retain responsibility for reviewing and approving research findings and the associated Evidence Confidence.

---

## 7.3 Document Review

This standard shall be reviewed periodically by the Founders to ensure it continues to support the Knowledge Curation System.

Revisions shall be managed in accordance with the PIP Documentation Standard.
