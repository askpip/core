# Chapter 1 — Sarah's Rose

Marie had never planned to grow roses.

Several years earlier, her sister had arrived with a beautiful bush rose as a gift.

"I thought this would look lovely in your garden."

Together, they had chosen a sunny spot and planted it.

The rose had quickly become more than just another shrub.

It reminded Marie of her sister.

Now Sarah's Rose was established. Each year it grew into a dense collection of branches, leaves and flowers. Marie watered it, admired it and removed the occasional spent bloom, but she had always avoided one task.

Pruning.

She had read that bush roses benefited from pruning, but every explanation seemed to assume she already knew what she was looking at.

Dead wood.

Crossing stems.

Inward growth.

A healthy framework.

The words made sense on a page. They made much less sense when Marie stood in front of the actual rose.

She worried about removing the wrong stem.

She worried about damaging the plant.

Most of all, she did not want uncertainty to turn Sarah's gift into a practice exercise.

A friend suggested she try Ask Pip.

"A gardening companion that helps you understand your plants."

Marie opened the app.

The splash screen appeared.

---

**Ask Pip**

*Growing understanding. Cultivating confidence.*

**Begin**

---

Marie tapped **Begin**.

Ask Pip offered a small number of guided starting points.

Marie chose:

**Help me prune my bush rose**

Pip welcomed her with a simple message.

*"We'll look at your rose together before you decide what to cut."*

*"I can suggest features that may be visible in your photographs and show you trusted examples. You'll check the actual rose and confirm what is there."*

*"You can cut, leave, decide later or get experienced local help at any point."*

Marie paused.

This did not feel like handing control of Sarah's Rose to an app.

It felt like being invited to look more carefully.

# Chapter 2 — Getting to Know Sarah's Rose

Pip began with a few questions.

It asked what Marie called the rose.

She typed:

**Sarah's Rose**

Pip asked whether she knew anything about its type or origin.

Marie remembered that it had arrived with a nursery label, but the label was long gone. She knew it was a bush rose and that Sarah had given it to her several years earlier. She did not know the cultivar.

Pip recorded what Marie knew and where that information had come from. It did not try to identify the cultivar from the rose's appearance.

Then it asked one optional question.

*"Would you like to record why this rose is special to you?"*

Marie wrote:

*"My sister gave me this rose. I'd like to understand it well enough to care for it with confidence."*

Pip asked for Marie's location, the time of year and when the rose had been planted. It asked whether the rose was dormant or actively growing and whether Marie had noticed severe stress, major damage or significant disease.

Next, it asked Marie to check her working conditions.

Her secateurs were clean and sharp.

She had gloves and eye protection.

The ground around the rose was firm and safely accessible.

Pip explained why each question mattered. The answers helped establish whether this rose, in this place, at this time, appeared to fall within the supported pruning journey.

Pip did not describe the checks as a guarantee.

The photographs and Marie's answers could support a decision to proceed, but they could not establish everything about the rose.

Once the initial checks were complete, Pip asked for a clear photograph of the whole plant.

Then another from the side.

Then several close-ups of areas Marie found confusing.

Pip explained the immediate purpose of each image rather than asking her to build a photographic record for its own sake.

One photograph did not clearly show where two stems met.

*"I can't reliably follow these stems to their base from this angle,"* Pip explained.

*"Could you take one closer photograph from the other side?"*

Marie moved around the rose and tried again.

The new photograph made the relationship easier to see, but Pip still described what followed as an interpretation rather than a fact.

Marie appreciated that.

The uncertainty had not magically vanished.

It had simply become smaller and more clearly understood.

# Chapter 3 — Learning to See the Rose

Before discussing a pruning plan, Pip helped Marie examine the rose.

It began with one possible feature in one of her photographs.

*"This section may contain a dead stem, but colour alone can be misleading."*

Beside Marie's photograph, Pip displayed an approved comparison showing dead and living rose wood from a similar viewpoint.

The example highlighted the visible features a beginner could compare. It also showed a common lookalike and explained what a photograph could not prove.

Pip asked Marie to look away from the screen and find the stem on Sarah's Rose.

Marie traced it carefully from the tip towards the base.

She compared its surface and flexibility with nearby growth and with the example Pip had shown.

*"Can you confirm that this is dead wood on the physical rose?"*

Marie looked again.

She was not ready to confirm it.

She selected:

**I can't tell yet**

Pip did not turn its proposal into a conclusion.

It showed another approved comparison and asked one more simple question. When Marie still could not resolve the stem confidently, Pip recorded the observation as unresolved.

Marie chose **Decide later**.

The next possible feature was easier.

Two stems appeared to cross in the photograph. Pip showed a comparison explaining the difference between stems that merely overlap from one camera angle and stems that actually touch or rub.

Marie went back to the rose.

She followed both stems with her gloved hand.

They did touch.

This time she confirmed the observation herself.

They continued slowly.

Pip proposed possible damaged growth, inward-growing stems, weak or congested growth and the main framework that might be worth retaining. Each time, it separated three things clearly:

- what Marie's photograph showed;
- what Pip thought might be present; and
- what the approved comparison demonstrated.

Marie examined the actual rose before confirming, correcting or leaving each observation unresolved.

At one point, Pip suggested that a stem might be growing towards the centre of the rose.

Marie traced it and realised that the camera angle was misleading. The stem curved outwards beyond the edge of the photograph.

She selected **Correct this observation** and described what she could see.

The record changed.

Pip did not defend its earlier interpretation. It used Marie's physical observation as the better evidence.

For the first time, the rose began to make sense.

Instead of seeing a tangled collection of branches, Marie began to see an organised, living structure.

She recognised several main stems that formed the framework.

She could see where one smaller stem rubbed against another.

She understood why a thin cluster in the centre might deserve attention, even though more than one reasonable choice remained.

Pip explained that experienced gardeners did not begin pruning by asking:

*"What should I cut?"*

They began by asking:

*"What am I looking at?"*

Marie smiled.

Only an hour earlier, she had wanted someone to show her where to cut.

Now she found herself studying Sarah's Rose before thinking about pruning.

# Chapter 4 — Deciding Before Cutting

Using Marie's confirmed observations and approved pruning knowledge, Pip assembled a proposed plan.

The first item concerned the two rubbing stems Marie had confirmed on the physical rose.

Pip explained why continued rubbing could damage them. It showed which confirmed observation the plan relied upon and what uncertainty remained.

Marie was offered four choices:

- **Cut**
- **Leave**
- **Decide later**
- **Get experienced local help**

Pip also explained that there could be more than one acceptable way to respond.

Marie looked at the two stems again.

One formed part of the stronger outward framework. The other crossed into it from the centre.

She chose **Cut** for the crossing stem.

Before she picked up her secateurs, Pip asked her to identify the intended stem on the actual rose and trace it from its tip to its point of origin.

Marie did so.

*"Have you confirmed the intended physical stem?"*

Marie checked once more before selecting **Yes**.

Only then did she make the cut.

Pip asked whether the planned action had been completed.

Marie selected **Completed**.

The next item concerned an inward-growing stem.

Marie understood the reason for considering its removal, but she was less certain that cutting it would improve the framework. She chose **Leave**.

Another item concerned the thin, congested growth near the centre. Several options appeared horticulturally acceptable. Marie removed one stem, left another and marked a third **Decide later**.

The dead-looking stem from earlier remained unresolved.

Marie could have asked for experienced local help. Instead, she chose to leave it untouched and observe it again later.

Pip recorded that no cut had been made.

With each decision, Marie remained responsible for what happened to Sarah's Rose.

Pip provided evidence, comparisons, explanations and boundaries.

Marie observed, confirmed and chose.

Some decisions became cuts.

Others became deliberate decisions not to cut.

When Marie could not confidently trace a proposed stem on the physical rose, that item stopped. A photograph alone was not enough.

After the final decision, Pip asked Marie to take another overview photograph and several useful close-ups.

The before-and-after views appeared together.

Pip did not declare that the rose now had a perfect framework or promise healthy spring growth.

Instead, it summarised what had actually happened:

- which observations Marie had confirmed;
- which observations she had corrected or left unresolved;
- which choices she had made;
- which cuts she had confirmed and completed;
- what she had left untouched; and
- what she wanted to observe next.

Marie reviewed the summary.

She corrected the description of one stem she had left in place.

Then she saved it.

She realised that Pip had done something more valuable than telling her where to prune.

It had helped her make decisions she could understand and later revisit.

# Chapter 5 — The Memory of the Rose

Marie expected Pip to ask whether she wanted to create a journal entry.

It did not.

The photographs, interpretations, comparisons, corrections, decisions and completed actions already formed the history of the pruning session.

Marie had not been asked to copy notes into a separate Garden Journal or remember which details might matter later.

Sarah's Rose now had a simple, correctable history created through the care itself.

Marie opened the session summary.

There were the original photographs.

Beside them were Pip's proposed observations, including the uncertainty it had expressed at the time.

There were the exact approved comparisons Marie had used.

There were her confirmations and corrections.

There were the four choices she had considered and the reasons she had made each decision.

There were the stems she had cut.

There were the stems she had deliberately left.

There was the unresolved stem she wanted to examine again.

Finally, there were the photographs taken after the session.

Looking through the record, Marie noticed something important.

The history did not pretend that Pip had known everything.

It preserved the difference between a suggestion made from a photograph and an observation she had confirmed on the rose.

It preserved her uncertainty as carefully as it preserved her completed cuts.

It also preserved her decisions not to act.

Together, those details told the truthful story of what had happened.

Pip proposed a follow-up window based on the pruning date, Marie's location, the season, local weather context and approved guidance.

Marie could accept it, change it or decline it.

She chose a slightly later window because she knew her garden was slower to warm than other gardens nearby.

Pip recorded her choice.

Before closing the app, Marie looked once more at Sarah's Rose.

She was still learning.

The rose was still complex.

But the decisions they had made together no longer existed only in her memory.

# Chapter 6 — Taking Another Look

Several weeks later, Marie returned to Sarah's Rose.

The season had begun to change.

Small signs of new growth were appearing along some stems. Other parts of the rose looked much as they had immediately after pruning.

Marie opened Ask Pip.

Pip continued from the earlier session.

It asked for an overview photograph from a similar position and close-ups of new shoots or buds, apparent dieback or damage, the overall structure and anything unexpected.

Marie used the earlier photographs to help her frame the new images.

Pip displayed the two sets side by side.

It proposed several changes that might be visible, while stating where differences in angle, light or growth made comparison uncertain.

Marie examined the physical rose again.

She confirmed new shoots on two retained stems.

She corrected one proposed sign of dieback after finding that the stem was alive beyond the area visible in the photograph.

Then she returned to the dead-looking stem she had left unresolved during pruning.

Now the difference between it and the surrounding living growth was easier for her to recognise.

Pip showed the same approved comparison she had used before, together with the earlier uncertainty.

Marie traced the stem on the physical rose and confirmed what she could now see.

The earlier decision to wait had not been a failure.

It had given her more evidence.

Pip asked whether the pruning journey had helped her understand Sarah's Rose better.

Marie thought about the first morning, when the plant had looked like a confusing tangle.

She thought about the rubbing stems she had traced with her own hands.

She thought about the observation she had corrected, the cuts she had chosen and the stem she had deliberately left alone.

She selected **Yes**.

It had not made every question disappear.

It had helped her know what she could see, what remained uncertain and what to do with that uncertainty.

Pip asked whether the comparisons had helped her recognise the structures.

Marie selected **Yes** again.

Then it asked whether remembering the earlier photographs and decisions had made the follow-up more useful.

Marie looked at the two sets of images side by side.

Without the earlier session, she might have noticed only that the rose was beginning to grow.

With it, she could see how particular parts of Sarah's Rose had responded to particular choices.

The follow-up became part of the same history.

Marie closed the app and slipped her phone into her pocket.

A few months earlier, she had wanted someone to tell her what to do.

Now she understood that good care did not always begin with an answer.

Sometimes it began with a photograph.

Then a comparison.

Then a closer look at the actual plant.

Then a decision to cut, leave, wait or ask for help.

Sarah's Rose had begun another season of growth.

So had Marie.

Pip had not replaced the gardener.

It had helped the gardener grow.

# Closing Reflection

Marie's first Ask Pip journey began with an established rose she cared about and a task she did not yet feel able to face.

It concluded with a series of supported decisions, a truthful history and a more observant gardener returning to learn from the rose's response.

The Bush Rose MVP does not depend on Pip being certain about every photograph or directing every cut. Its purpose is to help Marie understand the evidence available, compare it with trusted examples, confirm what is present on the physical rose and remain in control of each decision.

The value continues after pruning because the experience becomes part of Sarah's Rose's history automatically. When Marie returns, Pip remembers what they saw, what remained uncertain, what Marie chose and what happened next.

**Growing understanding. Cultivating confidence.**

## The Founders' Reflection

*"This story keeps the human purpose of the first Ask Pip journey in view. The architecture defines what the MVP must do. The guided-care journey describes how the experience works. Marie's story reminds us why it matters: Ask Pip exists to help a gardener understand an individual plant, make supported choices and keep learning from what happens over time."*
