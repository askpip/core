# 1. Purpose

Use this Skill to produce clear, professional and purpose-focused writing for PIP.

It governs the written expression of established content across documents, prompts, chat responses, correspondence, research summaries, public copy and other written outputs.

# 2. Scope

Apply this Skill whenever artificial intelligence creates, rewrites, expands, condenses or refines written content for PIP.

Where another approved PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System (PIP AI OS) document establishes additional requirements for a particular task or output, apply both.

This Skill governs written expression. It does not determine subject matter, authority, document structure, repository procedure or specialist content requirements.

# 3. Required Writing Characteristics

Completed writing shall:

- communicate the intended meaning accurately;
- serve its stated purpose and intended audience;
- present information clearly and logically;
- use approved terminology consistently;
- remain concise without losing necessary meaning;
- maintain a professional and appropriate tone;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, recommendation, proposal and uncertainty where applicable; and
- remain internally consistent.

# 4. Purpose-Focused Content

Include only content required to fulfil the requested purpose.

Do not transfer surrounding conversation into the writing unless it supplies necessary content or context for the intended output.

Do not answer objections, questions or discussion points that the requested writing does not need to address.

Do not add explanations merely because they appeared in the preceding conversation.

Keep background, qualifications and boundaries proportionate to the purpose of the writing.

# 5. Positive and Direct Expression

Describe the subject primarily through what it is, what it does and what it provides.

Use negative framing only when an exclusion, prohibition, limitation or distinction is necessary to the meaning or function of the writing.

Prefer direct statements over defensive, corrective or anticipatory explanation.

Do not define a subject through unnecessary statements about what it is not.

# 6. Repetition and Economy

Express each distinct idea once unless repetition serves a clear functional purpose.

Remove restatements that add emphasis without adding meaning.

Do not repeat the same principle in an introduction, body, summary and conclusion unless each occurrence serves a necessary and distinct function.

Remove filler, decorative wording and explanatory padding that do not improve understanding.

Do not make writing longer merely to make it sound formal, complete or persuasive.

# 7. Professional Boundaries

Completed writing shall not contain:

- conversational contamination from surrounding discussion;
- unnecessary negative framing;
- defensive or apologetic explanation unrelated to the purpose;
- imagined objections or responses to questions outside scope;
- unsupported promotional, persuasive or exaggerated language;
- speculative elaboration presented as established content;
- repeated statements that do not add meaning;
- inconsistent terminology for the same concept; or
- content introduced only to make the output appear comprehensive.

# 8. Preserve Meaning

Improve clarity, structure and economy without changing the intended meaning.

Do not remove a necessary distinction merely because two statements appear similar.

Do not convert a qualified statement into an absolute statement.

Do not replace precise technical or governed terminology with more conversational wording when precision would be reduced.

# 9. Apply the Appropriate Form

Match the writing to its intended use.

A controlled document shall read as a controlled document.

A prompt shall give the receiving system clear, complete and usable instructions.

A chat response shall answer the request directly without importing document-style formality unnecessarily.

Public copy shall communicate clearly to its intended audience without unsupported claims or promotional inflation.

Correspondence shall remain appropriate to its recipient, purpose and level of formality.

# 10. Completion

Before presenting or saving written content, ensure that it:

- fulfils the requested purpose;
- contains no unnecessary repetition;
- contains no conversational material that does not belong in the output;
- uses positive and direct expression where suitable;
- remains within scope;
- preserves the intended meaning;
- uses approved terminology consistently; and
- is ready for its intended use.
