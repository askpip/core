### Included classes (Brief section 5, group 1)

#### AF-1 — Flower arrangement separates the included classes | Confidence: High

> Hybrid Tea roses bear single or few blooms per stem, Floribundas bear clusters, and Grandifloras bear Hybrid Tea-form blooms in clusters. Every class description consulted uses this feature first.

[[claim:af1|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af1m|Backing and caveats|Nine sources across the UK, US, Australia and NZ agree (RHS, ARS, Rose Society of NSW, NZ Rose Society, Yates, Illinois Extension, Laidback Gardener, Heirloom, Clemson). Blooms are not visible on a dormant plant.]]

#### AF-2 — Grandiflora is described as the tallest | Confidence: High

> Sources that give heights describe Grandiflora as the tallest of the three included classes (Yates up to 1.8 m; ARS 6 to 8 feet or more; Heirloom 5 to 7 feet).

[[claim:af2|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af2m|Backing and caveats|Five sources of three kinds. Heights vary with climate; actual New Zealand heights are unknown.]]

#### AF-3 — Hybrid Tea and Floribunda heights overlap | Confidence: Moderate

> Height ranges for Hybrid Tea and Floribunda overlap across sources, no source gives a separating height, and one source (Oregon State) describes Floribunda as the larger plant.

[[claim:af3|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af3m|Backing and caveats|Six sources. The Oregon State statement sits in a pruning guide. Height is therefore not a dependable way to separate the two.]]

#### AF-4 — Grandiflora is missing from the NZ type lists | Confidence: Moderate

> Grandiflora is described by ARS, Illinois, Yates, Clemson and Heirloom. It is not in the NZ Rose Society type list, the Rose Society of NSW list, or the category lists of two NZ nurseries.

[[claim:af4|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af4m|Backing and caveats|Absence from a menu is not absence from gardens, and menus were read through an automated extraction. Real frequency in NZ is unknown.]]

#### AF-5 — Some cultivars are classed differently by different authorities | Confidence: High

> Some individual cultivars are placed in different classes by different authorities. 'Queen Elizabeth' is listed as both Hybrid Tea and cluster-flowered (Floribunda and Grandiflora).

[[claim:af5|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af5m|Backing and caveats|Illinois Extension, the NZ Rose Society directory and a plant database each show or state it. How often it happens was not measured.]]

#### AF-6 — Grandiflora pruning is grouped with, or lighter than, Hybrid Tea | Confidence: Low

> Oregon State groups 'Hybrid Tea-Grandiflora' for pruning, a nursery sheet gives one cut height for all three classes, and Heirloom advises lighter cutting for Grandiflora. RHS has no Grandiflora group.

[[claim:af6|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af6m|Backing and caveats|Two commercial or nursery sources and one extension source, none with evidence beyond practice. This is the question FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01 reserved for later.]]

### Excluded types (Brief section 5, group 2)

#### AF-7 — Shrub roses are larger, open and arching | Confidence: High

> Shrub roses are described as larger, open, spreading or arching bushes (RHS 'open and spreading', ARS 'sprawling', Rose Society of NSW 'lax arching habit').

[[claim:af7|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af7m|Backing and caveats|Typical, not universal descriptions.]]

#### AF-8 — The shrub class overlaps bush habit | Confidence: Moderate

> The shrub class is a mixed one: Illinois calls it a 'catch all' class, Yates gives 0.5 to 6 m, David Austin says 'quite short to tall', and one NZ nursery lists a shrub-category rose with habit 'Rose Bush'.

[[claim:af8|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af8m|Backing and caveats|Size and habit alone do not reliably separate shrub roses from bush roses. Four sources of differing kinds.]]

#### AF-9 — Patio roses are a separate category | Confidence: High

> Patio roses are a separate category from Hybrid Tea and Floribunda in RHS (small bush roses, 30 to 75 cm, Pruning Group 22), the NZ Rose Society type list, and NZ nurseries.

[[claim:af9|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af9m|Backing and caveats|Rose Society of NSW lists no patio category. Category lists were read through an extraction.]]

#### AF-10 — Sources differ on what a patio rose is nearest to | Confidence: Moderate

> Sources differ: with miniatures and polyanthas (RHS), a down-sized floribunda (Wairere), closer to dwarf floribundas and not miniatures (Peter Beales), or a general term for any container rose (Jackson & Perkins).

[[claim:af10|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af10m|Backing and caveats|Four sources, four positions. No formal NZ authority was found.]]

#### AF-11 — Miniature roses are small | Confidence: High

> Miniature roses are described as small, with stated upper heights of about 50 to 75 cm.

[[claim:af11|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af11m|Backing and caveats|Six sources agree in substance; several combine miniatures with minifloras.]]

#### AF-12 — Minifloras are separated by bloom and foliage size | Confidence: Moderate

> Miniflora roses are distinguished from miniatures and floribundas by bloom and foliage size, which cannot be seen on a dormant plant.

[[claim:af12|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af12m|Backing and caveats|Two society sources, both short statements.]]

#### AF-13 — Groundcover roses are separate but described as 'bush' | Confidence: Moderate

> Groundcover roses are a separate category, yet the Flower Carpet marketer calls its rose 'a low, dense and compact bush' that becomes 'a slightly larger ... shrub' when left unpruned.

[[claim:af13|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af13m|Backing and caveats|The category is well supported; the appearance evidence rests on one marketer.]]

#### AF-14 — Climbing sports of bush roses exist | Confidence: Moderate

> Climbing sports of bush-form Hybrid Teas exist (long canes of 6 to 10 feet, flowers on shorter laterals) and are catalogued as climbing types, including in the NZ Rose Society type list.

[[claim:af14|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af14m|Backing and caveats|Naming convention inferred from examples. No source says whether a climbing sport reverts to bush form.]]

#### AF-15 — Standard roses have a tall bare grafted stem | Confidence: High

> A standard rose has a single tall bare stem carrying a grafted crown. Weeping standards use 1.5 to 1.8 m stems.

[[claim:af15|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af15m|Backing and caveats|RHS, Rose Society of NSW and NZ nurseries agree.]]

#### AF-16 — Old Garden and species roses are defined by history and flowering | Confidence: Moderate

> Old Garden Roses and species roses are defined by date, lineage and flowering habit (for example once-flowering), not by plant form.

[[claim:af16|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af16m|Backing and caveats|ARS, Rose Society of NSW and the NZ Rose Society type list agree. Old Garden Roses are diverse.]]

### Identification and uncertainty (Brief section 5, group 4)

#### AF-20 — Identifying an unlabelled rose is difficult | Confidence: Moderate

> Sources describe identifying an unlabelled rose as difficult and dependent on many plant parts (foliage, prickles, habit, flowers), and often needing an experienced rosarian.

[[claim:af20|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af20m|Backing and caveats|Three sources of different kinds. One concerns older roses and one is a single sentence.]]

#### AF-21 — One nursery says dormant wood identifies most varieties | Confidence: Low

> One Australian nursery states that, with years in the business, it can identify well over 90% of the varieties it grows from bare canes.

[[claim:af21|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af21m|Backing and caveats|Single commercial source, about experienced staff and the nursery's own varieties, with no method given.]]

#### AF-22 — Photo-app accuracy evidence is about trees | Confidence: Low

> The only located evidence on photograph-app accuracy concerns 55 tree species: genus level 71.8% to 97.3%, species level 40.9% to 83.9%, and bark pictures much less accurate than leaves.

[[claim:af22|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af22m|Backing and caveats|Not roses, not cultivars, not leafless plants.]]

#### AF-23 — RHS: prune an unidentified inherited rose as a Hybrid Tea | Confidence: Low

> The RHS advises that if you inherit an overgrown rose and do not know its type (and do not want to wait for flowering), it is best to prune as for a Hybrid Tea.

[[claim:af23|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af23m|Backing and caveats|One source, one country, and it concerns pruning, not scope qualification.]]

#### AF-24 — Sources point an unsure gardener to experienced people | Confidence: Moderate

> Sources point an unsure gardener to a local rose society, a rosarian or a second opinion.

[[claim:af24|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af24m|Backing and caveats|Three sources of different kinds; two do not address classification directly.]]
