## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Ask Pip records each rose's location (GPS, or town or city, region and country) and derives its hemisphere and season. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for New Zealand. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-2 (High) records that pruning dates vary with climate and cites Dunedin's late July. `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F4 and F5 approve feeding and deadheading patterns with no cut-off date. New Zealand has no single climate-zone system that gardeners commonly use.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** Main winter pruning: months in each climate band, and the local signal sources tie it to.
- **Q2** Feeding: months to start in spring and to give the last feed, in each band.
- **Q3** Late-season cut-off: when to stop deadheading and summer pruning, in each band.
- **Q4** Planting and moving: months to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band.
- **Q5** Winter clear-up and protection: when to clear leaves and prunings; when any winter spray is done; when any winter protection goes on and comes off. Products, brands and rates are not findings.
- **Q6** Climate bands: how NZ sources divide the country, and whether an authoritative climate or frost source such as NIWA could support a band.
- **Q7** Placing the gardener: which bands a beginner could be placed in from region, town or GPS.
- **Q8** Year-to-year variation: how sources handle an early or late season or a garden near a band edge.

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting and moving for Q4. Every finding records its band. Months from one band are not applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are not converted. Months are context; they do not replace the bud-swell Suitability Gate in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

**Method.**

- Web searches were run for each question, including searches aimed at the New Zealand Rose Society, the Auckland Rose Society, the Canterbury Horticultural Society, Dunedin and Auckland Botanic Gardens, NIWA, Central Otago and Waikato sources, and New Zealand nurseries and garden centres.
- Every cited page was opened. Pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question; each extraction was asked for exact sentences. The quotations relied on for NZRS-FAQ, NZRS-W, ODT-GL, CHS-MAY, CHS-JUN, CHS-JUL, CHS-AUG, CHS-SEP, ABG, NIWA-NZ, NIWA-OT, GU-P, GU-F, GU-DH, NZH-W, TUI-PG, YAT-PR, YAT-NZ, PALM-YRC, MATT, KINGS, SPR-P, DALT-G, ODER and GF were re-checked against a second extraction asking for the sentence character for character.
- Every quotation used is under 25 words and is recorded, by finding, in the Source Register with the access date.
- Sources already opened for `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` (KINGS, PALM-G, PALM-FAQ, TUI-Q, TUI-T, YAT-NZ, NZRS-FAQ, ABG) and `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` (NZRS-W, NZRS-FAQ, ABG) were re-opened for their month statements.
- Each claim is a separate finding with its own Evidence Confidence Level (EAS §3.2). Each finding states the band it applies to, in the source's own words.
- Approved ARCs and the two pending dossiers were checked for agreement only (§7, final subsection).

**Anticipated challenges.** New Zealand non-commercial rose sources are few. Sources describe climate bands in loose words rather than defined lines. NIWA publishes frost data but not rose timing.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** All rose sources address New Zealand directly. NIWA sources address climate and frost, not roses, and are relevant to Q6 and Q7 only.

**Source reliability.** The New Zealand Rose Society, Dunedin and Auckland Botanic Gardens (both council-run), the Canterbury Horticultural Society and NIWA are recognised authorities. One rose columnist and two newspaper columns are knowledgeable but individual. Twelve companies are practised growers and sellers, given less weight as independent authority.

**Consistency.** Sources agree strongly that main pruning falls in June to August, most often July and August, that colder places prune later, that pruning waits for dormancy and for frosts to pass, and that pruning too early risks frost damage. They disagree on the exact month within the window, on when the north starts, on the last feed, on the first feed, and on whether autumn suits moving a rose.

**Independence.** Pages from one organisation are counted once (§4.2). Month statements for feeding, deadheading cut-off and moving come almost entirely from companies.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. Non-commercial NZ sources give months mainly for pruning and planting. No source defines where a band begins or ends.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1 — Which months nationally (AF-8).** June or July: SPR-P "June or July are generally the preferred months"; KINGS "June/July"; DALT-G "June/July". July and August: GU-P "July and August are the two main months"; NZRS-W "July and even August"; PALM-YRC "July through to mid-late August"; YAT-PR "July is the month". NZRS-DEMO spans both: "between late June and mid August depending on where you live". MATT gives "June, July and August". Unresolved; the overlap is July.

**C-2 — Colder places earlier or later (AF-1, AF-17).** NZRS-FAQ, DUN-PT ("the colder the climate, the later you prune"), CHS-JUL, CHS-AUG, ODT-GL, TUI-PG and PALM-YRC prune later where it is colder. DALT-G: "In cooler climates you can prune earlier once all the leaves have fallen", and in warmer climates delays to "mid-late July". DALT-G ties timing to leaf fall, the others to frost. Unresolved; DALT-G is a single commercial voice.

**C-3 — When the north starts (AF-9).** NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July". PALM-YRC: the north can start "a bit earlier" than July. GF (Auckland): "in July". TUI-PG (Kerikeri): "the end of July, early August is a good time to prune in Northland." Unresolved.

**C-4 — Christchurch (AF-10, AF-12).** TUI-PG treats Christchurch as "a colder climate" and advises "late winter or early spring after the last frost". CHS-JUL and CHS-AUG divide Canterbury: milder parts from the end of July, colder or inland districts in August or once the worst frosts have passed. Unresolved; the two describe the same city differently.

**C-5 — The last feed (AF-21).** GU-F: "no later than the end of February". SPR-F: "September to March". DALT-G: "mid-February through to early April". TUI-FEED: "late summer, early autumn". PALM-YRC: "a final feed before Winter". KINGS feeds "in spring, summer and autumn". Unresolved.

**C-6 — The first feed (AF-19, AF-20).** GU-F: "late August and into September". SPR-F: from September. ODER: "After August". CHS-SEP (Canterbury, September): "as new growth appears". DALT-G: "from mid-late October". YAT-PR: in frosty areas wait "until the last frosts"; in warm climates feed at pruning. Unresolved.

**C-7 — When to move a rose (AF-30, AF-31).** Winter while dormant: KINGS, TUI-Q, DALT-G ("June/July"), PALM-FAQ ("after pruning in July"). Autumn: PALM-YRC, "Autumn is the best time for transplanting". PALM-FAQ also says "make sure that the risk of frosts (or freezing weather) has passed". The two Palmers pages disagree with each other. Unresolved.

**C-8 — Bare-root planting season in Auckland (AF-27, AF-28).** National sources say winter, with some continuing into spring. ABG (Auckland) says "autumn or winter". Bare-root stock is stated to arrive from June (AF-26). Unresolved; ABG does not say whether it means bare-root or potted roses.

**C-9 — When deadheading stops (AF-23).** MATT: "until the end of February". GU-DH: "late summer". GF (Auckland): "from March". PALM-YRC: "until the end of March". Unresolved; the range is about one month.

**Uncertainty preserved.** No source defines a band boundary (AF-3); frost is local (AF-6); feeding, deadheading and moving months come almost entirely from companies; no NZ source describes winter protection or a summer-pruning cut-off (AF-25, AF-35); no NZ source names bud swell (AF-16).

## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim and states the band it applies to, in the source's words.

### Climate bands and placing the gardener (Q6, Q7)

**AF-1 (established; national).** NZ rose sources contrast the warmer north with the colder south, and milder with colder or frosty districts, and prune later where it is colder. NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July while in the colder parts of the South Island, it gets under-way in August." DUN-PT: "The general rule is the colder the climate, the later you prune in winter." PALM-YRC: "In the north, it is perfectly fine to start a bit earlier than this, and in the south a bit later." CHS-JUL: "In milder parts of Canterbury, pruning can begin towards the end of the month; colder areas may prefer to wait until August." CHS-AUG: "In colder inland districts, prune once the worst frosts have passed." Also TUI-PG, ODT-GL. DALT-G dissents (C-2).

**AF-2 (expert opinion, single source; national).** NZRS-FAQ splits spring peak bloom three ways: "first two weeks in November for the upper North Island, last two weeks for the lower North Island and from the 3rd week in November to mid December in the South Island." This concerns flowering, not pruning or feeding.

**AF-3 (unresolved question; national).** No NZ rose source located draws a fixed boundary for its bands. They use "up north" and "the colder parts of the South Island" (NZRS-FAQ), "In the north" and "in the south" (PALM-YRC), "milder parts of Canterbury" and "colder inland districts" (CHS-JUL, CHS-AUG), "frosty locations" (ODT-GL), "frosty areas" and "warm climates" (YAT-PR), "cooler climates" and "warmer climates" (DALT-G), or name a town (TUI-PG, NZH-W, DUN-PT).

**AF-4 (supported interpretation; national).** NIWA-NZ: "Frosts are common in the cooler months for most of the country, however they occur rarely north of Waikato." FIG-FROST (NIWA data, 1991 to 2020) gives mean annual ground-frost days of 0.14 at Kaitaia and 4.23 at Auckland, against 84.75 at Christchurch, 116.82 at Queenstown and 116.62 at Alexandra.

**AF-5 (supported interpretation; national).** Frosts are less frequent on the coast and in the far north, and most frequent inland. NIWA-NZ: frosts "occur less frequently in coastal and northernmost areas." NIWA-OT: "air frosts occur most frequently at the inland location of Alexandra." FIG-FROST: Dunedin 49.78 ground-frost days a year against Alexandra 116.62.

**AF-6 (supported interpretation; national).** Frost is local. NIWA-OT: "Frost is a local phenomenon and both its frequency of occurrence and intensity can vary widely over small areas." NIWA-NZ: "Areas most likely to experience frost are flat areas ... and in valleys and basins." ODT-GL makes the same point for roses (AF-36).

**AF-7 (expert opinion, single source; national).** NIWA-OV: "New Zealand's climate is complex and varies from warm subtropical in the far north to cool temperate climates in the far south", and mean annual temperatures range "from 10°C in the south to 16°C in the north". It names no boundary.

### Main winter pruning (Q1)

**AF-8 (established; national).** Main winter pruning falls between June and August, most often July and August. GU-P: "July and August are the two main months for pruning roses in New Zealand". NZRS-W: "Most people prune in July and even August." NZRS-DEMO: "roses are usually pruned between late June and mid August depending on where you live". YAT-PR: "July is the month when most roses are pruned." PALM-YRC: "July through to mid-late August." MATT: "June, July and August." SPR-P: "June or July are generally the preferred months but some folk will prune in August". KINGS: "June/July". DALT-G: "June/July". Sources differ within the window (C-1).

**AF-9 (supported interpretation; north).** Sources place main pruning in the north from early July to early August. NZRS-FAQ: "Pruning starts up north around early July". PALM-YRC: the north can "start a bit earlier" than July. GF (Auckland): "leave them be until pruning time, in July." TUI-PG (Kerikeri): "the end of July, early August is a good time to prune in Northland." (C-3.)

**AF-10 (supported interpretation; colder South Island and frost-prone inland districts).** Main pruning is in August or later, once the worst frosts have passed. NZRS-FAQ: "in the colder parts of the South Island, it gets under-way in August." CHS-JUL: "colder areas may prefer to wait until August." CHS-AUG: "In colder inland districts, prune once the worst frosts have passed." ODT-GL (Dunedin): "frosty locations can mean waiting until mid-August." TUI-PG (Christchurch): "late winter or early spring after the last frost." PALM-YRC: "in the south a bit later." (C-4.)

**AF-11 (expert opinion, one organisation; Dunedin, coastal Otago).** DUN-PT: "At Dunedin Botanic Garden we prune modern roses in late July". DUN-WM: "pruning is left until late July, after the worst of the frosts." ODT-GL: "in Dunedin tends to be late July." The garden's own practice, stated over 2014 to 2018.

**AF-12 (expert opinion, one organisation; milder parts of Canterbury).** CHS-JUL: "In milder parts of Canterbury, pruning can begin towards the end of the month" (July). CHS-AUG: "Complete winter pruning in milder areas." CHS-SEP: "Finish winter pruning".

**AF-13 (expert opinion, limited; named lower North Island towns).** NZH-W: "In Whanganui, roses and deciduous fruit trees such as apples and pears are best pruned in July." TUI-PG (Masterton): "mid to late winter which is July - August."

**AF-14 (established; national).** The local signals named for main pruning are full dormancy and the worst frosts having passed. DUN-PT: "when they are at their most dormant and hopefully the heaviest of frosts have passed." SPR-P: "when the rose bush has gone into it's Winter dormancy." CHS-AUG: "prune once the worst frosts have passed." ABG: "when they are not in active growth." TUI-PG: "after the last frost." DALT-G: "when the plant has lost all its leaves."

**AF-15 (established; national).** Pruning too early risks new growth being damaged by frost. TUI-PG: "If you prune too early you risk the plant sending out new growth which won't have had time to harden off". PALM-YRC: "Don't prune too early for your region as the new growth can be damaged by the last of the frosts." NZH-W: new growth "risks getting walloped with a July frost." DUN-WM: "If pruned too early, roses can be hit by frost but too late and growth will have already started."

**AF-16 (unresolved question; national).** No NZ rose source located names bud swell as the signal for main pruning. NZ sources name dormancy, leaf fall and frost (AF-14). The only mention of swelling buds is ODER, for spray timing: "apply liquid copper before buds begin to swell."

**AF-17 (dissenting opinion, one company; cooler and warmer climates).** DALT-G: "In cooler climates you can prune earlier once all the leaves have fallen"; "in warmer climates delay pruning until mid-late July when the plant has lost all its leaves." Contradicts AF-1 in direction (C-2).

### Feeding (Q2)

**AF-18 (established; national).** Feeding starts in spring, as new growth appears or after pruning. CHS-SEP: "begin feeding roses as new growth appears." GU-F: "as the roses are coming into growth." TUI-FEED: "in early spring when the buds burst into life". PALM-YRC: "Apply fertiliser in early Spring after pruning." ABG: "Apply fertiliser in spring". ODER: "Avoid fertilising in winter, as roses are dormant and won't absorb nutrients."

**AF-19 (supported interpretation; national).** Months named for the first feed run from late August to mid-late October, most often September. GU-F: "In late August and into September". SPR-F: "(September to March)". ODER: "After August". CHS-SEP (Canterbury) places it on its September list. DALT-G: "from mid-late October". (C-6.)

**AF-20 (expert opinion, single commercial source; frosty areas and warm climates).** YAT-PR: "In frosty areas, it's better to wait until the last frosts before feeding your roses." In "warm climates" it feeds at pruning, before mulching. No month is given.

**AF-21 (expert opinion, conflicting; national).** Months named for the last feed differ. GU-F: "no later than the end of February". SPR-F: to "March". DALT-G: "mid-February through to early April". TUI-FEED: "late summer, early autumn". PALM-YRC: "a final feed before Winter". KINGS: "in spring, summer and autumn". (C-5.)

**AF-22 (expert opinion, single source; national).** GU-F gives the reason for the last feed: "After this time, let the roses prepare for winter by allowing the canes to harden up."

### Late-season cut-off (Q3)

**AF-23 (supported interpretation; national, with one Auckland source).** Deadheading stops in late summer or early autumn. MATT: deadheading "until the end of February". GU-DH: "you may wish to stop deadheading in late summer". GF (Auckland): "it's best to stop deadheading from March". PALM-YRC: "continue dead-heading until the end of March". (C-9.)

**AF-24 (supported interpretation; national).** The stated reason is to let hips form. PALM-YRC: "leave for colourful hips to form." GF: "allow the plants to develop their rosehips". MATT: "Allow your rose plants to set their hips in Autumn". GU-DH: to allow hips "to develop". GF also says roses "need to rest" as winter approaches. No NZ source gives frost-hardening as the reason for stopping deadheading.

**AF-25 (unresolved question; national).** No NZ source located gives a month to stop summer pruning. YAT-PR and PALM-G describe a light summer prune after flowering but give no cut-off.

### Planting and moving (Q4)

**AF-26 (supported interpretation; national).** New-season bare-root roses reach garden centres in June. KINGS: "New seasons Roses arrive in store from June." NZRS-W: "most garden centres promote new roses early in June." YAT-NZ: they "arrive in garden centres in time for winter planting".

**AF-27 (supported interpretation; national and Canterbury).** Bare-root roses are planted in winter, named as June to August, with some sources continuing into spring. NZRS-FAQ: "Winter is the best time to plant". DALT-G: "June/July are the best months to plant". CHS-JUN, CHS-JUL and CHS-AUG (Canterbury) list June, July ("one of the best months to plant roses") and August ("an excellent time to plant roses"). KINGS: "best planted in winter, however they can be planted right through spring." PALM-G: "from winter through to spring."

**AF-28 (expert opinion, single source; Auckland).** ABG: "Planting is best done in autumn or winter." The page does not separate bare-root from potted roses (C-8).

**AF-29 (established; national).** Potted roses can be planted at any time of year. YAT-NZ: "potted roses are often available (and can be planted) throughout the year." PALM-G: "Container grown roses can be planted in your garden at any time of the year." NZRS-FAQ: "If planting potted roses during spring and summer, keep them well watered." MATT also describes planting potted roses in "Spring/Summer".

**AF-30 (supported interpretation; national).** An established rose is moved in winter while dormant, named as June or July. TUI-Q: "the best time of year to move a rose is winter when it is dormant". KINGS: "This is best done when dormant during Winter." DALT-G: "June/July are the best months to plant, relocate and prune roses." PALM-FAQ: "after pruning in July when the rose is dormant with no active growth." All are commercial.

**AF-31 (dissenting opinion, single commercial page; national).** PALM-YRC: "Autumn is the best time for transplanting as they'll have time to get established over Winter." The same company's PALM-FAQ says July (C-7).

### Winter clear-up, sprays and protection (Q5)

**AF-32 (established; national and Canterbury).** Fallen and diseased leaves are cleared through autumn and winter and at pruning. NZRS-W (June): "Rake up all fallen leaves and burn them". GU-P: "fallen leaves which you must pick up after pruning." YAT-PR: "clean up any fallen foliage around the bush and discard it." PALM-YRC (autumn): "Collect any infected leaves that have fallen to the ground". GF (Auckland, autumn): "clear the ground beneath them of spent foliage". CHS-MAY, CHS-JUN and CHS-JUL (Canterbury) list removing diseased foliage or leaves in May, June and July.

**AF-33 (supported interpretation; national).** A winter clean-up spray is applied in winter after pruning; the months named are June and July. NZRS-W: "it is highly recommended that clean up sprays be applied in June." ODER: "In June/July , after pruning, apply lime sulphur". GU-P: "leave it a few days after pruning." YAT-NZ: "Immediately after pruning". YAT-PR: "Pruning time is the ideal window". KINGS: "In winter after leaf fall". PALM-YRC: "a good Winter spray". DALT-Q: "at any time during the next few months" (from June). Products and rates are not collected.

**AF-34 (expert opinion, limited; national).** Some sources follow the winter spray with a further spray up to bud swell or bud burst. ODER: "apply liquid copper before buds begin to swell." KINGS: "At bud burst spray with a copper spray". PALM-FAQ: "at the time of pruning and again one month after."

**AF-35 (unresolved question; national).** No NZ source located describes winter protection such as mounding soil or mulch over the base, or covering roses, for any band, including Central Otago (§4.3).

### Year-to-year variation and band edges (Q8)

**AF-36 (established; national).** Sources say the month windows depend on where you live and on the garden's own microclimate. NZRS-DEMO: "depending on where you live". SPR-P: "it all depends on where you live in the country." PALM-YRC: "Don't prune too early for your region". ODT-GL: "Take into consideration the microclimate in your garden, though, as frosty locations can mean waiting until mid-August."

**AF-37 (expert opinion, single commercial source; national).** DALT-Q, answering a gardener whose roses were still flowering in early June: "Pruning any earlier with the very mild autumn we are experiencing may result in new growth appearing", and advises waiting until the rose is fully dormant, which may move pruning into July.

**AF-38 (expert opinion, single source; national).** NZRS-FAQ: "Your local rose society can provide advice suited to your own climate."

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority. Each states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. Where New Zealand's band boundaries fall.**
No NZ rose source draws a line (AF-3). NIWA describes frost patterns but not rose bands (AF-4 to AF-6). Settling it needs either Founder judgement on a boundary or direct consultation with the New Zealand Rose Society or regional societies, a different method from published-source research.
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Point 2.
*Recommended default:* until decided, Pip shows the national window (AF-8) to every NZ gardener, with the sources' own words that the north starts earlier and colder or frosty places later (AF-1).

**R-2. Non-commercial NZ months for feeding, deadheading cut-off and moving.**
The months in AF-19, AF-21, AF-23 and AF-30 come almost entirely from companies. Answering needs direct enquiry to the New Zealand Rose Society or regional rose societies.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission consultation.
*Recommended default:* Pip shows these months labelled as coming from New Zealand garden companies, alongside the approved patterns in `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F4 and F5 (Founder Decision Point 6).

**R-3. Winter protection in New Zealand's coldest inland districts.**
No NZ source describes it (AF-35). Answering needs consultation with growers in Central Otago or the Mackenzie.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission it.
*Recommended default:* Pip shows no winter-protection months for New Zealand.

**R-4. First and last frost dates by location.**

### 11.8 Decision points in §10

A Founder answered §10 on the Review Form on 3 October 2026. The Pip Knowledge Rules and the standing decisions made since then settle each point, and none is asked again:

- **Point 2 (climate bands).** Answered (a), no automatic bands. Unchanged: every New Zealand gardener sees the national months, with the sources' own words on the north and on colder places.
- **Point 3 (placing a gardener).** Settled by the standing decisions "Where a gardener is" (5 October 2026) and "Places inside a country" (6 October 2026): a statement is shown only in the country, region or town its source names, and the app works the place out on the gardener's own device. Frost is said in words (standing decision "Frost", 6 October 2026).
- **Point 4 (months beside the plant check).** Answered (a). It is now rule 6: months are only a guide and the check on the plant decides.
- **Point 5 (band edges and unusual seasons).** Answered (b). Rule 3 gives the range; AF-36 and AF-37 give the sources' words on the garden itself and on a mild autumn; AF-38 and rule 5 point to a local rose society.
- **Point 6 (months from companies only).** Answered (a). It is now the standing decision "Garden companies and local months" (5 October 2026): Pip says how many sources there are and that months are only a guide.
- **Point 7 (R-1 to R-4).** Answered "not needed". Unchanged.

The commission's Q5 said products were not collected as findings. Under the standing decisions "Products" and "Exclusions" (5 October 2026), the types of spray the pages name (lime sulphur, copper, oil) are recorded as they name them; no brand and no mixing amount is.
