## 0. Note on This Revision

**Why this is one of two dossiers, not one.** This dossier and its companion, `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`, were researched together under a single Research Commission Record, because the Founder's working expectation was that an inward-growing stem would often turn out to be one that crosses another. That expectation is not itself evidence, and the ROC tested it directly (Q1, below) rather than assuming it. The research found — like a related question tested during the pruning-framework commission — that no source anywhere states an occurrence rate for this, and that only direct, real-world observation over time could ever settle it; it is recorded as a permanent limitation, not a pending search item. Since the premise for combining the two observations did not hold up, and since the Bush Rose V1 Architecture always treated crossing/rubbing stems and inward-growing stems as two separate supported observations, each needing its own Observation PKR, the research is presented as two separate dossiers. This required reorganising already-completed findings, not new research. A small number of findings that genuinely apply to both observations (photographic limits, AF-3; general deferral timing, AF-6) are stated here and in the companion dossier alike.

**Outcome, in brief, for this dossier's subject (inward-growing stems).** This is the thinner of the two dossiers from the same research effort — inward-growing stems attracted noticeably less dedicated coverage across the source base than crossing or rubbing stems did. Only three sources (Kansas City Rose Society, Dunedin Botanic Garden, University of Georgia) name inward or centre-directed growth as its own thing to recognise and remove, rather than addressing it only prospectively (cutting to an outward-facing bud, so that *future* growth doesn't point inward). This thinness is itself a finding worth the Founders' attention (AF-1, AF-4) — not a research shortfall, since the same broad source pool was searched for both dossiers.

## 8. Evidence Assessment

### AF-1 — Inward-growing or centre-directed growth is addressed mainly as a shape/airflow goal, not as its own named defect

**Question:** Q1
**Assessed Finding:** Most sources address inward or centre-directed growth through a prospective instruction — cut to an outward-facing bud, so new growth points away from the centre — rather than treating an existing inward-growing stem as its own named defect with dedicated recognition criteria, the way crossing and rubbing are treated (see `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` AF-1).
**Evidence Confidence Level: Moderate**

**Evidence.** University of Maryland Extension: "Pruning to an outward bud encourages the branch to grow outwards, not into the center of the shrub." Yates New Zealand, South Pacific Roses, Rose Society of Victoria, and others give the same outward-bud instruction without separately naming or defining "inward-growing" as a category to recognise on an existing stem. Dunedin Botanic Garden and Kansas City Rose Society are exceptions — both do name inward/centre-directed growth as its own thing to look for and remove (AF-2). American Rose Society: "Such cuts avoid the growth from being directed inward and colliding with other stems," and Texas A&M AgriLife (general): "the new shoots will not grow through the interior of the plants or crisscross" — both frame inward direction prospectively, as an outcome to prevent by cutting correctly, not a defect to diagnose on an existing stem.

**Strengths.** The pattern (outward-bud cutting as the primary mechanism, rather than inward-stem recognition) is consistent across many independent sources and publisher types, including sources not shared with the companion dossier.

**Limitations.** This is a pattern observed across the source base, not a single stated rule; a small number of sources (AF-2) do treat it as its own recognisable category.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Whether the sources that only give the outward-bud instruction consider existing inward growth unimportant, or simply address it prospectively rather than diagnostically, was not tested.

---

### AF-2 — Where an indicator for inward growth is stated, it is the stem's growth direction toward the bush's interior

**Question:** Q2
**Assessed Finding:** The small number of sources that do name inward-growing stems as their own category describe the indicator simply as the direction of growth — toward the bush's centre — rather than any more specific visual marker.
**Evidence Confidence Level: Low** (few sources)

**Evidence.** Kansas City Rose Society: "canes will grow toward the center of the bush or are otherwise not growing in a graceful, natural direction" — notably a soft, subjective standard, not a hard rule. Dunedin Botanic Garden: "remove inward facing growth." University of Georgia: "canes that... grow inward."

**Strengths.** The three sources are independent and agree on the basic indicator (direction toward the centre).

**Limitations.** Only three sources; none gives a more specific visual marker than direction itself, and Kansas City Rose Society's own wording signals this is a soft judgement.

**Remaining uncertainty.** How confidently growth direction can be judged — especially from a photograph — is addressed separately at AF-3.

---

### AF-3 — A single photograph has real limits for judging structural questions of this kind

**Question:** Q3
**Assessed Finding:** No rose-specific source addresses what a photograph can or cannot establish about inward-growing stems — the literature is uniformly written for a gardener standing in front of the plant. Drawing on general professional pruning practice and one directly relevant academic study, the ROC's own synthesis is that a single, fixed-viewpoint photograph has real limits for judging a stem's true growth direction — the kind of 3D structural question professional practice addresses by physically viewing a plant from multiple sides before cutting. **This finding is stated identically in the companion dossier, `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` (its AF-4), since the underlying evidence concerns structural photo-assessment generally, not this observation specifically.**
**Evidence Confidence Level: Low** (a ROC synthesis of general, non-rose-specific evidence, extrapolated per EAS §2.10)

**Evidence.** NC State Extension: "step back and look over the plant from all sides." University of Missouri Extension, Master Gardener Core Manual Ch. 8: "Walk around the plant to view it from all sides." Berland et al. (2019), *Forests* 10(4):349: "virtual surveys are not appropriate for detailed assessments of tree risk because the analyst cannot examine the tree up close or from all angles."

**Strengths.** The extension guidance independently and consistently treats multi-angle physical viewing as standard practice; the academic study directly tests the underlying principle.

**Limitations.** No source states this principle for inward-growth judgments specifically. Judging a stem's growth direction from a single photograph specifically (as opposed to whether two stems touch) was not addressed by any source at all, rose-specific or general — this is a genuine, undiluted gap (U-5).

**Remaining uncertainty.** Whether a second photograph, or a specific camera angle (e.g., looking down into the bush from above), would materially help judge growth direction was not addressed by any source.

---

### AF-4 — Once confirmed, an inward-growing stem is removed, on the same basis as crossing/rubbing stems

**Question:** Q5
**Assessed Finding:** Where sources do name inward-growing stems as their own category, they instruct removing them, on the same footing as crossing or rubbing stems — as ordinary dormant-season maintenance.
**Evidence Confidence Level: Low** (fewer, but consistent, sources)

**Evidence.** Kansas City Rose Society: "Remove them to the ground or back to the next outward facing bud." Dunedin Botanic Garden: "Open up bush – remove inward facing growth." University of Georgia: "Remove any canes that cross through the center of the plant or grow inward."

**Strengths.** Three independent sources, unanimous in direction (remove).

**Limitations.** Markedly fewer sources than the equivalent finding for crossing/rubbing stems (`FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` AF-5, sixteen sources) — this dossier's own AF-1 explains why: most sources address inward growth only prospectively, via the outward-bud instruction, rather than as a diagnosis-and-removal category. This finding should be read as Low confidence specifically because of that thinner coverage, not because the instruction itself is contested; no source disagrees with removing a confirmed inward-growing stem.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Whether the many sources that only give the outward-bud instruction would also recommend removing an already-established inward-growing stem is a reasonable inference, not something they state directly.

---

### AF-5 — Removal is a full cut back to the stem's point of origin, not a shortening

**Question:** Q5
**Assessed Finding:** Where sources specify where the cut is made, an inward-growing stem is removed entirely — back to the ground, the bud union, or the larger cane it originates from — not merely shortened, consistent with the equivalent finding for crossing/rubbing stems.
**Evidence Confidence Level: Moderate**

**Evidence.** Kansas City Rose Society: "removed to the ground or back to the next outward facing bud" (stated for inward-growing stems specifically, in the same passage as AF-4's evidence). The general cut-type evidence in `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` AF-6 (Missouri G6870, Arkansas FSA-6149, USDA Forest Service) supports the same cut type for structural removal generally, not limited to crossing stems.

**Strengths.** Directly stated for this observation by Kansas City Rose Society, and consistent with the general structural-removal cut type evidenced at greater depth in the companion dossier.

**Limitations.** Only one source states this for inward-growing stems specifically; the rest of the support is inferred from the general cut-type finding rather than restated per-observation.

**Remaining uncertainty.** None beyond what is stated; consistent with, and does not duplicate, `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`'s general cut-mechanics findings.

---

### AF-6 — Deferral guidance in the literature concerns general pruning timing, not this observation specifically

**Question:** Q6
**Assessed Finding:** Where sources advise waiting or deferring a pruning decision, the reason given is general dormant-season timing — not anything specific to a confirmed inward-growing stem. **This finding is stated identically in the companion dossier, `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`.**
**Evidence Confidence Level: Moderate**

**Evidence.** Oregon State University Extension, Iowa State University Extension (Linn County), and Colorado State University Extension / Ask Extension (full citations in the companion dossier's AF-8) all give general timing/deferral guidance tied to cold-damage avoidance and distinguishing live from dead wood, not to any specific observation.

**Strengths.** Three independent university-extension sources agree on the general principle.

**Limitations.** None ties deferral to inward growth specifically.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Whether a gardener should ever defer specifically because they are unsure whether a stem is genuinely inward-growing was not addressed by any source (U-1, U-2).

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research, and Practical Defaults

Each unresolved question from §7.2 is given a stated, ROC-recommended practical default. Each is the ROC's own recommendation under EAS §2.10, not a sourced finding; any Founder may override any of them; none blocks approval.

**U-1 — Beginner lookalikes and confounders.** No source names any. **Default:** no specific confounder list is offered; the existing requirement to confirm any observation on the physical rose before acting is the primary safeguard. A phototropism-related confusion is plausible but unsourced and offered only as a caution worth bearing in mind, not a rule.

**U-2 — Priority when a stem is both inward-growing and crossing/rubbing.** Not addressed by any source. **No default is needed**, because this is not, in practice, a live conflict — see `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` U-3.

**U-3 — Whether professional/experienced help is ever specifically warranted.** No source ties escalation to this observation specifically. **Default:** governed by the app's general uncertainty/confidence gates.

**U-4 — Task-specific safety for reaching into congested/interior growth.** No dedicated finding for this observation. **Default:** apply `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` U-6's default here too — cross-refer to `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`'s generic safety content, and take the same care (avoid damaging kept stems; cut a tangled stem in sections) when removing an inward-growing stem from the bush's interior.

**U-5 — Whether a second photograph from a different angle would help.** Not addressed by any source. **Default:** where a first photograph leaves confidence low on growth-direction judgment (AF-3), request a second photograph — ideally one giving a clearer view into the bush's interior — as reasonable operational practice.
