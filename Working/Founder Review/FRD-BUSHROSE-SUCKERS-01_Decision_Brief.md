This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 2. The short version

Most roses sold are two plants joined together: a tough "rootstock" underneath, and the chosen rose grafted on top at a knobbly join called the **bud union**. A **sucker** is a shoot from the rootstock. It isn't the rose you bought, and if left it can take over. A **basal shoot** is a new cane from the rose itself, and it's valuable.

- **The one reliable test is where the shoot starts.** Below the bud union means sucker; from the union or just above means the rose's own shoot. Thirteen sources agree (**High**).
- **The start point is often under the soil.** Sources say to trace the shoot down or clear soil to see it (**High**).
- **Leaves and flowers can help, but they can mislead, and they aren't there in winter.** The old "seven leaflets means sucker" rule is not dependable (**Moderate**). **No source explains how to spot a sucker on a bare, dormant rose** (**Low**).
- **Remove it where it starts, never at soil level**, or it regrows (**High**). Remove it early (**High**).
- **Sources disagree on tearing versus cutting.** The RHS and most sources say tear or pull it off; others say cut it cleanly at the start point.
- **People easily mistake one for the other** (**Moderate**), and removing a real basal shoot loses good growth (**High**). Sources say act only when certain, and ask an experienced grower if in doubt (**Low**).
- **No source says what a photo can show.**

## 3. What was researched

Q1: features that tell a sucker from the rose's own new cane, and which show on a dormant plant. Q2: how reliable they are, including own-root roses and a buried bud union. Q3: how a sucker is removed, why, and with what care. Q4: what a photo can and can't show. Q5: when to leave it or get help.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Tear or cut (AF-18).** RHS and most sources: tear or pull. University of California Ventura, Treloar, Fine Gardening and an American Rose Society rosarian: cut cleanly at the start point. Everyone agrees: at the start point, not at soil level.
- **C-2 Leaf clues (AF-3, AF-4).** Some sources say sucker leaves are clearly different; others say they can look the same.
- **C-3 Own-root "suckers" (AF-11).** Sources agree the shoots are the same rose but disagree on whether to call them suckers.
- **C-4 Bud-union depth (AF-12).** RHS: at soil level. Harkness: 2.5 to 5 cm below. Fine Gardening: always bury it. Eastcroft: above soil. Oregon State: depends on climate.
- **C-5 When to act (AF-20).** Most sources: remove as soon as spotted. Fine Gardening: the first spring flowering is the best time to identify them.
- **C-6 Growth above the union (AF-1, AF-14).** Most sources: growth from above the union is the rose's own. One nursery: a leftover rootstock stub above the union can also sucker.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve the findings as `ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline.

2. **Where does sucker handling go in Pip?**
   (a) a step inside the main framework observation (PKR-OBS-000006 / PKR-DEC-000006); (b) its own separate observation item, alongside the other "remove first" items (dead, damaged, crossing, inward, weak), with the framework's "new shoots from the base" row pointing to it; (c) a recognition note only, with removal sent to experienced help.
   *ROC recommendation: (b).* Spotting a sucker needs its own check (tracing where it starts) and its own "not sure" route, and removing it is a different job from a pruning cut.

3. **Should Pip guide beginners to remove suckers?**
   (a) yes, recognition and removal; (b) removal only when the gardener confirms it starts below a visible bud union, otherwise leave it and suggest help; (c) recognition only, removal always referred; (d) keep the current hold.
   *ROC recommendation: (b).* The start-point test and "remove where it starts" are both High, but other signs are weaker, confusion is common, and removing a real basal shoot is a loss.

4. **Which removal method does Pip give?**
   (a) tear or pull; (b) cut cleanly; (c) the agreed core (uncover the start point and remove it there, never at soil level), with tearing as the main method and cutting cleanly at the start point as the alternative; (d) no method, refer to help.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).*

5. **In the winter (dormant) pruning session, which signs does Pip use?**
   (a) start point only; leaf and flower signs only in the growing season, as supporting signs, never alone; (b) all signs at any time.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

6. **What if no bud union can be seen?**
   (a) Pip doesn't call anything a sucker, and suggests experienced help if the gardener is worried; (b) Pip asks the gardener to clear soil to look for the union first.
   *ROC recommendation: (a),* with (b) offered as an optional step.
