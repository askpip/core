# Decision Brief — Can Pruning Too Hard Kill or Harm a Bush Rose?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief. The decisions are on the 25 findings, on three further-research items, and on four questions about how Pip should answer, ending with one overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

- **Sources consistently say you won't kill an established rose by pruning it, even hard or badly** (**High**). The NZ Rose Society: "Absolutely not! Roses are very tough and very forgiving". Rose societies in South Australia, Portland and Orange County, a Chicago botanic garden, a NZ rose columnist and three rose nurseries say the same.
- **Some attach conditions:** the rose is otherwise healthy, it isn't cut below the ground line or graft, and its roots are intact (**Moderate**).
- **Ordinary winter pruning in rose sources removes about half to two-thirds** of a Hybrid Tea's or Floribunda's growth or height (**High**). That is more than the general "no more than a third" rule Pip already shows as an advisory, and two sources call a one-third cut "light" or "moderate" pruning.
- **The real risks sources name** are: pruning before frosts have passed (**High**); cutting below the graft or ground line (**Moderate**); pruning a weak plant as hard as a vigorous one (**Moderate**); disease entering through cuts and stubs (**Moderate**); and, from single sources, autumn pruning in cold climates, routine over-pruning reducing flowers, and white and yellow roses disliking hard pruning (**Low**).
- **"Hard", "moderate" and "light" mean different things in different sources** (**Moderate**).
- **Old or neglected roses can be rejuvenated by hard pruning** (**Moderate**). Some sources do it in one go; one says a third of the old canes a year. No rose source says how to choose (**Moderate**).
- **Any setback is described as temporary**; one source calls the first year "a recovery year" (**Moderate**). **No rose source gives signs or a waiting time for judging recovery** (**Low**).
- **One commercial website disagrees,** saying cutting back too far "could stress it out or even kill it" (**Very Low**).

## 3. What was researched

Q1: can hard pruning kill an established bush rose? Q2: what "hard", "moderate" and "light" mean. Q3: what risks sources name. Q4: renovating an old or neglected bush, in one go or over two to three years, compared with the one-third advisory. Q5: recovery signs, and what to do if a rose seems pruned too hard.

## 5. What the research found

### Can pruning kill a rose? (group 1)

| ID | Finding | Confidence |

| ID | Finding | Confidence |

|---|---|---|
| AF-21 | Old or neglected roses can be rejuvenated by hard pruning. | Moderate |

### Recovery, and getting help (group 5)

| ID | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| AF-23 | Aftercare named after hard renovation: feed and mulch, water, thin the new shoots later (general shrub guidance). | Low |
| AF-24 | No rose source gives signs or a waiting time for judging recovery. | Low |
| AF-25 | The NZ Rose Society points gardeners to a local rose society or pruning demonstration. | Low |

**Fit with what you've already approved.** The findings agree with the pruning-framework ARC that Hybrid Teas are pruned lower than Floribundas, that a weak or sickly bush is sometimes better replaced, and that height is commonly reduced by a third to a half (they extend this to two-thirds). They agree with the weak-growth ARC (caution with a weak or small plant) and the dormancy ARC (prune after the coldest weather). **They conflict in figure with the "no more than a third" advisory (`PKR-CGD-000003`),** which that advisory itself already describes as a cautious guide, not a limit. They partly conflict with the staged-renovation default, because several sources renovate in one go. Three sources say newly planted roses are pruned hard; that's outside this commission and is noted against the recently-planted ARC for possible future research.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Can hard pruning kill?** Nearly all sources: no. One commercial website: it could. Some add conditions.
- **C-2 Weak growth.** Clemson, Illinois, Rose Society of NSW, Victoria State Rose Garden: prune a weak plant lightly. RHS and Landscape Ontario: cut weak shoots back further. One set talks about the whole plant, the other about single shoots.
- **C-3 How much in one go, versus the one-third advisory.** Rose sources: about half to two-thirds at winter pruning. Approved advisory: no more than about a third (general woody-plant rule). The sources measure different things (growth, height, cane length).
- **C-4 Renovation.** Chicago Botanic Garden, Royal National Rose Society, Landscape Ontario: hard renovation in one go. UC Fresno: a third of the old canes a year, never all at once. RHS (general shrubs): one go for robust shrubs, staged over two or three years when the response is unknown.
- **C-5 When to make big cuts.** A NZ rose columnist: in autumn, to avoid silver leaf. Iowa State and New Mexico State: not in autumn, because of winter injury. Different climates and reasons.
- **C-6 What "hard" means.** From "three to five buds from the base" to "removing one-half to two-thirds of the height".
