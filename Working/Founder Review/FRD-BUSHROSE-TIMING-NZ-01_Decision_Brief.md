## 2. The short version

- **Main winter pruning in New Zealand is June to August, most often July and August** (**High**). The NZ Rose Society: "between late June and mid August depending on where you live".
- **Sources agree that the north prunes earlier and colder places later** (**High**). The NZ Rose Society: "Pruning starts up north around early July while in the colder parts of the South Island, it gets under-way in August."
- **No source draws a line between "north" and "south" or "mild" and "frosty"** (**Low**). They use loose words or name a town. That is why the band boundary is your decision.
- **NIWA (the national climate institute) says frosts are rare north of Waikato, commoner inland, and vary a lot over short distances** (**Moderate**). It doesn't say anything about roses.
- **The signals sources tie pruning to are full dormancy and the worst frosts having passed** (**High**). **No NZ source mentions bud swell** (**Low**), so Pip's bud-swell check and the months sit side by side; neither is in the sources as a replacement for the other.
- **Feeding starts in spring as new growth appears** (**High**), usually September. **The last feed is given anywhere from the end of February to early April**; sources disagree (**Low**).
- **Deadheading stops at the end of summer, end of February to end of March,** so hips can form (**Moderate**).
- **Bare-root roses arrive in June and are planted in winter** (**Moderate**); **potted roses can go in any time** (**High**); **established roses are moved in winter while dormant** (**Moderate**).
- **Leaves are cleared through autumn and winter** (**High**); **a winter clean-up spray goes on after pruning, in June or July** (**Moderate**).
- **No NZ source describes winter protection** (mounding soil over the base) or **a cut-off for summer pruning** (**Low**).
- **Most months for feeding, deadheading and moving come only from garden companies.** NZ non-commercial rose sources are thin.

## 3. What was researched

Q1: main pruning months by area, and the signal to watch for. Q2: when to start feeding and when to give the last feed. Q3: when to stop deadheading and summer pruning. Q4: when to plant bare-root and potted roses, and move an established one. Q5: winter clear-up, winter spraying and winter protection. Q6: how NZ sources divide the country. Q7: how a gardener could be placed in an area. Q8: what sources say about early or late seasons and edges between areas.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Which month nationally.** Some say June or July; others July and August. They overlap in July.
- **C-2 Colder earlier or later.** Nearly all: colder places prune later. One company (Daltons): cooler places can prune earlier, once the leaves have fallen.
- **C-3 When the north starts.** NZ Rose Society: early July. Tui, for Northland: end of July to early August.
- **C-4 Christchurch.** Tui calls it colder and says wait until after the last frost, even into early spring. The Canterbury Horticultural Society splits Canterbury into milder parts (end of July) and colder inland parts (August or later).
- **C-5 The last feed.** End of February, March, early April, or "before winter", depending on the source.
- **C-6 The first feed.** Late August or September for most; mid-late October for one; one says wait for the last frosts in frosty areas.
- **C-7 Moving a rose.** Most say winter while dormant; one Palmers page says autumn, and another Palmers page says July.
- **C-8 Planting in Auckland.** Most say winter; Auckland Botanic Gardens says autumn or winter.
- **C-9 Stopping deadheading.** End of February or end of March.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve the findings as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-NZ-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline.

2. **Where do the area boundaries fall for New Zealand?**
   (a) no automatic areas for now: everyone sees June to August, with "earlier in the north, later where it's colder or frosty"; (b) two areas, north and colder south, with a line you set (for example North Island and South Island); (c) three areas following the NZ Rose Society's flowering split; (d) wait and ask the NZ Rose Society first.
   *ROC recommendation: (a), with (d) if you want areas.* No source draws a line, and frost changes over short distances.

3. **How is a gardener placed in an area?**
   (a) from the town and region they already enter; (b) from GPS; (c) by asking them; (d) from the region they enter, then confirmed by asking whether their garden gets regular hard frosts.
   *ROC recommendation: (d).* Sources name towns but also say your own garden's frost matters.

4. **How does Pip show months alongside the bud-swell check?**
   (a) months shown as background ("roses here are usually pruned in July to August"), with the bud check still deciding whether to prune now; (b) months shown only after the bud check passes; (c) months only when asked.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).* Months never replace the bud check.

5. **What does Pip say near an area edge, or in an early or late season?**
   (a) show both areas' months; (b) show the wider window, plus "wait until the worst frosts have passed and the rose is dormant, and check the buds"; (c) suggest the local rose society.
   *ROC recommendation: (b), with (c) added.*

6. **How does Pip show months that come only from garden companies (feeding, deadheading cut-off, moving)?**
   (a) show them, labelled as from NZ garden companies; (b) show the approved patterns only, no NZ months; (c) wait for further research.
   *ROC recommendation: (a)*, in line with your decision that Pip should tell gardeners where its information comes from. Where sources disagree, Pip shows the range.
