## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Ask Pip gives season-level timing only, derived from hemisphere. No approved ARC gives a national pattern of US months; `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` carries month cut-offs for particular states (Illinois, Penn State, UGA Cobb County, Iowa State). US guidance is commonly organised by USDA Plant Hardiness Zone and by state extension service. Months are context only: they do not replace the bud-swell Suitability Gate in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** Main winter pruning months, and the local signal tied to them, in each climate band.
- **Q2** Months to start feeding, and for the last feed before winter, in each band.
- **Q3** When to stop deadheading and summer pruning, in each band.
- **Q4** Months to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band.
- **Q5** When to clear fallen leaves and prunings; when a winter spray is done, where described; when winter protection goes on and comes off.
- **Q6** How US sources tie timing to USDA zones, frost dates or state, and which zone bands are needed.
- **Q7** Whether gardeners can readily find their USDA zone, so that Ask Pip could ask for it.
- **Q8** How sources handle an early or late season, or a garden near a band edge.

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting and moving for Q4. United States, zones roughly 4 to 10. Product names, brands and rates are not collected. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

## 3. Research Plan

| Question | Information required | Source categories sought |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–Q5 | Month windows by task, for each area a source covers, with the area named | State extension services and Master Gardener programs, American Rose Society and local rose societies, public botanic gardens |
| Q6 | How sources organise timing (zone, state, county, district); the zone of each source's area | USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map; extension and state climatologist pages on the 2023 map |
| Q7 | Whether and how gardeners find their zone | USDA; extension pages on the 2023 map |
| Q8 | Signals, early or late seasons, edges of zones | All of the above |

**Method.**

- Web searches were run for each question and for each band, aimed at extension services, the American Rose Society, local rose societies and botanic gardens, and at pages stating the USDA zone of each source's area.
- Approved and pending US sources were re-opened first: from `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` (Illinois, Penn State, UGA Cobb County, Iowa State, Missouri G6601, UNH), from `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` (Colorado State, Missouri G6600, Arkansas, Nashville Rose Society), and from `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01` (Clemson, Oregon State, Portland Rose Society).
- Pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question; each extraction asked for exact sentences. For ZLESAK, OCRS, UF-SFYL, KSU-JO and UGA-COBB the quotations relied on were re-checked against a second extraction asking for the wording character for character. Where a page is a month-by-month calendar, the month is taken from the heading under which the quoted entry appears.
- Every quotation used is under 25 words and is recorded, by finding, in the Source Register.
- A source is placed in a band only where the zone of its area is stated by the source itself or by another opened page. Sources whose area's zone was not stated in any opened page are reported as "Not placed" and their months are not attached to any band.
- Findings are separated by task and band so each receives its own Evidence Confidence Level (EAS §3.2).

**Anticipated challenges.** US rose calendars are written for a state, county or district, not for a zone. Many are rose society or Master Gardener pages rather than formal extension publications. Several predate the 2023 zone map.

### 4.2 Source characteristics that limit independence or weight

- **No commercial source is relied on.** All relied-on sources are federal, university extension, state climatologist, rose society or botanic garden pages.
- **NRS-T, NRS-W, NRS-F, NRS-S, NRS-SP and NRS-MAR** are one rose society and count as one voice. NRS-MAR and NRS-SP describe one member's own practice in places.
- **ISU-RC, ISU-HT and ISU-ZONE** are one institution. **ILL-CARE and ILL-PR** are one institution. **PIMA-J and PIMA-F** are one guide series. **CLE-P and CLE-GR** are one institution and one author pair. **CSU-W and CSU-PL**, **MO-G6601 and MO-G6600**, and **UAEX-P and UAEX-T** are each one institution.
- **ZLESAK** names its region only as the "northern Midwest" and states no zone for it. It is placed in band A only because it is hosted by a Minnesota rose club and ARS-NCD states that Minnesota is in zones 3–5. That placement is the weakest in band A.
- **MRS-MN** is placed in band A on ARS-NCD's statement that Minnesota lies in zones 3–5; Minnesota's own zones were not stated in a Minnesota page opened (UMN Extension could not be opened, §4.3).
- **Band B rests on two statewide sources**, Illinois and Pennsylvania, each covering zones 5a to 7b. Their months are given for the whole state, not for a zone.
- **AZ-CZ's** Tucson zone (9b) comes from a table as returned by the extraction step, not a quoted sentence.
- **UGA-COBB** is a county guide written by a Master Gardener and consulting rosarian, not an extension specialist; product and brand names in it were not collected.
- **Several sources predate the 2023 zone map** (AZ-LD 2002; BEXAR 2003; CLE-P and CLE-GR 1999; UGA-COBB 2016; MRS-MN 2013). Their months are written for their area, not for a zone, so the map change does not alter what they say, but it may alter which band their area falls in.
- **BEXAR** describes a twice-yearly pruning (October and mid-February) common in Texas; only the mid-February main pruning is used for Q1.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** Every rose source in §4.1 addresses bush or modern rose care directly for a named US area. The zone sources (USDA, OSU-LP, ISU-ZONE, ISWS, UGA-ZONE, UGA-CLIM, UF-ZONE, AZ-CZ, UC-MARIN-Z, UC-PLACER) are not rose-specific; they are used only to place areas in zones and for Q6 and Q7.

**Source reliability.** USDA is the authority for zones. Thirty-one relied-on sources are university extension pages, several written by Master Gardeners. Rose societies (American Rose Society, Minnesota, Nashville, Marin, Orange County) are recognised practitioner authorities. Red Butte Garden is a university botanic garden.

**Consistency.** Sources agree on the direction of timing: colder areas prune, feed and plant later, and stop feeding and deadheading earlier. They agree on tying winter protection to hard freezes and its removal to the passing of severe frost. Within bands they differ on exact months, most visibly for the last feed and stopping deadheading in bands A and C, and for when mounding starts in band A.

**Independence.** Each band rests on few independent voices: band A on ARS-NCD, Minnesota Rose Society, Zlesak and Iowa State; band B on Illinois and Penn State; band C on the Nashville Rose Society and UGA Cobb County; band D on Arizona extension and the Marin Rose Society.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. No source gives months by zone, so every band is built from state, county or district sources. Twelve areas could not be placed in a band.

### How US sources organise timing, and placing the gardener (Q6, Q7, Q8)

**AF-1 (established). National.** USDA: "The map is based on the average annual extreme minimum winter temperature, displayed as 10-degree F zones and 5-degree F half zones." The current map is the 2023 map. OSU-LP: "These Zones are based on the average annual extreme minimum temperature". UC-PLACER describes the same basis.

**AF-2 (established). National.** A gardener can find their zone by ZIP code. USDA: "enter your zip code in the Quick Zip Code Search box in the map above". UGA-CLIM: the 2023 map "allows you to search for your location using the zip code or zooming in to your location."

**AF-3 (supported interpretation). National.** The 2023 map moved much of the country about half a zone warmer, and older zone information still circulates. UGA-CLIM: "more than half of the Southeast has shifted its category by half a zone towards a warmer climate." UGA-ZONE: Fulton, Cobb, Forsyth and Cherokee counties "have been reassigned to Planting Zones 8" (previously 7b), and "If you do an internet search to determine your planting zone, you will find two sources of information." ISU-ZONE: "Nearly all of Iowa is now in Zone 5".

**AF-4 (supported interpretation). National.** USDA zones measure winter cold only. OSU-LP: zones "do not take into account other aspects influencing plant survival and performance, such as snow cover, summer heat, humidity, soil moisture, spring frosts". AZ-CZ: "cold is only one extreme plants experience during the year". ISU-ZONE: "The maps are only guides."

**AF-5 (expert opinion, limited). California.** UC-PLACER: "A much more useful guide for California gardeners are the zones set out in the Sunset Western Garden book." UC-MARIN-Z gives both: "Marin County is situated in Sunset Zones 15, 16, and 17" and USDA zones 9b and 10a.

**AF-6 (supported interpretation). National.** Rose sources give months for their own state, county or rose society district, not for a USDA zone. Three name the zone of their area: ARS-NCD ("situated in USDA plant hardiness zones 3, 4, and 5"), PSU ("Pennsylvania's climate varies from USDA Plant Hardiness Zones 5a through 7b") and NRS-T ("In our zone #7"). For other areas the zone is stated by a separate page: ISU-ZONE (Iowa 5a–5b), ISWS (Illinois 5a–7b), UGA-ZONE (Cobb County 8), AZ-CZ (Phoenix metro 10), UC-MARIN-Z (Marin 9b and 10a), UF-ZONE ("In sunny Florida, we've have Zones 8 through 11."). **AF-7 (established). All bands.** Sources tie timing to local signals as well as, or instead of, months. Bud swell: CLE-P "when they begin to swell, go ahead and prune"; ZLESAK prunes "after new buds start to swell"; RBG prunes "after buds have started to swell". ## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority. Each states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. The zone of the twelve "Not placed" areas.**
South Carolina, Arkansas, Georgia statewide, the Willamette Valley, Florida (north, central, south), Contra Costa and Orange Counties, San Antonio, the Kansas City area, Missouri, Colorado and the Wasatch Front. Searches for pages stating their zones were tried; the USDA ZIP-code lookup could not be read through the tools used. Settling it needs either that lookup done by another means or the Founders' acceptance of a different placement method (a different evidentiary standard).
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission the placement, and by what method.
*Recommended default:* these months are shown only to gardeners in the same state (and the same part of the state where the source splits it), named with the source, and are not attached to any band.

**R-2. Band-B pruning and planting months.**
No band-B source opened gives a month for main pruning, bare-root or potted planting, or a winter spray. Further searching (for example Ohio, Indiana, New York, New Jersey, Kentucky extension calendars) could fill this, but is disproportionate within this commission given the bud-swell gate already governs pruning.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission it.
*Recommended default:* in band B, Pip shows "spring" with the forsythia and bud-swell signals and no month for pruning, and no month for planting.

**R-3. Sources that could not be opened.**
University of Minnesota Extension, UF/IFAS EP339 and CIR 344, the UC San Luis Obispo rose calendar, the Kansas City Rose Society and two rose society pages behind unencrypted redirects (§4.3). Opening them needs another means of access.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to have them opened by another means.
*Recommended default:* none is relied on.

**R-4. Which zone system and band boundaries to use.**
USDA zones measure winter cold only (AF-4), some California sources prefer Sunset zones (AF-5), and the band boundaries overlap (C-8). This turns on Founder judgement about how Pip places a gardener, not on further searching.
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Points 2 and 3.
*Recommended default:* see those decision points.

**R-5. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis, recorded as such and assigned Not Assigned (EAS §2.10).**
These are questions arising from the findings that the evidence does not itself state. They are not findings and are not proposed for adoption.

- R-5a. Whether Missouri's −10 °F bare-root threshold (MO-G6600) coincides with a USDA zone boundary, so it could mark a band edge for spring-only bare-root planting.
- R-5b. Whether the band-A months, written for Iowa, Minnesota and the North Central District, hold in other zone 3–5 areas such as Colorado, Nebraska or northern New England.
- R-5c. Whether Clemson's "six weeks before the expected first frost" rule (AF-26) reproduces the last-feed months other bands give (August in band B, September in band C).
- R-5d. Whether areas that moved half a zone warmer on the 2023 map have also moved their rose months, given that several sources predate the map.
- R-5e. Whether pruning months move by a roughly steady step from one zone to the next, given the December to mid-April span (AF-15).
- R-5f. Whether ZLESAK's "northern Midwest" lies wholly within zones 3–5 (§4.2).

*Founder decision needed:* whether any of these is worth commissioning.
*Recommended default:* none is acted on until independent evidence is found.

## 10. Founder Decision Points

The options are listed for each decision. The ROC recommendation is labelled as a recommendation; the decision is the Founders'.

1. **Approve the findings for an Approved Research Compilation (`ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-US-01`)?** Options: approve as stated; approve with amendments; defer pending further research; decline. Each finding carries its own Evidence Confidence Level in §1 and §8.

2. **Which zone bands does Pip use, and where are the boundaries?**
   - (a) Four bands as provisionally grouped in this dossier, with fixed boundaries: A zones 3–5, B zone 6, C zones 7–8, D zones 9–10 (Illinois and Pennsylvania statewide months used for zone 6).
   - (b) No bands: months shown only for the gardener's own state (or part of state) where a source exists; season and signals elsewhere.
   - (c) Both: the gardener's own-state months where a source exists; otherwise the months of their band, named with the source they come from; season and signals where neither exists.
   - (d) Defer pending R-1 and R-2.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (c). It keeps every month tied to the area its source wrote for (AF-6) while still covering gardens in states with no source. Band B would show no pruning month under any option (R-2).

   - (a) Ask the gardener for their USDA zone.

   - (d) Derive zone and state from the recorded location, and show them so the gardener can correct them.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (d). Gardeners can find their zone by ZIP (AF-2), but older zone information still circulates (AF-3), and state is needed for state-sourced months (option 2(c)).
