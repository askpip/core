## What Pip says about spraying

[[choice:s1|1. When a gardener asks "Should I spray my roses?", what does Pip say first? The ROC recommends the first answer.|Spraying is optional. Start with the basics (clear fallen leaves, pick off infected leaves, water at the base, give the rose sun and air). Consider a spray only if disease keeps coming back.;Spray regularly through the growing season to prevent disease.;Don't spray.;Give both views and no starting answer.|comment]]
[[more:s1m|What the research says|Growing roses well with little or no spraying, choosing disease-tolerant varieties, the basics, and keeping sprays as a last resort are all rated High or Very High. The need for regular spraying is rated Moderate, and sources disagree on it. The RHS recommends against using fungicides on roses. A US extension service, an American Rose Society article and the Rose Society of NSW say susceptible roses, which include many popular Hybrid Teas and Floribundas, need regular spraying to stay clean. The ROC's recommendation includes telling the gardener that sources disagree.]]
