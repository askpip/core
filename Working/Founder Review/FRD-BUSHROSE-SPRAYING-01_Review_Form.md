## How Pip handles this

The options are set out in Brief section 8. The ROC's recommendation is shown with each question.

[[choice:d2|2. What is Pip's starting answer to "Should I spray my roses?" ROC recommends (a), with the disagreement disclosed.|(a) Spraying is optional, basics first;(b) Regular preventive spraying;(c) Don't spray;(d) Both views, no starting answer|comment]]
[[more:d2m|The options|(a) Spraying is optional. Start with the measures sources agree on (clearing fallen leaves, removing infected leaves, watering at the base, sun and air, a healthy rose); consider a spray only if disease keeps coming back, and then only a product labelled for roses, used as its label says. (b) Recommend regular preventive spraying through the growing season. (c) Recommend not spraying (the RHS position). (d) Present both views without a starting answer. ROC reason: AF-1, AF-2, AF-6, AF-14, AF-15, AF-17 and AF-19 are High or Very High; the need for regular spraying (AF-4) is Moderate and contested (C-1, C-2).]]

[[choice:d3|3. Should Pip name any spray products at all? ROC recommends (a) now, with (b) reconsidered once R-1 is settled.|(a) No, only "a spray labelled for roses";(b) General types, no brands or rates;(c) Specific products|comment]]
[[more:d3m|The options|(a) No. Pip refers only to "a spray labelled for roses" (fungicide or insecticide as appropriate), never brands, active ingredients, rates or schedules. (b) Pip names general product types as sources describe them (for example lime sulphur, copper, horticultural oil, insecticidal soap), never brands or rates, always with "follow the label and local rules". (c) Pip names specific products. ROC reason: approvals differ by country and change (AF-34), the label governs rates and frequency (AF-30), and the current approval status of the commonly named winter-spray types was not established for all four markets. Note: If you choose (b) or (c), AF-28 would need allowing too. It is Low: one rose society says lime sulphur and copper are kept two weeks apart.]]

[[choice:d4|4. What does Pip say about winter clean-up sprays after pruning? ROC recommends (a) for NZ and Australian gardeners and (b) for UK and US gardeners.|(a) Optional practice common in NZ and Australia;(b) Nothing, only the winter clear-up;(c) Recommend it as standard|comment]]
[[more:d4m|The options|(a) Describe it as an optional practice that New Zealand and Australian rose growers commonly use, done after clearing leaves and prunings, with the label and pollinator precautions. (b) Say nothing about winter sprays; give only the winter clean-up of leaves and prunings. (c) Recommend a winter spray as a standard step. ROC reason: winter clean-up of leaves is Very High (AF-14); the spray itself is Moderate and regional (AF-25, AF-29). The split by region holds until R-1 and R-2 are settled.]]

[[choice:d5|5. How does Pip describe when and how often to spray, if a gardener chooses to? ROC recommends (a).|(a) General terms, label interval;(b) A fixed interval;(c) No timing guidance|comment]]
[[more:d5m|The options|(a) In general terms only: sprays protect leaves rather than cure them, so they are started early and repeated, more often in wet weather, at the interval the label gives; alternate products; don't spray open flowers, foraging bees or in heat. (b) Give a fixed interval (for example every one to two weeks). (c) Give no timing guidance. ROC reason: AF-7, AF-11, AF-12, AF-30 and AF-32 are High or Very High; the specific interval is Moderate and differs by product (AF-9, C-7).]]

[[choice:d6|6. How does Pip handle aphids? ROC recommends (a).|(a) Tolerate, squash or hose, natural enemies first;(b) Treat like leaf diseases|comment]]
[[more:d6m|The options|(a) Tolerate small numbers, squash or hose them off, and let natural enemies work; soaps or oils only if numbers stay high; no broad-spectrum insecticides. (b) Treat aphids the same as leaf diseases. ROC reason: AF-22 and AF-24 (High) and AF-23 (Moderate).]]
