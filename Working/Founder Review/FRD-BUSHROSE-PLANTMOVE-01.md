## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Approved knowledge covers what follows planting (`ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`, pruning restraint; `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F2, closer watering in roughly the first two years) and records that bud-union depth varies (`ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` AF-12). No approved knowledge covers planting a new bush rose or moving an established one.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** When are bare-root and potted bush roses planted, including the winter bare-root season in New Zealand and Australia and autumn/spring in the UK and US?
- **Q2** What site basics do sources give: sun, drainage, spacing, and soil where roses have grown before?
- **Q3** Soaking bare roots, the planting hole, planting and bud-union depth by climate, and watering in.
- **Q4** Moving an established rose: timing, root ball, pruning before or after, and aftercare.
- **Q5** Age limits or success odds for moving a rose.

**Scope.** Hybrid Tea and Floribunda bush roses, Grandiflora provisionally. New Zealand, Australian, UK and US sources; commercial sources labelled. Every online source recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** All sources in §4.1 address rose planting, rose replant disease or moving roses directly, except RHS-MOVE (trees and shrubs, naming *Rosa*), ISU (shrubs and roses together) and parts of UAEX (plants generally).

**Source reliability.** The RHS, five US university extension services, a university library, the American Rose Society, three rose societies in Australia and the US, a New Zealand botanic garden and a public broadcaster are recognised authorities. Nurseries and garden companies are practised growers and sellers, given less weight as independent authority.

**Consistency.** Sources agree strongly on planting bare-root roses while dormant, a sunny and well-drained site, soaking bare roots, watering in, moving while dormant, a large root ball, and watering after a move. They disagree on bud-union depth, soaking time, autumn versus spring planting in cold climates, replant remedies, and pruning at planting and before moving.

**Independence.** Commercial pages may draw on common trade knowledge. Pages from the same organisation or author are counted once (§4.2).

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. Evidence on moving comes mainly from nurseries, garden companies, rosarians and short extension answers; no trial evidence was found. New Zealand non-commercial evidence is thin.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1 — Bud-union depth (AF-20, AF-21, AF-22).** Above the soil: YAT-NZ "5cm above", PALM-G "2-5cm above", RSNSW-CAL "3-5cm above", GA-2014P "just above", OSU-AE one inch above in mild Eugene. At soil level: RHS-PL ("not below as this is reported to increase the risk of rose dieback"), RSSA, AMORE ("up to the crown"). Below: CSU and ISU 2 to 4 inches, MU slightly below to 2 inches, DA-BR 5 to 8 cm, OSU-AE two inches in cold Central Oregon. The difference follows climate in the sources that give a reason (OSU-AE, MU). Unresolved.

**C-2 — Soaking time (AF-18).** One hour or more (RSSA), one to two hours (ISU), a couple of hours (DA-BR), at least two hours (RSNSW-CAL), several hours (GA-MAG, MU), overnight (CSU, MU), 24 hours (RSNSW-G, AMORE). CSU and MU set 24 hours as the maximum, MU stating longer "may damage the root system". Unresolved.

**C-3 — Autumn or spring for bare-root roses (AF-1, AF-3).** RHS-PL: late autumn at leaf fall, or late winter to early spring. CSU: "spring planting is preferred over fall"; MU prefers spring where winters are colder; ISU plants bare-root shrubs in spring. The difference reflects winter severity. Unresolved.

**C-4 — Replant disease: how real, and what to do (AF-11, AF-13).** RHS-RD calls it "recognised but poorly understood"; FG-RRD reports "mixed feelings" about whether it exists and says replacing soil is "the old method". Remedies: replace soil 45 cm deep and wide (RHS-PL), about 60 cm across and at least 30 cm deep (RHS-RD), or the old roots plus two extra buckets of soil (RSSA); plant elsewhere (RHS-RD); crop rotation (ARS-RRD, a nursery practice); compost and mycorrhizal fungi instead of soil replacement (FG-RRD). Unresolved.

**C-5 — Pruning before moving (AF-29).** Cut back by a third (KINGS), by half (NRS-T), to at least half in the growing season (TRE-T), hard to 3 to 5 buds (TUI-Q), hard (TUI-T), to three or four canes (UW-PAL), to 10 to 12 inches (HEIR), some top growth (DA-MOVE). AE-T (summer move): "There's no need to prune them back first". RHS-MOVE (shrubs generally): "do not prune hard". Unresolved.

**C-6 — Pruning at planting (AF-25).** GA-2019 cuts top growth by a third to a half and roots by a third; CSU leaves about 5 to 12 inches of cane; MU says packaged plants "rarely need additional pruning"; RHS-PL prunes "in the first winter after planting". Unresolved.

**C-7 — How hard it is to move a rose (AF-34, AF-35).** RHS-MOVE names *Rosa* among plants "resenting root disturbance" that "can be tricky to move"; TRE-T: "The older the rose, the more likely that it will not appreciate being moved". DA-MOVE: "Moving a rose is quite forgiving"; NRS-T reports moving over 100 roses "with great success". Unresolved.

**C-8 — Why roses are spaced (AF-10).** GA-MAG spaces roses for "good air circulation", especially in humid climates; FG-SP questions "that magic air circulation argument". Unresolved.

**C-9 — Timing within the dormant season (AF-26, AF-28).** UAEX: February, "the same time we prune them"; PALM-FAQ: after pruning in July, once frost risk has passed; NRS-T: late winter to early spring; UW-PAL: early spring or late fall; RHS-MOVE: any time late October to mid-March (UK). All are within or at the edges of dormancy. Unresolved.

**Uncertainty preserved.** Success odds and a rose-specific age limit for moving are not answered by the evidence (§4.3, AF-37).

## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim.

### Planting time (Q1)

**AF-1 (established).** Bare-root roses are planted while dormant. RHS-PL: "Plant in late autumn at leaf fall, and from late winter to early spring, before growth resumes." DA-BR: "during dormancy, from late autumn to early spring." MU: "planted in a dormant condition if purchased bare rooted". NZRS-FAQ: "Winter is the best time to plant as it allows the plant to establish before summer arrives." RSNSW-G: bare-rooted plants "are only available in winter." Also YAT-NZ, KINGS, PALM-G, RSSA, GA-2014P, GA-MAG.

**AF-2 (supported interpretation).** In Australia, GA-MAG: "May, June and July are the months to plant bare-rooted roses in Australia." RSSA: "best done in June or July"; RSNSW-CAL prepares soil "for planting in June/July". In New Zealand, KINGS: "New seasons Roses arrive in store from June", and roses "can be planted right through spring"; PALM-G: "from winter through to spring"; NZRS-FAQ and YAT-NZ say winter without naming months.

**AF-3 (supported interpretation).** Cold-winter US sources prefer spring for bare-root roses. CSU: "spring planting is preferred over fall." MU: where winters are colder, "spring planting is preferred". ISU (Iowa): bare-root shrubs "must be planted in spring before growth begins." The RHS gives late autumn or late winter to early spring (C-3).

**AF-4 (established).** Container (potted) roses can be planted through most of the year, with conditions. RHS-PL: "Plant all year round, provided the ground is neither frozen, nor very dry." MU: "throughout the growing season". CSU: "any time after the last killing frost." YAT-NZ and PALM-G: throughout the year. NZRS-FAQ: "If planting potted roses during spring and summer, keep them well watered."

**AF-5 (established).** Bare-root roses are planted promptly, and the roots are not allowed to dry out. YAT-NZ: "they need to go into the ground promptly." GA-2019: "planted almost immediately after purchasing." GA-2014P: "it's vital not to let the roots dry out before planting". Where planting is delayed, DA-BR keeps them "in a cool, dry place, protected from frost, for up to three weeks", and ISU stores them at "around 40°F". NZRS-FAQ: shrivelled or wrinkled canes are "a sign it has dried out."

### Site (Q2)

**AF-6 (established).** Roses need a sunny site; stated minimums are four hours (DA-BR), four to six (GA-MAG), five (PALM-G, "Five hours of sunlight is adequate"), five to six (CSU, RSSA) and six (MU, AMORE). ABG: roses "do best in full sun"; KINGS and YAT-NZ say full sun or a sunny spot.

**AF-7 (established).** Roses need well-drained soil, with raised beds or mounds where soil is heavy or drains poorly. ABG: "have good drainage." RSSA: "Soil must be well drained". MU gives a test: water "should drain from the hole in 90 to 120 minutes." PALM-G: "try raised beds (at least 30cm above ground level)"; GA-MAG and KINGS: raised bed or mound in heavy soil. Also CSU, YAT-NZ.

**AF-8 (supported interpretation).** Avoid competition from the roots of trees and shrubs. CSU, MU ("The roots of most roses don't compete well with those of trees and shrubs planted close by"), RSSA.

**AF-9 (supported interpretation).** Spacing depends on the rose's mature size. RHS-PL: "Spacing depends on type and habit. Check catalogue or label details." MU: hybrid teas "2-1/2 to 3 feet apart" (about 75 to 90 cm). AMORE: "60-100cm from other roses". FG-SP: "20 inches to 30 inches apart" (about 50 to 75 cm) for garden roses generally.

**AF-10 (expert opinion, conflicting).** GA-MAG: leave space "so there's good air circulation", especially in humid climates. FG-SP questions "that magic air circulation argument" for disease-resistant roses (C-8).

**AF-11 (supported interpretation).** New roses planted where roses grew before may establish and grow poorly. RHS-RD: "New plantings may struggle to establish, make poor growth and may even die". RHS-FAQ: "replant disorder or rose sickness". ARS-RRD: they "may not grow as well". RSSA: "The new rose will not tolerate any old rose roots remaining in its root zone." RHS-PL and DA-MOVE give precautions. FG-RRD reports mixed views on whether it exists (C-4).

**AF-12 (supported interpretation).** The cause is not established. RHS-RD: "recognised but poorly understood". ARS-RRD: "Nothing specific has been identified as the cause", listing suspected factors (nutrients, soil structure, nematodes, toxins, microbes).

**AF-13 (supported interpretation).** Sources give different remedies: replace soil (RHS-PL 45 cm deep and wide; RHS-RD about 60 cm across and at least 30 cm deep; RSSA old roots plus "an extra 2 buckets of soil"; DA-MOVE fresh soil); plant in another site (RHS-RD); rotate with other plants (ARS-RRD, of German nurseries); or compost and mycorrhizal fungi instead of replacing soil (FG-RRD) (C-4).

**AF-14 (expert opinion, single source).** RHS-RD: "The problem can persist for at least nine years following removal of the first planting."

### Planting method (Q3)

**AF-15 (supported interpretation).** The hole is wider than the roots; sizes vary. RHS-PL: "roughly twice the width of the plant's roots and the depth of a spade's blade." ISU: two to two-and-a-half times the root spread. CSU: 18 to 24 inches across, 14 to 18 deep. MU: at least 12 to 16 inches deep and 18 to 20 wide. RSSA: "30cm square by a spade depth." PALM-G: twice the depth and width of the root ball.

**AF-16 (supported interpretation).** Bare roots are spread over a mound of soil in the hole. GA-2014P: "a mound of earth in the centre of the hole, to encourage the roots to grow down over the mound." RSNSW-CAL, RSSA, GA-2019, PALM-G ("a mound 5-7cm high"), and UW-PAL when replanting a moved rose.

**AF-17 (established).** Bare roots are soaked in water before planting. RSNSW-G, RSNSW-CAL, RSSA, GA-2014P, GA-MAG, YAT-NZ ("to hydrate them and remove traces of soil"), DA-BR, ISU, MU, AMORE. CSU soaks "If bare-root plants appear dry". RHS-PL does not mention soaking.

**AF-18 (supported interpretation).** Soaking times differ, from one hour (RSSA) to 24 hours (RSNSW-G, AMORE); CSU and MU set 24 hours as the limit, MU: "Soaking for more than 24 hours may damage the root system." (C-2).

**AF-19 (supported interpretation).** Damaged or broken roots are trimmed. ISU: "prune off damaged or broken roots". GA-MAG: "Trim damaged roots". NRS-T, when moving: "Prune any broken roots". GA-2019 goes further: "Prune root system back by around one third."

**AF-20 (established).** Recommended bud-union depth differs: above, at or below the soil (C-1). This agrees with `ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` AF-12.

**AF-21 (supported interpretation).** New Zealand and Australian sources that state a depth put the union at or above soil level: YAT-NZ ("5cm above"), PALM-G ("2-5cm above"), RSNSW-CAL ("3-5cm above"), GA-2014P ("just above"), GA-2019 ("soil level is no higher than the graft"), RSSA ("at soil level"), AMORE ("up to the crown"). TUI-T, when moving: "ensure the graft is above the soil". No NZ or Australian source located recommends burying it.

**AF-22 (supported interpretation).** Burying the union is linked to cold winters. OSU-AE: where winter temperatures "predictably drop below 20 degrees" (Fahrenheit), plant 1 to 2 inches below; "The reasons for planting deeper include avoidance of cold damage and stabilizing against wind rock." MU: slightly below in southeast Missouri, 2 inches below in the colder north. CSU and ISU (Colorado, Iowa) bury 2 to 4 inches. RHS-PL: at soil level, "not below as this is reported to increase the risk of rose dieback."

**AF-23 (supported interpretation).** A potted rose goes in at the depth it grew. ABG: "Always plant at the same depth as they are in their container." GA-MAG: "Ensure the soil mark on the stem aligns with the level of the garden bed." KINGS: roots' top "level with the surrounding ground".

**AF-24 (established).** A newly planted rose is watered in thoroughly. CSU: "Water deeply at planting." MU: "A minimum of one gallon of water". DA-BR: "Water deeply after planting so the soil settles around the roots." RSSA and RSNSW-CAL fill the half-backfilled hole with water before finishing. Also ISU, YAT-NZ, GA-2014P, KINGS, PALM-G.

**AF-25 (supported interpretation).** Sources differ on pruning at planting: cut top growth by a third to a half (GA-2019); leave 5 to 12 inches of cane (CSU); packaged plants "rarely need additional pruning" (MU); "Prune back in the first winter after planting" (RHS-PL) (C-6).

### Moving an established rose (Q4)

**AF-26 (established).** The best time to move a rose is while it is dormant. TRE-T: "The best time to move a rose is in the winter dormancy when the plant is asleep". KINGS: "best done when dormant during Winter." TUI-Q: "winter when it is dormant". DA-MOVE: "wait until your rose is fully dormant" (November to February in the UK). RHS-MOVE (shrubs): "late October to mid-March". UAEX: the dormant season, November through February (Arkansas). Also HEIR, NRS-T, UW-PAL, TUI-T, PALM-FAQ.

**AF-27 (supported interpretation).** Some sources describe moving outside dormancy, with extra pruning or water. TRE-T (growing season): "Prune to at least half of its size, the balance of roots to growth will be important". AE-T (June): "You can still transplant the roses now", with extra water through summer. UAEX: "With proper care--mainly watering, you can successfully move most plants year-round." HEIR: "roses are adaptable to being moved".

**AF-28 (expert opinion, limited).** Within the dormant season, UAEX: "February, the same time we prune them." PALM-FAQ: "after pruning in July", once frost risk "has passed". NRS-T: "Late winter to early spring" (C-9).

**AF-29 (supported interpretation).** Most sources cut back top growth before moving, by differing amounts: a third (KINGS), half (NRS-T, "to compensate for root loss"), hard to 3 to 5 buds (TUI-Q), three or four canes left (UW-PAL), 10 to 12 inches (HEIR), some (DA-MOVE). AE-T: "There's no need to prune them back first"; RHS-MOVE (shrubs): "do not prune hard" (C-5).

**AF-30 (established).** Dig as large a root ball as practicable. KINGS: "dig out as large a root ball as you can manage." TRE-T: "(the larger the better)". HEIR: "Take as many of the roots with you as possible." AE-T: "a good-sized root ball". Also DA-MOVE, NRS-T, UW-PAL, TUI-Q.

**AF-31 (supported interpretation).** Stated digging distances: "at least a spade's width from the base" (DA-MOVE); 25 to 30 cm from the base to about a spade's depth (TUI-Q); one foot (about 30 cm) from the crown (UW-PAL); a circle "about 45cm in diameter" (TRE-T); 18 to 24 inches (about 45 to 60 cm) (NRS-T, not stated whether radius or width).

**AF-32 (expert opinion, limited).** TRE-T: "It is very important to keep the roots moist until planting into the new location." NRS-T: "The day before you transplant your rose bushes, water the soil around the rose bush very well."

**AF-33 (established).** A moved rose is watered well and kept watered until it recovers. KINGS: "keep your plant well watered until it recovers." DA-MOVE: for "its first year in the new position", treat it "much as you would a newly planted rose. Water regularly". HEIR: "every day for a week or two depending on your weather." Also TRE-T, NRS-T, UAEX, AE-T, PALM-FAQ, TUI-T, TUI-Q, RHS-MOVE.

### Limits (Q5)

**AF-34 (expert opinion, limited).** Older roses are described as harder to move. TRE-T: "If your plant has been settled for many years it does become tricky." RHS-MOVE (shrubs generally): plants growing in place "more than five years are much less likely to survive transplanting", and *Rosa* is among plants that "can be tricky to move". No source gives a rose-specific age limit.

**AF-35 (expert opinion, limited).** DA-MOVE: "Moving a rose is quite forgiving". NRS-T: "I have moved over 100 rose bushes with great success." (C-7).

**AF-36 (expert opinion, single source).** DA-MOVE: if the new position "has previously been home to another rose", replace the old soil with fresh soil before planting.

**AF-37 (unresolved question).** No source consulted gives success rates or odds for moving a rose (§4.3).
