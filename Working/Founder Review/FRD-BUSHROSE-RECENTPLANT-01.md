## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

This commission was opened while reviewing the "add a plant" onboarding flow against `MVP/Architecture/Ask_Pip_MVP_Bush_Rose_V1_Architecture.md` §5.4, which lists **recent planting** as one of nine Suitability and Safety Areas the journey must address before pruning guidance proceeds — an area not yet researched anywhere in this project, despite having been flagged and deferred three times previously (FRD-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01 §9; ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01 v1.1 Decision Point 3; Project_Backlog.md).

Two questions were commissioned together, because both feed the same Suitability Gate decision and were raised together by the Founder:

**Q1 (primary):** What pruning restraint, if any, should apply when an otherwise-supported bush rose was recently planted? Specifically, how long after planting should the MVP's six supported dormant-pruning observations (dead versus living wood; damaged growth; crossing or rubbing stems; inward-growing stems; weak or congested growth; the main framework to retain) be deferred?

**Q2 (secondary):** Where the gardener does not know when their rose was planted, is there an evidence-based method — practical for a beginner gardener working from photographs, consistent with this MVP's assisted-photo-interpretation approach (Architecture §4.1) — for estimating a rose's approximate age or degree of establishment?

**Scope boundary:** This commission is bounded to what the "recent planting" Suitability Gate needs to decide proceed-or-defer. It does not extend to cultivar or variety identification (already deferred, Architecture §5.5), nor to general rose-growth-stage botany unconnected to that gate decision.

## 3. Research Plan

Live web search and source evaluation, per ROC Operations Manual Chapter 5, in two passes matching Q1 and Q2:

- **Q1:** Searched for extension, recognised-horticultural-publication, and rosarian guidance specifically addressing pruning timing relative to planting date — how soon after planting normal pruning may begin, and whether a newly planted rose should be treated differently from an established one. Sources already used elsewhere in this project (RHS, American Rose Society, several state extension services) were re-checked for content on this specific question, since a source cited for one finding is not assumed to be silent on another.
- **Q2:** Searched for rose-specific and general woody-ornamental-shrub guidance on estimating plant age from visible or photographable characteristics, without relying on planting records — including cane thickness, bark appearance, basal-break count, bud-union condition, and any documented ring- or growth-based ageing method.

## 4. Evidence Collected — Source Log

### Q1 — Pruning restraint for recently planted roses

1. **Cornell Cooperative Extension, Nassau County**, "Rose Culture and Pruning" (D-2-13 MG/DWM, reviewed RT 11/01), https://ccenassau.org/resources/-rose-culture-and-pruning — states plainly that in the first season after planting, only spent blooms should be removed, preserving stems and leaves to nourish the plant. Directly on point for Q1.
2. **Johnson County K-State Research and Extension, Master Gardeners**, "Rose Care," https://www.johnson.k-state.edu/programs/lawn-garden/agent-articles-fact-sheets-and-more/agent-articles/emg-fact-sheets/roses-docs/Rose%20Care.pdf — describes an initial cutback performed at planting time (3–5 healthy canes to 8–12 inches), then transitions directly to a section titled "ESTABLISHED ROSES" for the ongoing spring-pruning cycle, with no guidance bridging the two. Indirectly supports treating the first period after planting as distinct from the established-rose cycle, without stating a duration.
3. **RHS**, "Roses: Planting," https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/roses/planting — states normal pruning should begin "in the first winter after planting," in late winter or early spring.
4. **Fine Gardening** (Paul Zimmerman), "When to Prune a Young Rose," https://www.finegardening.com/project-guides/pruning/how-soon-can-you-prune-a-young-rose — a rose planted the previous year should not be pruned at all; the original nursery canes will typically be gone "by the third year or so" as the plant replaces them naturally; a light trim is acceptable only if a plant grows unusually fast and risks wind damage.

**Sources checked and found not to bear on Q1** (re-checked despite prior use elsewhere in this project): RHS "Rose pruning: general tips"; American Rose Society "Basic Pruning Principles"; Colorado State University Extension (Pueblo County), "Maintaining Rose Bushes"; Arkansas Cooperative Extension, "Pruning" and "Transplanting"; Ask Extension FAQ #929174 and #827989; UC ANR "Tips for Confident Rose Pruning"; Illinois Extension "Pruning"; Chicago Botanic Garden "Pruning Mature Roses?" — none of these address pruning timing relative to planting date.

### Q2 — Estimating age when planting date is unknown

5. Search results for rose-specific age estimation returned no extension, university or recognised horticultural source describing a photo-observable method. Results were dominated by unrelated content (identifying a *dead* rose bush, rather than an *old* one; nursery/collector pages about historically famous long-lived roses).
6. **ResearchGate**, "Growth Rings in Herbs and Shrubs: Life Span, Age Determination and Stem Anatomy" (abstract only; full text not accessible) — the one academic source located that squarely addresses age determination in shrubs generally. Ring-based ageing methods in the wider dendrochronology literature (checked via conifers.org and Duke Gardens' "Determining a Tree's Age") uniformly require either a cross-sectioned stem or specialised increment coring — both destructive or equipment-dependent, and neither compatible with a photograph of a living, intact garden plant.
7. **Chicago Botanic Garden**, "Pruning Mature Roses?", https://www.chicagobotanic.org/plant-information/smart-gardener/pruning-mature-roses — distinguishes "old and thick" canes from "young, new shoots," but only as a *within-plant* comparison (which of this rose's own canes are older), not a whole-plant age estimate. This is the same distinction the MVP's existing framework-selection observation already makes; it does not answer how old the plant itself is.

No source located during this search described counting basal breaks, bud-union size or condition, or overall plant size as a validated method for estimating a bush rose's age. Sources on bud unions and rootstock (Ohio State PlantFacts "Bud union"; Heirloom Roses "About Rootstock") describe grafting mechanics, not age indicators.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

Evaluated against the Evidence Assessment Factors (EAS Chapter 4):

**Relevance:** Sources 1–4 are directly relevant to Q1 — each addresses pruning timing in relation to planting, not pruning in general. Sources 5–7 are relevant to Q2 only in establishing that no directly relevant, applicable method exists; this is itself the relevant finding.

**Source Reliability:** Sources 1 and 2 are extension-service publications, the tier this project has treated as most reliable throughout. Source 3 (RHS) is a recognised horticultural publication, the same tier as sources already relied on for AF-1/AF-2/AF-7 of the dead-wood commission. Source 4 (Fine Gardening) is a commercial gardening publication by a named, experienced rosarian and author, not an institutional or extension source — lower reliability than 1–3, consistent with how this project has weighted non-institutional sources previously.

**Consistency:** Sources 1 and 2 agree with each other (first-period restraint, no exact duration stated). Sources 3 and 4 disagree substantially on when normal pruning may resume — RHS's "first winter" versus Zimmerman's "third year or so" — see §6.

**Independence:** All four Q1 sources are independent publications from different organisations; none appears to repeat another's original material.

**Limitations:** The evidence base for Q1 is thin — four sources total, two of which (1, 2) support only the narrower, better-supported claim (first-season restraint), and two of which (3, 4) conflict on the broader question of total duration. For Q2, the limitation is more fundamental: the search did not locate any evidence base of the kind this question needs at all.

**Uncertainty:** Material uncertainty remains on exactly how long "recently planted" status should restrict pruning beyond the first growing season. This uncertainty is preserved in §6 and carried into the Founder Decision Points rather than resolved by assessor judgement.

**Overall Evaluation:** AF-1 (first-season restraint) is well enough supported by two independent, reasonably reliable sources, consistent with general horticultural practice for newly planted woody ornamentals, to warrant Moderate confidence. The broader duration question does not meet the bar for an Assessed Finding at any confidence level — the evidence conflicts too directly and too thinly to support one figure over another. Q2 is assessed as an absence-of-evidence outcome, not a claim requiring a confidence level.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

Two distinct uncertainties were encountered and are preserved here rather than resolved:

**Conflicting evidence on total restraint duration (Q1).** RHS states normal pruning should begin in the rose's first winter after planting. Fine Gardening's Zimmerman states a newly planted rose should not be pruned at all until its original canes are naturally replaced, "by the third year or so." These may not be strictly contradictory — they could describe different things (RHS's "first winter" pruning may itself be a lighter, establishment-focused cutback rather than the MVP's full dormant-pruning observation set; Zimmerman's "no pruning" may refer specifically to not disturbing the original nursery canes rather than a blanket prohibition on any cut). This possible reconciliation is plausible but is the assessor's own reasoned synthesis, not something either source states — per EAS §2.10, it is recorded here as a research lead, not adopted as a finding, and carried to §9 as a Recommendation for Further Research rather than resolved by inference.

**Sparse, then absent, evidence on age estimation (Q2).** The evidence base for "how long is recently planted" was thin (four sources). The evidence base for "how do you tell without a known date" was effectively empty — extensive, ring-based age-determination methods exist in the broader dendrochronology literature, but require destructive sampling or specialised tools incompatible with a beginner gardener's photograph of a living plant. No rose-specific or general-shrub method suited to this MVP's approach was found. This absence is itself preserved as the finding, per §8.2, rather than papered over with a speculative method drawn from adjacent (tree-focused) literature that was never validated for roses or for non-destructive observation.

## 7. Principal Findings

**Established finding:**

- **AF-1:** A newly planted bush rose's first growing season after planting should be limited to removing spent blooms (deadheading); the MVP's six supported dormant-pruning observations should not be applied during this period.

**Unresolved question (not an Assessed Finding):**

- How long the "recently planted" restriction should continue beyond the first growing season is not established by the evidence gathered. RHS suggests normal pruning by the first winter; Zimmerman suggests roughly three years. No single duration is supported strongly enough to adopt.

**Absence-of-evidence outcome (not an Assessed Finding):**

- No evidence-based, photo-appropriate method for estimating a bush rose's age when planting date is unknown was located. Existing ageing methods for woody plants require destructive sampling or specialised tools, and none was found validated for roses specifically or for non-destructive, photograph-based use.

## 8. Evidence Assessment

### 8.1 AF-1 — First-Season Restraint

**Evidence Confidence Level: Moderate.**

Supported directly by one extension source stating the rule plainly (Cornell Cooperative Extension, Nassau County) and indirectly by a second extension source's structural treatment of newly planted versus established roses as separate categories (Johnson County K-State Extension). Consistent with the general horticultural principle — already implicit elsewhere in this project's approved content — of not stressing a plant still establishing its root system. Not raised to High confidence because the evidence base is only two extension sources, one of them indirect, and no rose-specific research (as opposed to extension guidance) was located to corroborate it.

### 8.2 Q2 — Age Estimation

**Evidence Confidence Level: Not Applicable.**

This is not a positive claim requiring a confidence rating. It is the outcome of a genuine search: no evidence-based, non-destructive, photo-appropriate method for estimating a bush rose's age was located. Per EAS §2.9/§3.2, confidence is assigned to a claim the evidence supports; there is no such claim here to rate. Recording "Not Applicable" here follows the same logic already established for Source and Definition PKRs (PKR Standard §5.5/§5.6) — some record types, and some research outcomes, simply do not carry an evidentiary confidence level, and forcing one would misrepresent what was actually found.

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

- **Reconciling the RHS/Zimmerman disagreement (§6):** targeted research specifically on whether "first winter" (RHS) and "third year" (Zimmerman) guidance describe the same kind of pruning or two different things (a lighter establishment cutback versus the MVP's full observation set) would resolve what is currently the largest open uncertainty in this dossier, and would let a future revision replace the Founder Decision Point below with an actual Assessed Finding.
- **A structural idea for the gate question itself — ROC's own reasoned synthesis, not evidence, per EAS §2.10:** rather than asking the gardener to count years since planting (which requires a known date this whole commission exists partly because gardeners may not have), the gate could instead ask a state-based question — for example, whether the rose has completed at least one full growing season and gone dormant and returned to active growth at least once since planting. This would parallel the pattern already approved for PKR-SGT-000001 (asking about bud state rather than a calendar date, because location makes a fixed date unreliable) and could sidestep the years-versus-winters disagreement entirely by asking about the plant's observable history rather than the calendar. This is flagged as a lead for further research and design discussion, not adopted as a finding or built into any PKR, per the same standing policy that routed ROC's earlier "bud swell in mild climates" observation to further research rather than direct adoption.
- **Age estimation (Q2):** if the Founders still want a way to handle "planting date unknown" beyond simply treating it as its own defined gate answer (see Decision Point 3 below), further research would need to look specifically at rose-focused nursery, arboriculture or plant-science literature on non-destructive age indicators — cane basal-diameter growth curves, for example — which this search did not locate but did not exhaustively rule out either. This should not be assumed to be readily available; the broader ageing literature checked here suggests it may not exist in a form suitable for a beginner gardener's photograph.
