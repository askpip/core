## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

This commission extends `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`'s Recommendation R-2, which found that "no source located states what a New Zealand plant tag or pot label prints about rose type" and left commissioning further work on this point as a Founder decision. On review of that dossier, Shaphan identified this as the unresolved core of his original commissioning question: a definitive, gardener-facing list of the rose-type terms a beginner would actually encounter at retail, mapped onto the approved Bush Rose scope, was missing. Shaphan directed that new research be commissioned before any such list is assembled or any Approved Research Compilation is drafted for `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`.

Because Ask Pip's add-plant sequence already collects the gardener's location and the launch market is not yet decided, Shaphan directed that the commission cover **New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States, in that priority order**, with findings kept strictly separate by market rather than blended, or treated as mere corroboration of a New Zealand-only answer.

**Commissioned question.** What does a retail plant tag, pot label, receipt, point-of-sale listing, or product description actually print, display or state about a rose's type in each of the four markets — and, of the terms actually found, which would a beginner gardener in that market realistically encounter, and how does each map onto the approved Bush Rose scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora provisionally, per `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`) versus the excluded lookalike types researched in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` (Shrub, Patio, Miniature, Miniflora, Groundcover, Climbing sports, Standard, Old Garden/species)?

Full detail, including the five commissioned sub-questions (Q1–Q5) and the Founder Decision Points the resulting dossier was directed to reserve, is in the Research Commission Record itself (`Working/AI Outputs/Research_Commission_Record_Rose_Type_Labels.md`), which this dossier does not repeat in full.

## 3. Research Plan

**Approach.** Research proceeded market by market in the directed priority order (New Zealand, then Australia, then the United Kingdom, then the United States), with each market's evidence gathered, recorded and assessed separately. Within each market, both a specialist/mid-market nursery tier and a general or big-box retail tier were checked, alongside the highest-authority classification source available in that market (a rose society, breeder or horticultural body), so that a market's overall pattern was not read from either tier alone.

| Market | Information sought | Source categories checked |
|---|---|---|
| New Zealand | Buyer-facing category names and product-title wording for "bush"/type terms; any physical tag/label content | Big-box retailer (Bunnings NZ), garden centres (Oderings, Kings Plant Barn, Palmers), specialist nurseries (Matthews, Wairere), commercial guide (Yates), the New Zealand Rose Society directory (re-checked) |
| Australia | Same | Specialist nurseries (Treloar, Ross Roses, Wagner's, Knight's), big-box retailer (Bunnings Australia), a wholesale tag/label supplier page |
| United Kingdom | Same | Breeder/retailer sites (David Austin, Peter Beales, Harkness/roses.co.uk), a specialist online nursery (Ashridge), general garden centres (Dobbies, and — checked and ruled out as a distinct company — Dobies), a general DIY/big-box retailer (B&Q) |
| United States | Same, with a deliberate additional effort on physical tag content, judged most likely to be findable in this market | Big-box retailers (Home Depot, Lowe's) and their named suppliers (Wekiva Foliage, Altman Plants, Spring Hill Nurseries), specialist commercial retailers (Jackson & Perkins, Heirloom Roses), consumer forums, video and a general plant-tag-content guide |

**Method.** Research was conducted by web search and automated page retrieval, delegated market-by-market to separate research passes so that no market's findings could be blended with another's during collection. Each pass recorded, for every claim, whether the supporting text was an exact quotation returned by the retrieval tool or the tool's own paraphrase of page content, and this distinction is preserved in §7 below. Searches and fetches that failed or returned nothing usable are recorded in §4.3, following the practice established in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §4.3.

**Anticipated challenges, and how they materialised.** The Research Commission Record anticipated that physical tag/label content would be hard to establish by web search alone and that market coverage might end up uneven. Both proved true: no market yielded any photographed or transcribed example of tag text (§7, AF-7, AF-13, AF-20, AF-25, AF-29), despite a specifically thorough effort in the United States (§4.3). Additionally, some retailers' catalogue pages are dynamically loaded (JavaScript-rendered), which the automated retrieval tool could not always fully capture; this produced at least one internally inconsistent result (Bunnings NZ, AF-3) and several confirmed-inconclusive rather than confirmed-negative results (B&Q's full facet structure, AF-20; Lowe's facet structure, AF-23), which are reported as such rather than treated as findings either way.

### 4.2 Source characteristics that limit independence or weight

- All four research passes used an automated web-search and page-retrieval tool that converts pages to text and, in places, summarises rather than returns raw HTML. Where a pass reported a claim as an "exact quote," the retrieval tool represented it as verbatim page text; where reported as a "paraphrase," it is the tool's own summary. This distinction is preserved in §7 and should be read as the same caution `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §3 recorded for its own extraction step: quotations have not been independently checked against the raw page.
- **BWNZ** produced internally inconsistent results across two fetches of the same category page (§6, C-1) and is treated with reduced weight pending manual or screenshot re-verification.
- **BWAU, HD-T, HD-W, HD-A** and **LOW-S** are individual product listings or a small, possibly incomplete set of category facets rather than a full, confirmed site-wide taxonomy; several dynamically-loaded facet or filter panels (B&Q, Lowe's) could not be fully retrieved (§4.3), so their absence from a report is not evidence of absence on the retailer's site.
- **ROSS-M, ROSS-VL, TRE-P, WRN-P** are, respectively, a category page, a catalogue PDF and two individual product pages; none is a claim about the whole of that retailer's stock.
- **NPO** and **BBG** are general, non-rose-specific pages (a wholesale label-supplier page and a generic plant-tag guide) consulted only for indirect, contextual evidence; neither is rose-specific and neither should be read as evidence about rose tags in particular.
- **SD** is a consumer forum thread and is used only to record a negative result (no tag content discussed), consistent with the caution `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §4.2 applied to a Houzz forum thread.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

**Relevance.** All sources bear directly on the commissioned question of buyer-facing category names and product-title wording (Q2 of the Research Commission Record). None bears on physical tag/label content (Q1); that question remains unanswered in every market, as it was in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` for New Zealand (§4.3, and now confirmed for all four markets, AF-29).

**Source reliability.** Rose societies (NZRS-F, RSNSW), breeders (David Austin, Treloar, Harkness) and specialist commercial retailers (Jackson & Perkins, Heirloom Roses) are given more weight as records of how a market's trade actually names rose types. General big-box retailers (Bunnings NZ and Australia, Home Depot, Lowe's, B&Q) are treated as records of what an ordinary beginner shopper is most likely to actually encounter, which is directly relevant to the commissioned question even where their own internal terminology is inconsistent.

**Consistency.** No market shows a uniform practice across its own retailers (AF-26). Retailers within the same market frequently disagree with each other on what "bush rose" includes, and in several cases (Bunnings NZ, Wagner's Rose Nursery, Home Depot) a single retailer's own site is internally inconsistent (§6).

**Independence.** Each market's retailers were researched independently of one another within that market's pass, but retailers that source their retail stock from the same wholesale grower (for example, several New Zealand and Australian nurseries) may ultimately draw on shared cultivar-naming conventions not visible from their retail websites alone; this was not investigated and is not assumed either way.

**Limitations.** As in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`, page content was read through an automated retrieval step, and some category or filter structures could not be fully retrieved because they are dynamically rendered (§4.2, §4.3). Source publication or last-updated dates were not captured, so currency is not assessed. No physical retail stock was examined in person in any market.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1. Bunnings NZ's own category content, fetched twice, gave inconsistent results.** One fetch of the "Bush Roses" page implied Carpet (groundcover) and Climbing roses are separate categories from Bush; a second fetch of the same page showed products described as Carpet and Climbing co-mingled on the Bush Roses listing itself (AF-3). This may reflect genuinely dynamic, frequently-changing stock, a caching or pagination artefact of the retrieval tool, or a genuine navigation inconsistency on Bunnings' own site. It is not resolved by this commission and is flagged for manual or screenshot re-verification before being relied on as settled evidence of New Zealand big-box practice.

**C-2. Wagner's Rose Nursery (Australia) tags a single product with two different classes at once.** The product "Beautiful Girl" carries the category/type fields "Bush (bare rooted)," "Hybrid tea" and "Grandiflora" simultaneously, and its own description text states "Classified as Grandiflora" (AF-11). This is preserved as an example of a retailer's internal inconsistency, not resolved by treating one label as authoritative over the other.

**C-3. Whether "bush rose" reliably identifies Hybrid Tea/Floribunda stock differs by retailer within every market examined, not only between markets.** New Zealand: Oderings and Kings Plant Barn are comparatively precise (AF-1) while Bunnings NZ, Matthews and Palmers are comparatively loose or peer-category usages (AF-2, AF-3, AF-4). Australia: Treloar and Bunnings Australia are comparatively precise (AF-8, AF-10) while Wagner's is comparatively loose (AF-11), and Ross Roses avoids the term altogether (AF-9). The United Kingdom: Harkness is mostly precise (AF-17) while Ashridge is the loosest usage found in any market (AF-15), Peter Beales is overlapping (AF-16), and David Austin avoids the term (AF-14). The United States: category-level facets are precise everywhere examined (AF-21, AF-24), but product-title precision still varies by individual supplier even within one retailer's own site (AF-22, AF-23). This is carried forward as AF-26 rather than resolved into a single per-market rule, because no per-market rule is supported by the evidence: retailer-level inconsistency is the dominant pattern, not market-level uniformity.

**Uncertainty preserved.** Whether any of the retailers examined in this commission print rose type on the physical tag attached to the actual potted plant a gardener buys — as distinct from the website description a gardener may or may not have read before buying — is not answered by any evidence located in any market (§4.3, AF-7, AF-13, AF-20, AF-25, AF-29).

## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim, kept within its own market except where explicitly marked as a cross-market synthesis. "Later use" states what the finding could support in later knowledge work: **Q** for a gate-question input, **A** for an accepted mapping onto the approved scope, **U** for the "unknown" outcome, **N** for none of these, **S** for cross-market synthesis informing how Pip's scope-qualification behaviour might need to vary (or not) by the gardener's stated location.

### New Zealand

**AF-1 (established).** Oderings' "Bush Roses" retail category is populated specifically with Hybrid Tea and Floribunda cultivars (example: "Rose Scott Base (F.L)"), and is kept structurally separate from a distinct "Patio Bush" category (URL segment `roses-new-seasons-patio-bush`). Kings Plant Barn uses a compound product-title convention — "[Cultivar name] Bush Rose Ht" or "...Bush Rose Fl" — pairing the habit word "Bush" directly with a precise type abbreviation in the buyer-facing title itself (exact quote, product titles). Later use: Q, A.

**AF-2 (supported interpretation).** Yates NZ gives "Bush Roses" its own height-based definition ("compact in size, usually between 0.8m and 1.2m high, with rigid, woody stems that need pruning each winter to force an abundant show of blooms," exact quote) as one section among separately-named Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora sections in the same guide, rather than treating "Bush Roses" as the container for those named classes. Palmers similarly lists "Bush Roses" as its own category alongside Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and others, and states that Hybrid Tea is "available in both bush or standard form" (paraphrase), implying "bush" functions there as a growth-habit modifier that cuts across type rather than a type of its own. Later use: U, S.

**AF-3 (established, limited weight — see C-1).** Bunnings NZ's "Bush Roses" web page, at the time it was fetched, listed products including a potted "Summer Rose Bush," a miniature mixed assortment, a "Flower Carpet" (groundcover) cultivar, and "Showpiece"-branded stock; no product on that page was named or described as Hybrid Tea, Floribunda or Grandiflora. This is the loosest "Bush Roses" bucket usage found in New Zealand retail, though a second fetch produced an inconsistent picture of the same page (C-1), so this finding carries reduced weight pending manual re-verification. Later use: U.

**AF-4 (established).** Matthews Roses' "Bush Roses by Type" is a whole-catalogue, top-level site section (parallel to its own "Bush Roses by Colour" and "by Name" sections) that houses Hybrid Tea (23 varieties) and Floribunda (51 varieties) as clean subcategories, but also houses Climbers, David Austin English Roses, "Easy Care Shrubs & Shrublets," Miniature Patio and Old Fashioned roses as sibling sub-tabs within that same "Bush Roses" section. At Matthews, the section-level label "Bush Roses" therefore does not equal the type-level term "bush rose." Later use: U, S.

**AF-5 (established).** At Wairere Nursery, two products within the same backend "Shrub Rose" category carry different buyer-facing "Habit" field values on their individual product pages: "Bonica" is labelled Habit "Shrub," while "Everlasting Hope" is labelled Habit "Rose Bush." The retail label "Rose Bush" can therefore appear on stock the retailer's own category structure places outside the approved bush-form scope. Later use: Q, U.

**AF-6 (established, re-confirmed).** The New Zealand Rose Society's Rose Finder Type list — re-checked in this commission — contains no "Bush" value and, separately, no "Grandiflora" value, consistent with `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` AF-4 and AF-19. Later use: Q.

**AF-7 (negative finding).** No source located in this commission states or shows what is physically printed on a New Zealand retail plant tag or pot label regarding rose type, extending the same gap `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` Recommendation R-2 already recorded. Later use: U.

### Australia

**AF-8 (established).** Treloar Roses' buyer-facing navigation subdivides "Bush Roses" into "Hybrid Tea Bush Roses," "Floribunda Bush Roses," "Modern Shrub Roses," "Ground Cover Roses," "Miniature & Miniflora Roses" and "Heritage Roses" as sibling categories, and keeps "Climbing Roses" and "Standard Roses" as separate site-wide categories rather than folding them into "Bush." No browsable "Grandiflora" category exists; the one located instance of the word ("Pink Illusion Grandiflora Rose") is unstructured product-page description text, and that product's own "Category" field files it under "Bush Rose." Later use: Q, A, S.

**AF-9 (established).** Ross Roses does not use "Bush Rose," "Hybrid Tea" or "Floribunda" as primary navigation labels, states directly on its own site that "for us terms like Floribunda and Hybrid Tea are being phased out in favour of use as breeders make new varieties that fit between these old terms" (exact quote), and its downloadable Complete Variety List catalogue never uses "Grandiflora" anywhere (confirmed by direct search of the document). Later use: U, S.

**AF-10 (established, small sample).** Bunnings Australia keeps "Climbing Roses" and "Standard Roses" as category pages separate from "Bush Roses." At least one individual "Bush Rose" product description states directly: "Assorted bush rose. Available in floribunda and hybrid tea" (exact quote). **AF-21 (established).** The Home Depot's own site taxonomy exposes separate, explicitly named "Type" facets for "Hybrid tea," "Floribunda" and "Grandiflora," distinct from separate "Shrub" and "Tree Roses" facets, on its "Rose Bushes" listing page. The one adjacent data point found, a general (non-rose-specific) US gardening guide to reading plant tags, lists common tag fields (common name, botanical/Latin name, light exposure, mature size, growth habit, hardiness zone, spacing, disease resistance) and does not include rose class among them; this is suggestive only, is not rose-specific, and is not treated as settling the question either way. Later use: U.

### Cross-market synthesis (ROC's own reasoned synthesis — capped per EAS §2.10, tagged as such, not directly sourced from any single source)

**AF-26 (ROC synthesis, Moderate).** Across all four markets, "bush rose"/"bush roses" functions inconsistently as a retail term, ranging along a spectrum from total avoidance of the term (David Austin, United Kingdom) through peer/overlapping category usage (Palmers and Yates, New Zealand; Peter Beales, United Kingdom; Wagner's, Australia) to a genuinely loose catch-all (Bunnings NZ's Bush Roses page at time of access; Ashridge, United Kingdom) to a mostly-precise bucket (Oderings and Kings Plant Barn, New Zealand; Treloar, Australia; Harkness, United Kingdom; Home Depot's category facets and Jackson & Perkins/Heirloom Roses, United States). No market examined shows uniform practice across its own retailers. Later use: S.

**AF-27 (ROC synthesis, Moderate).** "Grandiflora" specifically shows the widest cross-market divergence in whether it is offered as a distinct, named retail category at all: absent from every New Zealand and Australian retail source examined in this and the prior commission (appearing only as unstructured product-description text at two Australian retailers), used inconsistently in the United Kingdom (the RHS names it nowhere in the material `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` reviewed, and no UK retailer examined in this round named it as a category), and offered as a first-class, explicitly named category at every specialist and general United States retailer examined (Home Depot, Jackson & Perkins, Heirloom Roses; Lowe's at product-title level). Later use: S.

**AF-28 (ROC synthesis, Not Assigned per EAS §2.10 — goes beyond what any single source states).** Precise, class-naming retail labelling appears more consistently achievable at the individual product-title level, where an individual supplier chooses to state it, than at the category/navigation level, where a retailer's own umbrella-term choice governs; no market or retailer examined combines full precision at both levels simultaneously across every product checked. Later use: S.

**AF-29 (negative finding, cross-market, Not Assigned).** Across all four markets and across both this commission and `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`, no source located states or shows what is physically printed on a retail plant tag or pot label regarding rose type. This is the single most consistent finding of either commission and remains the central unresolved evidentiary gap. Later use: U.

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within this commission's scope and authority, and states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. What is physically printed on a plant tag or pot label, in any of the four markets.**
Neither this commission nor `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` located any source addressing this, despite a genuinely thorough, multi-channel effort in the United States market specifically. Answering it needs in-person observation of retail stock in one or more markets — this is the single most consistent gap across both commissions (AF-7, AF-13, AF-20, AF-25, AF-29).
*Founder decision needed:* whether to arrange an in-store survey (in one market, several, or all four), and if so, in what priority order — the Research Commission Record's own priority order (New Zealand, then Australia, then the United Kingdom, then the United States) is one option but was set for desk research, not necessarily for a physical survey.
*Recommended default:* a stated type term found on a tag, receipt, listing or product title is accepted as evidence where present (per AF-1, AF-8, AF-21, AF-22, AF-23, AF-24); the word "bush" alone, without a named type accompanying it, is not — consistent with the default `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` R-2 already set for New Zealand, now extended to all four markets.

**R-2. Whether Bunnings NZ's "Bush Roses" category content (AF-3) reflects a genuine, stable pattern or a retrieval artefact.**
The two fetches of the same page disagreed (C-1). Answering it needs a manual visit to the live page, or an in-store check, since the automated retrieval tool could not resolve the inconsistency itself.
*Founder decision needed:* whether this is worth resolving before treating AF-3 as settled evidence of New Zealand big-box practice.
*Recommended default:* treat AF-3 as Low-Moderate confidence only, as assessed in §8, and do not rely on it alone to characterise New Zealand big-box retail as uniformly loose.

**R-3. Whether markedly uneven cross-market coverage — particularly the thinness of Australian and United Kingdom big-box evidence compared with the United States — is acceptable given the launch market is not yet decided.**
This commission examined two big-box retailers in New Zealand and Australia each, one in the United Kingdom (with a second, B&Q, only partially retrievable), and two in the United States (with deep supplier-level detail). Coverage is not equal across markets.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission further big-box research specifically for Australia and the United Kingdom to bring their evidentiary depth closer to the United States, or to accept the present depth as sufficient for a first version of Pip's scope-qualification reference.
*Recommended default:* proceed with the present findings for all four markets, flagging in any resulting Approved Research Compilation that United Kingdom and Australian big-box coverage is thinner than New Zealand's and the United States', rather than delaying the whole compilation on this point.

**R-4. Whether Ask Pip's scope-qualification logic should treat "Grandiflora" differently by market, given AF-27.**
Grandiflora is a first-class, named retail category in the United States, essentially absent from New Zealand and Australian retail, and inconsistently present in the United Kingdom. This bears directly on Founder Decision Point 4 already reserved (and not yet decided) in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §10, on whether Grandiflora's provisional inclusion in the approved scope continues at all.
*Founder decision needed:* whether Grandiflora's treatment in Pip's scope-qualification behaviour should vary by the gardener's stated market/location, independent of whichever way `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`'s own Grandiflora decision point is eventually resolved.
*Recommended default:* no market-specific variation is built until both this question and `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`'s own Founder Decision Point 4 are decided together, since deciding one without the other risks an inconsistent result.

**R-5. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis, recorded as such and assigned Not Assigned (EAS §2.10).**
These are questions arising from the findings that the evidence does not itself state. They are not findings and are not proposed for adoption.

- R-5a. Whether the retailer-level inconsistency found within every single market (C-3, AF-26) means a per-market default rule for interpreting "bush rose" alone (without an accompanying named type) is achievable at all, or whether "bush" alone should simply never be treated as sufficient evidence of scope in any market.
- R-5b. Whether product-title precision (AF-22, AF-23, AF-28) is a better practical signal for Pip to look for than category-page precision, given that a gardener is more likely to have seen or kept a product label/receipt than to recall a retailer's website navigation.

*Founder decision needed:* whether either of these is worth commissioning further, or should simply inform how the eventual Approved Research Compilation's Recommended Practical Defaults are worded.
*Recommended default:* none of them is acted on until independent evidence is found; in the meantime, the R-1 default (a named type, not the word "bush" alone) already reflects the cautious reading R-5a would otherwise recommend.
