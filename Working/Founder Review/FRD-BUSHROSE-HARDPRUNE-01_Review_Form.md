### Can pruning kill a rose? (Brief section 5, group 1)

#### AF-1 — Sources across NZ, Australia, the UK and the US say pruning, even hard or badly done, doesn't kill an established rose | Confidence: High

> Rose societies, rose growers and a botanic garden across NZ, Australia, the UK and the US state that pruning, even hard or badly done, does not kill an established rose.

[[claim:af1|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af1m|Backing and caveats|Nine sources (counting GU once) across New Zealand, Australia, the UK and the US, including four rose societies, a botanic garden, a rose columnist and three nurseries, state it, seven in direct terms and two with conditions. Limits: Practitioner judgement, not measured; three are commercial; GORG dissents (C-1). Still uncertain: How a very old or weak bush responds (AF-2).]]

#### AF-2 — Some add conditions: otherwise healthy, not cut below the ground line, roots intact | Confidence: Moderate

> Several of those sources attach conditions: the rose is otherwise healthy, it is not cut below the ground line, and its roots are intact.

[[claim:af2|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af2m|Backing and caveats|Four sources (CBG, PRS, SARS, RHS-REN) state a condition. Limits: Each states a different condition; RHS-REN is general shrub guidance. Still uncertain: Whether the conditions are complete.]]

#### AF-3 — A Royal National Rose Society trial found more flowers with minimal and hedge-trimmer pruning than conventional pruning | Confidence: Low

> A Royal National Rose Society trial found more flowers with minimal and hedge-trimmer pruning than with conventional pruning.

[[claim:af3|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af3m|Backing and caveats|A rose society's own trial report, echoed by a nursery. Limits: No years, measures or full variety list; the two pages may report the same trial. Still uncertain: Whether results apply to Hybrid Teas and Floribundas in NZ conditions.]]

#### AF-4 — Harm from over-pruning is described as a short-term setback; the first year is a recovery year | Confidence: Moderate

> Sources describe any harm from over-pruning as a temporary setback, with the first year after hard pruning a recovery year.

[[claim:af4|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af4m|Backing and caveats|Three sources of different kinds (botanic garden, NZ columnist, nursery) agree. Limits: DA-SR is written for shrub roses; none gives a measure or time frame. Still uncertain: Length of the setback.]]

### What the words mean (Brief section 5, group 2)

#### AF-5 — "Hard", "moderate" and "light" are defined differently by different sources | Confidence: Moderate

> "Hard", "moderate" and "light" pruning are defined differently from source to source.

[[claim:af5|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af5m|Backing and caveats|Five sources give differing definitions, so the variation itself is well supported. Limits: LO and NYBG lack named authors; PRS's definitions could not be quoted verbatim. Still uncertain: Which usage NZ gardeners meet.]]

#### AF-6 — Where defined, "hard" means canes cut to a few buds near the base | Confidence: Moderate

> Where defined, "hard" pruning means cutting canes back to a few buds near the base.

[[claim:af6|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af6m|Backing and caveats|Three sources (LO, NYBG, RHS-MB) agree on a few buds near the base. Limits: RHS-MB does not use the word "hard"; ILL and YAT-AU use "hard" for less severe cuts (C-6).]]

> Hard pruning is described as giving fewer but larger flowers.

[[claim:af7|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]

### Risks (Brief section 5, group 3)

#### AF-11 — A weak or slow-growing rose is pruned less hard than a vigorous one | Confidence: Moderate

> A weak or slow-growing rose is pruned less hard than a vigorous one.

[[claim:af11|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af11m|Backing and caveats|Four sources (two extension, one rose society, one public rose garden) agree at whole-plant level. Limits: RHS-MB and LO say weak shoots are cut further (C-2). Still uncertain: Whether the two statements describe different things (R-5b).]]

#### AF-12 — One source's reason: heavy pruning removes much of the plant's food-making ability | Confidence: Low

> One source gives the reason: heavy pruning removes much of the plant's food-making ability.

[[claim:af12|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af12m|Backing and caveats|Stated directly by an extension source. Limits: Single source; mechanism stated, not measured.]]

#### AF-13 — One source: routine excessive pruning reduces flowering | Confidence: Low

[[claim:af13|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af13m|Backing and caveats|Stated directly by an extension source. Limits: Single source; "excessive" not defined. Still uncertain: How much is "excessive".]]

#### AF-14 — Cutting below the graft or ground line is the cut sources say can lose the rose | Confidence: Moderate

#### AF-15 — One source: an own-root rose whose canes die back often regrows from its roots | Confidence: Low

> One source says an own-root rose whose canes die back often regrows from its roots.

[[claim:af15|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af15m|Backing and caveats|Stated by an extension Master Gardener article. Limits: Single source; concerns winter dieback. Still uncertain: Whether it applies after pruning.]]

#### AF-16 — Pruning before frosts have passed risks frost damage to new growth and dieback | Confidence: High

#### AF-17 — Autumn or early-winter pruning is linked with winter injury in cold climates | Confidence: Low

## Where the sources differ

Six points of disagreement are on record (Brief section 6). Each is carried in the findings above as it stands, without a resolution.

[[more:confm|The six disagreements|C-1 Can hard pruning kill? Nearly all sources: no. One commercial website: it could. Some add conditions. C-2 Weak growth. Clemson, Illinois, Rose Society of NSW, Victoria State Rose Garden: prune a weak plant lightly. RHS and Landscape Ontario: cut weak shoots back further. One set talks about the whole plant, the other about single shoots. C-3 How much in one go, versus the one-third advisory. Rose sources: about half to two-thirds at winter pruning.
