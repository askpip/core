# Decision Brief — Which Months Should I Do Each Rose Job, Where I Live in Canada?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief, then answer the seven questions in the Review Form: five on how Pip should give Canadian months; one on further research; and the overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA-01`, approve with changes, request further work, or decline.**

You are not asked to decide each of the 50 findings. Section 4 explains which ones Pip may use, and section 5 marks each one. You can still change, exclude or flag any finding in the form's last box.

## 2. The short version

- **Canada has its own hardiness zones, and they are not the same as US zones** (**High**). Canada's zones use several kinds of weather; US zones use only the coldest temperature. Sources say Canadian and US zone numbers can't be swapped, and that a Canadian number is usually about one higher (**Moderate**). One garden centre says many Canadian box-store labels use US zones without saying so (**Low**). Canada's zones went up by half a zone to two zones in 2025 (**High**).
- **Canadian rose sources mostly tie timing to places and signals, not to zones** (**Moderate**). They name towns and regions (Toronto, Georgina, the Lower Mainland, the Fraser Valley, Kingston). The Canadian Rose Society uses three zone bands (2–4, 5–6, 7–9) for winter protection but doesn't say whether they are Canadian or US zones (**Low**).
- **The strongest timing signals are local, not calendar:** prune when forsythia flowers (**High**); put winter protection on once the ground is freezing, after hard frosts (**High**); take it off as the ground thaws (**High**); start feeding when spring growth begins (**High**).
- **Months, where sources give them:** coastal BC prunes in late February to mid-March (**Moderate**); Toronto in April or May (**Low**); the Canadian Rose Society says "usually April" (**Low**). Ontario sources put winter protection on in November (**Moderate**).
- **The last feed is disputed:** mid-July in four sources (**Moderate**), but end of July to late August in other Ontario sources (**Moderate**).
- **Hybrid Teas and Floribundas need winter protection across most of Canada** (**High**), but not usually on the BC coast (**Moderate**).
- **Gaps:** no source found for the Maritimes, no pruning month for Quebec or the Prairies, and no Canadian source on when to move an established rose (**Low**). The Montreal Botanical Garden's pages could not be opened.
- **Several months come from one company only** (Alberta: last feed mid-August, uncover around the May long weekend; Georgina, Ontario: uncover late April or early May) (**Low**).

## 3. What was researched

Q1: when to do the main spring pruning. Q2: when to start and stop feeding. Q3: when to stop deadheading and summer pruning. Q4: when to plant bare-root and potted roses, and when to move an established rose. Q5: fall clear-up, winter sprays, and when winter protection goes on and comes off. Q6: how Canadian sources divide the country (Canadian zones, US zones, frost dates, province). Q7: how a gardener finds their zone. Q8: what sources say about early or late seasons and band edges. Explorer, Parkland and other hardy shrub roses were not researched.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Last feed.** National rose society, Huronia Rose Society, Landscape Ontario and a Quebec nursery: by mid-July. Toronto Master Gardeners: end of July. Palatine Roses: early August. Georgina Garden Centre and an Alberta nursery: mid-August. Landscape Ontario also says weekly to late August. Coastal BC companies: late summer, none from September.
- **C-2 Fall planting.** Canadian Rose Society: September, but not on the Prairies. Royal Botanical Gardens and Kingston Master Gardeners: late autumn or late fall.
- **C-3 When protection goes on.** Most sources: once the ground is freezing, after hard frosts, "not too early". One Quebec company: just before the first frosts. Another Quebec company: wait for November.
- **C-4 How soon protection comes off.** Canadian Rose Society: late spring, once a hard freeze is no longer expected. A Quebec writer and a Quebec company: as soon as days stay above freezing or the snow allows. Two sources: in stages.
- **C-5 The spring signal.** Most sources: forsythia flowering. Some: buds swelling. Canadian Rose Society for zones 2–4: when the bush starts to leaf out. One BC source: pruning after leaf-out only delays flowers.
- **C-6 Which zone system.** The Canadian Rose Society doesn't say. One writer uses both kinds in one article. Other sources say Canadian.
- **C-7 How much to cut in fall.** Canadian Rose Society cuts canes back in every zone band. Landscape Ontario: only if canes are tall enough to whip about. A BC garden centre: no fall pruning in the Okanagan.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve this dossier as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline. Approval accepts the research record; which findings Pip may use follows from section 4.

2. **Which bands does Pip use for Canada?**
   (a) regions: coastal BC; BC interior and north; the Prairies; Ontario; Quebec; the Maritimes, each showing only what sources give, with boundaries you set; (b) the Canadian Rose Society's three zone bands; (c) no bands, one national set of windows with the local signals; (d) defer.
   *ROC recommendation: (a), with Quebec limited and no months for the Maritimes for now.* Sources name places, and the society's bands don't say which zone system they use.

3. **How does Pip work out which band a gardener is in?**
   (a) ask their zone and whether it's Canadian or US; (b) work it out from the town and province they already enter; (c) province only; (d) work it out from their location, then check by asking whether they protect their roses over winter or get hard frosts.
   *ROC recommendation: (d).* Zone numbers are easy to mix up and changed in 2025; province alone would put the Okanagan with Vancouver.

4. **How does Pip show a month alongside the bud-swell check?**
   (a) the month is shown as background ("roses here are usually pruned in late February to mid-March, when forsythia flowers"), and the bud-swell check still decides whether to prune now; (b) the month only after the check passes; (c) months only when asked.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).* Months never replace the bud-swell check.

5. **What does Pip say near a band edge or in an early or late season?**
   (a) show the neighbouring bands' months together; (b) show the wider window with the signals: prune when forsythia flowers and buds swell, after the hard frosts; protection on as the ground freezes and off as it thaws, in stages; (c) suggest a local rose society or Master Gardener group.
   *ROC recommendation: (b), plus (c).*
   *Note:* "In stages" in option (b) rests on AF-50, which is Low and comes from two companies. Pip uses it only if you choose (b).

6. **How does Pip show months that come from only one company?**
   (a) show them, labelled as from a Canadian garden company; (b) show only the signals and the non-company months; (c) defer.
   *ROC recommendation: (a),* in line with your decision that Pip tells gardeners where its information comes from. Where sources disagree, Pip shows the range.
   *Note:* These months are Low and come only from garden companies (AF-28, AF-29, AF-40, AF-43, AF-44, AF-45, AF-47, AF-48). They stay on record only unless you choose (a).
