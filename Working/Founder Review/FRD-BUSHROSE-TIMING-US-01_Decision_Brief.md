# Decision Brief — Which Months for Each Rose Job, Where I Live in the United States?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-TIMING-US-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief. The decisions are on the 60 findings, on four further-research items, and on six questions about how Pip should show US months, ending with one overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-US-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

- **US gardeners can look up their USDA zone by ZIP code** on the USDA map (**High**). The zone is based on how cold the winters get (**High**). The map was updated in 2023 and much of the country moved about half a zone warmer, so older zone numbers are still around (**Moderate**). Zones say nothing about summer heat or late frosts (**Moderate**).
- **No rose source gives months by zone.** Every US rose calendar found is written for a state, county or rose society district (**Moderate**). We grouped them into four provisional bands using the zone each area is stated to be in: **A** zones 3–5 (Iowa, Minnesota, the Dakotas, Wisconsin), **B** zones 5–7 (Illinois, Pennsylvania), **C** zones 7–8 (Nashville, Atlanta's Cobb County), **D** zones 9–10 (Arizona low desert, Marin in California). Twelve more areas have months but no stated zone, so they are kept separate.
- **Main pruning runs from December to mid-April across the country**, later where it's colder (**High**): about March in Nashville, late February to mid-March near Atlanta, mid-January to early February in Tucson, January in Marin, late March to mid-April in Iowa. Illinois and Pennsylvania give no month, just "spring".
- **Sources tie timing to signs in the garden as well as months** (**High**): buds swelling, forsythia flowering, hard freezes, the last frost.
- **Feeding:** cold areas start in May and stop by late July to August; Illinois and Pennsylvania stop in August; warmer areas run to September, October or, in Florida, mid-November (mostly **Low** to **Moderate**, with some disagreement inside bands).
- **Winter protection (mounding):** goes on after hard freezes, from November in the coldest band to December in band C, and comes off from about March (Nashville) to mid-April (the coldest band) (**Moderate**). No zone 9–10 source mentions it.
- **Planting bare-root roses:** December to February in the warmest band, March in Nashville, April to mid-May in the coldest band (**Low** to **Moderate**).

## 3. What was researched

Q1: months for main pruning. Q2: months to start feeding and to give the last feed. Q3: when to stop deadheading. Q4: months to plant bare-root and potted roses and to move a rose. Q5: winter clear-up, winter sprays, and winter protection on and off. Q6: how US sources divide the country by climate. Q7: whether gardeners can find their zone. Q8: how sources handle an early or late season, or a garden near a band edge.

## 4. How to read the strength ratings

| Level | Meaning in this dossier |

| High | Reliable and generally consistent across independent sources |
| Moderate | Reasonably supported, with notable limitations or some disagreement |
| Low | Limited evidence or significant limitations |

Each finding has its own rating. None is averaged into an overall figure. Many findings are Low simply because each area has only one or two sources.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Last feed in the coldest band.** Late July (a Wisconsin rose professor), 15 August (Minnesota Rose Society), after August (American Rose Society).
- **C-2 Nashville against Cobb County.** Cobb County feeds through September and stops deadheading in October; Nashville feeds liquids later, stops in November, and cuts flowers into November.
- **C-3 When mounding starts in the coldest band.** September (begin mounding gradually) against early to mid-November; Illinois and Pennsylvania wait for hard frosts.
- **C-4 When to start feeding.** Most sources: when new growth appears. Marin: after the first flowers.
- **C-5 Which zone system.** USDA zones measure winter cold only; a California source prefers Sunset zones; an Arizona source notes Arizona and the Oregon coast share zones but not climates.
- **C-6 Winter sprays.** October (coldest band), November to December (Cobb County), January to February (Nashville, Marin, Orange County).
- **C-7 Iowa State's feeding cut-off.** The current page differs from what the basic-care ARC records.
- **C-8 Overlapping bands.** Iowa (zone 5) sits in the coldest band, but Illinois and Pennsylvania also reach down to zone 5; Florida spans zones 8 to 11.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve the findings as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-US-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline.

2. **Which zone bands does Pip use, and where are the edges?**
   (a) four fixed bands: zones 3–5, 6, 7–8, 9–10; (b) no bands, months only for the gardener's own state where a source exists; (c) both: own-state months where a source exists, otherwise the band's months named with their source, otherwise season and signs; (d) defer.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).*

3. **How is a gardener placed?**
   (a) ask for their USDA zone; (b) work it out from the rose's recorded location; (c) by state only; (d) work out zone and state from the location and show them so the gardener can correct them.
   *ROC recommendation: (d).* Older zone numbers still circulate, so asking alone risks the wrong one.

4. **How does Pip show months next to the bud-swell check?** (Months never replace the check.)
   (a) months shown as background under the check; (b) months used to prompt "start looking for bud swell"; (c) both; (d) no months for pruning, months for other jobs only.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).*

5. **What does Pip say near a band edge, or in an early or late year?**
   (a) show both neighbouring bands' months; (b) show the gardener's own months, with a note that the signs in their garden come first in any year; (c) widen the window by a fixed margin.
   *ROC recommendation: (b).*

6. **Show planting, moving and winter-spray months now?**
   (a) hold them until the planting-and-moving and spraying decisions are made; (b) show them now.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

7. **What happens to the twelve areas with no stated zone?**
   (a) shown only to gardeners in the same state; (b) placed in bands after a zone check; (c) left out.
   *ROC recommendation: (a) now, (b) if you commission R-1.*
