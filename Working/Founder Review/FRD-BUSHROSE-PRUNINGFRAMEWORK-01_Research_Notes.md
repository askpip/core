### OSU

# Extraction: "Pruning Roses" - OSU Extension Service

**Publisher:** Oregon State University Extension Service  
**Author:** Barbara McMullen  
**Date:** Published May 2007, Reviewed 2024  
**Rose types addressed:** Hybrid Tea, Grandiflora, Floribunda, Climbers, Shrub, Standard, Miniature, Ramblers  
**Visual content:** Yes—photographs and illustrations included

---

## (1) PURPOSE/OBJECTIVES

"Roses should be pruned annually in spring to encourage new growth and a succession of flowers throughout the growing season." Plants "will reward you with beautiful blooms and vigorous health that helps ward off disease."

---

## (2) STEM CATEGORIES

**Dead/diseased:** "Start by removing dead, dying or diseased canes. Cut canes to white or pale green live pith. Brown coloration indicates a dead or dying cane."

**Crossing/rubbing:** "Remove any crossing or rubbing canes. These tend to promote damage to the canes, and that encourages disease."

**Weak:** "Underpruning is the most common cause of an unproductive rose. If pruned improperly, plants will have weak canes, poor form and little air circulation."

**Young/basal shoots:** "Select three to six strong canes from the previous year's growth to keep."

**Interior lateral canes (Floribunda):** "Remove interior lateral canes to promote good air circulation."

---

## (3) STATED ORDER OF STEPS

For Hybrid Tea/Floribunda:
1. "Start by removing dead, dying or diseased canes."
2. "Remove any crossing or rubbing canes."
3. "Select three to six strong canes from the previous year's growth to keep. Remove all other growth."
4. "Prune back the selected canes...Cut above an outward facing bud."

---

## (4) HANDLING CONFLICTS

Not addressed

---

## (5) TARGET STRUCTURE/SHAPE

"The natural plant shape is like a large vase" (Hybrid Tea-Grandiflora). General approach: "keep the plant open in the middle" by cutting to "outward facing bud." 

**Number of main stems retained:** "Select three to six strong canes from the previous year's growth to keep."

---

## (6) LIMITS ON REMOVAL BY CLASS

**Hybrid Tea-Grandiflora:** "Moderately prune to 12–18 inches tall"

**Floribunda:** "Moderately prune to 24-36 inches tall"

**Climbers:** Maintain "major canes...horizontally" and "only the lateral branches are pruned."

**Fall pruning:** "Take several inches off Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, and Grandiflora roses to reduce wind damage."

---

## (7) DEFER, SPREAD, OR SEEK HELP

"Neglected roses can easily be rejuvenated by pruning." No mention of deferring or spreading work. Generic advice: "Check with your county OSU Extension office if you have additional questions."

---

## (8) ASSESSING BY EYE/DISTANCE/PHOTOGRAPHS

"If the buds are difficult to see, try this technique: cut the foliage from the bush two weeks before pruning. This causes the new buds to swell and become easier to see."

---

## (9) CUT MECHANICS

**Angle:** "Make a clean 45-degree cut on a downward slant, away from the bud."

**Distance above bud:** "About a quarter inch above an outward facing bud" / "Leaving 1/4" of cane above the bud ensures the wound can heal properly."

**Bud direction:** "Cutting to bud that is facing the outside of the plant increases air circulation by keeping the plant open in the middle."

**Tool choice:** "Bypass pruners will cut a woody stem up to three-quarters of an inch thick." "Loppers...make it easier to cut thick canes." "Pruning saws" for larger cuts.

**Sharp/clean tools:** "Keep your pruner sharp so it makes a clean cut." "Sanitize your pruners by dipping the blades into rubbing alcohol (70% isopropyl alcohol)...before working on each plant."

**Cut end treatment:** "Follow up with a dormant oil spray after pruning. Dormant oil coats canes, buds, and leaves, suffocating pests."

### ILL

# Rose Pruning Page Analysis

**Publisher:** Illinois Extension (University of Illinois College of Agricultural, Consumer & Environmental Sciences)

**Author:** Not stated

**Date Published/Last Reviewed:** Not stated

**Rose Types Addressed:** Hybrid teas, grandifloras, floribundas, miniatures, rugosa, shrub roses, English roses, species roses, old garden roses (Alba, Gallica, Centifolia, Damasks, Mosses, Bourbons, Hybrid Perpetuals, Portlands), climbers, ramblers

**Visual Content:** Yes—includes photographs/illustrations of deadheading, pruning twigs, and ever-blooming roses

---

## (1) PURPOSE/OBJECTIVES OF PRUNING
- "Remove dead, damaged, or diseased wood; increase air circulation; keep the shrub from becoming a tangled mess; shape the plant; and encourage the growth of flowering wood"
- Lessen likelihood of diseases like botrytis
- Produce "an open centered plant" allowing "air and light to penetrate easily"

---

## (2) STEM CATEGORIES & DESCRIPTIONS

**REMOVE:**
- **Dead/Dying:** "Canes that are shriveled, dark brown, or black" or "at the base or point of discoloration"
- **Diseased:** Part of general removal of "dead, damaged, or diseased wood"
- **Weak/Thin:** "Remove all thin, weak canes that are smaller than a pencil in diameter"
- **Suckers (on grafted roses):** "Remove it. The best way is to dig down to the root where the sucker is originating and tear it off"

**RETAIN:**
- **New/Current season's wood:** Essential for modern ever-blooming types; "produce the best flowers on new or current season's wood"
- **Young canes:** In shrub roses via "one-third" method, identify "one-third of the very youngest canes that grew the previous season"
- **Mature/Old wood:** Old once-blooming roses "produce flowers on old wood"; repeat-flowering old garden roses "bloom on both new and old wood"
- **Healthy, stout canes:** "Leave 3 to 5 healthy, stout canes evenly spaced around the plant"

**NOT ADDRESSED:** Crossing, rubbing, inward/centre-growing stems

---

## (3) STATED ORDER OF STEPS

For modern ever-blooming roses:
1. "Remove all dead canes; cut them off at the base or point of discoloration"
2. "Remove small, weak canes"
3. "Leave 3 to 5 healthy, stout canes evenly spaced around the plant"
4. "Cut these canes back, leaving 3 to 5 outward-facing buds"

For modern shrub roses (one-third method):
1. "Remove one-third of the very oldest canes"
2. "Replace these canes by identifying about one-third of the very youngest canes"
3. "Remove the remaining canes"

---

## (4) HANDLING STEMS WITH MULTIPLE CATEGORIES/CONFLICTS

Not explicitly addressed. The page does not discuss prioritization when a stem fits multiple categories.

---

## (5) TARGET STRUCTURE/SHAPE & MAIN STEMS RETAINED

- Goal: "An open centered plant" for air/light penetration
- **Modern ever-blooming roses:** "Leave 3 to 5 healthy, stout canes evenly spaced around the plant"
- **Modern shrub roses:** No fixed number specified; "wait to see what shape develops and then try to prune so that the shape is maintained"
- **Old garden roses:** Maintain the plant through "thinning and removing old wood"
- **Climbers/ramblers:** Training canes "to grow more horizontally encourages the growth of bloom producing side shoots"

---

## (6) LIMITS ON REMOVAL & CLASS DIFFERENCES

- **Modern ever-blooming (hybrid tea, floribunda, grandiflora, miniature):** "Removing about one-half to two-thirds of the plant's height"—"pruned very hard in early spring"
- **Modern shrub roses:** "Severe pruning of these roses would result in reduced flower production"; can remain "unpruned" in first 2-3 seasons
- **Dead-heading guidance for weak plants:** "If the plant is weak or small, you may not want to cut off as much material"
- **Timing restriction:** "Flowers should not be cut after October 1 to allow the plant to begin hardening off for the winter"

---

## (7) DEFERRALS, SPREADING OVER TIME, SEEKING HELP

- **Climbers/ramblers:** "May need a few seasons in the garden before pruning is necessary"
- **Shrub roses:** Can remain "unpruned" in first two or three seasons
- **Once-blooming old garden roses:** "May not need annual pruning if there is no dead or damaged wood present"
- Spreading/renewal mentioned for shrub roses via one-third method but not explicitly framed as deferral

Not addressed: advice to seek professional help

---

## (8) ASSESSING PLANT BY EYE/DISTANCE/PHOTOGRAPHS

Not addressed

---

## (9) CUT MECHANICS

**Angle & Position:** "Cut at a 45-degree angle about 1/4 inch above outward-facing bud. The cut should slant away from the bud"

**Bud Direction:** Cut to "outward-facing bud" (mentioned multiple times)

**Tools:** "Use clean, sharp equipment"

**Stem Thickness:** Thin weak canes "smaller than a pencil in diameter" should be removed; no guidance on tool selection by thickness

**Cut End Treatment:** "If cane borers are a problem, it is suggested to seal the ends of the cuts to prevent the entry of cane borers. White glue works well"

**Sucker Removal:** Tear off at source rather than cutting to prevent regrowth

## Extracted Content by Topic

**(1) PURPOSE/OBJECTIVES**

"Pruning keeps the plant healthy. It promotes new growth, removes dead, broken or diseased canes and trains roses to a desired shape. Pruning encourages flowering, either more blooms or larger blooms, and is essential to keep modern rose varieties blooming repeatedly all summer long."

**(2) STEM CATEGORIES & DESCRIPTIONS**

- **Dead/damaged/diseased:** "Remove all dead, damaged or weak stems"; "Dead, diseased or damaged wood on any rose should be removed as soon as it is seen"; stems should show "no sign of discoloration. The center of the canes (rose stems) should be white and plump, not brown and withered."

- **Weak/thin/spindly:** "Remove all dead, damaged or weak stems"; "Keep any weak, small or short stems pruned away. Generally with hybrid teas, any cane thinner than a pencil should be removed."

- **Crossing:** "Whenever two canes cross each other, one can be removed."

- **Inward/centre-growing:** "Prune the bush to make it more open in the center...try to make pruning cuts above a leaf bud facing out from the center of the plant."

- **Old stems:** Old garden roses: "remove only the oldest stems that are no longer productive"; climbers: "remove the oldest canes."

- **Invigorated/vigorous:** "leaving only the most vigorous, healthy canes" should remain; "Prune weak-growing varieties lightly and vigorous varieties more severely."

**(3) STATED ORDER OF STEPS**

"The first step in pruning roses is the same for all rose types. Remove all dead, damaged or weak stems leaving only the most vigorous, healthy canes." Subsequent steps involve opening the center and making directional cuts above outward-facing buds.

**(4) HANDLING CONFLICTS BETWEEN CATEGORIES**

Not explicitly addressed.

**(5) TARGET STRUCTURE/SHAPE & STEM RETENTION**

- **Hybrid teas, floribundas, grandifloras:** "In the spring, cut out all but three to five of the healthiest, most vigorous canes. Prune these canes down to 15 to 18 inches from ground level."

- **Climbing roses:** "leaving five to seven strong canes untouched."

- **Old garden roses:** Preserve "informal habit...by removing no more than one-third of each bush."

General guidance: "Prune the bush to make it more open in the center."

**(6) LIMITS ON REMOVAL & DIFFERENCES BY CLASS**

- **Hybrid teas/floribundas/grandifloras:** "Prune hard if you want large blooms suitable for cut flowers. This will produce fewer total blooms." Fall pruning: "prune roses back by about one-third of their height."

- **Old garden roses:** "Hard pruning can ruin their graceful shape and severely reduce their flowering"; remove "no more than one-third of each bush."

- **Miniature roses:** "can be lightly trimmed or tip-pruned several times a year."

- **First-year plants:** "Prune first-year plants lightly to allow them to put more energy into establishing a strong root system."

**(7) DEFERRING, SPREADING, OR SEEKING HELP**

Not addressed.

**(8) ASSESSING BY EYE/DISTANCE/PHOTOGRAPHS**

"The best way to judge when to prune is to look at the buds; when they begin to swell, go ahead and prune." (Visual assessment of dormancy breaking)

**(9) CUT MECHANICS**

- **Angle/distance/bud direction:** "Make a cut ¼ inch above the bud and angled at the same angle as the bud...try to make pruning cuts above a leaf bud facing out from the center of the plant."

- **Deadheading specific:** "remove the flower by making a diagonal cut just above the next five or seven-leaf branch down on the stem."

- **Tool choice by thickness:** "All pruning cuts on canes greater than the thickness of a pencil should be sealed"; bypass pruners recommended over anvil types.

- **Clean tools:** "Dip your pruning shears periodically in a 70 percent alcohol solution to avoid spreading diseases."

- **Cut end treatment:** "All pruning cuts on canes greater than the thickness of a pencil should be sealed with nail polish or wood glue to prevent cane borers."

- **Equipment needed:** "sharp pruning shears; long-handled lopping shears; and a pruning saw for any large, heavy, old stems. Leather gloves will help protect your hands."

# Rose Pruning Guide: Content Extraction

**PUBLISHER, AUTHOR, DATE:**

## CONTENT EXTRACTION

**(1) PURPOSE/OBJECTIVES OF PRUNING:**
"The goal of pruning is to remove dead, diseased, weak, and ill-placed canes and select strong healthy canes that will support the new year's growth." Also: creates "an open center that allows for air circulation and sunshine in the middle of the plant." Stated benefits include minimizing disease/insects, achieving "healthy, beautiful roses," and supporting "good future bloom."

**(2) STEM CATEGORIES - REMOVE OR RETAIN:**

*Remove:*
- **Dead:** "Cut any dead...canes down to the bud union"
- **Diseased:** "Cut any dead or diseased canes down to the bud union"
- **Old/unproductive:** "Cut out any old craggy, gray canes that have no productive growth"
- **Weak/thin/spindly:** "Ideally remaining canes should be larger than a pencil" and "Avoid the temptation to leave small branches and stems"
- **Crossing:** "Cut out any cane crossing directly over the center of the plant" to increase airflow
- **Crowding:** "Cut out any cane that is seriously crowding another"
- **Below bud union:** "Remove any rootstock suckers that come from below the bud union"
- **Deformed:** "Cut off spindly, weak, deformed growth from the canes"

*Retain:*
- **New/healthy canes:** "You want to identify 5-7 new, green, sturdy canes to keep" that are "larger than a pencil"
- **Outward-facing:** Cut "at an outward-facing dormant bud"
- **Basal shoots:** Notes that "brushing away...old scaly wood...encourage[s] future basal breaks (new shoots coming from the bud union)"

**(3) STATED ORDER OF STEPS:**
The page presents a sequence: (1) Make preliminary plan for main canes; (2) Remove weak/dead/crossing branches (thinning cuts); (3) Reduce height by finding outward-facing dormant buds and cutting above them (heading cuts); (4) Strip all leaves and clean debris.

**(4) HANDLING CONFLICTS/MULTIPLE CATEGORIES:**
When a cane meets multiple criteria, the approach is: "If a cane is small, the stem coming off it will be even smaller"—implying weak canes are rejected even if healthy. For canes "horizontally spreading," an "interior bud may be a better choice as it will grow more upright"—prioritizing structure over strict outward-facing orientation. No explicit conflict resolution matrix provided.

**(5) TARGET STRUCTURE/SHAPE & STEM COUNT:**
"Your aim is to have an attractive, vase-shaped bush with 5-7 healthy, well-spaced canes." The "vase pattern" is explicitly mentioned as ideal.

**(6) LIMITS ON REMOVAL & CLASS DIFFERENCES:**
"Pruning back 1/3 to ½ of the growth is a good guideline in mild climates" and "Reduce the height of the canes by 1/3 to 1/2." 

Difference noted: "This is less important on floribundas, which generally branch more than hybrid tea roses" (regarding retaining thicker pencil-sized canes).

No separate guidance given for hybrid tea vs. grandiflora pruning severity.

**(7) ADVICE TO DEFER, SPREAD, OR SEEK HELP:**
"Not addressed." The article recommends consulting other resources but does not suggest deferring pruning, spreading it over years, or seeking professional help.

**(8) ASSESSING BY EYE/DISTANCE/PHOTOGRAPHS:**
"Not addressed." The article focuses on hands-on inspection during pruning but provides no guidance on remote assessment.

**(9) CUT MECHANICS:**

- **Angle & distance:** "Cut about 1/4 inch above it at a 45 degree angle down and away from the bud"
- **Bud direction:** "Find an outward-facing dormant bud" for most cuts; "an interior bud may be a better choice as it will grow more upright" in horizontal cases
- **Bud assessment:** "Find a dormant bud" (not flushed); "The pith should be creamy white, not brown or gray"
- **Cutting technique:** "Use the sharp end of the pruner towards the cane to assure a clean cut"
- **Tool choice by thickness:** Hand clippers for standard canes; "loppers" and "curved pruning saw" mentioned but no explicit size thresholds provided
- **Tool hygiene:** "Using Lysol spray on your tools between roses will help reduce the possibility of disease transmission"
- **Leaves:** "Strip the bush of all leaves"
- **Cut ends/sealing:** "Not addressed"

## General Pruning Cuts

"Do not leave stubs since they are usually where die back occurs"

**Tool by Thickness:**
Hand shears for stems up to 1/2 inch; loppers for 1/2 to 2+ inches; pruning saws for larger branches

**Sharp/Clean Tools:**
"keep a sharp edge"; "using the correct tools" is essential; disinfect equipment between cuts on diseased plants

**Sealing Wounds:**
"wound dressings are not recommended or necessary" except on oak trees in oak wilt areas

## Rose-Specific Pruning

- Prune 3-4 weeks before last frost date
- Remove damaged canes and those smaller than pencil diameter
- Hybrid teas/florabundas: cut back to 12 inches (large flowers) or 18-24 inches (smaller flowers)
- Climbing roses: remove old canes; prune back for maintenance
- When cutting flowers, leave at least two leaf sets
- Cut at 45° angles above outside buds, 1/2 inch above the bud

# Rose Bush Pruning Guide Analysis

**Publisher:** UC Agriculture and Natural Resources (UCANR)

**Author:** Not explicitly stated (article attributed to "Rosie's Corner" column)

**Date:** February 1, 2025

**Copyright Statement:** "Copyright ©2026 All rights reserved" (quoted verbatim from meta tag)

---

## Pruning Objectives
The article aims to rejuvenate rose bushes and encourage healthy new growth by removing problematic canes.

## Growth Categories Removed
- Dead, diseased, or damaged canes (showing black color or no signs of life)
- Suckers on grafted roses
- Crossing or rubbing canes
- "Older gray canes in established plants to stimulate new cane growth"
- Weak, small stems
- Remaining leaves

## Growth Categories Kept
New canes described as "a nice, shiny green or greenish red color"

## Order/Sequence
Pruning proceeds "from the bottom up," following 13 numbered steps that progress from removing problem wood to final shaping.

## Stem Size Standards
Retained stems should be approximately "the size of a #2 pencil"

## Height Target
Hybrid tea, shrub and grandiflora roses should reach "2.5 to 3.5 feet"

## Removal Limits
Remove "between 1/3 to ½ of last year's growth"; remove "about a third" of old canes yearly in multi-year rejuvenation

## Timing Deferral
"Wait to fertilize until frost/freeze danger has passed...usually around Feb. 15"

## Cut Mechanics
- Cut "slightly above a bud that faces outward about ¼ inch above the bud"
- Cut "below any dieback" to reveal "healthy, white center"
- Tools: sharpen before use; clean with alcohol wipes between bushes
- Use leather gloves or welding sleeves

Four images credited to Wikimedia Commons, Sierra Foothill Rose Society, "Rosie D," and Martin Stott
