# Decision Brief — Why Isn't My Bush Rose Flowering?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-NOFLOWERS-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief, then decide on the 34 findings, the four further-research items, and six questions about how Pip should answer "why isn't my rose flowering?". There is one overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-NOFLOWERS-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

- **Not enough sun is the clearest cause.** Twelve sources agree that less sun means fewer flowers (**High**). They disagree on how many hours roses need: from four to eight, most often six. The two New Zealand figures are five (**Moderate**).
- **Too much nitrogen fertiliser gives lots of leaves and few flowers** (**High**). But sources also name *too little* feeding as a cause, and recommend feeding as a fix (**Moderate**). No source says where "enough" becomes "too much".
- **Trees and shrubs nearby compete** for light, water and food, and sources say to plant roses away from them (**Moderate**).
- **Pruning matters, but sources are vague on "too much".** Pruning badly (too much, too little, wrong time) is listed as a cause (**Moderate**). Harder pruning gives fewer, bigger flowers (**Moderate**). Pip's approved winter prune is normal for these roses, and no source says where it becomes too hard.
- **Some roses only flower once a year.** If a rose flowered once in early summer and never again, sources say it may be a once-flowering type (**Moderate**). Pruning those in winter cuts off their flowers (**High**). They are outside Pip's scope.
- **Deadheading keeps repeat roses flowering** (**High**).
- **Blind shoots:** healthy-looking stems with no bud at the tip (**High**). Nobody knows the exact cause (**Moderate**). Sources cut them back to get a new flowering shoot, but disagree on where to cut.
- **Buds that form but don't open:** cool wet weather ("balling"), grey mould, thrips (streaked petals, **High**) or budworm caterpillars (holes in buds). Sources say to pick off the bad buds.
- **Black spot** strips the leaves, and a weakened rose flowers less (**Moderate**).
- **No source gives a tested way to tell the causes apart, or says which is most common.** No source says what a photo can show.

## 3. What was researched

Q1: what causes few or no flowers on an established bush rose. Q2: what a beginner can see that tells the causes apart. Q3: what sources say to do. Q4: where sources disagree and what's left open.

## 4. How to read the strength ratings

| Level | Meaning in this dossier |

| Moderate | Reasonably supported, with notable limitations or some disagreement |

**Fit with what you've already approved.** The findings agree with Basic Care (watering, feeding pattern, deadheading, problems to watch for), the pruning framework (pruning encourages flowering; once-flowering roses are pruned after flowering) and the suckers ARC (rootstock flowers differ). One difference: an Illinois source describes cutting modern roses back by one-half to two-thirds, harder than the one-third to one-half in the approved pruning ARC.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Sun hours.** 4 (David Austin), 5 (Tui, Palmers, one US extension answer), 6 (most), 6–8 (Colorado State, American Rose Society, Yates Australia).
- **C-2 Feeding.** Too much nitrogen stops flowers; but too little feeding is also blamed, and feeding is a recommended fix.
- **C-3 Blind shoot cause.** Unknown; each source suggests different factors.
- **C-4 Where to cut a blind shoot.** RHS: back by half to a strong bud. Victoria State Rose Garden: to the next lower stipule (the small leafy flap at a leaf base). Tantau: to the next full five-leaflet leaf.
- **C-5 First-season flowering.** Treloar: most roses flower in their first season. David Austin and Colorado State: expect fewer flowers.
- **C-6 How hard is too hard.** Over-pruning is listed as a cause, yet hard pruning is standard for these roses; no source draws the line.
- **C-7 Balling.** RHS: a weather disorder with no chemical treatment. Treloar: a sign of grey mould, treated with fungicide.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve the findings as `ARC-BUSHROSE-NOFLOWERS-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline.

2. **How does Pip answer "why isn't my rose flowering?"**
   (a) a list of possible causes, each with its sign and what sources say to do, strongest first, without naming one cause; (b) a guided sort by what the gardener sees (no buds / buds that fail / flowered once / different flowers); (c) general care reminders and experienced help.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).* The sorting in (b) is the ROC's own idea and no source backs it yet.

3. **Which sun figure?**
   (a) at least six hours; (b) the range, four to eight, most often six; (c) at least five (the NZ figures).
   *ROC recommendation: (b).*

4. **How is feeding presented?**
   (a) warn only about too much nitrogen; (b) both: too much nitrogen gives leaves not flowers, and too little feeding is also a cause, pointing to the approved feeding pattern; (c) leave feeding out.
   *ROC recommendation: (b),* without the potassium claim (one fertiliser company).

5. **A rose that flowered once in early summer and stopped?**
   (a) Pip says it may be a once-flowering type, outside Pip's scope, and suggests help or the rose-type check; (b) Pip treats it as a bush rose and works through the causes.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

6. **Blind shoots: guide the cut?**
   (a) yes: cut back to get new growth, using the RHS cut (back by half to a strong bud), with the other cut points recorded; (b) recognition only.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

7. **Buds that don't open: what does Pip give?**
   (a) the sign and the non-spray steps sources give (pick off affected buds, better air flow, water at the base); (b) recognition only, with help for treatment; (c) everything, including sprays.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

8. **Accept the defaults in R-1 to R-4, or commission any of them?**
