# Pip's Answers — Which Months to Do Each Rose Job in the United Kingdom

This is everything Pip would say about which months to prune, feed, deadhead, plant, move and clear up around a rose in the United Kingdom. **You are asked for one thing: approval of these answers.** Approving them also approves the research behind them and lets them be published. Nothing else needs reading.

## 1. What gardeners will see

The six questions are already in the app, with answers for New Zealand and Australia. Today a gardener with a rose in the United Kingdom who opens them is told Pip has nothing for that place yet. On approval they see the statements below.

Every statement is shown only for a rose in the United Kingdom. Two are shown only in part of it, and that is marked. Each statement is shown with the label in brackets. A list of the sources sits under each answer for a gardener who wants it.

### Which months do I prune my rose?

- In the UK, the main pruning is done in late winter or early spring, in February or March. Two rose growers say January or February, and one garden company says on a frost-free day between November and March. Months are only a guide, because frosts vary from year to year. *[High confidence]*
- In the south of the UK, do the main pruning around mid-February. In northern and colder areas, wait until March; one garden company says mid to late March. Months are only a guide, because frosts vary from year to year. *[Moderate confidence]*
- In the UK, do the main pruning in February or March. For the Midlands and the south of England, one rose nursery says November or December instead. Months are only a guide, because frosts vary from year to year. *[Sources disagree]* **Gardeners in the Midlands and the south of England only.**
- In northern and colder areas of the UK, wait until March for the main pruning. For the north of England and Scotland, one rose nursery says between January and late February instead. Months are only a guide, because frosts vary from year to year. *[Sources disagree]* **Gardeners in the north of England or in Scotland only.**
- Prune on a frost-free day, before new growth begins and the buds start to open. One source says to prune when growth is just resuming. *[Moderate confidence]*
- Two rose growers say a rose can be trimmed in autumn, between September and November, so that winter wind does not rock or damage it. One of them says this is for tall roses, and that October is too early to cut a rose back hard. Months are only a guide. *[Low confidence]*
- One rose grower says not to cut a rose back hard in autumn, because frost can then damage the cut tips. *[Precaution · Low confidence]*

### When do I start and stop feeding?

- Give the first feed in spring, in March or April, before the rose flowers. One rose grower says towards the end of February or in early March. Months are only a guide. *[High confidence]*
- Feed the rose again in mid-summer, after its first round of flowers. One rose grower says in July. Months are only a guide. *[High confidence]*
- One rose grower says to give the last feed before the end of August, so that the rose can slow down for winter and frost does not damage new growth. Months are only a guide, because frosts vary from year to year. *[Precaution · Low confidence]*

### When do I stop deadheading?

- Deadhead (remove the faded flowers) while the rose is in flower, through summer and into autumn. One rose grower says to keep deadheading in October. Months are only a guide. *[Moderate confidence]*
- If your rose is grown for its hips (the rose's fruit), stop deadheading in time for the hips to form. One source says to stop in mid-summer, and one rose grower says after the second round of flowers. *[Moderate confidence]*
- For a rose that is not grown for its hips (the rose's fruit), I couldn't find a month to stop deadheading in the UK. A local rose society is the place to ask. *[No source found]*

### Which months do I plant or move a rose?

- In the UK, plant a bare-root rose (one sold without soil on its roots) while it is dormant (not actively growing), between October and early March. Months are only a guide. *[High confidence]*
- Don't plant a bare-root rose while the ground is frozen. One rose grower says to keep it in a bucket of water or compost until the ground has thawed. *[Moderate confidence]*
- A rose in a pot can be planted at any time of year, as long as the ground is not frozen, waterlogged or very dry. One garden company says the ideal months are September to November and March to May. Months are only a guide. *[High confidence]*
- One rose grower says not to plant a potted rose in extreme heat. *[Precaution · Low confidence]*
- Move an established rose while it is dormant, between November and February. One source says shrubs that drop their leaves in winter can be moved at any time from late October to mid-March. Months are only a guide. *[Moderate confidence]*

### When do I clear up and spray in winter?

- Clear away fallen rose leaves in autumn, so that disease has nowhere to linger. One source says to destroy them, or bury them under a layer of mulch (material spread over the soil). *[High confidence]*
- One rose grower says to pick up fallen leaves and cut off diseased leaves in October and November, and to tidy the beds in February, ready for spring. Months are only a guide. *[Low confidence]*
- One source says to pick off any leaves still on the rose over the winter months, if practical, and dispose of them, to help against the disease black spot. *[Low confidence]*
- One rose grower says to check the base of your rose for exposed roots before the winter weather takes hold, and to build up a layer of mulch around any that show, to protect them from the cold. *[Precaution · Low confidence]*
- I couldn't find UK advice from a gardening body or rose society on spraying roses in winter. A local rose society is the place to ask. *[No source found]*
- I couldn't find UK advice on mounding soil over the base of a rose for winter. A local rose society is the place to ask. *[No source found]*

### How do I allow for frost and my own garden?

- On average, the last frost of spring comes earlier in southern and coastal parts of the UK, often before the end of April, the national weather service says. Further north and on higher ground it often comes well into May, and in a few places in June. Late frosts still happen, so months are only a guide. *[Moderate confidence]*
- Frosts go on later in spring on an exposed, rural or high site than near the coast. In a valley, cold air drains downhill and pools overnight, so frost is especially likely there. *[Moderate confidence]*

## 2. What you should know

- **One decision inside the approval: the nursery that prunes early.** One rose nursery says to do the main pruning in November or December in the Midlands and the south, and in January or February in the north of England and Scotland. That runs against the Royal Horticultural Society and the rest. The rules say a disagreement is told, so Pip shows it: second, as one source, marked "Sources disagree", and only in the regions the nursery names. The research had recommended leaving it out. If you would rather Pip did not show it, say so and I'll remove those two statements; nothing else depends on them.
- **The UK needed far fewer local statements than Australia.** Nearly every UK page is written for the whole country. 24 of the 26 statements are for all of the United Kingdom. "The south" and "northern and colder areas" are said in words to everyone, as you decided for frost, because no source says where the line is.
- **No winter spray for the UK.** The only page that describes one is from a company that sells it, and the research could not establish whether such sprays are authorised for home gardens there. Pip says it found no advice from a gardening body or rose society.
- **Where Pip couldn't find an answer, it says so.** There are three: a month to stop deadheading, winter spraying, and mounding soil over the base for winter.
- **Four precautions,** each from one rose grower: don't cut a rose back hard in autumn; give the last feed before the end of August; don't plant a potted rose in extreme heat; cover exposed roots with mulch before winter.
- **7 statements carry a Low label,** because they rest on one source or on one or two rose growers, and say so.
- **Two general care answers already published sit awkwardly beside these.** They have no country set, so a UK gardener sees them too. One says to stop deadheading in early to mid autumn; the other says to mound soil over the base of the rose for winter. Neither comes from a UK source, and here Pip says it found no UK month to stop deadheading and no UK advice on mounding. I'd suggest a follow-up to mark those two general statements for the countries their sources speak of. That needs your agreement.
- **7 findings are left out.** Three are about the research (how sources describe areas). One is the winter spray. One says pruning that slips into March is still worth doing; it is one grower's view, and the rules keep it on record. The other two are frost dates for tender plants and a climate trend.
- **The check corrected the research before you saw it.** Every quotation was on its page. The main fault was months: ranges wider than any one page, built by joining pages that each give one month or only a season. 28 findings were reworded or had their level changed (two lowered a level). 4 were added and 6 disagreements the research had not recorded.
- **Tested in a local copy of the app** for fourteen plants, eleven of them across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Each saw its own place's statements and no others. Not checked: the signed-in screens and a real phone.
