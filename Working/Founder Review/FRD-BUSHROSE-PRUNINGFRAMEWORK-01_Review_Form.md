### What comes out (Brief 5.2)

#### AF-2 — Damaged or diseased wood | Strength: High

> Damaged wood (broken, cold-injured, or damaged by wind or insects) and diseased wood is cut out.

[[claim:af2|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af2m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 21 sources; 19 organisations; 4 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: The most widely supported finding in the research. Dead wood itself is covered by an already approved record and was not re-assessed here.]]

#### AF-3 — Crossing or rubbing stems | Strength: High

> Stems that cross or rub against each other are cut out.

[[claim:af3|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af3m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 11 sources; 10 organisations; 4 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Sources say to remove them, but none says how to choose which of two crossing stems to take out (gap U-2).]]

#### AF-4 — Inward-growing or centre-crowding stems | Strength: Moderate

> Stems that grow in towards the centre of the bush, or crowd it, are cut out.

[[claim:af4|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af4m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 8 sources; 8 organisations; 3 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: No source defines inward growth or separates it from crossing or general crowding, so the count includes some loosely matching statements. No UK source addresses it.]]

#### AF-5 — Weak, thin or spindly growth | Strength: High

> Weak, thin, spindly or twiggy growth is cut out.

[[claim:af5|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af5m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 13 sources; 12 organisations; 3 of 4 groups.]]

#### AF-6 — The pencil-thickness rule | Strength: Moderate

> Sources use 'about as thick as a pencil' as the dividing line: canes thinner than that are treated as weak, canes thicker are worth keeping.

[[claim:af6|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af6m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 9 sources; 8 organisations; 2 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: All nine sources are American, and the rule may be one rule of thumb repeated, not independently confirmed. One source describes it as less important for floribundas.]]

#### AF-7 — Old, woody or unproductive canes | Strength: Moderate

> Old, woody or unproductive canes are removed in favour of younger canes.

[[claim:af7|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af7m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 13 sources; 12 organisations; 4 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Sources differ on how old is 'old' (C6), and none explains how to recognise old wood (gap U-9). Age of wood is not one of the six supported observations.]]

#### AF-8 — Rootstock suckers | Strength: Moderate

> On grafted roses, shoots growing from below the graft (rootstock suckers) are removed. Two sources say to tear them away at the point they start.

[[claim:af8|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af8m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 7 sources; 6 organisations; 3 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Concerns grafted roses only. Sources give little on recognising a sucker. Suckers are not one of the six supported observations.]]

#### AF-9 — Which canes are kept | Strength: Moderate

> The canes that are kept are the strong, healthy, vigorous ones, and in several sources the younger canes or those grown last year.

[[claim:af9|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af9m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 10 sources; 9 organisations; 3 of 4 groups.]]

#### AF-10 — New shoots from the base | Strength: Low

> New shoots that arise from the base of the bush or from the graft are treated as a sign of good health and as a way of renewing the framework.

[[claim:af10|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]

#### AF-11 — Reasons for removing crossing stems | Strength: Low

[[claim:af11|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]

### How the cut is made (Brief 5.6)

#### AF-21 — Bud direction | Strength: High

> For most cuts back to a bud, choose a bud that faces outward, away from the centre of the bush.

[[claim:af21|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af21m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 18 sources; 16 organisations; 3 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Applies to cuts back to a bud. Cutting a stem off at its origin is a different cut (AF-29). No UK source supports it (the RHS gives no bud-direction advice), and two sources allow exceptions.]]

#### AF-22 — Distance above the bud | Strength: Moderate

> Sources that give a distance most often say to cut about one-quarter inch (about 6 mm) above the bud.

[[claim:af22|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af22m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 11 sources; 9 organisations; 2 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: All eleven sources are American, and a quarter inch is a widely repeated rule of thumb. Three American sources give a different distance (C2).]]

#### AF-23 — Angle of the cut | Strength: Moderate

> Sources that give an angle describe a slanting cut of roughly 45 degrees, not a flat one.

[[claim:af23|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af23m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 7 sources; 7 organisations; 2 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Support is from New Zealand and US sources only. One source gives a range of 30 to 45 degrees.]]

#### AF-24 — Direction of the slant | Strength: Low

> Sources that say which way the slant falls most often say it slopes away from the bud.

[[claim:af24|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af24m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 5 sources; 5 organisations; 3 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Rated Low because the wording conflicts: some sources say 'away from the bud', others 'in the same direction as the bud'. They may describe one shape or two, and the research could not settle it (C3).]]

#### AF-25 — Tool by stem thickness | Strength: High

> The tool is chosen by the thickness of the stem: bypass pruners for thin canes, loppers for thicker canes, and a pruning saw for the thickest or oldest.

[[claim:af25|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af25m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 9 sources; 9 organisations; 3 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Sources differ over bypass and anvil pruners (C9).]]

#### AF-26 — Where hand pruners give way to loppers | Strength: Low

> Sources that give a figure say hand pruners are suitable up to about three-quarters of an inch. One general woody-plant text says one-half inch.

[[claim:af26|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af26m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 3 sources (OSU, ISU, TAMK); 3 organisations; 1 of 4 groups. Worth knowing: Only three sources (C8).]]

#### AF-27 — Sharp, clean tools | Strength: High

> Tools should be sharp and clean, so that the cut is clean and the cane is not crushed.

[[claim:af27|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af27m|Backing and caveats|Supported by 12 sources; 11 organisations; 3 of 4 groups.]]

#### AF-28 — Disinfecting tools | Strength: Moderate

[[claim:af28|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]

#### AF-29 — Cutting removed stems at their origin | Strength: Moderate

#### C1 — Final height and how hard to prune

[[claim:c1|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c1m|Why they differ, and what it affects|Some reasons: the bloom size wanted, winter damage, and rose class. No source reconciles the figures. Affects: AF-17, AF-18.]]

#### C2 — Distance of the cut above the bud

> Eleven sources say a quarter inch. KCRS says a quarter to a half inch; TAMK and TAMN say a half inch.

[[claim:c2|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c2m|Why they differ, and what it affects|OSU says a quarter inch 'ensures the wound can heal properly'. No source gives a reason for a half inch. Affects: AF-22.]]

#### C3 — Which way the slant falls

> OSU, ILL, UCG and DUN say the cut slopes away from the bud, and ARS explains that sap then runs down the far side. ISU and ISUF say the slant is 'in the same direction as the bud'; CLE says 'angled at the same angle as the bud'. UMD and TAMK say only that the slant lets water run off. CSU gives a range of 30 to 45 degrees without a direction.

[[claim:c3|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c3m|Why they differ, and what it affects|The wordings may describe one shape or two. The research could not settle it. Affects: AF-24.]]

#### C4 — Sealing cut ends

> CLE, ILL, UMD, KCRS and ARS advise sealing the cut ends of larger canes to deter cane borers. UGA says sealing is optional. TAMK, a general woody-plant text, says wound dressings are not recommended or necessary. UK and New Zealand sources do not mention sealing.

[[claim:c4|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c4m|Why they differ, and what it affects|The one reason given for sealing is cane borers. Affects: AF-30.]]

#### C5 — How boldly to prune

> UMD says to start conservatively and that routine excessive pruning harms flowering. UCG says 'When in doubt, cut it out.' OSU says underpruning is the most common cause of an unproductive rose. AEO says 'Be brave.' CLE says to prune weak-growing varieties lightly and vigorous ones more severely.

[[claim:c5|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c5m|Why they differ, and what it affects|No source gives a reason for its stance, and the audiences are not stated. Affects: gap U-1 (how doubt is treated).]]

#### C6 — How old is 'old enough to remove'

> RHS: all three-year-old wood. UCG and UCF: old, craggy or gray canes. YAT: the oldest, thick, woody canes. UAEX: one or two of the older, woodier canes. ILL: about one-third of the oldest canes (shrub roses).

[[claim:c6|Accept as recorded;Commission research;Give a direction]]
[[more:c6m|Why they differ, and what it affects|Not given. Affects: AF-7.]]

> Counts of 3 to 5, 3 to 6, 3 to 7, 4 to 5, 4 to 8, 5 to 6, 5 to 7 and 6 to 8, depending on the source.
