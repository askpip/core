# Decision Brief — Which Months Should I Do Each Rose Job, Where I Live in Australia?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-TIMING-AU-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief, then answer the eight questions in the Review Form: six on how Pip should show months in Australia; one on further research; and the overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-AU-01`, approve with changes, request further work, or decline.**

You are not asked to decide each of the 45 findings. Section 4 explains which ones Pip may use, and section 5 marks each one. You can still change, exclude or flag any finding in the form's last box.

## 2. The short version

- **Australian rose sources mostly don't use the five gardening climate zones.** They split gardens by frost: "mild" or "near the coast" against "cold", "frosty" or "inland" (**High**). None of them says where those lines fall (**Low**).
- **The gardening zone guides and the Bureau of Meteorology don't agree with each other.** The guides name five zones by listing towns (**Moderate**) and give the same towns different names (**Moderate**). The Bureau puts Brisbane and Alice Springs in the same "subtropical" zone (**High**).
- **Main winter pruning: July in most areas** (**High**); late June to the end of July near the coast (**Moderate**); some sources start in June (**Moderate**); **August in cold, frosty or inland areas** (**High**), because frost kills the new shoots pruning brings on (**High**). One Canberra source goes to early September (**Low**).
- **Feeding: start in late August or September** (**High**); **last feed in February or March** (**Moderate**). Two sources feed later, in April or May (**Low**).
- **Summer trim: January to March**, to time the autumn flowers (**High**). No Australian source gives a month to stop deadheading (**Low**).
- **Bare-root roses: plant in June and July** (**High**), with some sources allowing May or August (**Moderate**). **Potted roses: any time, avoiding hot weather** (**Moderate**). **Move a rose in winter** (**Moderate**).
- **Clear up leaves and prunings, and spray if you spray, straight after pruning**, usually July (**High**). No Australian source describes winter protection like mounding soil (**Low**).
- **The tropics, subtropics and arid inland are thinly covered.** Roses are harder there because of disease (**Moderate**). A Darwin-area garden prunes lightly all year (**Low**); near Brisbane, a rose farm and a public rose garden prune in June or July (**Moderate**). No source gives arid-zone months (**Low**).
- **No Australian rose source uses bud swell** to time pruning; they use the month, dormancy or frost (**Low**). That doesn't conflict with the bud-swell check Pip already uses.

## 3. What was researched

Q1: which months for the main winter pruning, and what signal. Q2: when to start and stop feeding. Q3: when to stop deadheading and summer pruning. Q4: when to plant bare-root and potted roses and move a rose. Q5: when to clear up, spray and protect for winter. Q6: which climate zones rose sources use, where their edges are, and which suit Hybrid Teas and Floribundas. Q7: how a gardener can be placed in a zone. Q8: what sources say about early or late seasons and zone edges.

- **Yes, locally.** The finding says when or how a job is done in one named country, region or place, and comes from a reliable source: a rose or horticultural society, a botanic garden, a university or extension service, or a government body. Pip shows it only to gardeners in that place, with the source named.

**Fit with what you've already approved.** The findings agree with the dormancy ARC that timing varies by climate and must be set locally, and they supply Australian months for it. No Australian source uses bud swell, but none contradicts it, so the bud-swell check stays as it is. They agree with the basic-care ARC on a spring-start, summer-taper feeding pattern and on clearing leaves in winter, and supply months. **They partly conflict with that ARC on stopping deadheading in autumn,** because Victorian and Western Australian sources carry on into April and May. **The basic-care ARC's winter mounding finding has no Australian support.** The bare-root months, moving in winter, the winter spray after pruning and the frost risk agree with the pending planting, spraying and hard-pruning dossiers; the sources shared with them were re-opened and confirmed, and two South Australian sources and a Canberra source extend bare-root planting into August.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Zone names and edges.** Two gardening guides use the same towns but different names ("Cold Temperate" or "Cool"; "Warm Temperate" or "Temperate"). A nursery puts Adelaide with Melbourne. The Bureau of Meteorology puts Alice Springs with Brisbane; the gardening guides put Alice Springs in the arid zone.
- **C-2 Can pruning start in June?** Rose Society of Victoria's pruning guide, ABC Gardening Australia (2008), Brisbane's New Farm Park, Ross Roses and the WA society: yes. The Victorian society's monthly calendar, the SA society and ABC Gardening Australia magazine: July.
- **C-3 When cold areas prune.** Early August, the whole of August, late August, the end of winter, or (Canberra) late July to early September. One SA nursery says frosty districts prune in July.
- **C-4 When feeding starts.** Late August, end of August, early September, mid-spring after the first flowers, or by the plant's growth or three weeks after pruning.
- **C-5 When the last feed is.** February, March, early autumn, April (sandy soils, SA) or May (WA). Near Brisbane, stop before winter.
- **C-6 The edges of the bare-root window.** From "May, June and July" to "June, July or early August"; August in Canberra.
- **C-7 The month of the winter spray.** July in most; August in one SA calendar; June to July in WA; August or early September in Canberra. It always follows pruning.
- **C-8 Brisbane roses "without a rest".** The same Brisbane-area segment says roses grow through winter without a rest, and that the farm holds back feed so they can rest before July pruning.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve this dossier as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-AU-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline. Approval accepts the research record; which findings Pip may use follows from section 4.

2. **Which bands does Pip use, and where are the edges?**
   (a) The two bands rose sources use (mild, coastal or frost-free; cold, frosty or inland), with subtropical and tropical shown separately where sources give months, and no line drawn; (b) the five gardening zones with their town lists; (c) the Bureau of Meteorology zones; (d) one band per state, using each state rose society's calendar.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).* The months in the sources hang on frost, not on the five zones, and the zone schemes disagree.
   *Note:* AF-17 (the tropics) is Low and comes from one broadcast segment, so it stays on record only. Under option (a) Pip would then give no pruning timing for the tropics, as decision 7 option (a) also says.

3. **How does Pip work out which band a gardener is in?**
   (a) From the town or region they enter, using the town lists; (b) from GPS and a climate map; (c) the gardener answers two questions (frosts most winters? near the coast or inland?); (d) suggest from the town where it's listed, then confirm with the questions.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).* GPS placement isn't recommended until it's researched (R-5a in the FRD).

4. **How does Pip show the months alongside the bud-swell check?**
   (a) Months as context ("Rose societies in your area usually prune in July"), with the bud-swell check still deciding; (b) months replace the check in Australia; (c) no months, season only.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

5. **What does Pip say near a band edge, or in an unusually early or late season?**
   (a) Show both neighbouring windows, say conditions vary locally, and in frost areas wait for the last frost, with the bud-swell check deciding; (b) show the gardener's own band only; (c) refer to the state rose society.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

6. **Where sources give different months, what does Pip show?**
   (a) The full range for the band ("June or July, most often July"); (b) one month, the most common; (c) the gardener's state rose society's month.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).*

7. **What does Pip do in the tropics, subtropics and arid inland?**
   (a) Months only where sources give them (subtropical pruning June or July; none for tropical or arid), season-level otherwise, with a note that roses are harder in hot, humid areas; (b) no months for any of the three; (c) use southern months for all three.
   *ROC recommendation: (a).* Option (c) would borrow months from another band, which the commission rules out.
