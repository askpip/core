## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Ask Pip gives season-level timing only, derived from the gardener's hemisphere. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for Canada. Canada has its own Plant Hardiness Zones (Natural Resources Canada), which differ from the USDA zones used in the United States, and Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses need winter protection across much of the country.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band, and what local signal do they tie it to?
- **Q2** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
- **Q3** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
- **Q4** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
- **Q5** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where described, when is a winter spray done; and when does winter protection go on and come off?
- **Q6** How do Canadian sources tie rose timing to Canada's Plant Hardiness Zones, to USDA zones, to local frost dates, or to province? Which bands are needed, and what month windows do sources give for each?
- **Q7** Do Canadian sources say gardeners can readily find their hardiness zone, and whether it is Canadian or USDA? If not, what would a beginner need to be asked?
- **Q8** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band?

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Hardy shrub-rose series bred for Canada (for example Explorer and Parkland) are out of scope. Every finding records which band it applies to. Months from one band are not applied to another. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

## 3. Research Plan

| Question | Information required | Source categories sought |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Pruning months and signals, by region or zone | Canadian Rose Society and local rose societies, Master Gardener groups, Landscape Ontario, botanic gardens, Canadian rose growers |
| Q2 | First and last feed months, by region | The same |
| Q3 | Deadheading and summer-pruning cut-offs | The same |
| Q4 | Planting months for bare-root and potted roses; moving months | The same |
| Q5 | Fall clear-up, winter sprays, winter protection on and off | The same, plus Quebec French-language sources |
| Q6 | How sources divide Canada: Canadian zones, USDA zones, frost, province or town | Natural Resources Canada, peer-reviewed zone research, the sources above |
| Q7 | How a gardener finds their zone; Canadian versus USDA labelling | Natural Resources Canada, garden writers, garden centres |
| Q8 | Handling of early or late seasons and band edges | The sources above |

**Method.**

- Web searches were run for each question and for each region (coastal British Columbia, BC interior, the Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, the Maritimes), in English and French.
- Every source cited was opened. Pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question; each extraction was asked for exact sentences. For CRS-WP, CRS-SP, CRS-PL, CRS-OC, HRS, LO-TP, LO-YR, RBG, TIMG, PG, LBG-WP, GGC, PAL, BCL, FVRF, GW, DNA, MOSS, JD, PV, LP, TC and TMG-F the quotations relied on were re-checked against a second extraction asking for the sentence character for character.
- Every quotation used is under 25 words and is recorded, by finding, in the Source Register. French quotations are given in French with an English gloss.
- Pages that could not be opened, or that were opened but are outside scope or not rose-specific, are recorded in §4.3 and support no finding.
- Findings are separated by claim so each receives its own Evidence Confidence Level (EAS §3.2), and each states the band or province it applies to.
- Approved findings in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`, and the pending `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` and `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01`, were checked for agreement only (§7, final subsection). Landscape Ontario's "Roses: type and pruning", cited in `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01`, was re-opened and re-quoted for this commission.

**Anticipated challenges.** Canadian rose guidance is concentrated in Ontario and in national pages; Hybrid Teas are less often recommended on the Prairies and in Quebec, where sources favour hardy shrub roses (out of scope). Many sources describe signals (forsythia, ground freeze, thaw) rather than months.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** All rose sources in §4.1 address Hybrid Tea and Floribunda care in Canada, or tender roses including them. NRCAN-ST, NRCAN-OD, SR-MCK, LBG-MAP and PG address hardiness zones, not roses; they bear on Q6 and Q7 only.

**Source reliability.** The Canadian Rose Society, a local rose society, two Master Gardener groups, a botanic garden, a trade association, Natural Resources Canada and a peer-reviewed study are recognised authorities. Garden writers, a newspaper column and a magazine are given less weight. Nurseries and garden centres are practised growers, given less weight as independent authority.

**Consistency.** Sources agree strongly that Hybrid Teas and Floribundas need winter protection across most of Canada, that protection goes on after hard frosts or ground freeze and comes off with the thaw, that spring pruning is tied to forsythia flowering, that feeding starts with spring growth, and that fall cutting is limited to shortening tall canes. They differ on the last feed (mid-July to late August), on when fall planting is done, on how early protection goes on in Quebec, and on how soon it comes off.

**Independence.** The four Canadian Rose Society pages count once; HRS and MOSS partly relay CRS; the two Landscape Ontario, two Toronto Master Gardener and two Larry Hodgson pages each count once per organisation or writer.

**Coverage.** Ontario is well covered; coastal British Columbia moderately; the Prairies and Quebec mainly through the Canadian Rose Society and commercial sources; the Maritimes not at all. The main botanic garden source for Quebec (Espace pour la vie) could not be opened.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. Many statements are signals rather than months. Most sources do not say which zone system they use.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1 — When is the last feed (AF-26 to AF-29)?** CRS-OC (national): "a third application may be made in mid-July. Later applications are not recommended"; HRS and LO-YR (Ontario) and PV (Quebec) agree on mid-July. TMG-F (Toronto): not "after the end of July"; PAL (Ontario): "early August"; GGC (Ontario) and DNA (Alberta): mid-August; LO-YR also says "fed every week from early spring to late August"; GW (coastal BC): "by late summer"; FVRF (coastal BC): none "from September onward". The range within Ontario alone is mid-July to late August. Unresolved.

**C-2 — When is fall planting done, and where (AF-35, AF-36)?** CRS-PL: "in September for fall planting", but "not recommended across the Prairies Provinces". RBG (Ontario): "spring or late autumn". TIMG (Kingston): bare-root grafted roses "for late fall planting". September and late autumn are different windows; none of these sources explains the difference. Unresolved.

**C-3 — When does protection go on (AF-41, AF-42, AF-43)?** CRS-WP, LO-YR, GGC and LBG-WP: after hard frosts or once the ground is freezing; LO-YR adds "not too early". LP (Quebec, commercial): roses are cut "juste avant les premiers gels" (just before the first frosts) and covered "rapidement" (quickly) with a cone. JD (Quebec, commercial): wait for the cooler temperatures of November. LP's "before the first frosts" conflicts in direction with the others. Unresolved.

**C-4 — How soon does protection come off (AF-46 to AF-48, AF-50)?** CRS-SP: "In late spring, when the soil has thawed and a hard freeze is no longer expected". LBG-WP: "as soon as temperatures start to regularly rise above freezing during the day". JD: as soon as snow and ice allow. LO-TP: "As soon as thawing permits". MOSS and GGC uncover in stages. The sources agree that thaw governs but differ on whether to remove early or wait until hard freezes are past. Unresolved.

**C-5 — What is the spring signal (AF-13, AF-14, AF-20, AF-23)?** Most sources use forsythia flowering. LO-TP: at forsythia, "The buds should be swelling and quite obvious but showing no leaves." TC and GW name bud swell; RBG "just before bud break". CRS-WP (Zones 2–4) removes winter kill "when the bush begins to leaf out again". BCL says late pruning "even after the rose has already leafed out" only delays flowering. The sources describe stages from bud swell to leaf-out. Unresolved; see `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` in §7.

**C-6 — Which zone system is meant (AF-3, AF-5, AF-8, AF-9)?** CRS-WP gives Zones 2–4, 5–6 and 7–9 without naming the system. LBG-WP gives "zone 5" label ratings and "(AgCan zone 6)" in the same article. TIMG and GGC state Canadian zones. PG says Canadian and USDA numbers "can not be used interchangeably". A zone number in a Canadian rose source can therefore mean either system. Unresolved; R-2.

**C-7 — Fall pruning (AF-31, AF-32, AF-33).** CRS-WP cuts canes back in fall in every zone band (to about 70 cm around Thanksgiving in Zones 2–4). LO-YR: "Do not prune at this time, unless the canes are so tall they could whip about". GGC trims the top third of tall canes but leaves major shaping until spring. GW (Okanagan): "Avoid fall pruning". The sources agree that major pruning waits for spring; they differ on how much is cut in fall. Unresolved.

**Uncertainty preserved.** No source gives Maritimes timing (AF-12); no Canadian source gives a pruning month for Quebec or the Prairies (AF-21); no Canadian source gives timing for moving an established rose (AF-38); several months rest on single commercial sources (AF-29, AF-44, AF-45, AF-47, AF-48).

## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim and states the band or province it applies to.

### Climate bands and placing the gardener (Q6, Q7)

**AF-1 (established; national).** Canada's official zones are calculated from several climate variables; the USDA system uses average extreme minimum temperature alone. NRCAN-OD: "The map is based on a formula using seven climate variables that influence plant survival". NRCAN-ST lists "lowest average daily temperature in the region's coldest month; length of frost-free periods; rainfall totals from June to November". SR-MCK: "In the United States, the plant hardiness system involves averaging annual extreme minimum temperatures over a period of interest". LBG-MAP: the USDA approach is "based solely on the average annual minimum temperature."

**AF-2 (supported interpretation, single issuing body; national).** NRCAN-OD: Natural Resources Canada also shows "an alternative plant hardiness zone approach using just one climate variable: average extreme minimum temperature for the period 1991 to 2020."

**AF-3 (supported interpretation; national, with an Ontario and an Alberta source).** Canadian and USDA zone numbers are not interchangeable. PG: the two maps "are not the same, and can not be used interchangeably"; for American material, "just remember to mentally add a number to any zone that is indicated." TIMG (Kingston): "The United States Department of Agriculture Map's zones are different- zone 6 here may be zone 5 there."

**AF-4 (expert opinion, single commercial source; national).** PG: "Many Canadian box stores use, without ever stating it, the American hardiness zone system."

**AF-5 (expert opinion, one writer; national, writer in Quebec).** LBG-WP: "The label on bush roses usually accords them a zone 5 hardiness rating, but that's actually a blatant lie." LBG-HZ: "Their zone 5 label is designed to give you an idea of what the plants can take" if carefully protected, and "Most are hardy only to zone 8." The writer does not state which zone system the zone 8 figure uses.

**AF-6 (established; national).** NRCAN-ST: "many areas shifting by half a zone or more and some by as much as two full zones". SR-MCK: "zone values have increased by between half a zone and two full zones across the country". LBG-MAP: "a widespread increase in hardiness zones across Canada". MOSS (Alberta): "Edmonton and Calgary are both zone 4a on the 2025 hardiness map".

**AF-7 (expert opinion, limited; national).** LBG-MAP: "Consult the hardiness zone map by municipality to find out which zone you are in." NRCAN-ST refers gardeners to "the plant hardiness zone website" for "choosing plants that will thrive in a particular area" but, as extracted, does not describe the lookup. The NRCan map page itself could not be opened (§4.3).

**AF-8 (single source; national, by zone band).** CRS-WP sets out winter protection under the headings "Zones 2-4", "Zones 5-6" and "Zones 7-9", mentions "areas where Chinook winds are common" under Zones 2–4 and "Coastal B.C" under Zones 7–9, and does not state whether the zones are Canadian or USDA.

**AF-9 (supported interpretation; Ontario, BC, Quebec, Alberta).** Other sources tie timing to named places. TMG-P: "In Toronto this is usually in April or May". GGC: "around late April or early May in Georgina". BCL: "early March in the Lower Mainland, later in the north and interior of the province." FVRF: "On average, that happens here in late February to mid-March." Where a zone is named, it is the Canadian one: TIMG, "Zone 5a-6a on the Natural Resources Canada Map"; GGC, "Canadian zone 5"; LBG-WP, "(AgCan zone 6)"; MOSS, zone 4a "on the 2025 hardiness map". No source located ties rose timing to a local frost date.

**AF-10 (established; Ontario, Quebec, the Prairies, national).** Hybrid Teas and Floribundas are described as needing winter protection. CRS-WP (Zones 2–4): "Tender roses (Hybrid Teas, Floribundas, Austins and Grandifloras) require layers of winter protection." TIMG (Kingston): "Winter protection is advised for this group". PV (Quebec): "les rosiers hybrides de thé, floribunda et grandiflora ont absolument besoin d'une protection hivernale" (absolutely need winter protection). LP (Quebec) puts them among "rosiers non rustiques" (non-hardy roses). MOSS (Alberta): they "need real protection every year." LBG-WP: "for gardeners in colder climates, good winter protection is absolutely necessary."

**AF-11 (supported interpretation; coastal British Columbia).** CRS-WP (Zones 7–9): mounding "For tender cultivars", "Otherwise, winter protection is not usually necessary in Coastal B.C". GW: "Protect newly planted roses if extreme cold is forecast". FVRF describes Fraser Valley winters as "generally mild and wet, but are punctuated by some cold arctic outflow systems."

**AF-12 (unresolved question; the Maritimes).** No source giving rose timing for Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island or Newfoundland and Labrador was located.

### Main spring pruning (Q1)

**AF-13 (established; national, Ontario, coastal BC).** Spring pruning is tied to forsythia flowering. CRS-SP: "usually April or when the forsythia is in bloom in your area". HRS: "when the FORSTYHIA is in bloom in your area." LO-TP: "When the yellow flowers of forsythia are in bloom, your roses should be showing signs of life." PAL: "In spring when the Forsythias bloom un-mound and prune your roses." TIMG: "a reblooming rose should be pruned in spring when the forsythia blooms." RBG: "This timing lines up with when forsythia is in flower, a natural reminder!" BCL: pruning "when the yellow-flowered forsythia blooms". FVRF: "watch for Forsythia (pictured) or other early-flowering shrubs to bloom before picking up the pruners."

**AF-14 (supported interpretation; Ontario, coastal BC).** Four sources tie main pruning to buds swelling or bud break. LO-TP: "The buds should be swelling and quite obvious but showing no leaves." TC: "Leave the main pruning of bush roses until late winter, ideally as growth buds show the first signs of swelling." GW: "Late February to early March, just as buds begin to swell." RBG: "in spring just before bud break, but after the last frost" (RBG's sentence concerns dead, damaged or diseased canes).

**AF-15 (supported interpretation; national and Ontario).** In cold-winter areas, pruning follows removal of winter protection. CRS-SP: "Established rose bushes are pruned after the soil mounds have been removed in the spring". HRS: "Rose bushes are pruned after the winter protection has been removed in the spring". PAL: "un-mound and prune". LO-TP removes collars "As soon as thawing permits" and "Somewhat later" removes the soil mound before pruning.

**AF-16 (single source; national, no band named).** CRS-SP: "usually April or when the forsythia is in bloom in your area".

**AF-17 (single source; Ontario, Toronto).** TMG-P: "In Toronto this is usually in April or May, just before new growth begins."

**AF-18 (supported interpretation; coastal British Columbia).** FVRF (Fraser Valley): "On average, that happens here in late February to mid-March." GW: "Late February to early March". BCL: "early March in the Lower Mainland". TC (Vancouver Island): "until late winter". No coastal BC rose society source was opened (§4.3).

**AF-19 (expert opinion, limited; BC north and interior).** BCL: forsythia blooms "later in the north and interior of the province", giving no month. GW, in its Okanagan guidance: "Avoid fall pruning — wait until early spring".

**AF-20 (single source; CRS Zones 2–4).** CRS-WP: "In the spring, when the bush begins to leaf out again, any winter kill can be removed and the bush shaped if necessary." No month is given.

**AF-21 (unresolved question; Quebec, the Prairies, the Maritimes).** No opened source gives a month for main pruning of Hybrid Teas or Floribundas in these regions. The Quebec sources opened give "Au printemps" (in spring) without a month (PV).

**AF-22 (supported interpretation; Ontario, BC).** Pruning too early risks frost damage. TMG-P: "Wait until the danger of a hard frost is past or newly pruned tips may be killed." BCL: "Earlier pruning risks damage from late frosts to the tender, exposed canes". RBG: "after the last frost".

**AF-23 (expert opinion, single source; BC).** BCL: "late pruning, even after the rose has already leafed out, will merely delay flowering by a week or two."

### Feeding (Q2)

**AF-24 (established; national, Ontario, coastal BC).** Spring feeding is tied to clean-up, pruning or new growth. CRS-OC: "When spring clean-up has been completed, fertilize established roses with granular fertilizer". HRS: "When spring clean up has been completed fertilize ESTABLISHED roses." RBG: "Apply organic fertilizers, such as manure and compost, after spring pruning." LO-YR: "Start to fertilize when growth begins." GW: "Apply a balanced rose fertilizer in early spring as new growth appears". PAL: "Spring is the best time to fertilize your roses."

**AF-25 (single source; Ontario).** LO-YR: "Apply in early May, mid-June and again in mid-July."

**AF-26 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario, Quebec).** The last feed is at or before mid-July. CRS-OC: "a third application may be made in mid-July. Later applications are not recommended as plants must ripen wood before winter sets in." HRS: "APPLICATIONS after July ARE NOT RECOMMENDED". LO-YR: "Start thinking of over-wintering your roses in mid-July at the time of your last fertilization." PV (Quebec): "toujours avant la mi-juillet" (always before mid-July).

**AF-27 (supported interpretation; Ontario).** Other Ontario sources give later cut-offs. TMG-F: "Don't feed your roses after the end of July". PAL: "Stop fertilizing in early August." GGC: "Around mid-August, stop fertilizing your roses." LO-YR: "fed every week from early spring to late August." (C-1.)

**AF-28 (expert opinion, commercial; coastal BC).** GW: "Stop fertilizing by late summer to allow plants to prepare for winter." FVRF: "Refrain from fertilizing and pruning from September onward."

**AF-29 (expert opinion, single commercial source; Alberta).** DNA: "In mid August stop all fertilizer."

### Late-season cut-off (Q3)

**AF-30 (supported interpretation; Ontario, BC).** The end of deadheading is tied to frost or season. PAL: "Stop deadheading a month before first expected frost." LO-YR, under "Fall or early winter": "Let the last flowers go to seed; do not prune." BCL: "At the end of the season, leave any rosehips to overwinter on the stems". No Canadian source located gives a month.

**AF-31 (supported interpretation; Alberta, coastal BC, Okanagan, Ontario).** Late-summer and fall pruning are avoided. DNA (Alberta): "Do not prune in late summer." FVRF (coastal BC): no pruning "from September onward." GW (Okanagan): "Avoid fall pruning — wait until early spring". GGC (Ontario): "Leave the major shaping until spring". LO-YR (Ontario): "do not prune."

**AF-32 (established; national, Ontario, Vancouver Island).** Fall cutting is limited to shortening tall canes against winter wind. CRS-WP (Zones 5–6): "Cut canes back to about 70cm to 1m (28-40 in) to prevent them whipping about in the wind." TC: "Restrict pruning in the fall to shortening over-long canes prone to being whipped about". LO-YR: "Do not prune at this time, unless the canes are so tall they could whip about in the winter winds". GGC: "Trim the top third of tall canes on shrub, hybrid tea, and floribunda roses to prevent them from snapping in winter winds."

**AF-33 (single source; CRS Zones 2–4).** CRS-WP: "Around Thanksgiving cut canes back to about 70cm (28 in) and defoliate them." The page does not say which Thanksgiving; MOSS (Alberta) relays the same advice.

### Planting and moving (Q4)

**AF-34 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario).** Bare-root roses are planted in spring. CRS-PL: beds prepared "at least 3 weeks before planting in April, or as soon as the soil is workable for spring planting". HRS (Ontario): "Ideal planting time is early spring, as soon as the ground has thawed." TG: "buy and plant early, usually 1st week of April in most parts of Canada, certainly in Toronto." TIMG (Kingston): bagged roses "best in early spring."

**AF-35 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario).** Some sources allow fall planting, at differing times. CRS-PL: "or in September for fall planting." RBG: "It's best to plant roses in either spring or late autumn." TIMG: "If possible, order bare-root grafted roses for late fall planting." (C-2.)

**AF-36 (single source; the Prairies).** CRS-PL: "Note that fall planting is not recommended across the Prairies Provinces".

**AF-37 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario, Quebec).** CRS-PL: "container-grown roses can be planted anytime during the growing season." PV (Quebec): "La plantation peut s'effectuer à n'importe quel moment de la saison" (planting can be done at any time in the season). TIMG (Kingston): "Potted roses are best planted in the spring or early summer".

**AF-38 (unresolved question; all bands).** No Canadian source located gives timing for moving an established rose. A Toronto Master Gardeners page on transplanting roses returned HTTP 404 (§4.3).

### Winter clear-up, sprays and protection (Q5)

**AF-39 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario, coastal BC).** Dead leaves and debris are cleared from the bed in fall. CRS-WP (Zones 5–6): "Remove all dead leaves and any clippings from the bed." HRS: "Remove all dead leaves and any debris from the bed." GGC: "Clean up debris under and around your rose bushes." GW: "Clean up fallen leaves". CRS-WP places this, in Zones 2–4, with the Thanksgiving cut (AF-33).

**AF-40 (expert opinion, single commercial source; Quebec).** PV: "Au printemps, avant la sortie des bourgeons, il est prudent d'appliquer de l'huile dormance" (in spring, before the buds emerge, it is prudent to apply dormant oil). No other Canadian source located describes a winter spray. Product names are not collected (Research Commission Record §4, Q5).

**AF-41 (established; national, Ontario, Quebec, Alberta).** Protection goes on after hard frosts or ground freezing. CRS-WP (Zones 2–4): "When the ground is at least partially frozen, place a collar around each bush"; (Zones 5–6) "When the ground is frozen, place leaves or branches over the mounds". LO-YR: "When the ground is frozen, (not too early, mid- to late November)". GGC: "Once the ground is starting to freeze, mound 8-12 inches of soil or compost". LBG-WP: "wait until all growth has stopped on your rose bushes and the leaves have dropped off, usually after several bouts with subzero cold". MOSS: "Wait until the rose is fully dormant."

**AF-42 (supported interpretation; Ontario).** LO-YR: "mid- to late November". GGC (Georgina): "aim to start your rose winterizing routine in early to mid-November, or after the first few hard frosts."

**AF-43 (expert opinion, commercial; Quebec).** JD: "il est préférable d'attendre les températures plus fraîches du mois de novembre pour installer cônes de styromousse, toiles et jutes" (it is better to wait for the cooler temperatures of November to install foam cones, fabrics and jute). LP: roses are cut "juste avant les premiers gels" (just before the first frosts) and covered quickly with a foam cone. (C-3.)

**AF-44 (expert opinion, single commercial source; BC, Okanagan).** GW: "In late fall, mound soil or compost around the base of grafted roses".

**AF-45 (expert opinion, single commercial source; Alberta).** DNA: "October 15th at this time, water your roses for the winter." The page then cuts roses to 6 inches and mounds soil; as extracted, it gives no date for the mounding itself.

**AF-46 (established; national, Ontario, Quebec, Alberta).** Protection comes off with the thaw, not by a fixed date. CRS-SP: "In late spring, when the soil has thawed and a hard freeze is no longer expected, carefully remove soil mounds". LO-TP: "As soon as thawing permits, take away the evergreen boughs or remove the rose collars". LO-YR: "Remove the collar and then the soil as it thaws." LBG-WP: "as soon as temperatures start to regularly rise above freezing during the day, it's time to remove cones and fabrics." JD: "Il faut retirer les protections dès que la neige et la glace permettent de le faire" (remove protection as soon as snow and ice allow). GGC: "Once the snow melts and daytime temperatures consistently stay above freezing". (C-4.)

**AF-47 (expert opinion, single commercial source; Ontario, Georgina).** GGC: "around late April or early May in Georgina".

**AF-48 (expert opinion, single commercial source; Alberta).** DNA: "Approximately May long weekend remove pail." and "Leave pail on the roses until night time temperature is plus 10." The page does not state the unit.

### Year-to-year variation and band edges (Q8)

**AF-49 (supported interpretation; national, Ontario, BC, Alberta).** Months are presented as usual or average. CRS-SP: "usually April". TMG-P: "usually in April or May". FVRF: "On average, that happens here in late February to mid-March." GGC: "around late April or early May". DNA: "Approximately May long weekend". BCL: "As spring arrives at different times in different places", pruning follows forsythia. No source located gives specific advice for a garden near a band edge.

**AF-50 (expert opinion, limited; Alberta, Ontario).** MOSS: "Uncover in stages as the ground thaws, don't take everything off in one go". GGC: "start pulling back mulch and soil mounds a little at a time."

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority. Each states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. Quebec botanic garden advice, and the Maritimes.**
Espace pour la vie (Montreal Botanical Garden) pages returned HTTP 403; Select Roses and the Natural Resources Canada map page refused automated access; no Maritime source was located (AF-12, AF-21). Answering it needs a different access route (a direct request to the institutions) or consultation with the Canadian Rose Society or Maritime gardening groups, which is outside the commission's method.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to request the material directly or commission consultation.
*Recommended default:* Pip shows no months for the Maritimes, and for Quebec shows only the windows sources give (AF-26, AF-37, AF-40, AF-43, AF-46), labelled as commercial where they are.

**R-2. Which zone system the Canadian Rose Society's bands use.**
The society's pages do not say (AF-8, C-6), and the 2025 update moved many places by half a zone or more (AF-6). Answering it needs the society's own answer.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to ask the Canadian Rose Society.
*Recommended default:* Pip does not place gardeners by the society's zone bands; it shows them as context only (Founder Decision Point 3).

**R-3. Months for moving an established rose in Canada.**
No Canadian source was located (AF-38).
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission it, after `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` decides whether moving advice is in scope.
*Recommended default:* Pip gives no Canadian months for moving, and follows whatever `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` decides.

**R-4. Where band boundaries fall.**
Sources name places (AF-9) and the national society names zone bands (AF-8), but no source draws regional boundaries. This depends on Founder judgement, not further searching.
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Point 2.

## 10. Founder Decision Points

The options are listed for each decision. The ROC recommendation is labelled as a recommendation; the decision is the Founders'.

1. **Approve the findings for an Approved Research Compilation (`ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-CA-01`)?** Options: approve as stated; approve with amendments; defer pending further research; decline. Each finding carries its own Evidence Confidence Level in §1 and §8.

2. **Which bands does Pip use for Canada, and where do their boundaries fall?**
   - (a) Regional bands: coastal British Columbia; BC interior and north; the Prairies; Ontario; Quebec; the Maritimes. Each shows only the windows sources give for it (§7 timing table), with boundaries the Founders set (for example coastal BC as the Lower Mainland, Fraser Valley and Vancouver Island).
   - (b) The Canadian Rose Society's three zone bands (2–4, 5–6, 7–9), using Canadian zones.
   - (c) No bands: every Canadian gardener sees the national windows (AF-16, AF-24, AF-26, AF-34, AF-37, AF-41, AF-46) with the local signals.
   - (d) Defer pending R-2 and R-4.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (a), with Quebec limited and the Maritimes showing no months until R-1. Sources tie windows to places (AF-9, Moderate); the society's zone bands do not state their system (AF-8, Low) and zones moved in 2025 (AF-6).

3. **How is a gardener placed?**
   - (a) Pip asks for their hardiness zone and whether it is Canadian or USDA.
   - (b) Pip derives the band from the town or city and province the gardener already enters.
   - (c) Pip uses province only.
   - (d) Pip derives the band from location, then confirms by asking whether they protect their roses over winter or get hard frosts.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (d). Zone numbers are easily confused (AF-3, AF-4, AF-5) and changed in 2025 (AF-6); sources tie timing to places (AF-9) and to frost and protection (AF-10, AF-41). Province alone would put the Okanagan with Vancouver (AF-19). If a gardener gives a zone, Pip treats it as context and says that labels may use USDA zones.

4. **How does Pip show a month window alongside the bud-swell check?**
   - (a) The month window is shown as context ("roses in your area are usually pruned in late February to mid-March, when forsythia flowers"), and the bud-swell check in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` stays the test of whether to prune now.
   - (b) The month window is shown only after the bud-swell check passes.
   - (c) Months are shown only when the gardener asks.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (a). Months never replace the gate (Research Commission Record §7). Canadian sources name bud swell (AF-14) and forsythia (AF-13, High); in cold areas, pruning also waits until winter protection is off (AF-15).

5. **What does Pip say near a band edge or in an unusually early or late season?**
   - (a) Show the neighbouring bands' windows together.
   - (b) Show the wider window with the sources' signals: prune when forsythia flowers and buds swell, after the hard-frost danger has passed (AF-13, AF-14, AF-22); put protection on once the ground is freezing and take it off with the thaw (AF-41, AF-46), in stages (AF-50).
   - (c) Point the gardener to a local rose society or Master Gardener group.
   - *ROC recommendation:* (b), with (c) added. Sources present months as usual or average (AF-49) and tie tasks to signals rather than dates (AF-13, AF-41, AF-46).

   - (a) Show them, labelled as coming from Canadian garden companies.
   - (b) Show only the signals and the windows from non-commercial sources.

   - *ROC recommendation:* (a), consistent with the Founder's 1 October 2026 decision, recorded in `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01`, that "Pip should tell gardeners where it gets its information from." Where sources conflict (C-1, C-3, C-4), Pip shows the range rather than one month.
