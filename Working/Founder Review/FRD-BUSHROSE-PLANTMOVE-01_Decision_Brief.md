# Decision Brief — Planting a New Bush Rose and Moving an Established One

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief. Then decide on the 37 findings, on four further-research items, and on five questions about how Pip should handle planting and moving, including whether this belongs in Pip at all. The overall decision is: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

- **Plant bare-root roses while they're dormant.** In New Zealand and Australia that's winter; in the UK, late autumn to early spring (**High**). Australian sources name May to July; New Zealand garden centres have new roses from June and plant into spring (**Moderate**).
- **Potted roses can go in most of the year**, if the ground isn't frozen or very dry, and kept watered in spring and summer (**High**).
- **Plant bare-root roses promptly and don't let the roots dry out. Soak them first** (**High**). How long to soak varies, from an hour to 24 hours, and two US universities say no longer than 24 hours (**Moderate**).
- **Pick a sunny spot, at least four to six hours of direct sun, with good drainage** (**High**). Keep clear of tree and shrub roots (**Moderate**).
- **Planting where a rose grew before can go badly ("rose sickness")** (**Moderate**). Nobody knows exactly why, and sources disagree on the fix: replace the soil, plant somewhere else, or other methods.
- **How deep the bud union goes depends on who you ask and where you live** (**High**). New Zealand and Australian sources put it at or just above soil level (**Moderate**); cold-winter US sources bury it; the RHS says soil level, not below.
- **Water in well after planting** (**High**).
- **Move an established rose while it's dormant** (**High**), **dig as big a root ball as you can** (**High**), and **keep it well watered afterwards** (**High**). Most sources cut it back first, by anything from a third to hard (**Moderate**).
- **Older roses are said to be harder to move, but no source gives an age limit or success odds** (**Low**).

## 3. What was researched

Q1: when to plant bare-root and potted roses, by region. Q2: sun, drainage, spacing and soil where roses grew before. Q3: soaking, the hole, bud-union depth, and watering in. Q4: moving an established rose: timing, root ball, pruning, aftercare. Q5: age limits or success odds for moving.

## 4. How to read the strength ratings

| Level | Meaning in this dossier |
|---|---|
| High | Reliable and generally consistent across independent sources |
| Moderate | Reasonably supported, with notable limitations or some disagreement |
| Low | Limited evidence or significant limitations |

Each finding has its own rating. None is averaged into an overall figure.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Bud-union depth.** NZ and Australia: at or just above soil. RHS: at soil level. Cold US states and one UK nursery: below.
- **C-2 Soaking time.** One hour to 24 hours.
- **C-3 Autumn or spring planting.** RHS: either. Cold US states: spring.
- **C-4 Rose sickness.** Whether it's real, and how to deal with it.
- **C-5 Pruning before a move.** A third, half, hard, or not at all.
- **C-6 Pruning at planting.** Cut back hard, lightly, not at all, or in the first winter.
- **C-7 How hard moving is.** "Tricky" and harder with age, or "forgiving".
- **C-8 Spacing.** For air flow, or not.
- **C-9 Best point in winter to move.** February, after the July prune, after frosts, or any time while dormant.
