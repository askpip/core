The Founder directed that this question be treated as top priority and safety-blocking: the journey must not guide a gardener to prune a rose that is not established enough to be pruned. That directive did not change any evidence, but it raised the bar for how hard this dossier should search before treating "unresolved" as a final answer. A further, more targeted round of research was carried out specifically chasing institutional (extension) sources on the Q1 duration question, which -01 had left as an unresolved conflict between RHS and a single rosarian source (Fine Gardening's Zimmerman).

That further search found a genuine extension source directly on point, changing Q1 from an unresolved two-source conflict into a Moderate-confidence, multi-source Assessed Finding. This revision also proposes, for the first time, a concrete design for the Suitability Gate question itself, given the Founder's explicit instruction that the journey must not proceed on an unconfirmed guess.

## Additional Evidence Collected — Source Log

11. **University of Illinois Extension**, "Roses are Red, Pruning's for You," *A Merry Gardener* blog, 24 February 2017, https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/merry-gardener/2017-02-24-roses-are-red-prunings-you — states plainly: "For ever-blooming shrub roses, pruning will not be required in the first few years of growth. When the shrub is three or more years old, prune out one-third of the oldest canes, keeping the shape of the bush even." This is an institutional (university extension) source directly stating a multi-year threshold — the first such source found on this question.
12. **Marin Rose Society** (Nanette Londeree, Master Rosarian, 2019), "Pruning Roses," https://www.marinrose.org/care-basics/pruning-roses/ — "Do not prune newly planted roses – they need to get established before you remove any growth **(other than dead wood)**." And separately: "Prune first year roses (those you have grown for one season) lightly. They may not have developed a lot of top growth the first season, and need all their canes to continue to thrive." Notably, this source explicitly exempts dead-wood removal from the newly-planted restriction — the only source found to draw that specific distinction.
13. **Fine Gardening** (Paul Zimmerman), "Got Young Climbing Roses? Let 'em Grow!", https://www.finegardening.com/article/got-young-climbing-roses-let-em-grow — climbing roses specifically, not bush roses, so treated as supporting context rather than direct evidence for this MVP's scope; reinforces the same author's general "let it grow for the first two-plus years" position already recorded in FRD-01.
14. **Hunt County Master Gardeners** (PJ LaRue Smith, 8 May 2013), "Own Root vs. Grafted Root Roses," https://huntcountymastergardeners.org/own-root-vs-grafted-root-roses-by-pj-larue-smith/ — own-root roses "do take a little longer to get established" than grafted roses, but no specific timeframe in years is given. Confirms the propagation-type dependency flagged in -02 is real, without resolving how large the difference is.

## Revised Evidence Evaluation

**Relevance:** Source 11 is directly relevant and is the clearest, most authoritative statement found on Q1's duration question across all three dossiers in this series. Source 12 is directly relevant and adds a genuinely new distinction (dead wood as an exception) not previously found. Sources 13–14 are supporting/contextual.

**Source Reliability:** Source 11 is a university extension publication — the tier this project treats as most reliable, the same tier as the sources underlying PKR-SRC-000001 through most of the dead-wood and dormancy commissions. Source 12 is a named Master Rosarian writing for an established rose society, the same tier already used for the American Rose Society elsewhere in this project. Source 14 is Master-Gardener-program-affiliated (extension-adjacent volunteer content), a tier used previously (for example, PlantTalk Colorado, El Paso County Master Gardeners).

**Consistency:** With Source 11 added, the evidence now shows Illinois Extension (institutional), Zimmerman/Fine Gardening (rosarian), Marin Rose Society/Londeree (rosarian), and ForeverYard (general shrub, non-institutional) all converging on a multi-year (roughly three-year) restraint period for full pruning. RHS's "first winter" guidance (FRD-01 Source 3) remains the sole source suggesting a much shorter window, now a clear minority position against four converging sources rather than a even two-source split. This raises confidence materially over -01's treatment of this as unresolved.

**Independence:** Sources 11, 12 and 14 are independent institutions/authors, not republications of one another.

**Limitations:** RHS's shorter-window guidance is not discarded — it may still describe a genuinely different, lighter kind of pruning than the MVP's dead-wood-removal-plus-framework-selection observation set (the possible reconciliation already flagged as ROC's own unadopted synthesis in FRD-01 §6, still unresolved). No source states an exact day or month threshold; "three or more years" (Source 11) and "first few years" (Source 11, same source, less precisely) are the most specific figures found. The own-root/grafted timing difference (Source 14) remains unquantified.

**Uncertainty:** Materially reduced from -01 for the general "wait several years" principle, which now has institutional support. Uncertainty remains high on the exact threshold, and on how the own-root/grafted distinction should adjust it.

**Overall Evaluation:** Moderate confidence for AF-1 as revised below. Not High, because even the strongest single source (Illinois Extension) gives an approximate range ("three or more years," "first few years") rather than a precise, validated threshold, and one source (RHS) still points a different direction, unreconciled.

## Revised Principal Findings

**AF-1 (revised, supersedes -01's "first season only" framing):** A bush rose's full pruning treatment — the MVP's complete supported observation set — should generally wait until the rose is established, which the best available evidence places at roughly three or more years after planting, not one season. **Dead wood removal specifically may reasonably proceed sooner**, per Source 12's explicit exception, since removing genuinely dead tissue does not remove the plant's own growth capacity the way framework or crossing-stem pruning does. This refines rather than contradicts -01's original, narrower finding (which only established that the *first season* needs restraint) — the evidence now supports a longer restraint period than -01 concluded, for everything except dead wood.

**AF-2 (unchanged, from -02):** Observable signals (new growth, cane count relative to nursery baseline, firmness when tested) correlate with establishment but are not validated in combination — Low confidence, useful only as a fallback when planting date is unknown.

**Remaining unresolved point:** The exact threshold within "three or more years" is not pinned down by any source, and RHS's much shorter guidance is not reconciled. Given the safety framing the Founder has set, the recommended design response (below) is to treat this imprecision as a reason to build in a wide safety margin and a conservative default, not as a reason to withhold a rule entirely.

## Recommended Gate Design (declarative, per PKR Standard §9.1 — presented for Founder decision, not built)

Given the Founder's direction that the journey must not proceed without reasonable confidence the rose is established, the following sequence is proposed for the "recently planted" Suitability Gate PKR. This is ROC's proposed structure for Founder review, not a finding — it combines AF-1 and AF-2 but the combination itself is a design choice, not directly evidenced:

1. **Ask the gardener whether the planting date is known.**
2. **If known:** apply AF-1 — fewer than approximately three years since planting routes to "defer full pruning; dead wood may still be addressed," consistent with Source 12's exception; three or more years passes the gate for full pruning.
3. **If unknown:** ask the AF-2 signal questions (new growth present; several thick canes rather than one or two thin ones; firm when gently tested at the base). Because AF-2 is Low confidence, **any ambiguous, mixed, or "not sure" answer should route to the same conservative outcome as "recently planted"** — dead wood only, or defer entirely — rather than guessing "established." This mirrors the existing fail-safe pattern already built into PKR-SGT-000001's "Not Sure" fallback and Architecture §4.2's general principle that uncertainty routes to deferral, not to proceeding.
4. **The gardener is never told a false level of certainty** — where the gate passes on AF-2 signals rather than a known date, Pip should be able to disclose (if asked, via the existing Definition PKR tap-to-reveal mechanism) that this assessment rests on Low-confidence visual signals rather than a confirmed date.
