### Is it acceptable after bud break? (Brief section 5, group 1)

#### AF-1 — Dead, damaged or diseased wood can be removed at any time | Confidence: Moderate

> Several sources say dead, damaged or diseased wood can be removed at any time, or as soon as it is seen.

[[claim:af1|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af1m|Backing and caveats|RHS, Chicago Botanic Garden, New York Botanical Garden. One further source (Colorado State) says it only for climbing roses. None speaks specifically about a bush rose in leaf.]]

#### AF-2 — Dead or damaged wood can still be removed from a rose in leaf | Confidence: Low

> Two sources about a rose already in leaf say dead or damaged wood can still be removed.

[[claim:af2|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af2m|Backing and caveats|One is about a climbing rose (Oregon State); one is a nursery (David Austin).]]

#### AF-3 — Heavy pruning in leaf costs flowers | Confidence: Moderate

> Heavy pruning once the rose is in leaf can reduce or delay flowering and reduce plant size.

[[claim:af3|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af3m|Backing and caveats|Oregon State, David Austin, an Ask Extension expert. Sources disagree on whether full pruning should still go ahead (C-1).]]

#### AF-4 — Guides put this removal first in the spring session | Confidence: High

> Pruning guides put dead, damaged and diseased removal first in the late-winter or spring session, and do not say whether it can happen later.

[[claim:af4|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af4m|Backing and caveats|Ten sources from the UK, US and NZ. Silence about later timing is not a ban.]]

#### AF-5 — Dead stems are removed in summer care | Confidence: Moderate

> Removing dead stems appears in summer and growing-season care advice.

[[claim:af5|Approve;Approve with a change;Flag;Do not approve]]
[[more:af5m|Backing and caveats|Maryland and LSU extension; Yates NZ and Australia (one company).]]
