## 4. Notes and failures

**The five failures, all corrected and checked again:**

- Three statements said "sources say" where one source says it: that popular Hybrid Teas and Floribundas are often susceptible; that black spot is the most common fungal disease; and that soaps and oils have only a moderate effect on aphids' natural enemies. Each now names its source.
- One said insecticides "kill" natural enemies, which is one source's word. It now uses the sources' own terms.
- One said a two-to-three-week spray interval was Auckland Botanic Gardens' own former programme. The paper says it was a regime the Gardens once recommended to home gardeners.

**The four notes on the final version, each acted on as the checker recommended:**

- **"Should I spray my roses?", statement 3.** This is a disagreement, and was shown with a confidence level. It is now marked "Sources disagree".
- **"Should I spray my roses?", statement 6.** The University of Maryland is named on one side, though another of its pages treats pesticides as a last resort. The limit line now says so.
- **"If I spray, when and how often?", statement 5.** This statement failed the recheck and was corrected. It now follows the paper; the research record still has the old wording (section 4).
- **"What should I do about aphids?", statement 3.** "Stronger" was the checker's paraphrase. The statement now uses the sources' own words, "higher-toxicity or broad-spectrum".

These four changes were made after the last round, using the checker's own wording.

## 5. What was left out

Of the 45 findings in the research:

- **40 are used** in Pip's words.
- **4 are on record only**, as the research record already had them: that keeping a rose completely disease-free can mean more spraying than is justified; what a winter spray is said to achieve; asking a local rose society or garden centre before spraying; and that copper washes off in rain.
- **1 is held:** where the winter spray goes (section 4). If you approve the corrections it stays out. Pip still tells gardeners to clear fallen leaves and prunings in winter, which is rated Very High.

Also left out, as the research record directs: brand names, mixing amounts, any recipe for a home-made spray, sprays for pests other than aphids, and the four questions the ROC raised from its own reasoning.
