### 3. Research Plan

**Research question:** In a genuinely mild or warm-winter climate (where AF-3 documents incomplete leaf loss and premature-bud-break risk), do institutional or reliable sources confirm bud swell as the signal to watch for — or does mild-climate guidance rely on different indicators (calendar date, phenological markers like azalea bloom) instead?

**Anticipated source types:** Cooperative extension and Master Gardener program sources from genuinely mild/warm-winter US regions (Gulf Coast, Florida, southern Texas, arid Southwest) — regions distinct from the moderate-cold sources already underlying AF-1.

**Anticipated challenge, confirmed during research:** many mild-climate sources give calendar dates or phenological substitutes (azalea bloom, a fixed February date) without stating *why*, making it hard to tell whether bud swell was considered and rejected, or simply not discussed. Absence of a bud-swell mention is not treated as evidence against the finding, per ROC Operations Manual §5.7.

### 5. Evidence Evaluation

**AF-1 — Bud swell as signal, specifically in mild/warm-winter climates.** *Relevance:* direct — all three sources speak to pruning-readiness signals in climates materially warmer than the moderate-cold sources underlying the original AF-1 (Oregon, Arkansas, Minnesota, New York). *Source reliability:* the UF/IFAS and Alabama Cooperative Extension System sources are direct land-grant university extension publications, high reliability; the El Paso County source is a Texas A&M AgriLife-affiliated county Master Gardener program, a category already treated as reasonably reliable elsewhere in this project's work (compare the UC Master Gardener Program source underlying AF-2/AF-3 of ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01, and the Colorado Master Gardener Program source underlying AF-5 of ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01). *Consistency:* all three agree without contradiction — bud swell is the signal in a semi-arid warm climate (Texas), a humid subtropical climate (Florida), and a Deep South/Gulf Coast-adjacent climate (Alabama). *Independence:* genuine — three different states, three different institutions, no shared authorship apparent. *Limitations:* three sources address the question directly — the same count that supported a Moderate (not High) rating for AF-3 in the original commission, so this finding is held to the same bar rather than promoted on source count alone. Seven additional mild/warm-climate sources were checked across this and the prior round and did not address the question either way (§4), which narrows how much can be inferred about how *commonly* mild-climate guidance relies on this signal versus alternatives (calendar date, phenological substitutes) — it establishes that the signal holds where sources do address it, not that it is the dominant approach across all mild-climate guidance. Despite the additional search effort this round specifically aimed at finding it, no source was found that explicitly connects bud swell to leaf retention in the same passage (i.e., states outright "watch the buds even though the leaves haven't dropped") — the El Paso source's warm-spell/premature-bud-eyes discussion remains the closest direct tie to AF-3's underlying concern. This is now treated as a persisting limitation rather than an open search task, per §9.

### 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

No genuine disagreement was found. The four sources that did not address bud swell also did not contradict it — they simply used different guidance (calendar dates, phenological substitutes) without explaining why, which is treated as absence of evidence rather than as competing evidence, per ROC Operations Manual §5.7 and §7.2.

### 7. Principal Findings

**7.1 AF-1 — Bud swell holds as the readiness signal in mild/warm-winter climates (established finding, moderate evidence).** In the two climates checked that are genuinely warmer and more prone to incomplete dormancy than the sources underlying the original AF-1 — semi-arid West Texas and humid-subtropical North Florida — bud swell (or bud emergence) is independently confirmed as the pruning-readiness signal, consistent with AF-1's original finding. The El Paso source specifically ties this to the warm-winter premature-growth risk AF-3 already documents, without treating that risk as displacing the bud-based signal — it recommends watching the plant more closely, not switching to a different indicator.

No ROC synthesis is offered beyond this. Whether this finding is strong enough, on its own, to justify folding AF-3's caveat back into a single unified rule (bud swell, watched carefully, regardless of climate) rather than keeping it as a separate documented caveat is left to Founder decision at §10.

### 9. Recommendations for Further Research

**Both recommendations below were pursued this round** (23 August 2026), per Founder instruction to commission them:

- *Investigate Gulf Coast/Deep South sources further.* **Done** — Mississippi State, University of Georgia (two sources) and Alabama were all checked this round. Only Alabama addressed the signal directly; Mississippi and Georgia gave calendar-only guidance without explaining why. This appears to reflect a real pattern in this region's published guidance (calendar-first, signal-based reasoning less commonly stated explicitly) rather than a search failure — noted as an observation, not further pursued this round, consistent with EAS's caution against inferring anything from absence of evidence (ROC Operations Manual §5.7).

**Carried forward, still open:**

- A fourth independent source, ideally one explicitly stating that bud swell should be watched *even where* leaves have not dropped, would be the most direct way to raise this finding to High.
