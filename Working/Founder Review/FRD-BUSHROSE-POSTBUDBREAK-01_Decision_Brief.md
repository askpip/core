# Decision Brief — Removing Dead, Damaged or Diseased Wood After Bud Break

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief. The Review Form then asks for your decision on each of the 20 findings, on the three points where sources disagree, and on the further-research items. It also asks two practical questions: whether the dormancy gate should still cover removing dead, damaged or diseased wood, and whether the dead-wood and damaged-growth records should change. It finishes with one overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

Ask Pip currently stops all pruning once a rose is well into leaf. The research asked whether that should include cutting out dead, damaged or diseased wood.

- **No source says dead or damaged wood must wait until next winter.** The RHS says to remove it "as soon as it is seen". Chicago Botanic Garden says dead and diseased branches "can be removed at any time". The New York Botanical Garden says diseased growth can be cut "at any time, including the fall".
- **The spring "bud swell" window is stated for main pruning**, not for this kind of removal.
- **Heavy pruning once the rose is in leaf has a cost**: fewer or later flowers. Sources that allow late removal say to go gently and avoid harming the new leaves.
- **How to cut does not change**: cut down until the pith is white, and for canker cut well below the affected patch.
- **The main weakness**: only two sources speak directly about a rose already in leaf. One is about a climbing rose and the other is a nursery. Those findings are rated Low.

## 3. What was researched

Q1: is removal after bud break acceptable, and how does it differ from main pruning? Q2: does it depend on the cause (frost, breakage, canker, borer)? Q3: are there timing limits? Q4: how far to cut, and does the pith check still apply? Q5: what this means for the dormancy gate (reported for you to decide).

## 5. What the research found

### Is it acceptable after bud break? (group 1)

| ID | Finding | Confidence | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| AF-1 | Several sources say dead, damaged or diseased wood can be removed at any time, or as soon as it is seen. | Moderate | RHS, Chicago Botanic Garden, New York Botanical Garden. One further source says it only for climbing roses. |
| AF-2 | Two sources about a rose already in leaf say dead or damaged wood can still be removed. | Low | One is about a climbing rose (Oregon State); one is a nursery (David Austin). |
| AF-3 | Heavy pruning once in leaf can reduce or delay flowering and reduce plant size. | Moderate | Sources disagree on whether full pruning should still go ahead (C-1). |
| AF-4 | Pruning guides put dead, damaged and diseased removal first in the late-winter or spring session, and do not say whether it can happen later. | High | Ten sources. Silence is not a ban. |
| AF-5 | Removing dead stems appears in summer and growing-season care advice. | Moderate | US extension and the Yates brand in New Zealand and Australia. |

### Does the cause matter? (group 2)

| ID | Finding | Confidence | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| AF-6 | Frost can kill young shoot tips after spring growth has started, and a second cut can be needed. | Moderate | RHS and Oregon State. |
| AF-7 | Sources differ on judging winter damage before new leaves appear or after growth starts. | Moderate | Same split as already recorded in the damaged-growth ARC (C-2). |
| AF-8 | Diseased or infected wood should be removed promptly, or at any time. | Moderate | One source disagrees (AF-11, C-3). |
| AF-9 | A canker fungus lives in dead wood and can spread into healthy wood. | Low | One source, about trees and shrubs generally. |
| AF-10 | Cuts and other wounds let canker fungi in. | High | Four university disease sources. |
| AF-11 | One source says to prune out infected canes in dry weather during the dormant season. | Low | University of California, about cane blight. |
| AF-12 | Borer-infested canes are removed when found in spring and summer. | Low | A rose society and a source written mainly for raspberries. |
| AF-13 | For borer damage, cut below the wilted or swollen part until the pith is white. | Moderate | Four sources. |
| AF-14 | Only the RHS addresses damaged (for example broken) wood outside pruning time: remove it as soon as seen. | Low | One source. |

## 6. Where the sources differ

**C-1 — Full pruning once in leaf (AF-3).** David Austin: still worth pruning, "much better to prune late than not at all". Oregon State: "proceed with caution". An Ask Extension expert: a problem once there are "a couple of inches" of new leafy growth. Unresolved.

**C-2 — When to judge winter damage (AF-7).** Purdue: before new foliage appears. Nebraska (shrub roses) and a rosarian: after new growth begins. Unresolved.

**C-3 — When to cut out canker (AF-8, AF-11).** University of California: dry weather in the dormant season. RHS, New York Botanical Garden and an Ask Extension expert: promptly or at any time. Unresolved.
