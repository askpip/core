## The findings, by group

The formal wording of every finding is in FRD §7–8. This is a summary, not a re-ask — you're not required to rule on each one. Use the comment box under a group only if something specific needs to change.

**Why we prune.** AF-1: sources agree on four purposes — removing unhealthy wood, opening the bush to light and air, encouraging strong flowering, and keeping size and shape controlled.

**What comes out.** Damaged/diseased wood, crossing or rubbing stems, inward-growing or centre-crowding stems, and weak/thin/spindly growth (AF-2 to AF-6, AF-11) — all High or Moderate strength. Old wood, rootstock suckers, which canes are kept, and new basal shoots (AF-7 to AF-10, AF-36) — mostly Moderate, one Low.

[[comment:g1m|Anything in this group you want to flag or amend?]]

**Order of work and shape.** Dead/damaged/diseased first, then thin before shortening (AF-12, AF-13, both High); removal-cuts-vs-shortening-cuts genuinely are different techniques (AF-31, Moderate). Open-centred shape, small number of strong canes, choosing keepers in congested growth, a crossing-cane tie-break rule (AF-14 to AF-16, AF-32, AF-33).

[[comment:g2m|Anything in this group you want to flag or amend?]]

**How much to take off.** A third to a half of length kept back (AF-17, Moderate); hybrid teas lower than floribundas, grandifloras like hybrid teas (AF-18, AF-37); once-flowering roses out of scope (AF-19, High, already settled by FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01); multi-year renewal and the one-third-per-session limit (AF-20, AF-40); replacing rather than renovating a badly overgrown bush (AF-39).

[[comment:g3m|Anything in this group you want to flag or amend?]]

**Cut mechanics.** Outward-facing bud, roughly a quarter inch above it, ~45° slant, tool by thickness, sharp/clean/disinfected tools, cut at origin, sealing larger cuts (AF-21 to AF-30, AF-35, AF-38) — the best-attested part of the dossier (several High-strength findings), with the disputed points (distance, slant wording, sealing, whether angle matters at all) carried in the conflicts table below.

[[comment:g4m|Anything in this group you want to flag or amend?]]

**What can be judged from a photograph.** New in Revisions 3–4: general guidance favours hands-on examination over photos alone (AF-34); for this project specifically, shape and crossing canes are visually assessable, cane vigour and true internal health are not (AF-41) — both Low strength, and AF-41 is the ROC's own synthesis of several partial sources rather than one source stating it outright.

## Where the sources disagree, and the defaults adopted

Eleven conflicts are on record (Brief §6 has the full table): final height, cut distance, slant direction, sealing, how boldly to prune, age of old wood, cane count, the hand-pruner/lopper threshold, pruner type, disinfecting tools, and whether cut angle matters at all. None is resolved by picking a winner — each keeps its place as a documented disagreement — but each now carries a stated default so Pip has something usable without you ruling on it item by item.

## The one real judgement call

No source — including the formal ANSI A300 standard, checked directly — ranks what to do when a single stem is both a removal candidate and one of the bush's few structural canes. This is the one place a source will never give an answer, so it's a genuine Founder-level call rather than something more research would fix. The ROC recommends leaning conservative and retaining the stem for reassessment at the next pruning.
