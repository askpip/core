## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Ask Pip gives season-level timing derived from the gardener's hemisphere. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for the United Kingdom. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` makes bud swell the test of pruning readiness and states that calendar timing varies by climate; `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` gives feeding and deadheading as general patterns with no fixed dates.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** In which months is the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda done in each climate band, and what local signal is it tied to?
- **Q2** When does spring feeding start, and when is the last feed, in each band?
- **Q3** When do deadheading and summer pruning stop before winter, in each band?
- **Q4** In which months are bare-root roses planted, potted roses planted, and established roses moved, in each band?
- **Q5** When are fallen leaves and prunings cleared; where a winter spray is described, when; where winter protection is described, when it goes on and comes off?
- **Q6** Do UK sources give one national window per task, or different windows for colder and milder areas, and how are those areas described?
- **Q7** Could a beginner be placed in the right band from the region or GPS location already recorded, or would they need to be asked?
- **Q8** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band?

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. United Kingdom only. Months from one band are not applied to another. Product names, brands and rates are not collected. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5).

**Method.**

Pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question; each extraction was asked for exact sentences. For RHS-MB, RHS-GG, RHS-DH, RHS-FROST, LTG-PR, LTG-GG, STYLE, PB-HT, PB-NOV, PB-FEB, PB-OCT, PB-CAL, PB-FEED, HARK-PT, HARK-EG, DA-PR and DA-BASICS the quotations relied on were re-checked with a second extraction asking for the sentence character for character.
- Every quotation used is under 25 words and is recorded, by finding, in the Source Register.
- The UK sources already opened for `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` (RHS-PL, RHS-MOVE, DA-BR, DA-MOVE) and `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` (RHS-BS, RHS-RUST, EUTREMA) were re-opened and their timing statements confirmed.

**Anticipated challenges.** UK rose timing is mostly given as one national window. Much month-by-month guidance comes from commercial growers. The Royal National Rose Society's advice is in a members' area, and BBC Gardeners' World pages are served through an access gateway.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** RHS, David Austin, Peter Beales, Harkness, Love the Garden, Style Roses and Thompson & Morgan pages address UK rose care directly. Met Office and two RHS pages address UK frost and climate, not roses.

**Source reliability.** The RHS is the national horticultural authority and the Met Office the national weather service. The rose growers are experienced practitioners but commercial. Love the Garden and Eutrema are product brands.

**Consistency.** Sources agree closely that the main pruning falls in late winter to early spring, that feeding starts in spring with a further feed after the first flush, that bare-root roses are planted while dormant, that container roses can go in at any time in suitable ground, and that fallen leaves are cleared in autumn. They disagree on the exact pruning months, the last feed, the moving window, and whether deadheading has a stop month.

**Independence.** Each organisation's pages count once. Peter Beales's calendar and monthly pages repeat one practice.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. Most month detail is commercial. The only area distinctions found concern pruning and come from three sources.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1 — Which months for the main pruning (AF-1, AF-4, AF-5, AF-6).** February to March: RHS-GT "Late winter (February or March)"; PB-WF "during February and March"; LTG-PR "stick to February to March"; HARK-PT "mid-February to mid-March"; HARK-EG "around March"; MO-MAR "a classic March task". January to February: DA-PR "for most gardens that means January or February"; PB-FEB "January and February are the best months"; PB-HT "February is the ideal month" and "January – February: Main pruning period". November to March: TM. November and December in the south: STYLE. Peter Beales gives both February–March (2017) and January–February (2025, 2026). All windows overlap in February. Unresolved.

**C-2 — The local signal for pruning (AF-7; `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-1).** RHS-MB: "when growth is just resuming". LTG-PR: "before leaf buds are starting to swell". HARK-EG: "before fresh buds start to open". DA-PR: "while roses are dormant". HARK-PT, TM and STYLE: a frost-free day or period. The approved Suitability Gate uses bud swell; LTG-PR places pruning before buds swell. Whether these describe the same moment is not stated. Unresolved; R-5d.

**C-3 — The last feed (AF-11, AF-12).** RHS-GG: established roses fed "in March/April" and again "in mid-summer after the first flush"; containers "from mid-spring to late summer". HARK-EG and LTG-GG: twice, the second in mid-summer. PB-FEED and PB-CAL: liquid feeds continue, last "before the end of August". Unresolved.

**C-4 — Bare-root planting in mid-winter (AF-17, AF-18).** RHS-PL: "Avoid planting in the middle of winter when the ground is frozen." PB-DEC: "December is one of the best months of the year to plant your bare root roses"; PB-JAN plants in January and stores roses if the ground is frozen. Both make frozen ground the limit; they differ on whether mid-winter is to be avoided. Unresolved.

**C-5 — Moving window (AF-21).** RHS-MOVE (general shrubs): "late October to mid-March". DA-MOVE: "between November and February". PB-CAL: November. PB-FEB: "February is the time to do it." All are within dormancy. Unresolved.

**C-6 — Stopping deadheading (AF-14; `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F5; `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` AF-15).** UK sources give no stop month for repeat-flowering bush roses; RHS-DH says "whatever the time of year"; PB-OCT is "still deadheading" in October. The approved F5 records a common stop in early-to-mid autumn and AF-15 discourages late-season deadheading, both from US sources. Unresolved; Founder decision point 8.

**C-7 — Timing of autumn height reduction (AF-9).** PB-CAL: September. PB-HT: "Autumn (October – November)". HARK-PT: after flowering "in November". Unresolved.

**C-8 — Winter protection by mounding (AF-25, AF-26; `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F9).** F9 (US sources) mounds soil 10–12 inches over the base after hard frosts. No UK source located describes this; PB-NOV mulches only where roots are exposed. Different climates. Unresolved.

**C-9 — Southern pruning in early winter (AF-5; `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-2).** STYLE prunes in November and December in the Midlands and south. The approved AF-2 places pruning after the coldest part of winter, and RHS-MB and LTG-PR give mid-February for the south. STYLE is a single commercial source. Preserved as unresolved.

**Uncertainty preserved.** No source states where the milder and colder areas divide (AF-27, AF-32); no source tests whether pruning month affects flowering in the UK (R-2); the current UK status of winter spray products is not stated by any source located (R-3).

### Main pruning (Q1)

**AF-1 (established; national).** UK sources place the main pruning of established bush roses in late winter to early spring, most commonly February to March. RHS-GT: "Late winter (February or March) is often a good time for pruning roses". PB-WF: "The best time to prune your roses is during February and March." LTG-PR: "stick to February to March, the most common pruning time for roses." HARK-PT: "a frost-free period from mid-February to mid-March". HARK-EG: "Prune fully around March". MO-MAR: "Pruning roses is a classic March task". DA-BASICS: "In late winter or early spring remove anything that is obviously dead, diseased, or rubbing."

**AF-2 (supported interpretation; south or milder band).** In the south, main pruning is usually around mid-February. RHS-MB: "usually in mid-February in the south". LTG-PR: "In the south you are safest to prune roses in mid-February before new growth begins on rose plants."

**AF-3 (supported interpretation; north or colder band).** In the north and colder areas, main pruning waits until March, or mid to late March. RHS-MB: "but in northern and colder areas wait until March." LTG-PR: "in the north and other colder areas of the UK we would recommend waiting until mid to late March".

**AF-4 (supported interpretation; national).** Several commercial UK growers put the main window earlier, in January and February. DA-PR: "Winter pruning is best completed while roses are dormant, and for most gardens that means January or February." PB-FEB: "January and February are the best months of the year to prune your shrubs, hybrid teas, and any other roses". PB-HT: "February is the ideal month to prune hybrid tea roses" and "January – February: Main pruning period for hybrid tea and climbing roses before new growth begins." (C-1.)

**AF-5 (dissenting opinion, single source; two bands).** STYLE: pruning "should be completed every year during November/December for Midlands & South counties, January/Late February for North of England and Scotland", and says delaying to March "will simply delay your roses from flowering in the following summer by up to 6 weeks!" It gives no evidence for the delay (C-1, C-9).

**AF-6 (expert opinion, single source; national).** TM: "the best time to prune is a frost-free day between November and March."

**AF-7 (supported interpretation; national).** Sources tie pruning timing to local signals as well as months. RHS-MB: roses "pruned during late winter when growth is just resuming". LTG-PR: pruned "before leaf buds are starting to swell, but this normally depends on your climate and where you are." HARK-EG: "prune a bush rose before fresh buds start to open". DA-PR: "while roses are dormant". HARK-PT, TM and STYLE: a frost-free period or day. The signals named differ (C-2).

**AF-8 (expert opinion, limited; national).** DA-PR says late pruning is still worthwhile: "If you notice signs of new growth and you still haven't pruned, don't worry." and "if March arrives and you still haven't done it, you haven't missed your chance."

**AF-9 (expert opinion, limited; national).** Two growers describe a light autumn height reduction of tall bush roses against winter wind. PB-HT: "Autumn (October – November): Light prune tall hybrid tea roses to prevent wind damage." PB-CAL (September): "removing approximately 30cm (12 inches) from the top of established plants". HARK-PT: "Roses can be trimmed back after they finish flowering in November to stop wind-rock". PB-OCT: "not to do anything too drastic, as it is too early to do a hard prune." Months differ (C-7).

### Feeding (Q2)

**AF-10 (established; national).** The first feed is given in spring, most often March or April. RHS-GG: "in March/April before flowering". DA-FEED: "Apply in late March or early April, just before new growth begins." PB-FEED: "ideally in March, as the rose starts to leave its winter, dormant state." PB-FEB: "Towards the end of February or in early March" slow-release fertiliser and mulch. HARK-EG: "before flowering". LTG-GG: "in early spring, or after pruning". DA-BASICS: "A feed in spring encourages fresh growth".

**AF-11 (established; national).** A further feed is given in mid-summer after the first flush. RHS-GG: "in mid-summer after the first flush of flowers (especially for repeat-flowering types)". HARK-EG: "Apply feed twice a year - before flowering, then again in mid-summer after the first flush." LTG-GG: "and again in mid-summer." PB-FEED: "after the first flush of blooms has gone over in July." DA-BASICS: "another in midsummer helps repeat flowering varieties."

**AF-12 (expert opinion, single organisation; national).** Peter Beales gives the last feed by the end of August. PB-FEED: "do your last liquid feed before the end of August". PB-CAL: "Late August is usually the time you apply your last feed, to encourage the final flush". RHS-GG feeds container roses "from mid-spring to late summer". (C-3.)

**AF-13 (expert opinion, single source; national).** PB-FEED's reason: ending feeding "encourages the plant to start entering its dormant state as the weather cools and prevents any new growth from being damaged by the frost."

### Deadheading and late-season cut-off (Q3)

**AF-14 (supported interpretation; national).** UK sources give no month to stop deadheading repeat-flowering bush roses. RHS-DH: "remove spent flowers as soon as they look scruffy, whatever the time of year." PB-OCT: "make sure you are still deadheading your roses, once the flowers have gone over." LTG-GG: "typically from late spring to autumn." (C-6.)

**AF-15 (supported interpretation; national).** Deadheading stops early only for roses grown for their hips. RHS-GG: "stop deadheading these plants in mid-summer to give the hips time to form". DA-DH: "Do not deadhead roses renowned for their autumn fruit after the second flush." Most Hybrid Teas and Floribundas are not grown for hips; neither source names them.

**AF-16 (expert opinion, single organisation; national).** PB-WF: "It is not recommended to hard prune them during the autumn though as the tips can then become damaged by frost." PB-OCT: "too early to do a hard prune." No UK source located gives a date to stop summer pruning.

### Planting and moving (Q4)

**AF-17 (established; national).** Bare-root roses are planted while dormant, from late autumn to early spring. RHS-PL: "Plant in late autumn at leaf fall, and from late winter to early spring, before growth resumes." RHS-GG: "usually in late autumn or from late winter to early spring". DA-BR: "The ideal moment is during dormancy, from late autumn to early spring." DA-BRC: "The winter planting season usually begins in October", delivery "between November and April". LTG-GG: "Bare-root roses are best planted between October and early March." PB-CAL (November): "Traditionally, this is the best time to plant bare rooted roses". PB-DEC, PB-JAN and PB-FEB name December, January and February.

**AF-18 (supported interpretation; national).** Bare-root planting waits while the ground is frozen. RHS-PL: "Avoid planting in the middle of winter when the ground is frozen." PB-JAN: store the rose "until the ground has thawed enough for you to plant it." (C-4.)

**AF-19 (established; national).** Container-grown roses can be planted at any time of year in suitable ground. RHS-PL: "Plant all year round, provided the ground is neither frozen, nor very dry." DA-POT: "Potted roses can be planted at almost any time of year, provided the ground is not frozen, waterlogged or in drought conditions." HARK-EG: "at any time of the year, so long as ground conditions aren't frozen or waterlogged." LTG-GG: "Can be planted anytime of year". DA-POT adds: "Avoid planting during periods of extreme heat".

**AF-20 (expert opinion, single source; national).** LTG-GG prefers "ideally September to November and March to May" for container roses.

**AF-21 (supported interpretation; national).** An established rose is moved while dormant; the stated windows differ. RHS-MOVE (general shrubs, naming *Rosa* as "tricky to move"): "Move at any time during the dormant season from late October to mid-March". DA-MOVE: "In the UK and Europe, this is usually between November and February". PB-CAL (November): "This is also the time to transplant mature roses". PB-FEB: "February is the time to do it." (C-5.)

### Winter clear-up, spraying and protection (Q5)

**AF-22 (established; national).** Fallen leaves are cleared in autumn. RHS-BS: "Collect and destroy fallen leaves in the autumn, or bury under a layer of mulch." RHS-RUST: "Collect and destroy fallen leaves in autumn to reduce the number of overwintering resting spores". PB-CAL (October): "pick up any foliage that has dropped onto the soil". PB-NOV: "removing any fallen leaves from the ground". PB-FEB: "February is the perfect time to tidy up your flower beds, ready for the spring." DA-BASICS: "Fallen leaves should be cleared so disease has nowhere to linger."

**AF-23 (expert opinion, single source; national).** RHS-BS: "If practical, pick off and dispose of any leaves that remain on the plant over the winter months."

**AF-24 (expert opinion, single commercial source; national).** EUTREMA describes lime sulphur applied "during the dormant season", which "typically occurs in late winter or early spring before new growth begins." The RHS pages opened describe no winter spray and recommend against fungicides (as recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` AF-5 and AF-29). No product or rate is recorded here.

**AF-25 (expert opinion, single source; national).** PB-NOV: "Before the winter weather takes hold, it is crucial to check the base of your roses, for root exposure." Where roots show, "you will need to build up a mulch layer around them to protect them from the cold." No source located gives when such mulch comes off.

**AF-26 (established absence, limited search; national).** No UK source located describes mounding soil over the base of a bush rose for winter, or when it is removed.

### Climate bands, placing the gardener, and variation (Q6 to Q8)

**AF-27 (supported interpretation).** Where UK rose sources distinguish areas, they use two broad descriptions and state no boundary. RHS-MB: "the south" against "northern and colder areas". LTG-PR: "In the south" against "the north and other colder areas of the UK"; "February is prime time in milder areas of the UK, but in colder regions March is best" (patio roses). STYLE: "Midlands & South counties" against "North of England and Scotland". STYLE places the Midlands with the south; RHS-MB and LTG-PR do not mention the Midlands.

**AF-28 (established absence, limited search).** Area-specific months are given only for main pruning. RHS-GG states feeding, deadheading and planting with no regional variation, and none of the other pages opened gives different months by area for feeding, deadheading, planting, moving, clear-up or protection.

**AF-29 (supported interpretation; frost, not rose-specific).** RHS-FROST gives "the typical first safe planting time in an average year" for tender plants in five regions: "South-west tip and along the south coast of England: first to second half of May"; "Southern England and south Wales: second half of May"; "Midlands, north-west England, Yorkshire and north Wales: end of May"; "North-east England, southern Scotland and Northern Ireland: early June"; "The Highlands and northern Scotland: mid June". It is the only located UK source that divides the country into named regions with dates.

**AF-30 (established; frost, not rose-specific).** The Met Office describes a north–south pattern. MO-FROST: "In southern and coastal regions, the average last air frost tends to occur earlier in the spring, often before April comes to an end." and "Further north, and over higher ground, the average last frost often extends well into May and, in a few places, even into June." MO-SPRING: "Northern areas, including Scotland, typically remain cooler for longer, while southern parts of the UK tend to warm up earlier." MO-FEB: "Cornwall seeing around 11 days of air frost and with Eastern Scotland seeing over 14 days" in February.

**AF-31 (established; frost, not rose-specific).** Site factors within a region shift frost timing. RHS-FROST: sheltered, urban or coastal spots "may be 1–2 weeks earlier"; "Exposed, rural or higher elevation sites (e.g. Shropshire, Cotswolds, Pennines): delay 1–3 weeks". MO-FROST: "valleys are especially prone as colder air drains downhill and pools near the surface overnight." RHS-MICRO: "A garden in a frost pocket is at significant risk of late and early frosts and has a shortened growing season".

**AF-32 (established absence, limited search).** No UK source located explains how a gardener should tell which band they are in. The sources name regions (AF-27, AF-29) and site factors (AF-31); LTG-PR says timing "normally depends on your climate and where you are." None mentions latitude or postcode.

**AF-33 (supported interpretation).** UK springs vary from year to year and late frosts still occur. RHS-FROST: "April 2021 recorded the most April air frosts in at least 60 years" and spring "is variable and late frosts still occur." MO-FROST: "frosts can and do still occur well into late spring, including May." MO-SPRING: "March sees a higher likelihood of snow or sleet than December".

**AF-34 (supported interpretation).** MO-FROST: "the average date of the last spring frost has been shifting earlier in the year. This is consistent with a warming climate." STYLE cites warming to argue for earlier pruning (AF-5); no other rose source located connects the two.

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority. Each states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. Where the milder and colder areas divide.**
No UK rose source states a boundary (AF-27, AF-32). Drawing one from Met Office climate data would need analysis of climate statistics, a different evidentiary standard from what sources state. Choosing a line is Founder judgement.
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Points 2 and 3.
*Recommended default:* one national window, with the sources' south-earlier and north-later note for pruning (Founder Decision Point 2 (b)).

**R-2. Whether the pruning month changes UK flowering time.**
STYLE claims March pruning delays flowering by up to six weeks (AF-5); no trial was located. Answering it needs trial evidence or specialist consultation.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to seek trial evidence.
*Recommended default:* the claim is not used in Pip.

**R-3. Whether winter spray products are currently authorised for UK home gardens on roses.**
A regulatory question that changes over time, as recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` R-1. It needs a direct check of the HSE register, outside a horticultural evidence commission.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission a regulatory check.
*Recommended default:* Pip shows no month for a winter spray in the UK.

**R-4. Royal National Rose Society and BBC Gardeners' World guidance.**
The RNRS's advice is members-only and BBC Gardeners' World pages are behind an access gateway (§4.3). Reading them needs membership or access the commission does not have.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to obtain access.
*Recommended default:* proceed on the RHS, Met Office and grower sources, with the commercial limitation disclosed.

**R-5. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis, recorded as such and assigned Not Assigned (EAS §2.10).**
These are questions arising from the findings that the evidence does not itself state. They are not findings and are not proposed for adoption.

- R-5a. Whether the RHS's five frost regions (AF-29), set for tender plants, would serve as rose pruning bands, and where "south" (AF-2) and "northern and colder areas" (AF-3) fall within them.
- R-5b. Whether the January to February window in recent commercial pages (AF-4) reflects the earlier last frost the Met Office reports (AF-34), the rose classes those pages cover, or a different practice.
- R-5c. Whether GPS latitude and altitude would place a gardener more accurately than a region name, given that site factors move frost timing by one to three weeks (AF-31).
- R-5d. Whether "when growth is just resuming" (RHS-MB) and "before leaf buds are starting to swell" (LTG-PR) describe the same moment as the approved bud-swell signal (C-2).
- R-5e. Whether continued October deadheading in the UK (AF-14) leads to the frost-damaged soft growth that `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` AF-15 warns of, given milder UK autumns.

*Founder decision needed:* whether any of these is worth commissioning.
*Recommended default:* none is acted on until independent evidence is found.
