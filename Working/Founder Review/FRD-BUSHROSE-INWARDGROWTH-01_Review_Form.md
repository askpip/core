## The findings

Summarised here; full wording is in FRD §7–8. You're not required to rule on each one — use the comment box if something specific needs to change.

**Definitions (Q1).** Most sources address inward growth prospectively — cut to an outward bud so future growth points away from the centre — rather than as an existing defect to diagnose. Only three sources name it as its own category.

**Recognising it, and what a photo can tell you (Q2, Q3).** Thin evidence — where stated, the indicator is simply growth direction toward the centre, a soft standard. No rose source addresses photographic limits; the ROC's own synthesis, drawing on general pruning practice and one academic study, is that a single photo has real limits here.

**What to do about it (Q5, Q6).** Where sources do name it, they agree: remove it, same as crossing/rubbing stems, full cut to point of origin. Fewer sources than the crossing/rubbing equivalent, but none disagrees. No source says which condition wins when a stem is both inward and crossing, but it doesn't matter in practice — both point to the same action.

**Lookalikes and safety (Q4, Q7).** Close to complete gaps, confirmed by real searching. One unsourced caution noted (a light-seeking stem could look similar). Given a plain default each rather than left open.
