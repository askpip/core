[[choice:d2|2. Does the UK need climate bands? ROC recommends (b).|(a) No bands;(b) One national window for every task, with a note on main pruning that…;(c) Two bands for main pruning only (south or milder, north or colder)…;(d) More bands, based on the RHS's five frost regions (AF-29)|comment]]
[[more:d2m|The options|(a) No bands: one national window for every task (AF-1, AF-10 to AF-22). (b) One national window for every task, with a note on main pruning that sources say the south is usually around mid-February and the north and colder areas wait until March (AF-2, AF-3). (c) Two bands for main pruning only (south or milder; north or colder), national windows for every other task (AF-28). (d) More bands, based on the RHS's five frost regions (AF-29). ROC recommendation: (b). AF-1 is High; AF-2 and AF-3 are Moderate and give no boundary (AF-27, R-1); sources give area-specific months for pruning only (AF-28).]]

[[choice:d3|3. If bands are used, where does the boundary fall? ROC recommends (c).|(a) South or milder;(b) As STYLE;(c) No fixed line|comment]]
[[more:d3m|The options|(a) South or milder: south-west, south coast, southern England and south Wales; north or colder: the Midlands northwards, north Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. (b) As STYLE: the Midlands with the south; north of England and Scotland as colder. (c) No fixed line: Pip shows both windows and the gardener chooses. ROC recommendation: (c). No source states a line, and two sources disagree on the Midlands (AF-27); a fixed line would be the ROC's own synthesis (R-5a).]]

[[choice:d4|4. How is a gardener placed in a band? ROC recommends (c).|(a) Automatically from the region or town or city they entered;(b) Automatically from GPS latitude;(c) Pip suggests a band from the region entered and asks the gardener to…|comment]]
[[more:d4m|The options|(a) Automatically from the region or town or city they entered. (b) Automatically from GPS latitude. (c) Pip suggests a band from the region entered and asks the gardener to confirm, noting that sheltered, urban and coastal gardens tend to be earlier and exposed, high or frost-pocket gardens later. ROC recommendation: (c). Sources describe areas by region names, not latitude (AF-27, AF-29, AF-32), and site factors shift timing within a region (AF-31).]]

[[choice:d5|5. How does Pip show a month window alongside the bud-swell check? ROC recommends (c).|(a) The bud-swell check stays the gate, the month window appears beneath…;(b) The month window appears as a reminder before the season, and the…;(c) Both (a) and (b)|comment]]
[[more:d5m|The options|(a) The bud-swell check stays the gate; the month window appears beneath it as context ("UK sources usually prune in February to March"). (b) The month window appears as a reminder before the season, and the bud-swell check is still required before the pruning journey starts. (c) Both (a) and (b). ROC recommendation: (c). Months never replace the ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01 gate; UK sources themselves pair months with a signal (AF-7).]]

[[choice:d6|6. What does Pip say near a band edge or in an unusually early or late season? ROC recommends (a) with (c).|(a) Show both the southern and northern windows and say that the rose's…;(b) Show only the gardener's band window;(c) Show the window and add that late frosts still occur and the season…|comment]]
[[more:d6m|The options|(a) Show both the southern and northern windows and say that the rose's buds and a frost-free day decide, not the calendar. (b) Show only the gardener's band window. (c) Show the window and add that late frosts still occur and the season varies from year to year (AF-33), pointing to the local forecast. ROC recommendation: (a) with (c). AF-7, AF-31 and AF-33 support it; AF-8 (Low) adds that slightly late pruning is not lost.]]

[[choice:d7|7. Which national pruning window does Pip show, given C-1? ROC recommends (c).|(a) February to March (AF-1);(b) January to March, covering AF-1 and AF-4;(c) February to March, noting that some rose growers prune from January|comment]]
[[more:d7m|The options|(a) February to March (AF-1). (b) January to March, covering AF-1 and AF-4. (c) February to March, noting that some rose growers prune from January. ROC recommendation: (c). AF-1 is High and includes the RHS; AF-4 is Moderate and commercial; AF-5 and AF-6 are not shown.]]

[[choice:d8|8. How does Pip handle UK deadheading in autumn, given C-6? ROC recommends (b).|(a) Keep the approved general pattern (ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01 F5)…;(b) For the UK, say sources give no stop month for repeat-flowering bush…;(c) Defer pending R-5e|comment]]

(b) For the UK, say sources give no stop month for repeat-flowering bush roses and some deadhead into October (AF-14), while keeping the caution against hard pruning in autumn (AF-16; ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01 AF-15). (c) Defer pending R-5e. ROC recommendation: (b).]]
