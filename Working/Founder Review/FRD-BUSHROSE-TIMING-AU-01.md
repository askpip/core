## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

**Background.** Ask Pip records each rose's location (GPS, or town or city, region and country) and derives its hemisphere and current season. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for Australia. Australian gardening sources commonly use climate zones, and roses are grown very differently across them. Month-level timing is a Founder requirement. Until findings are approved, Pip shows season-level timing with the bud-swell check and no months for Australia.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band, and what local signal do they tie it to?
- **Q2** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
- **Q3** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
- **Q4** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
- **Q5** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where a winter spray is described, when; and where winter protection is described, when it goes on and comes off? Product names, brands and rates are not collected as findings.
- **Q6** Which climate zones do Australian rose sources use for timing, how are they bounded, and which zones do sources say suit Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses at all?
- **Q7** Which zone could a beginner place themselves in, using the region they enter in Ask Pip, their GPS location, or by naming their zone?
- **Q8** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band? Do they prefer local signals over fixed months?

**Scope.** Established Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and (provisionally) Grandiflora bush roses, plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Australian sources only. Every finding records the band or state it applies to. Months from one band are not applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are not converted. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC Operations Manual §5.5). The months do not replace the bud-swell Suitability Gate in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.

**Method.**

- Web searches were run for each question, including searches directed at the rose societies of New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, Queensland and Tasmania, ABC Gardening Australia, the Bureau of Meteorology, Canberra and Brisbane sources, and Australian rose nurseries.
- Pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question; each extraction was asked for exact sentences. For RSNSW-G, OGO, TSC, RSWA, TRE-CAL, FP, GA-SUB, OGTR, BOM-CZ and BOM-FROST the quotations relied on were re-checked against a second extraction asking for the sentence character for character.
- RSNSW-G, RSV-PB and SARS-WIN were each checked directly for any statement timing pruning by bud swell or new growth. RSNSW-CAL was checked directly for definitions of its areas.
- Every quotation used is under 25 words and is recorded, by finding, in the Source Register. Month labels added in square brackets in the Register show the calendar heading under which the quotation appears; they are not part of the quotation.
- Pages that could not be opened, or that were opened but are not relied on, are recorded in §4.3 and support no finding.
- Findings are separated by claim so each receives its own Evidence Confidence Level (EAS §3.2).
- Approved findings in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`, `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`, and the Australian months in the pending `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SPRAYING-01` and `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01`, were checked for agreement only (§7, final subsection). Sources shared with those dossiers were re-opened on 1 October 2026 for this commission.

**Anticipated challenges.** Rose calendars are written by state societies for their own state and rarely define the areas they call mild or cold. Gardening climate zones and meteorological zones are drawn on different principles. Tropical and arid gardens have few rose-specific sources.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** The rose society, broadcaster, public garden and nursery sources address rose timing in Australia directly. OGO, TSC (zone definitions) and the three Bureau of Meteorology pages address climate zones and frost, not roses. OGTR addresses rose biology and cultivation in Australia briefly.

**Source reliability.** The state rose societies of NSW, Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia and Queensland, the Horticultural Society of Canberra, ABC Gardening Australia, Brisbane City Council, the Bureau of Meteorology and the Office of the Gene Technology Regulator are recognised bodies. Nurseries and retailers are practised growers, given less weight as independent authority.

**Consistency.** Sources agree strongly that the main pruning is in winter, with July the usual month and August in cold or frosty areas; that spring feeding begins in late August or September; that a summer trim falls between January and March; that bare-root roses are planted in June and July; and that leaves and prunings are cleared and a clean-up spray applied after pruning. They disagree on whether pruning may begin in June, on the end of the cold-area window, on the month of the first and last feeds, and on the edges of the bare-root window. Zone names and boundaries differ between gardening guides and between those guides and the Bureau of Meteorology.

**Independence.** Each rose society counts once. GAM and GA-MAG count once for July pruning; RSV-PB and GA-RP08 count once for "June or July". OGO and TSC count once for the town lists.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. Most month evidence comes from the temperate southern states (NSW, Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, ACT). Tropical and subtropical evidence rests on single segments and one public garden; no arid-zone, Tasmanian or Northern Territory rose society month was found. No rose source defines the boundaries of the areas it calls mild, cold or frosty.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**C-1 — Zone names and boundaries (AF-1, AF-2, AF-3).** OGO calls Melbourne, Canberra and Hobart "Cold Temperate" and Sydney, Perth and Adelaide "Warm Temperate"; TSC lists the same towns as "Cool" and "Temperate". PEN groups Adelaide with Melbourne, Canberra and Hobart as "Cool Southern Zones". OGO and TSC place Alice Springs, Mildura, Albury-Wodonga and Shepparton in an arid zone; BOM-CZ: "Places like Brisbane and Alice Springs are considered subtropical." The schemes are drawn on different principles (gardening experience against latitude and native vegetation). Unresolved; Founder Decision Point 2.

**C-2 — Can main pruning begin in June (AF-11, AF-13)?** June: RSV-PB and GA-RP08 "June or July"; NFP "June/July"; ROSS-CAL "Pruning may commence in late June"; RSNSW-G "late June to the end of July" near the coast; RSWA prunes under "WINTER (June - July)". ## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim and states the zone or state it applies to.

### Climate bands (Q6)

**AF-1 (supported interpretation; national).** Gardening climate-zone guides divide Australia into five zones, bounded by lists of example towns rather than measured limits. OGO: tropical "from Exmouth, Western Australia, to south Townsville, Queensland"; subtropical (Brisbane, Bundaberg, Coffs Harbour, Gold Coast and others); arid/semi-arid (Alice Springs, Mildura, Albury-Wodonga, Shepparton, Wagga Wagga, Dubbo); warm temperate, "Typically, cool winters with light frost, usually non-existent on the coast" (Sydney, Perth, Adelaide, Newcastle, Toowoomba, Wollongong, Bunbury and others); cold temperate (Melbourne, Canberra, Hobart, Launceston, Ballarat, Bendigo, Geelong, Mt Gambier). TSC lists the same towns.

**AF-2 (supported interpretation; national).** The same towns carry different names. TSC calls the cold temperate towns "Cool" and the warm temperate towns "Temperate". PEN (commercial) groups Adelaide with them: "Places like Melbourne, Canberra, Adelaide, and Hobart offer ideal growing conditions for most rose types." (C-1.)

**AF-3 (established; national).** The Bureau of Meteorology uses a different scheme. BOM-CZ: "Places like Brisbane and Alice Springs are considered subtropical. Summers are hot, winters are mild." Hobart and Melbourne are temperate; Cairns and Darwin tropical; the Red Centre "mainly classified as desert". "The location of climate zones is largely determined by their distance from the equator", with mountains and distance from the sea also playing a part; Australia has "6 major climate zones using the Köppen classification, which is mainly based on native vegetation".

**AF-4 (established; national).** Rose sources time their tasks mainly by frost, coast and inland, not by the five gardening zones. RSNSW-G: "If you live near the coast, and rarely or never get frosts", against "inland or an area that gets any frosts over a regular period". RSNSW-CAL: "In mild areas" against "In cold areas". RSV-PB: "a really cold area of Australia". GAM: "very cold zones, especially those subject to late frosts". RSSA-GG: "frost areas". ROSS-CAL: "frosty districts". TRE-CAL: "an area that receives frosts". FP: "frost-prone areas" against "warm and coastal climates". Of the rose sources opened, only GA-TROP, GA-SUB and NFP address the tropical or subtropical zones by name or place.

**AF-5 (unresolved question; national).** Rose sources that name "mild", "cold", "cool", "warm" or "frosty" areas do not define them by place, temperature or number of frosts. RSNSW-CAL, checked directly, uses all four of "mild", "cool", "cold" and "warm" without defining any. RSNSW-G's "near the coast ... rarely or never get frosts" is the closest to a definition.

**AF-6 (supported interpretation; national, especially tropical and subtropical).** Roses grow in most of Australia but are harder to manage in hot, humid and tropical areas because of fungal disease. OGTR: roses "will grow in most parts of Australia, but are more difficult to manage in hot tropical regions, due to the diseases such as black spot". PEN: "Tropical and subtropical regions like Brisbane, Darwin, and parts of NSW face high humidity, summer rain, and increased risk of fungal disease." GA-MAG: "select a rose that is not prone to fungal diseases, particularly if you live in a humid climate." TSC: "Roses may be more susceptible to fungal diseases in more humid areas."

**AF-7 (expert opinion, limited; temperate and cool southern).** Temperate and cool southern climates are described as the best for roses. OGTR: roses "grow best in full sun, in a temperate climate". PEN, of Melbourne, Canberra, Adelaide and Hobart: "ideal growing conditions for most rose types."

**AF-8 (expert opinion, single source; tropical, Northern Territory).** GA-TROP reports a garden at Berry Springs, just south of Darwin, growing "climbers and ramblers, to hybrid teas, floribundas, bushes, shrubs and miniatures", where roses "peak during the dry season and flower all year." No source located says Hybrid Teas or Floribundas cannot be grown in any Australian zone.

**AF-9 (expert opinion, single source; subtropical, Brisbane).** GA-SUB: "Roses growing in the sub tropics grow as evergreens, growing through the winter without a rest period." (C-8.)

**AF-10 (expert opinion, single source; temperate).** OGTR: "In temperate climates, rose plants growing outside tend to shed leaves in autumn and enter a dormant phase until bud break in spring."

### Main winter pruning (Q1)

**AF-11 (established; southern states generally, and one Brisbane source).** July is the month most often named for the main winter pruning. GAM and GA-MAG: "July is the ideal time to prune roses in most areas". ROSS-CAL (July): "Pruning time in most districts." RSSA-GG: "Pruning is best done in July". SARS-WIN: "Pruning in July is recommended". SARS-CAL (July): "It's rose pruning time!" RSV-M (July): "Start pruning roses." GA-SUB (near Brisbane): roses rest "before they're pruned in July."

**AF-12 (supported interpretation; NSW coast, and warm and coastal climates).** In mild, coastal or frost-free areas, main pruning is late June to the end of July, with early July ideal. RSNSW-G: near the coast, with rare or no frosts, "the most dormant period is late June to the end of July." RSNSW-CAL (July): "In mild areas this is the ideal month for pruning roses." FP (commercial, Sydney): "in warm and coastal climates it is safe to prune roses in mid-winter (early July is ideal)."

**AF-13 (supported interpretation; Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, Brisbane).** Several sources open the window in June. RSV-PB and GA-RP08 (one voice): "The best time to prune is in June or July." NFP (Brisbane): "The best time to prune roses is in June/July". ROSS-CAL (South Australia): "Pruning may commence in late June." RSWA prunes under "WINTER (June - July)". (C-2.)

**AF-14 (established; NSW inland, Victoria, South Australia frost areas, ACT).** In cold, frosty or inland areas, main pruning is delayed to August. RSNSW-G: inland or frosty, "it is best to prune from the beginning to the end of August." RSNSW-CAL: "In cold areas it is better not to prune until August." RSV-PB: "wait until early August". RSV-M (August): "Prune now in cold areas so as to avoid frost damage." RSSA-GG: "(or August in frost areas)". GA-FAQ: "delay pruning until late August". GAM: "leave your pruning until the end of winter." HSOC-AUT (Canberra): "Wait till late July or early August". FP: "late winter in frost-prone areas". ROSS-CAL differs (C-3).

**AF-15 (expert opinion, single source; ACT).** HSOC-AUG, in its August list: "Prune most roses and spray this month or in early September."

**AF-16 (established; national, frost areas).** The stated reason for delaying is frost damage to the new shoots that pruning stimulates. RSNSW-CAL: "Young growth in those areas is very subject to damage by frosts." RSV-PB: "so that the frosts don't knock back the new shoots." GA-FAQ: "so that the new growth that is stimulated will not be killed off by a late frost." OGTR: pruning is done "when there is no chance of frost damaging the tender new shoots." Agrees with `FRD-BUSHROSE-HARDPRUNE-01` AF-16.

**AF-17 (expert opinion, single source; tropical, Northern Territory).** GA-TROP: "In the tropics we do a light prune throughout the year". No source located gives a winter pruning month for the tropics.

**AF-18 (unresolved question; national).** No Australian rose page opened times the main pruning by bud swell. RSNSW-G, RSV-PB and SARS-WIN, checked directly, use month, dormancy ("when they are in their most dormant period", RSNSW-G) or frost. OGTR mentions "bud break in spring" only as the end of dormancy.

### Feeding (Q2)

**AF-19 (established; southern states).** Spring feeding starts in late August or September. RSNSW-CAL: "The latter half of August is ideal for feeding roses". RSV-M (August): "At end of month feed all roses". RSSA-GG: "early September". SARS-CAL (September): "Fertilise early in the month". ROSS-CAL (September): "This is the main fertilising for the year." RSWA: "Feed as new growth appears" under "EARLY SPRING (August - September)".

**AF-20 (supported interpretation; NSW, South Australia, Western Australia, national).** Several sources tie the first feed to a growth stage or to pruning rather than a month. RSNSW-G: "First feed after the winter prune". GA-RP08: "Fertilise about three weeks after you've pruned." SARS-SPR: when "the young shoots are around 2cms in length." RSWA: "as new growth appears". TRE-CAL (commercial): "once the roses have produced a significant amount of foliage and have produced the first blooms" (C-4).

**AF-21 (expert opinion, single source; NSW cold or cooler areas).** RSNSW-CAL: the August feed "in cold areas ... can be delayed until September." RSNSW-G: the second feed is "in mid September in time in the warmer zones or early to mid-October in the cooler zones".

**AF-22 (supported interpretation; southern states).** The last feed is in late summer to early autumn. RSNSW-G: "the last feed in mid to late February." SARS-CAL (February): "This is the month to fertilise"; SARS-AUT: if missed, "ensure the rose plants receive it in March." TRE-CAL: "Fertilise your roses in early Autumn. Do not fertilise in late Autumn." FP: "Feed through spring to early autumn". GA-MAG: a feed "at the start of autumn." (C-5.)

**AF-23 (expert opinion, limited; South Australia sandy soils, Western Australia).** RSSA-GG: four feeds "in September, November, February and April in sandy soils". RSWA, under "LATE AUTUMN (May)": "Continue feeding and deadheading."

**AF-24 (expert opinion, single source; subtropical, Brisbane).** GA-SUB: the growers "try and give them a break by not feeding in the lead up to winter". No month is given.

**AF-25 (expert opinion, single source; tropical, Northern Territory).** GA-TROP: "Jenny fertilises about every six weeks." No month or rest period is given.

**AF-26 (expert opinion, single source; Victoria).** RSV-M: "newly planted bare-root roses which should not be fed until late November or early December", at half the recommended amount.

### Late-season cut-off (Q3)

**AF-27 (established; southern states).** A light summer trim is done between January and March to time the autumn flowers. RSNSW-CAL (February): "trim your plants early this month in mild climate areas." SARS-CAL (February): "Roses can be trimmed in summer". SARS-AUT: "A light, late summer or early autumn trim". ROSS-CAL: "lightly trim all over the bush in mid-February". FP: "around mid January to early February". RSV-M (February), for a show or event. RSWA, "EARLY AUTUMN (March - April)": "Light summer prune (about one-third)".

**AF-28 (expert opinion, single source; NSW).** RSNSW-CAL varies the trim by area: early February in mild areas; "In cool areas do this in January"; "in warm areas trim in March for blooms in May."

**AF-29 (unresolved question; southern states).** No Australian source located gives a month to stop deadheading before winter. RSV-M (April): "Deadhead and enjoy." RSWA (May): "Continue feeding and deadheading." ROSS-CAL (May): "Good blooms can continue well into the month."

**AF-30 (expert opinion, single source; NSW).** RSNSW-CAL, on basal shoots: "those growing in March and April will mature adequately before mid-winter."

### Planting and moving (Q4)

**AF-31 (established; southern states, and one national statement).** Bare-root roses are planted in June and July. GA-MAG: "May, June and July are the months to plant bare-rooted roses in Australia." RSSA-GG: "This is best done in June or July." RSNSW-CAL: "Prepare the soil for planting in June/July." SARS-WIN: "Planting is best done in June, July or early August." ROSS-CAL (June): "Planting time for new plants." RSV-M (June): "Start planting new roses". TRE-CAL: "Planting is best done during winter". RSNSW-G: bare-root roses "are only available in winter."

**AF-32 (supported interpretation; national, South Australia, Western Australia, ACT).** Some sources widen the window. May: GA-MAG; RSWA (May) "Plant new roses for future seasons of colour"; PEN (commercial) dispatches "between May – July". August: SARS-WIN "early August"; ROSS-CAL (August) "Bare-rooted plants can still be planted this month"; HSOC-AUG (Canberra, August) "Plant new roses this month." (C-6.)

**AF-33 (supported interpretation; NSW, South Australia).** Potted roses can be planted at any time of year, avoiding hot weather. FP: "potted roses can be planted year-round (however, you should avoid planting in very hot weather)." SARS-POT: "Remember don't plant in hot weather." RSNSW-G: potted roses "are available for sale all year round".

**AF-34 (supported interpretation; Victoria, South Australia, national nursery).** An established rose is best moved in winter, while dormant. TRE-T: "The best time to move a rose is in the winter dormancy when the plant is asleep". TRE-CAL: "winter is the best time to do so." SARS-WIN: "Winter is the best time to transplant existing roses." RSV-M names June: "Move established roses to new locations".

### Winter clear-up, spray and protection (Q5)

**AF-35 (established; southern states).** Fallen leaves and prunings are cleared at winter pruning, July to August. RSV-M (July): "Remove all leaves from bushes and rake up all around the rose beds." ROSS-CAL (July): "Rake up cuttings and leaves and dispose of them." SARS-CAL (August): "Ensure rose prunings and as many fallen leaves as possible are removed." SARS-WIN and GAM say the same without a month.

**AF-36 (established; southern states, ACT).** Where a winter clean-up spray is described, it follows pruning. July: RSNSW-CAL (lime sulphur under July), RSV-M (spray "on bare roses and lightly on ground", repeated in two weeks), ROSS-SP "After pruning in July", ROSS-CAL. June to July: RSWA "Spray after pruning to clean up pests and disease." August: SARS-CAL "After tidying up, ensure roses are sprayed". August or early September (Canberra): HSOC-AUG. After pruning, no month: RSSA-GG, TRE-CAL, FP, GAM. (C-7.)

**AF-37 (supported interpretation; national).** The winter spray is applied before new growth appears. GAM: "This must be done before the new growth appears, as lime burns new growth." FP: "After pruning and before new growth appears". GA-RP08: "while the plants have no leaves and are dormant."

**AF-38 (unresolved question; national).** No Australian source located describes winter protection, such as mounding soil or mulch around the base, for bush roses. This is an absence across the pages opened, not a statement that it is never needed.

### Placing the gardener and year-to-year variation (Q7, Q8)

**AF-39 (supported interpretation; national).** Zone guides place a gardener by listing example towns for each zone, and OGO asks gardeners to find their location on its zone map. TSC, tropical: "Includes: Darwin, Cairns, Townsville, Exmouth". OGO: "Think of our planting guides as a starting point."

**AF-40 (established; national, Queensland, South Australia).** Sources state that conditions, and so timing, vary within zones and states. OGO: conditions vary "even between neighbouring suburbs". TRE-CAL: "Due to Australia's vast climatic variations, seasonal tasks may vary". QRS-G: "Growing and climate conditions will vary across the State of Queensland." SARS-WIN: "colder and wetter in the Adelaide Hills" and "Frosts are also common in country areas." SARS-SPR: first flowers "in mid to late October in warmer areas, including Adelaide, and in early to mid-November in cooler areas of the State".

**AF-41 (established; national).** The Bureau of Meteorology maps the average number of potential frost days each month and year (climatology 1976 to 2005). BOM-FROST: "extensive areas in northern Australia are generally frost free"; frost potential "increases with elevation"; "Potential frost days are mainly confined to a period from late autumn to early spring".

**AF-42 (supported interpretation; national).** BOM-WIF: "Frost is a localised phenomenon, the severity of which can vary significantly over a very small area", with frost hollows forming in valleys through cold air drainage.

**AF-43 (supported interpretation; frost areas, national).** Some sources tell gardeners in frost areas to wait for the last frost, or for local conditions, rather than a fixed month. TRE-CAL: "If you live in an area that receives frosts, wait until the last frost has passed." PEN: "pruned after the last frost." OGTR: "when there is no chance of frost damaging the tender new shoots." FP: "in July or early August depending on local conditions."

**AF-44 (unresolved question; national).** No Australian source located gives guidance for an unusually early or late season, beyond the frost advice in AF-43.

**AF-45 (unresolved question; arid).** No Australian rose source located gives month windows for the arid zone. OGTR names a rootstock for "inland Australia" but no timing.

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority. Each states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. Where the mild and cold bands begin and end.**
No rose source defines its bands (AF-5); zone guides list towns only (AF-1, AF-39). Drawing boundaries would mean adopting a climate measure, such as frost days (AF-41), that no rose source uses, which is a different evidentiary standard.
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Points 2 and 3.
*Recommended default:* Pip asks the gardener whether they get frosts most winters and whether they are near the coast or inland, as the rose sources frame it (AF-4), and uses the answer to choose the band.

**R-2. Months for the arid and tropical zones.**
No rose source gives arid months (AF-45); tropical evidence is one 2006 garden (AF-8, AF-17, AF-25). Searches of Queensland, Northern Territory and arid-region sources found nothing further. Closing it would need local growers or societies by consultation, a different evidentiary standard.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission consultation.
*Recommended default:* Pip shows no months for the arid and tropical zones and keeps season-level timing with the bud-swell check, noting that roses are harder to manage in hot, humid areas (AF-6).

**R-3. What to say in an unusually early or late season.**
No source gives guidance (AF-44) beyond waiting for the last frost (AF-43).
*Founder decision needed:* Founder Decision Point 5.
*Recommended default:* Pip treats the month window as a guide and defers to the bud-swell check and, in frost areas, to the last frost.

**R-4. Tasmania and alpine areas.**
No Tasmanian or alpine rose source with months was located; Hobart and Launceston fall in the cold band of the zone guides (AF-1), and BOM-FROST notes frost is common in the alpine regions (AF-41). Further sources would need direct contact with societies.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission it.
*Recommended default:* Pip uses the cold band (AF-14) for Tasmania and alpine areas, with the last-frost advice (AF-43).

**R-5. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis, recorded as such and assigned Not Assigned (EAS §2.10).**
These are questions arising from the findings that the evidence does not itself state. They are not findings and are not proposed for adoption.

### 11.8 Decision points in §10

§10 asked eight questions. No Founder answered them on the Review Form. The Pip Knowledge Rules (approved 5 October 2026) and the standing decisions made since settle points 2 to 7, so none is asked:

- **Point 2 (which bands).** No bands or zones are used. Under the standing decision "Places inside a country" (6 October 2026), a statement is shown only in the country, state or town its source names. Under the standing decision "Frost" (6 October 2026), where sources split timing by frost or cold, Pip says the condition in words.
- **Point 3 (placing a gardener).** Settled by the standing decisions "Where a gardener is" (5 October 2026) and "Places inside a country": the app works out the state and nearest named town on the gardener's own device, and Pip does not ask about frost or the coast.
- **Point 4 (months beside the plant check).** It is now rule 6: months are only a guide and the check on the plant decides.
- **Point 5 (band edges and unusual seasons).** There are no band edges. Rule 3 gives the range and rule 6 the guide; near a state border the app asks which state the rose is in.
- **Point 6 (different months).** Rule 3: the range is given, never one figure. Rule 2: where sources disagree, Pip says so.
- **Point 7 (tropical, subtropical and arid).** Rule 5: where no source was found Pip says so (AF-45, AF-52, AF-71). Brisbane's pruning months are local information (AF-50). What sources say of humid climates is AF-6.
- **Point 8 (R-1 to R-4).** None is commissioned by this approval. R-1 and R-3 concern bands, which are not used. R-2 and R-4 are said to gardeners as gaps. A Founder may commission any of them at any time.

The commission said products were not collected as findings. Under the standing decisions "Products" and "Exclusions" (5 October 2026), the types of spray the pages name (lime sulphur, copper, oil) are recorded as they name them; no brand and no mixing amount is.

One decision remains, and it is asked on the Pip's Answers Form: approval of Pip's answers, which also approves these findings as corrected for an Approved Research Compilation.
