# Decision Brief — Which Months for Each Rose Job in the United Kingdom?

This is the plain-language companion to `FRD-BUSHROSE-TIMING-UK-01`. It adds no evidence, changes no finding and changes no confidence level. If the Brief and the FRD differ, the FRD governs.

## 1. What you are being asked to do

Read this Brief. The decisions are on the 34 findings, on four further-research items, and on seven questions about how Pip shows months to UK gardeners, ending with one overall decision: **approve as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-UK-01`, approve with amendments, request further work, or decline.**

## 2. The short version

- **UK sources mostly give one window for the whole country.** The only job where they say colder areas differ is the main pruning.
- **Main pruning: February to March** (**High**). The RHS: "Late winter (February or March)". The Met Office calls it "a classic March task".
- **South earlier, north later.** The RHS: "usually in mid-February in the south, but in northern and colder areas wait until March" (**Moderate** each). No source says where "south" ends and "north" begins.
- **Some rose growers prune earlier,** in January and February (**Moderate**). One nursery prunes in November and December in the south (**Very Low**).
- **Months come with a signal.** Sources pair the month with "when growth is just resuming", buds, dormancy or a frost-free day (**Moderate**). That fits Pip's bud-swell check, which stays the test.
- **Feeding:** first feed in March or April (**High**), another in mid-summer after the first flush (**High**); one grower gives a last feed by the end of August (**Low**).
- **Deadheading:** UK sources give no month to stop for repeat-flowering roses, and one grower is still deadheading in October (**Moderate**). That differs from the general pattern you approved, which came from US sources.
- **Planting and moving:** bare-root roses late autumn to early spring (**High**); potted roses any time in suitable ground (**High**); moving while dormant, with windows from late October to mid-March (**Moderate**).
- **Winter:** clear fallen leaves in autumn (**High**). Only one product seller describes a winter spray (**Very Low**). No UK source describes heaping soil over the base for winter (**Low**).
- **Frost by region:** the Met Office says the last frost is earlier in the south and on coasts, later in the north and on high ground (**High**), and sheltered or exposed gardens shift this by a week or more (**High**).

## 3. What was researched

Q1: when the main pruning is done, and what signal it is tied to. Q2: when feeding starts and when the last feed is given. Q3: when deadheading and summer pruning stop. Q4: when to plant bare-root and potted roses and when to move one. Q5: when to clear up, spray and protect for winter. Q6: whether the UK splits into colder and milder areas. Q7: how a gardener could be placed in an area. Q8: how sources deal with early or late seasons.

**Fit with what you've already approved.** The findings agree with the dormancy ARC that the calendar window depends on climate and comes later where it's colder, and none of the UK sources uses months in place of a signal on the plant. One source (Love the Garden) says to prune "before leaf buds are starting to swell", which isn't quite the same as Pip's bud-swell check. The feeding months fit the approved spring-start, summer-taper pattern. **Two differ:** UK sources give no autumn stop for deadheading, where the approved pattern (from US sources) stops in early-to-mid autumn; and no UK source describes the soil-heaping winter protection approved from US sources. The planting, moving, leaf and spray sources from the planting and spraying dossiers were re-opened and agree.

## 6. Where the sources differ

- **C-1 Which pruning months.** The RHS and most others: February to March. David Austin and Peter Beales (recent pages): January to February. Thompson & Morgan: November to March. Style Roses: November and December in the south.
- **C-2 Which signal.** The RHS: when growth is just resuming. Love the Garden: before buds swell. Harkness: before buds open. Others: a frost-free day.
- **C-3 Last feed.** The RHS: mid-summer. Peter Beales: end of August.
- **C-4 Bare-root in mid-winter.** The RHS: avoid mid-winter when the ground is frozen. Peter Beales: December and January are good months, if the ground isn't frozen.
- **C-5 Moving window.** The RHS: late October to mid-March. David Austin: November to February. Peter Beales: November or February.
- **C-6 Stopping deadheading.** UK sources: no stop month. The approved pattern: early-to-mid autumn.
- **C-7 Autumn trim.** September, October to November, or November, depending on the source.
- **C-8 Winter protection.** The approved US pattern heaps soil over the base. No UK source does this.
- **C-9 Early-winter pruning in the south.** One nursery prunes in November and December; this goes against the RHS and the approved dormancy ARC.

## 8. The decisions you are asked to make

Recommendations are the ROC's, not decisions.

1. **Approve the findings as `ARC-BUSHROSE-TIMING-UK-01`?** Approve; approve with named changes; request further work; or decline.

2. **Does the UK need areas ("bands")?**
   (a) No, one window for everything; (b) one window for everything, with a note that the south usually prunes around mid-February and the north and colder areas wait until March; (c) two areas for pruning only; (d) five areas, using the RHS frost regions.
   *ROC recommendation: (b).*

3. **If there are areas, where's the line?**
   (a) South = south-west, south coast, southern England and south Wales; everywhere else colder; (b) the Midlands counted with the south; (c) no fixed line: Pip shows both and the gardener picks.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).* No source draws a line, and two disagree about the Midlands.

4. **How is a gardener placed in an area?**
   (a) Automatically from the town or region they entered; (b) automatically from GPS; (c) Pip suggests one from the region and asks them to confirm, mentioning that sheltered or town gardens are earlier and exposed or high ones later.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).*

5. **How does Pip show months alongside the bud-swell check?**
   (a) The check stays the gate, with the month window shown under it; (b) a reminder before the season, with the check still required; (c) both.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).* Months never replace the bud-swell check.

6. **What does Pip say near an area's edge, or in an early or late year?**
   (a) Show both areas' windows and say the buds and a frost-free day decide; (b) show only the gardener's area; (c) also say late frosts still happen and point to the local forecast.
   *ROC recommendation: (a) with (c).*

7. **Which pruning window does Pip show for the UK?**
   (a) February to March; (b) January to March; (c) February to March, noting some rose growers start in January.
   *ROC recommendation: (c).*

8. **How does Pip handle deadheading in a UK autumn?**
   (a) Keep the approved general pattern; (b) for the UK, say sources give no stop month and some deadhead into October, but still don't prune hard in autumn; (c) wait for more research.
   *ROC recommendation: (b).*
