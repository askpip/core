## 2. Research Commission Record (Summary)

The commission serves the Ask Pip minimum viable product (MVP).

**Commissioned question.** How can a beginner gardener's rose be qualified as inside or outside the approved Bush Rose scope, first from a label or purchase information and second, where none exists, from features observable on the plant and in a photograph, and what cannot be established without a label or expert help?

**Scope.** The Bush Rose scope approved through `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` is taken as given: Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses in bush form, and Grandiflora in bush form provisionally. Excluded types (shrub, Old Garden, species, climbing and rambling, groundcover, miniature and miniflora, patio, standard and weeping standard roses, and climbing sports of included cultivars) are researched as lookalikes. New Zealand sources come first, then Australian, United Kingdom and United States sources. Cultivar identification from appearance, pruning technique, disease, image sourcing, questionnaire wording and application changes are excluded.

**Commissioned questions.**

- **Q1** Included classes: how sources describe and distinguish Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora.
- **Q2** Excluded types: how sources describe each excluded type and which features a beginner can judge.
- **Q3** Labels, names and marketing terms used in New Zealand and Australia, how they map to the approved classes, and where a class can be checked.
- **Q4** Boundary cases: patio roses, Grandiflora against tall Hybrid Tea, shrub roses sold as bush roses, and climbing sports.
- **Q5** Features observable on a dormant established rose, and what photographs can and cannot establish.
- **Q6** What sources advise when the class is unknown.
- **Q7** What comparison images would need to show, without sourcing them.

## 3. Research Plan

**Approach.** Each commissioned question was broken into the statements a source would have to make to answer it.

| Question | Information required | Source categories sought |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Stated features and sizes for each included class; any statement of how the classes differ or overlap | Rose societies, the Royal Horticultural Society (RHS), university extension, nurseries |
| Q2 | Stated features, sizes and habits for each excluded type | The same, plus breeder and marketer pages for named product lines |
| Q3 | Category names and habit labels used by New Zealand nurseries and the New Zealand Rose Society; any directory that gives a cultivar's class | New Zealand society and retailer pages |
| Q4 | Statements on patio classification, Grandiflora pruning, shrub roses sold as bush roses, and climbing sports | Societies, nurseries, extension |
| Q5 | Statements on which plant parts identify a rose, on winter or dormant identification, and on photograph and application accuracy | Societies, nurseries, extension |
| Q6 | Statements on what to do when the class is unknown | RHS, societies, nurseries |
| Q7 | Statements on what an identification image must show | Any of the above |

**Method.**

- Web searches were run for each question, and pages were read through an automated extraction step that returns text drawn from the page in answer to a stated question.
- Every extraction result is recorded as returned. Quotations in this dossier are as returned by that step and have not been checked against the raw page.
- Every source consulted is logged in §4, and searches that returned nothing usable are recorded in §4.3.
- Findings are separated by claim so that each receives its own Evidence Confidence Level (EAS §3.2).
- Quotations relied on for AF-3, AF-5, AF-8, AF-10, AF-20, AF-21 and AF-23 were re-checked against a second extraction of the same page.

**Anticipated challenges.**

- New Zealand sources may be mostly commercial.
- Class assignment for a cultivar may differ between authorities.
- Dormancy removes flowers and foliage from view.
- Extraction summaries may compress or omit page detail.

## 5. Evidence Evaluation

The evaluation applies the EAS factors (Chapter 4) to each finding individually in §8. The general position across the body of evidence is as follows.

**Relevance.** Sources on class descriptions, categories and labels bear directly on Q1 to Q4. Sources on identification (HRA, ROSS, AEX, ILL-A) bear on Q5 and Q6 only indirectly, because none addresses a beginner placing an established dormant garden rose in a modern class.

**Source reliability.** The RHS, ARS, the New Zealand Rose Society and the Rose Society of New South Wales are recognised rose bodies. Extension services are recognised horticultural authorities. Commercial pages are reliable as records of how roses are described and sold, and are given less weight as classification authority.

**Consistency.** Sources agree on the central features of each included class and on the separateness of most excluded types. They disagree on plant heights, on the nearest class for patio roses, and on the class of some cultivars.

**Independence.** POR repeats ARS. Commercial pages may draw on common sources, and agreement among them is weighed with that in mind.

**Limitations.** All page content was read through an extraction step. New Zealand evidence is largely from online retail and one society. Dormancy is not addressed by any class-description source. Source publication dates were not captured by the extraction, so currency is not assessed.

## 6. Managing Conflicting Evidence and Uncertainty

**Height of Hybrid Tea against Floribunda.** YAT gives about 1.5 m for Hybrid Tea and about 90 cm for Floribunda. LBG gives 1 to 1.5 m and about 90 cm. ILL-T gives 4 to 5 ft and 2 to 3 ft. HEI gives 3 to 5 ft and 2 to 4 ft. OSU describes Floribunda as "generally a larger plant than hybrid tea roses". RHS-MB gives 0.5 to 1.5 m for both together. The disagreement is unresolved and is carried in AF-3.

**Grandiflora as a class.** ARS, ILL-T, YAT, CLE and HEI describe it. The New Zealand Rose Society type list, the Rose Society of New South Wales list and the category lists of TPS and MAT do not include it. RHS-MB does not name it. The difference is preserved in AF-4.

**Class of individual cultivars.** ILL-T states that "the same rose may be placed in different classes by different authorities". NZRS-F notes that "some roses may fit into more than one category". DG lists 'Queen Elizabeth' as both Hybrid Tea and cluster-flowered (Floribunda and Grandiflora), with three different height ranges. These agree and are carried in AF-5.

**Nearest class for a patio rose.** RHS-SB and RHS-PG group patio roses with polyantha and miniature roses, while RHS-SBP says they are pruned as for a full-sized floribunda on a smaller scale. WAI-P calls them "a down-sized version of a floribunda". PB places them "closer to dwarf floribundas" and distinct from miniatures. JP calls "patio rose" a general term for any container-grown rose, most commonly floribunda-miniature crosses. The disagreement is unresolved and is carried in AF-10.

**Uncertainty preserved.** Whether beginners can reliably use any dormant-plant feature to place a rose is not answered by the evidence (§4.3, §9).

## 7. Principal Findings

Each finding is one claim. "Later use" states what the finding could support in later knowledge work: **Q** for a gate question, **A** for an accepted or stopping answer, **U** for the "unknown" outcome, **I** for a comparison-image requirement, and **N** for none of these.

### Included classes (Q1)

**AF-1 (established).** Flower arrangement is the feature all consulted class descriptions use first. Hybrid Tea roses bear single or few flowers per stem (RHS-MB, ARS, RSNSW, ILL-T, LBG), Floribundas bear clusters (RHS-MB, ARS, RSNSW, NZRS-A, YAT, ILL-T), and Grandifloras bear Hybrid Tea-form flowers in clusters (ARS, YAT, HEI, CLE). Later use: Q, U, I.

**AF-2 (established).** Sources that state a height describe Grandiflora as the tallest of the three included classes: YAT "up to 1.8m", ARS "6 to 8 feet or more", HEI "5 to 7 feet", ILL-T "one of the taller roses in the modern rose class", CLE "generally tall, slender plants". Later use: Q, I.

**AF-3 (supported interpretation).** Height ranges for Hybrid Tea and Floribunda overlap across sources, no source gives a height threshold separating them, and one source (OSU) describes Floribunda as the larger plant. Later use: U.

**AF-4 (established).** Grandiflora is described in ARS, ILL-T, YAT, CLE and HEI. It is not among the options in the NZRS-F Type filter, is not among the nine classifications listed by RSNSW, and does not appear in the category lists of TPS or MAT as extracted. Later use: Q, U.

**AF-5 (established).** Some individual cultivars are placed in different classes by different authorities (ILL-T, NZRS-F, DG). Later use: Q, U.

**AF-6 (expert opinion, limited).** For pruning, OSU treats "Hybrid Tea-Grandiflora" as one category (moderate pruning to 12 to 18 inches, three to six canes retained). WA gives one cut height (12 to 18 inches) for Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora, with 10 to 12 inches for Floribunda. HEI advises not cutting Grandifloras back as severely as Hybrid Teas. RHS has no Grandiflora pruning group, and CLE gives no Grandiflora-specific pruning in the factsheet consulted. Later use: N (a pruning matter reserved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` §10).

### Excluded types (Q2)

**AF-7 (established).** Shrub roses are described as larger, open, spreading or arching bushes: RHS-SH "an open and spreading shape, often with arching prickly branches", ARS "sprawling habit" growing "5 to 15 feet or more in every direction", RSNSW "large bushes, often tall and spreading with many branching canes of a lax arching habit". Later use: Q, A, I.

**AF-8 (supported interpretation).** The shrub class is heterogeneous and overlaps bush habit. ILL-T calls it a "catch all" class for roses "that do not fit well in other classes". YAT gives 0.5 to 6 m. DA describes English roses as "bushy, upright or elegantly arching" and "quite short to tall". WAI-S lists one shrub-category rose with the habit "Rose Bush". MAT lists "Easy Care Shrubs & Shrublets" as a category. Later use: U.

**AF-9 (established).** Patio roses are a category separate from Hybrid Tea and Floribunda. RHS groups Patio with Polyantha and Miniature as "small bush roses" of 30 to 75 cm (RHS-SB) in Pruning Group 22 (RHS-PG). NZRS-F lists "Patio" as a Type. TPS lists "Patio Roses", MAT lists "Miniature Patio", and WAI-P and TUI treat patio roses as their own group. RSNSW lists no patio category. Later use: Q, A.

**AF-10 (supported interpretation).** Sources differ on the class a patio rose is nearest to: with miniatures and polyanthas (RHS-SB, RHS-PG), a "down-sized version of a floribunda" (WAI-P), "closer to dwarf floribundas" and not miniatures (PB), or a general term for any container rose, most commonly floribunda-miniature crosses (JP). WAI-P shows mature sizes of 80 x 80 cm and 45 x 45 cm for two patio cultivars. Later use: A, U.

**AF-11 (established).** Miniature roses are described as small, with stated upper heights of about 50 to 75 cm: RSNSW-M "no more than 50 cm", RHS-SB "often no more than 25cm", ARS "about 15 to 30 inches", ILL-T "3 to 18 inches", YAT "20 to 50 cm", LBG "6 to 24 inches". Later use: Q, A, I.

**AF-12 (supported interpretation).** Miniflora roses are distinguished from miniatures and floribundas by bloom and foliage size (RSNSW-M "blooms and foliage larger than Miniature roses and smaller than Floribunda roses"; ARS "intermediate bloom size and foliage"), which is not observable on a dormant plant. Later use: U.

**AF-13 (supported interpretation).** Groundcover roses are a separate category (RSNSW "low spreading plants to 50cm"; YAT; NZRS-A; TPS; NZRS-F "Groundcover"). TES describes Flower Carpet as a "ground cover rose" forming "a low, dense and compact bush" occupying "a slightly flattened square metre", and as "a slightly larger but equally floriferous shrub" when left unpruned. Later use: Q, A, I.

**AF-14 (supported interpretation).** Climbing sports of bush-form roses exist and are catalogued as climbing types: FVR describes "climbing sports of bush-form Hybrid Teas that produce long canes (6' to 10')" with flowers "on shorter laterals". NZRS-F offers Type values "Climbing", "Min Climbing", "Pat Climbing" and "Tea Climbing". TPS and WAI list "Patio Climbing" and climbing categories. Later use: Q, A, I.

**AF-15 (established).** A standard rose is recognisable by a single tall bare stem carrying a grafted crown. RHS-ST: "a single tall stem, topped with foliage and flowers, like a small tree", "usually a modern rose or miniature rose grafted onto a strong, bare stem". RSNSW: Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, Shrub or Miniature "budded onto a single stem of a standard height"; weeping standards on stems 1.5 to 1.8 m. WAI lists a "Standard 45cm" category and MAT lists "Mini Standards 450mm". Later use: Q, A, I.

**AF-16 (supported interpretation).** Old Garden Roses and species roses are defined by history, lineage and flowering habit. ARS: Old Garden Roses are those that "existed prior to 1867"; species roses are "usually single-petaled (4 to 8 petals), once-blooming", 2 to 20 feet. RSNSW: heritage roses are "usually only spring flowering". NZRS-F lists many Old Garden Rose types (Alba, Bourbon, Damask and others). Later use: Q, U.

### Labels, names and marketing terms (Q3)

**AF-17 (established, sample limited).** New Zealand retailers use "bush" in more than one sense. TPS names "Hybrid Tea Bush Roses" and "Floribunda Bush Roses" and also a "Rose Bush" category. YAT uses "Bush Roses" as a group of 0.8 to 1.2 m. TUI pairs "standard and bush roses". WAI-P uses the habit "Rose Bush" for patio roses, and WAI-S uses it for one shrub-category rose. TES calls a groundcover rose "a low, dense and compact bush". The NZRS-F Type list has no "Bush" value. Later use: Q, A.

**AF-18 (supported interpretation).** New Zealand online nursery listings organise roses by type: TPS (Floribunda, Hybrid Tea, Patio, Shrub, David Austin, Groundcover, Climbing, Standard and others), MAT (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, Climbers, David Austin, Easy Care Shrubs & Shrublets, Miniature Patio, Old Fashioned), WAI (Patio, Shrub, Climbing Patio, Standard). What is printed on physical plant tags was not established. Later use: Q, U.

**AF-19 (established, coverage unknown).** NZRS-F is a searchable directory by rose name, breeder and international name, with a Type value for each entry, and states that "some roses may fit into more than one category so a general category has been listed". The Type options are: Alb, Alba, Ayrs, Bourbon, Centifolia, China, Climbing, Damask, Floribunda, Gallica, Gc/Shr, Groundcover, HBank, Hybrid macrantha, Hybrid multiflora, Hybrid Musk, Hybrid nitida, Hybrid Perpetual, Hybrid rugosa, Hybrid sempervirens, Hybrid spin, Hybrid Tea, Laevigata, Min Climbing, Miniature, Moss, Noisette, Pat Climbing, Patio, Polyantha, Portland, Rambler, Shrub, Species, Tea, Tea Climbing. How many New Zealand-available cultivars it holds was not established. Later use: Q.

### Observable features, identification and uncertainty (Q5, Q6)

**AF-20 (supported interpretation).** Sources that address identifying an unlabelled rose describe it as difficult. HRA lists "foliage, stipules ... armature (the prickles), sepals ... scent, bloom and growth habit" as the key identifying characteristics, "the latter two [bloom and growth habit] being the most variable", states that "the bloom is notoriously fickle" and that "both shape and colour vary with climate, soil and season", and advises taking "good quality blooms with leaves or a small healthy plant in bloom to a large collection or a rosarian". Its author adds, "I can rarely offer help, since most of the 'old roses' are Hybrid Teas." ROSS states that "the foliage is crucial for an accurate identification". AEX declined to identify a rose by type, citing "over 30,000 varieties". Later use: U.

**AF-21 (expert opinion).** ROSS states: "It is quite possible to identify, with years in the business, well over 90% of all varieties we grow just from bare 'sticks'", and adds that "the length of time working within the industry helps like nothing else can". The claim comes from one nursery, concerns the varieties that nursery grows and identification by experienced staff, and is not supported by a method or figures. Later use: N.

**AF-22 (established, indirect).** The one located evaluation of photograph-based identification applications (ILL-A) covered 55 tree species photographed by professional arborists. It reports genus-level accuracy of 71.8% to 97.3%, species-level accuracy of 40.9% to 83.9%, and finds bark pictures "not nearly as accurate as" leaf pictures. It does not address roses, cultivars, garden plants or leafless plants. Later use: N.

**AF-23 (expert opinion).** RHS-MBP: "If you inherit an overgrown rose and don't know what type it is (and don't want to wait until summer when it flowers), it is best to prune as for a hybrid tea." Later use: U.

**AF-24 (supported interpretation).** Sources direct an unsure gardener to experienced people: NZRS-Q "contact your local rose society or head along to a pruning demonstration" (in a pruning answer), HRA recommends a rosarian, ROSS advises being "prepared to say you don't know" and getting "a second opinion". Later use: U.

### Photographs

No new finding on photograph limits is made. `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` records that general plant-diagnostic guidance favours physical examination over photographs alone (AF-34, Low) and lists which whole-plant features can and cannot be assessed visually (AF-41); those remain the project's starting position. AF-20 and AF-22 add that identification of an unlabelled rose draws on plant parts and seasons a single dormant photograph does not show.

## 9. Recommendations for Further Research

Each recommendation could not be resolved within the commission's scope and authority, and states what a Founder decision would need to settle and the ROC's recommended practical default (ROC Operations Manual §7.7).

**R-1. Which dormant-plant features a beginner can use to place a rose.**
No source located addresses it (§4.3). Answering it needs observational testing or consultation with experienced rose growers, which is a different evidentiary standard than the commission applied.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission expert consultation or testing.
*Recommended default:* dormant-plant features are not used to establish that a rose is inside scope; a type stated on a label, receipt, listing or by an experienced grower is used where available, and otherwise the type is treated as unknown.

**R-2. What New Zealand plant tags and pot labels print about type.**
Answering it needs in-person observation of retail stock, which is outside the sources and methods available to the commission.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to arrange a small in-store survey.
*Recommended default:* a stated type term found on a tag, receipt or listing is accepted as evidence where present; the word "bush" alone is not (AF-17).

**R-3. How many commonly sold New Zealand cultivars appear in the New Zealand Rose Society Rose Finder, and with what type.**
Answering it needs interactive queries against the directory.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to commission a sample check or approach the Society.
*Recommended default:* treat the Rose Finder as one verification route for a named cultivar (AF-19) without relying on it for coverage.

**R-4. Whether Grandiflora pruning response is close enough to Hybrid Tea for shared guidance.**
Further searching found only Low-level evidence (AF-6). A firmer answer needs rose-specific extension pruning material naming Grandiflora, or specialist advice.
*Founder decision needed:* whether to accept Low confidence or to commission specialist advice.
*Recommended default:* retain the provisional inclusion approved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` and issue no Grandiflora-specific pruning guidance until stronger evidence exists.

**R-5. The ROC's own reasoned synthesis, recorded as such and assigned Not Assigned (EAS §2.10).**
These are questions arising from the findings that the evidence does not itself state. They are not findings and are not proposed for adoption.

- R-5a. Whether a gardener's recollection of whether the rose bore single blooms or clusters is a reliable basis for separating Hybrid Tea from Floribunda when the plant is dormant, given AF-1.
- R-5b. Whether pruning an unidentified rose as a Hybrid Tea (AF-23) would harm a shrub, climbing or standard rose, given AF-7, AF-14 and AF-15.
- R-5c. Whether a gardener can distinguish an unpruned groundcover rose from a small shrub or bush rose when dormant, given AF-13.

*Founder decision needed:* whether any of these is worth commissioning.
*Recommended default:* none of them is acted on until independent evidence is found.
