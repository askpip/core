## 1. What this covers

Three records published on 29 September 2026 have no comparison images yet:

- **PKR-OBS-000007, rootstock suckers:** a new observation.
- **PKR-SGT-000001 v1.1, the dormancy gate:** the gardener now judges dormant versus actively growing, and the answer decides between full pruning and the removal-only session.
- **The removal-only session:** dead, damaged or diseased wood on a rose already in leaf (PKR-DEC-000001 and 000004 v1.1).

Every entry below is drawn from an approved Assessed Finding. Nothing adds new horticultural content.

## 2. Cross-cutting requirements

The rules in §2 of the v0.2 brief apply unchanged:

- **Image rights are unresolved** for every source.
- **AI-generated placeholders are allowed for internal app-function testing only.** Each one must:
  - match its entry;
  - be labelled `Placeholder — AI-generated`;
  - never be marked Approved;
  - be replaced by a real or rights-confirmed photograph before any Comparison Image PKR is approved.
- **Signals that need physical contact** (the pith after a cut, how a cane bends) get no image.

Two points are specific to this brief:

- **No source says what a photograph can establish about a suspected sucker** (ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01, R-2). The approved default is that a photo only prompts the gardener to check the plant, and that Pip never decides a shoot is a sucker from a photo. The sucker images below are therefore for **teaching what to look for on the plant**, not for photo diagnosis.
- **Only the point of origin works on a dormant, leafless rose** (SUCKERS AF-10, D5). The leaf, flower and colour images (SUCKERS-05 and 06) are growing-season supporting signs only. Pip never uses them alone.

## 4. Rootstock Suckers (PKR-OBS-000007)

### 4.1 Sucker starting below a visible bud union (positive example)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** The deciding check. A shoot that starts below the bud union is a sucker (SUCKERS AF-1, High).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Ground level, close enough that the knobbly bud union and the exact point where the shoot starts are both sharp in one frame.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Grafted Hybrid Tea, Floribunda or Grandiflora. Dormant season preferred, because origin is the only sign that works when the rose is leafless (AF-10).
- **Common Lookalikes:** The rose's own new basal cane (§4.2). Sources warn the two are easily confused (AF-13, Moderate).
- **Limitations:**
  - The start point can be under the soil (AF-2); this image shows the easier, above-soil case.
  - One source says a rootstock stub left above the union can also sucker (AF-14, Low). This isn't shown.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder: OpenArt "GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst" (gpt-image-2-5-sunburst, text2image), generated 29 September 2026. Image Status: `Placeholder — AI-generated`. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01.png`, held at `Working/Drafts/Comparison Images/SUCKERS/`.
- **Accessible Label:** "The base of a winter rose. A thin shoot grows from below the knobbly graft join: a sucker."

### 4.2 The rose's own basal cane from the union (the lookalike to keep)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-02`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** A new cane starting at or just above the union is the rose's own. It is valuable and is kept (AF-1, AF-24, High).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Same framing as SUCKERS-01, for a side-by-side comparison.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** As SUCKERS-01.
- **Common Lookalikes:** A sucker (§4.1).
- **Limitations:** Two sources say the rose's own new shoots are often red or bronze. One source treats this as unreliable (AF-5, Low). Don't rely on colour.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-02.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "The base of a winter rose. A strong new cane grows from the top of the graft join: the rose's own cane, which you keep."

### 4.3 Sucker starting under the soil, traced by clearing soil

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-03`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** A sucker can start below the soil, sometimes a little away from the plant. Trace it down, clearing soil if needed (AF-2, High).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** From slightly above, with the soil cleared back so the shoot's connection to the root below the union can be seen. Include the union for reference.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Grafted bush rose, any season.
- **Common Lookalikes:** A seedling or weed coming up nearby (not addressed by any source found).
- **Limitations:** The image can show a cleared example. It can't show what the gardener will find without clearing.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-03.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Soil cleared from around a rose shows a shoot growing from a root below the graft join."

### 4.4 Bud union not visible (buried union)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-04`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** The case where Pip doesn't call anything a sucker (D6). Planting depth varies, so the union may be below the soil (AF-12, Moderate).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Ground level, the same framing as SUCKERS-01, showing canes going straight into the soil with no knot visible.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Bush rose, dormant.
- **Common Lookalikes:** An own-root rose. A missing knot doesn't by itself mean the rose is own-root (`ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` AF-3).
- **Limitations:** The photo can't tell a buried union from an own-root rose.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-04.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Rose canes go straight into the soil with no graft join visible."

### 4.5 Sucker leaves beside the rose's own leaves (growing-season supporting sign)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-05`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** Sucker leaves are often paler, smaller, or have a different number of leaflets (AF-3, Moderate).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Side by side, same scale, on a neutral background.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Growing season only (D5).
- **Common Lookalikes:** Some rootstocks have leaves much like the rose's, and the "seven leaflets" rule isn't reliable (AF-4, Moderate; C-2).
- **Limitations:** A supporting sign only, never used alone. Rootstocks differ by country, so no rootstock-specific cues are given (AF-9; R-3 default).
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-05.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Two rose stems. The rose's own large dark leaves on the left, and a sucker's smaller, paler leaves on the right."

### 4.6 Sucker flowers beside the rose's own flowers (growing-season supporting sign)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-06`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** Sucker flowers differ from the rose's own, for example small, single, white or dark red (AF-6, Moderate).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Both kinds of flower in one frame, on the same bush.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Flowering season only.
- **Common Lookalikes:** A different variety planted close by (not addressed by any source found).
- **Limitations:**
  - A supporting sign only.
  - One source waits for the first flowers to identify a sucker, while others say remove early (C-5). Pip follows early removal on confirmed origin.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-06.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "A rose with large pink blooms, and a tall shoot from its base carrying small white single flowers: a sucker."

### 4.7 Removing a sucker at its origin (technique)

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-07`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** Removal at the origin, not at soil level (AF-17, High). Tearing or pulling away is the main method (AF-18, Moderate), and a gloved hand supports the stem (AF-22, Low).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Close-up of gloved hands at the exposed origin, below the union.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Grafted bush rose, any season.
- **Common Lookalikes:** None. This is a technique image.
- **Limitations:** Sources disagree on tearing versus cutting cleanly at the origin (C-1). This image shows tearing only.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-07.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Gloved hands pull a sucker away right where it starts, below the graft join."

### 4.8 What not to do: a sucker cut at soil level grows back

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-08`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** A sucker cut at the surface regrows (AF-17, High). One nursery describes three coming up where one was cut.
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Soil level, showing a flat stub with regrowth around it.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Grafted bush rose, growing season.
- **Common Lookalikes:** None.
- **Limitations:** Illustrative of a stated outcome, not a measured one.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-08.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "A sucker stub cut at soil level, with several new shoots growing back from it."

## 5. Dormancy Gate and Removal After Bud Break (PKR-SGT-000001 v1.1; PKR-DEC-000001/000004 v1.1)

### 5.1 Still dormant: buds tight or just beginning to swell

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** The "Still dormant" answer. Buds are tight and closed, or just beginning to swell, and no leaves are open (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-1, High).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Macro of a cane section with several buds.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Established bush rose, late winter.
- **Common Lookalikes:** In mild climates a rose may keep leaves while still dormant, so judge by the buds, not the leaves (DORMANCY AF-3, Moderate).
- **Limitations:** A single cane section may not represent the whole plant.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01.png`, held at `Working/Drafts/Comparison Images/POSTBUDBREAK/`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Close-up of a rose cane with small tight buds, one just starting to swell; no leaves open."

### 5.2 Active growth well underway

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-02`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** The answer that sends the gardener to the removal-only session. Leaves have opened and shoots are clearly growing (DORMANCY AF-1; SGT-000001 v1.1).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** The whole bush from base to tips.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Established bush rose, spring.
- **Common Lookalikes:** A partly leafy bush in a mild winter (DORMANCY AF-3).
- **Limitations:** None beyond §5.1. The placeholder shows the bush in full flower, which is later than needed. A real photograph should show opened leaves and growing shoots before flowering.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-02.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "A whole rose bush in spring with new shoots and open leaves on every cane."

### 5.3 Dead cane on a rose already in leaf

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-03`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** What the removal-only session is for. Dead wood can be removed at any time, or as soon as it's seen (POSTBUDBREAK AF-1, Moderate; AF-2, Low).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** The whole bush, with the dead cane standing out among leafy ones.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Established bush rose in full leaf.
- **Common Lookalikes:** An older living cane with bronze-toned bark (see `IMG-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-03`).
- **Limitations:** Only the pith, once cut, confirms dead wood. The photo shows the outside only.
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-03.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "A leafy rose bush with one dry, brown, leafless cane among the green ones."

### 5.4 Frost dieback of young shoots after growth has begun

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-04`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** Frost can kill back young shoots after spring growth has begun (POSTBUDBREAK AF-6, Moderate). This is the case the frost-damage deferral in PKR-DEC-000004 now comes back to.
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Close enough to show browned tips against green growth below.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Established bush rose, early spring after a late frost.
- **Common Lookalikes:** A borer's wilted tip (see `IMG-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-03`).
- **Limitations:** The full extent of frost damage often doesn't show straight away (DAMAGEDGROWTH AF-14). Sources differ on when to judge it (C-2).
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-04.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "Young rose shoots with browned, blackened tips after a spring frost; green below."

### 5.5 Canker on a rose in leaf

- **Tag ID:** `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-05`
- **Feature Demonstrated:** Diseased wood is removed promptly, or at any time (POSTBUDBREAK AF-8, Moderate), and cut well below the affected area (AF-20, High).
- **Viewpoint, Scale and Visible Context:** Close-up of the lesion, with healthy cane below it and leaves in frame.
- **Rose and Seasonal/Growth-Stage Context:** Established bush rose, growing season.
- **Common Lookalikes:** The normal red or purple colour of young canes (DAMAGEDGROWTH AF-10, Low).
- **Limitations:** Experts differ on diagnosing canker from a photo (DAMAGEDGROWTH C-3). Sources also differ on when to remove it (POSTBUDBREAK C-3).
- **Image Source / Verification / Approval:** Placeholder, as §4.1. File: `IMG-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-05.png`.
- **Accessible Label:** "A leafy rose cane with a sunken purplish-brown patch and tiny black dots: canker."

**Not given images:**

- The pith check (needs a cut).
- The late-season caution (not visual).
- Borer damage in spring and summer, which is already covered by `IMG-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-03`.
