## 3. Background — the gap

Pip supports established bush roses (Architecture §5.1). Approved knowledge covers what happens after planting (pruning restraint, `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`; first-years watering, `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F2) and records that bud-union planting depth varies (`ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` AF-12). No approved knowledge covers when or how to plant a new bush rose, or whether and how to move an established one.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1. Planting time.** When do sources say to plant bare-root and potted (container) bush roses, including the winter bare-root season in New Zealand and Australia and autumn/spring in the UK and US?
2. **Q2. Site.** What site basics do sources give: sun hours, drainage, spacing, and avoiding soil where roses have grown before (replant disease, "rose sickness")?
3. **Q3. Planting method.** What do sources say about soaking bare roots, the planting hole, planting depth and the bud union's depth by climate, and watering in?
4. **Q4. Moving an established rose.** When, how much root ball, pruning before or after, and aftercare?
5. **Q5. Limits.** What age limits or success odds do sources give for moving a rose?

## 5. Scope and Method

Hybrid Tea and Floribunda bush roses, Grandiflora provisionally (Architecture §5.1). Sources from New Zealand (priority market), Australia, the UK and the US: horticultural bodies, university extension, rose societies, botanic gardens and public broadcasters, with commercial nurseries and garden-product companies allowed but labelled commercial. Strictly what sources say; no advice of the ROC's own. Every online source recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5), and every relied-on source carried in the Source Register (§12.6).
