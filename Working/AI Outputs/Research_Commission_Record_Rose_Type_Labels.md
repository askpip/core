## 1. Why This Commission Exists

`FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` researched how recognised horticultural authorities describe and distinguish rose types (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora as the approved scope; Shrub, Patio, Miniature, Miniflora, Groundcover, Climbing sports, Standard, and Old Garden/species roses as excluded lookalikes). It did not, and by its own account (§7, "Comparison-image needs") was not meant to, assemble that material into a single, gardener-facing list of the type-related terms a beginner would actually encounter on a real New Zealand plant tag, pot label, receipt, or retail listing, and how each maps onto the approved scope. Its own Recommendation R-2 states plainly: "No source located states what a New Zealand plant tag or pot label prints about rose type," and leaves "whether to arrange a small in-store survey" as a Founder decision.

On review of `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`, Shaphan confirmed this is exactly the gap: the original commissioned question behind that dossier was to establish what bush roses Pip could assist with, including a definitive list of the commonly known rose types a gardener could recognise from a label and use to begin the Pip journey — and that list is missing. Rather than build it now from the existing findings alone (which describe categories in horticultural-authority language, not necessarily in the words a shopper actually sees on stock), Shaphan directed that this be commissioned as new research first.

Ask Pip's add-plant sequence already collects the gardener's location, and Ask Pip's eventual launch market has not yet been decided. Shaphan accordingly directed that this commission cover New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States, in that priority order, rather than New Zealand alone — so the resulting list can be applied per the gardener's stated location however the launch decision eventually lands.

---

## 2. Scope

**In scope.** Direct observation of how rose type is actually presented to a buyer on retail stock and retail-facing material, across the markets Ask Pip may launch into: physical plant tags and pot labels, receipts, point-of-sale signage, and product listings and descriptions as shown to a buyer (not backend category menus alone, where those can be distinguished from buyer-facing text). Ask Pip's app already collects the gardener's location in the add-plant sequence, and the launch market is not yet decided, so this commission covers all four markets rather than treating three of them as mere corroboration: **New Zealand first, then Australia, then the United Kingdom, then the United States**, in that priority order. Findings are kept separate by market throughout — a term common in one market is not assumed to carry over to another — so that the resulting list can be applied per the gardener's stated location rather than as one blended global answer. The aim is a definitive, gardener-facing list of the type-related terms and labels a beginner is likely to actually encounter in each market, and how each maps onto the Bush Rose scope approved in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora provisionally, in scope) versus the excluded types researched in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` (Shrub, Patio, Miniature, Miniflora, Groundcover, Climbing sports, Standard, Old Garden/species — out of scope).

**Out of scope.** Cultivar-level identification; pruning technique; image sourcing for the app itself; questionnaire wording; any market outside New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States, unless a Founder later directs otherwise.

---

## 3. Commissioned Question

What does a retail plant tag, pot label, receipt, point-of-sale listing, or product description actually print, display or state about a rose's type in each of New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States — and, of the type-related terms actually found in that material, which ones would a beginner gardener in that market realistically encounter and recognise, and how does each map onto the approved Bush Rose scope?

Sub-questions, each answered separately per market (New Zealand, then Australia, then the United Kingdom, then the United States):

- **Q1.** What type-related words or category names appear on physical tags/labels attached to potted roses sold in that market (where photographs or descriptions of real stock, tags or labels can be found)?
- **Q2.** What type-related words or category names appear in that market's retailers' and nurseries' buyer-facing product listings and descriptions (as distinct from their backend navigation/category menus — `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` AF-17/AF-18 already cover New Zealand listings in this respect, and this commission extends the same treatment to Australia, the UK and the US)?
- **Q3.** Where a term appears that is ambiguous or used inconsistently within a market (for example, "bush rose" itself, per `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` AF-17), what, if anything, distinguishes its in-scope and out-of-scope uses in practice there?
- **Q4.** Where the same term is used differently, or a term common in one market is absent or means something different in another, is that difference recorded explicitly rather than blended away?
- **Q5.** Assembling the above, market by market, with the already-approved findings in `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` (AF-1, AF-2, AF-4, AF-7, AF-9, AF-11, AF-13, AF-14, AF-15, AF-16, AF-17, AF-18, AF-19), what is the resulting definitive, per-market list of recognisable label/listing terms mapped to in-scope versus out-of-scope, and what, if anything, remains genuinely unresolvable from available evidence in a given market?

---

## 4. Method

Per ROC Operations Manual Chapter 4, research will proceed by web search and page retrieval, market by market in priority order: New Zealand first (nurseries, garden centres, retailers, the New Zealand Rose Society), then Australia (nurseries, retailers, the Rose Society of New South Wales and other state societies), then the United Kingdom (nurseries, retailers, the RHS), then the United States (nurseries, retailers, the American Rose Society and university extension services). Each market's findings are recorded and assessed separately — no finding is blended across markets, and a term's presence or meaning in one market is not assumed for another. Each finding carries its own Evidence Confidence Level per the Evidence Assessment Standard (EAS). Searches that return nothing usable will be logged, consistent with `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` §4.3's practice.

**Anticipated challenges.** What is physically printed on a plant tag is inherently hard to establish by web search alone, since most retailers do not photograph their own tags; this commission may find only partial or indirect evidence in any given market (for example, retailers' own descriptive wording, customer photographs, or product images that happen to show a tag), and coverage may end up uneven across the four markets. The Research Origin Curator will state plainly wherever the evidence is thin, market by market, rather than overstate confidence or paper over a gap in one market with stronger evidence from another.

---

## 5. Founder Decision Points Reserved for the Resulting Dossier

This commission does not itself decide anything; it authorises research. The resulting Founder Review Dossier will reserve at least:

1. Whether the assembled list is accepted as Pip's scope-qualification reference, amended, or found still incomplete.
2. Whether any residual gap, in any one of the four markets (for example, if physical tag content remains unestablished there after this commission), is accepted as a standing limitation for that market, or commissioned further (an in-store survey, as `FRD-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01` R-2 originally contemplated for New Zealand).
3. Whether coverage is markedly uneven across the four markets, and if so, whether that is acceptable given Ask Pip's launch market is not yet decided, or whether further research should even out the weaker markets before this is treated as complete.
