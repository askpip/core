## 3. Background

The MVP journey currently assumes a rose that is ready to be pruned. The readiness check (Journey §3, "Getting Ready" in the flow proposal) can and will sometimes conclude that today isn't the day — the rose may be too recently planted, not yet dormant, or the gardener may simply not be ready. Until now, nothing has defined what Pip should say to that gardener beyond "come back later."

Shaphan's view, stated when commissioning this research, is that basic care belongs in the Ask Pip journey for exactly this reason, and should be part of the MVP rather than a later addition: a gardener turned away from pruning still has a rose to look after, and Pip should be able to help with that too.

Separately, the flow proposal's Learn area (section 9) already anticipates a plain answer to "what is a bush rose, and what does caring for it involve?" as part of "About Ask Pip" — general orientation content, not pruning-specific. Both needs point at the same gap: Ask Pip has no approved knowledge about basic rose care at all today, only the (approved, but pruning-specific) dead-wood, dormancy and recently-planted findings, and the (not yet approved) whole-bush pruning framework.

This commission is deliberately scoped smaller and less contested than the pruning framework. Ordinary rose care — watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading spent blooms, watching for pests and disease, protecting the plant over winter — is widely agreed across horticultural sources in a way that pruning mechanics (cut angle, exact distance above a bud) are not. The expectation, to be tested rather than assumed, is that this commission should move through review faster than the pruning framework did.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

**Q1 — What is a bush rose?** How do recognised sources define or classify a bush rose, as distinct from a climbing rose, a rambling rose, a standard (tree) rose, and a species or shrub rose grown informally? Is "bush rose" itself a term sources use consistently, or a growth habit description that sits across rose classes (as `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` already treats Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and, provisionally, Grandiflora)?

**Q2 — Ordinary care across a year.** What do sources say an established bush rose needs across a typical year, apart from pruning? This includes watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading spent blooms, and general pest and disease watchfulness. Where do sources agree, and where do they differ (for example, by climate or region)?

**Q3 — Care for a rose that isn't ready for pruning.** What do sources say is appropriate care for a rose that is too recently planted, not yet dormant, or otherwise not ready for pruning — the gap this commission exists to fill? This should connect to, and not repeat, the already-approved recently-planted and dormancy findings.

**Q4 — Winter protection.** What do sources say about protecting a bush rose over its dormant season, separate from the act of pruning itself?

**Q5 — Simple signs worth a gardener's attention.** Do sources describe simple, general signs of a rose in poor health (for example, wilting, yellowing leaves, visible pests) that a gardener could be told to watch for — without those signs amounting to a diagnosis? (Architecture §5.5 already defers diagnosis from a photograph; this question is about what Pip could gently point a gardener toward noticing, not what it would conclude.)

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The bush rose as defined by `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`.
- General, year-round care: watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading, winter protection, and general pest/disease watchfulness at an overview level.
- Care appropriate to a rose not yet ready for pruning (Q3), read alongside the approved dormancy and recently-planted findings.
- A plain classification of what a bush rose is (Q1).

### 5.2 Excluded

- Pruning itself, in any form — categories of growth, order of work, cut mechanics. That is `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`'s subject, under separate Founder review, and this commission shall not overlap it.
- Diagnosis of any specific pest, disease or disorder from a photograph or description (Architecture §5.5 already defers this).
- Any specific product, brand or chemical recommendation.
- Planting a new rose (site selection, hole preparation, initial watering-in) — a related but separate topic, not commissioned here.
- Climate- or region-specific schedules stated as if universal. Where sources vary by climate, the ROC records that variation rather than picking one.
- Rose types outside the approved scope.

### 5.3 Known limitations

- General care advice is widely available but not always rigorously sourced; the ROC should expect to draw on a mix of practitioner and institutional sources (extension services, rose societies, established gardening references) rather than the more clinical literature the pruning framework could sometimes draw on.
- Where sources disagree because of climate or region, the ROC records the disagreement and its likely cause rather than resolving it.
- Any synthesis the ROC arrives at by its own reasoning, not stated by sources, is not an Assessed Finding and is recorded separately.

### 5.4 Dependencies and follow-on work

- **Dependencies:** `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`, and the approved dormancy and recently-planted findings for Q3.
- **Follow-on work, not commissioned here:** how this content is presented in the app (the Learn area, and how the "not ready for pruning" moment in the journey offers it) is a product decision for the Founders once findings exist, not part of this commission.

## 6. Expected Deliverables

1. A Research Plan, in the same shape as the pruning framework's, setting out questions, anticipated source types and known challenges before any findings are gathered.
2. A Founder Review Dossier (FRD) with an Evidence Confidence Level stated per finding, kept proportionate to the evidence — this commission is expected to be substantially shorter than the pruning framework's.
3. A record of what was searched but not found, stated as an absence rather than support for a conclusion.
4. Recommendations for further research, including anything raised by Q5 about what "worth a gardener's attention" should mean for Ask Pip without becoming diagnosis.
