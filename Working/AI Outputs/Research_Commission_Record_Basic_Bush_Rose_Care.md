## 3. Background

The MVP journey currently assumes a rose that is ready to be pruned. The readiness check (Journey §3, "Getting Ready" in the flow proposal) can and will sometimes conclude that today isn't the day — the rose may be too recently planted, not yet dormant, or the gardener may simply not be ready. Until now, nothing has defined what Pip should say to that gardener beyond "come back later."

Shaphan's view, stated when commissioning this research, is that basic care belongs in the Ask Pip journey for exactly this reason, and should be part of the MVP rather than a later addition: a gardener turned away from pruning still has a rose to look after, and Pip should be able to help with that too.

Separately, the flow proposal's Learn area (section 9) already anticipates a plain answer to "what is a bush rose, and what does caring for it involve?" as part of "About Ask Pip" — general orientation content, not pruning-specific. Both needs point at the same gap: Ask Pip has no approved knowledge about basic rose care at all today, only the (approved, but pruning-specific) dead-wood, dormancy and recently-planted findings, and the (not yet approved) whole-bush pruning framework.

This commission is deliberately scoped smaller and less contested than the pruning framework. Ordinary rose care — watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading spent blooms, watching for pests and disease, protecting the plant over winter — is widely agreed across horticultural sources in a way that pruning mechanics (cut angle, exact distance above a bud) are not.

## 4. Round 1 — Commissioned Questions

**Q1 — What is a bush rose?** How do recognised sources define or classify a bush rose, as distinct from a climbing rose, a rambling rose, a standard (tree) rose, and a species or shrub rose grown informally? Is "bush rose" itself a term sources use consistently, or a growth habit description that sits across rose classes (as `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` already treats Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and, provisionally, Grandiflora)?

**Q2 — Ordinary care across a year.** What do sources say an established bush rose needs across a typical year, apart from pruning? This includes watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading spent blooms, and general pest and disease watchfulness. Where do sources agree, and where do they differ (for example, by climate or region)?

**Q3 — Care for a rose that isn't ready for pruning.** What do sources say is appropriate care for a rose that is too recently planted, not yet dormant, or otherwise not ready for pruning — the gap this commission exists to fill? This should connect to, and not repeat, the already-approved recently-planted and dormancy findings.

**Q4 — Winter protection.** What do sources say about protecting a bush rose over its dormant season, separate from the act of pruning itself?

**Q5 — Simple signs worth a gardener's attention.** Do sources describe simple, general signs of a rose in poor health (for example, wilting, yellowing leaves, visible pests) that a gardener could be told to watch for — without those signs amounting to a diagnosis?

**Round 1 outcome:** all five questions were researched and answered. The findings (F1–F13) are recorded in `FRD-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01.md`, submitted for Founder Review on 22 September 2026. That dossier's own §9 ("Recommendations for Further Research") identified five open items, which the Founders reviewed via the Decision Brief and Review Form. Shaphan's Review Form response (submitted 22 September 2026) marked all five "Commission next." Round 2, below, is that commissioning.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included (Round 1)

- The bush rose as defined by `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`.
- General, year-round care: watering, feeding, mulching, deadheading, winter protection, and general pest/disease watchfulness at an overview level.
- Care appropriate to a rose not yet ready for pruning (Q3), read alongside the approved dormancy and recently-planted findings.
- A plain classification of what a bush rose is (Q1).

### 5.2 Excluded (both rounds, unless §10 says otherwise)

- Pruning itself, in any form — categories of growth, order of work, cut mechanics. That is `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`'s subject, under separate Founder review, and this commission does not overlap it.
- Diagnosis of any specific pest, disease or disorder from a photograph or description (Architecture §5.5 already defers this).
- Any specific product, brand or chemical recommendation.
- Planting a new rose (site selection, hole preparation, initial watering-in) — a related but separate topic, not commissioned here.
- Rose types outside the approved scope.

### 5.3 Known limitations

- General care advice is widely available but not always rigorously sourced; the ROC drew on a mix of practitioner and institutional sources (extension services, rose societies, established gardening references) rather than the more clinical literature the pruning framework could sometimes draw on.
- Where sources disagree because of climate or region, the ROC records the disagreement and its likely cause rather than resolving it.
- Any synthesis the ROC arrives at by its own reasoning, not stated by sources, is not an Assessed Finding and is recorded separately.

### 5.4 Dependencies and follow-on work

- **Dependencies:** `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`, and the approved dormancy and recently-planted findings for Q3.
- **Follow-on work, not commissioned here:** how this content is presented in the app (the Learn area, and how the "not ready for pruning" moment in the journey offers it) is a product decision for the Founders once findings exist, not part of this commission.

## 10. Round 2 — Commissioned Questions (Authorised 22 September 2026)

**Authority.** Shaphan, holding AskPIP Founder Authority, authorised this round directly in conversation on 22 September 2026 ("go ahead and resolve as many issues as we can with research"), following his own completed Review Form response marking all five Round 1 Recommendations for Further Research "Commission next." This is a direct Founder instruction commissioning research, consistent with §9's noted practice, and is recorded here as this round's authorisation per ROC Operations Manual Chapter 3 rather than left informal.

**Scope — the five items, restated as commissioned questions:**

**R-1 — Consistency with already-approved findings.** Do `FRD-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01`'s F7 and the general watering/care pattern in F2–F6 (establishment-period care) sit consistently alongside the already-approved `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` findings, when checked against those findings' own source basis? Where the two use different timeframes or framings for what counts as "not yet established," the check should surface that rather than assume consistency.

**R-2 — Geographic diversity.** Can recognised, institutional-quality sources (the same evidentiary bar as Round 1's eleven sources) be found from a climate tradition or region outside the UK and continental US — Southern Hemisphere, continental Europe, or elsewhere — to corroborate or extend F2–F6 and F9–F11?

**R-3 — Q5 depth.** Can further, more targeted sources strengthen F12 and F13 (general, non-diagnostic signs of poor health), beyond the single University of Maryland source F12 currently rests most specifically on?

**R-4 — The "bush rose" terminology gap (F1a).** Is "bush rose" used as a formal classification term anywhere outside UK/Commonwealth horticultural literature, or does further searching continue to support F1a's observation that it does not appear to be?

**R-5 — F11's single-source status.** Can a second, independent institutional source be found for pre-winter watering and pre-winter debris removal (currently resting on one Penn State Extension source), to move F11 out of Low confidence?

**Excluded from Round 2:** everything listed in §5.2 remains excluded. Round 2 does not reopen Q1–Q5 themselves; it researches only the five items above.

## 11. Round 2 — Research Notes and Outcome

Full evidence gathered, evaluated and cited for each of the five items above is recorded separately in `Working/AI Outputs/FRD-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01_Round2_Research_Notes.md`, in the same spirit as the pruning framework's own `_Research_Notes.md` companion. Summary of outcome, pending Shaphan's decision on next steps (§8):

- **R-5 — resolved.** A second, independent institutional source found and evaluated (Nebraska Extension, Lancaster County), corroborating both halves of F11.
- **R-1 — resolved.** Checked directly against `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`. No conflict found, but a real distinction surfaced: RECENTPLANT's "three or more years" concerns pruning readiness specifically; BASICCARE's "roughly the first two years" concerns watering vigilance specifically. The two are compatible but easily conflated, and this is recorded as a clarification for the eventual ARC, not a new finding requiring new evidence.
- **R-4 — substantially resolved.** Further search across non-UK sources (US, Australian) continues to find no use of "bush rose" as a formal term outside UK/Commonwealth usage, strengthening F1a's existing observation.
- **R-3 — checked, confirmed thin as an absence, not a gap in search effort.** Several additional institutional sources were reviewed (UC IPM, University of Tennessee Extension, University of Arizona Extension); none frame general, non-diagnostic "notice this before diagnosing" guidance independently of the University of Maryland source. This is recorded as a searched-and-not-found absence per this RCR's own Expected Deliverables (§6, item 3) — it supports, rather than undermines, F12 and F13's existing Moderate and Low–Moderate confidence levels as an accurate reflection of genuinely thin literature, not insufficient research.
- **R-2 — not resolved; remains genuinely open.** Searches across Australian, South African and New Zealand sources found rose-care content that is almost entirely commercial (nurseries, garden centres) or enthusiast (rose societies) in nature, not the government or university-extension-equivalent institutional sources Round 1's evidentiary bar was built on. Continental European sources in English showed the same pattern. This is not a failure of search effort within the time available for this round; it reflects a real scarcity of the kind of source Round 1 used, at least in English and within ordinary web search. Resolving R-2 to the same evidentiary standard as the rest of this commission would likely require either accepting a lower evidentiary bar for non-UK/US sources (a Founder decision, not a research one) or searching non-English-language sources with translation, which is a materially larger undertaking than the other four items.
