**Published 29 September 2026.** Approved by Shaphan in chat, 29 September 2026 ("approve all"), under the standing condition that the records contain only content from already-approved ARCs. Built per the KIT Operations Manual from `ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01` v1.0 (approved by Shaphan, 29 September 2026). This package holds:

All are submitted together.

**Founder directions applied:**

- **Founder decisions recorded in the ARC (§2 D2–D7).** Sucker handling is its own observation (D2). Removal is guided only when the origin is confirmed below a visible bud union (D3). The method is the agreed core, with tearing as the main method and cutting as the alternative (D4). Only the point of origin is used in the dormant session (D5). Nothing is called a sucker when no bud union can be seen (D6). The R-1 to R-4 defaults apply (D7).
- **Standing journey directions.** The observation loops ("Can you see any more shoots like this coming from the base?"). The Comparison Image gap is documented and doesn't block. Gardener-facing copy says "your rose", never "bush rose".

In the tables below, "AF-n" refers to `ARC-BUSHROSE-SUCKERS-01`, and an ARC §6 default is shown as "Not Applicable — approved default".

**Own-root roses:** if the gardener knows the rose grows on its own roots (it wasn't grafted), shoots from the base are the same rose, so none of them is a sucker. Pip treats them as the rose's own new canes (AF-11, **High**). Sources use the word "sucker" differently here (C-3). No visible knot doesn't by itself mean the rose is own-root, because the union may be buried (AF-12; `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` AF-3).

**Easily confused:** sources warn that suckers and the rose's own new basal canes are easy to mix up (AF-13, **Moderate**). The rose's own basal canes are valuable and are kept (AF-24, **High**). This is why Pip decides only on where the shoot starts.

**What the Photo Cannot Establish:** where the shoot starts, which may be under the soil (AF-2). A photo is used only to prompt the gardener to check the origin on the plant. Pip never confirms a sucker from a photo alone (R-2 default; `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-34, AF-41).

**Linked Comparison Image PKR(s):** **None yet. Documented gap**, per Founder direction.

**Linked Decision Logic PKR:** PKR-DEC-000007.

**Confirmation Requirement:** Yes. The gardener traces the shoot down to where it starts, on the actual rose, before any decision.

**Confirmation Steps and Responses:**

| Step | What Pip asks | Response → Route |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Is there a shoot coming from the base of your rose, or from the ground near it?" | No → observation complete (no more). Yes → step 2. |
| 2 | "Can you see the bud union, the knobbly join near the base where your rose was grafted?" | Yes → step 3. No → Pip doesn't call the shoot a sucker (D6). It offers, as an optional step, to gently clear a little soil and look again (AF-2, AF-12). Still no → **leave the shoot**; if the gardener is concerned, suggest experienced local help. Never goes on to Cut. |
| 3 | "Follow the shoot down with your fingers. Does it start **below** the bud union, or at or above it?" | Below → **Confirmed** (sucker) → PKR-DEC-000007. At or above → **Doesn't match**: this is your rose's own new cane. Keep it (AF-24). It is considered in the framework step (PKR-OBS-000006). Not sure → Not Sure route. |
| Growing season only | Supporting signs (leaves, new-growth colour, flowers, vigour) may be mentioned to help the gardener look, but never replace step 3 (D5) | — |

**D1. When may a confirmed sucker be removed?** The ARC says to remove suckers early, while young (AF-20, High), and D5 already expects Pip to handle suckers in the growing season (supporting signs). But sucker removal isn't dead, damaged or diseased wood, so the narrowed dormancy gate (post-bud-break decision D2) doesn't cover it either way. KIT can't settle this by inference. Options:

- **(a)** Treat it like any other pruning Cut: only in a session where PKR-SGT-000001 (dormancy) and PKR-SGT-000002 (recently planted) pass.
- **(b)** Allow it in any season (exempt from PKR-SGT-000001), because AF-20 (High) says to remove suckers as soon as they are spotted. PKR-SGT-000002 still applies.
- **(c)** Allow it in any season and exempt it from both PKR-SGT-000001 and PKR-SGT-000002.

*KIT recommendation:* **(b)**. It follows AF-20 directly and matches D5's growing-season route. It keeps the recently-planted gate, since no finding addresses suckers on a newly planted rose.

## 7. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What these records are for:** They help a gardener spot shoots growing from the rootstock below the graft and remove them properly. The rose's own new canes, which are valuable, are never removed by mistake.

**How it works:**

1. Pip asks whether there's a shoot from the base.
2. Can you see the knobbly bud union? If not, Pip doesn't call anything a sucker.
3. Follow the shoot down: below the union means a sucker, at or above means your rose's own cane, which you keep.
4. Pip only guides removal when the gardener has confirmed this on the plant.
5. Removal: uncover where it starts, and pull or tear it off there (or cut it cleanly there). Never cut at soil level, and do it early.
6. It loops for each shoot.

**What evidence it rests on:**

- Where the shoot starts is the key test (High).
- Remove the sucker at its origin, never at soil level (High).
- Remove it early (High).
- Suckers weaken the rose (High).
- Keep the rose's own basal canes (High).
- Leaf and flower clues help in the growing season but can mislead (Moderate).
- Tear versus cut is disputed (Moderate).

**Preserved limitations:**

- No source explains how to spot a sucker on a leafless rose other than by where it starts.
- No source says what a photo can show, so Pip never decides from a photo.
- Comparison images are still a gap.

**Source identity notes for you (no substitution made):**
