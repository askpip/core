## 5. Remaining Decisions for the Founders

**Decision 1 — What kind of record these two answers are.** The PKR Standard (§5.7) gives Care Guidance three modes (Technique, Advisory, Care), and its presentation points are steps in the pruning journey. These two answers are shown when a gardener taps a question in "Questions gardeners ask", outside the journey. The existing common questions reuse statements from journey records, so this hasn't come up before.
- (a) Treat them as Advisory, with "when the gardener opens this question" as the presentation point. KIT then proposes a small PKR Standard amendment to name question answers as a presentation point. *KIT recommendation.*
- (b) Amend the PKR Standard first to add a "Question answer" mode, then build these records under it.

**Decision 2 — The blind-shoot cut (PKR-CGD-000008 item 15).** You chose (a) for D6: guide the cut. In a question answer, KIT can say how sources cut a blind shoot, as information. Making it a guided cut inside the pruning journey would need a Decision Logic record, because Care Guidance can't offer a Cut choice (§5.7 boundary).
- (a) Information in the answer only, as drafted. A guided journey step can follow later as its own record. *KIT recommendation.*
- (b) Also build a Decision Logic record now for a blind-shoot cut in the journey.

## 6. Wording Boundary Check (KIT OM §7.7)

- Every statement restates its finding in plainer words. No figure, condition or cause is added. Where a finding names its sources' position rather than a fact, the wording keeps that ("sources say", "sources link").
- "Doesn't kill an established rose" is attributed to the sources (CGD-000007 item 1), not stated by Pip as fact. Its conditions sit next to it (item 2), as D2(b) requires.
- The renovation and recovery items (CGD-000007 items 8–9) and the framing items (CGD-000008 items 1 and 22) are labelled "approved default", not given a borrowed confidence level.

## 7. Founder Review Rendering (plain summary)

**"Can I prune too hard?" (new).** Pip will say that rose societies, growers and a botanic garden in four countries say pruning, even hard or badly done, doesn't kill an established rose (strong evidence). It adds their conditions: the rose is otherwise healthy, isn't cut below the ground line, and its roots are intact. It then gives the real risks:
- cutting below the graft or ground line;
- pruning a weak rose as hard as a vigorous one (single weak shoots can still be cut back further);
- pruning before the frosts have passed.

Any setback is described as short-term. Old or neglected roses can be brought back with hard pruning; Pip spreads heavy renovation over two or three years, noting that some sources do it in one go. There's no recovery timetable, so if nothing regrows during the season, Pip suggests the local rose society.

**The one-third advisory (revised).** It stays as it is, and adds that rose growers commonly remove about half to two-thirds at winter pruning (strong evidence), and that some call a one-third cut "light" pruning.

**"Why isn't my rose flowering?" (new).** Pip says it can't tell which reason applies, then lists what sources give, each with what to look for:
- too little sun (four to eight hours, most often six);
- competition from trees and shrubs;
- too much nitrogen, or too little feeding;
- too little water;
- pruning too much, too little or at the wrong time;
- not deadheading;
- a once-flowering variety, which Pip can't support;
- blind shoots, and how sources cut them;
- buds that don't open: balling, grey mould, thrips or budworm, with non-spray steps;
- black spot.

It ends by saying a photo alone can't show the cause.

**Sources.** 49 new Source PKRs (PKR-SRC-000190 to 000238), and 26 existing ones get the new references added. All are listed in the Source PKR package.
