### Question or Check

> Do you know what type of rose this is — from its label or pot tag, a receipt or nursery listing, or someone experienced who has told you?

**Acceptable Answer(s):**

| Floribunda (cluster-flowered) | Not Applicable — scope rule | **Gate passes** | Architecture §5.1; R-1 |
| Grandiflora | Not Applicable — scope rule. | An excluded type: shrub rose (including English/David Austin roses), patio, miniature or miniflora, groundcover, climbing or rambling (including a "climbing" version of a bush variety), standard / tree / weeping standard, or an Old Garden / heritage / species rose | Not Applicable — scope rule | **Gate does not pass: journal only** (see Result below) | Architecture §5.1 (exclusions) |

**Result when the gate does not pass (journal only).** Pip says plainly that it can't yet help prune this rose, because its pruning guidance so far covers only Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora bush roses. For a known excluded type, Pip names the type. For an unknown type, Pip explains that the type can't be confirmed, and that it doesn't try to identify roses from photos because that's unreliable even for experts (AF-20). The rose stays in the gardener's garden list as a journal: photos, notes and history all remain available. If the gardener wants the type identified, Pip suggests a local rose society or an experienced rose grower (AF-24), and the Rose Finder where a variety name is known (AF-19). If the type is later confirmed as supported, the gardener can answer the question again.

**Stopping Threshold:** any answer other than a stated Hybrid Tea, Floribunda or Grandiflora stops the pruning journey for this rose.

## 5. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What this record is for:** before any pruning help, Pip checks the rose is one of the types the guidance was researched for: a Hybrid Tea, a Floribunda, or (provisionally) a Grandiflora bush rose. Pip asks what the gardener has been told, because it can't tell a type from a photo or a bare winter plant. Only those three stated types go on to pruning. Everything else stays as a plant journal, with Pip saying honestly that it can't support pruning that rose yet: a known shrub, patio, miniature, groundcover, climbing, standard or old garden rose; a label that just says "bush rose"; or a type nobody knows.

**What evidence it rests on:** the list of supported and unsupported types is your approved scope decision, not a research claim. "Bush" on a label doesn't identify a type: NZ retailers use it for patio, shrub and groundcover roses too (Moderate). The NZ Rose Society's Rose Finder gives a type for each rose it lists (Moderate). Identifying an unlabelled rose is difficult even for experienced people (Moderate).

**Preserved limitations:** the gate trusts the stated type. A mislabelled rose, or a cultivar that authorities class differently, can still pass.

**Open decisions:** none. The wording point ("unreliable even for experts") was confirmed by Shaphan, 29 September 2026.
