## 3. Background: the gap

`ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01` F6 (High) records that commonly cited rose problems include blackspot, powdery mildew, aphids and spider mites, and that sources advise watering at the base or in the morning to reduce wet foliage. F11 (Moderate) records clearing fallen leaf debris before winter to reduce overwintering fungal spores. Neither finding addresses whether a gardener should spray, what with in general terms, when, or how often. "Should I spray my roses?" is a common beginner question, and Pip has no approved answer to it.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1.** Do credible sources say an established bush rose needs to be sprayed at all? What do they say about disease-resistant varieties and growing roses without spraying?
2. **Q2.** What do sources say about preventive (regular) spraying compared with reactive spraying (only when a problem appears)?
3. **Q3.** For the common targets (blackspot, powdery mildew, rust, aphids), what do sources say about managing them, with and without sprays?
4. **Q4.** What do sources say about winter or dormant clean-up sprays after pruning (for example lime sulphur, copper, oil): what they are for, and when they are applied?
5. **Q5.** What general timing and frequency do sources give for spraying through the growing season?
6. **Q6.** What non-chemical and lower-toxicity options do sources describe (sanitation, removing infected leaves, watering at the base, spacing, natural enemies)?
7. **Q7.** What safety statements do sources make: following the product label, protective equipment, protecting bees and other pollinators, and differences in regulation between countries?

## 5. Scope and Method

Established Hybrid Tea and Floribunda bush roses (Grandiflora provisionally), within the approved scope. Standard ROC method, with New Zealand, Australian, United Kingdom and United States sources, and at least two to three from New Zealand and Australia where they exist. **Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5),** in the dossier's Source Log and in `Source_Register_SPRAYING.json`.

**Exclusion.** The commission does not compile specific pesticide product recommendations, brand names, mixing rates or dosages as findings, beyond what a source states in general terms. Where sources name products or give rates, the dossier records only that product choice, rates and intervals are governed by the product label and local regulations.
