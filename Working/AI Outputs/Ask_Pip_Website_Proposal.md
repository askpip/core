## Summary

A public website at askpip.garden becomes the front door to Ask Pip. It explains what Ask Pip is, shows what it does today, shares the vision, and gives people four ways to get involved. Gardeners join the beta by requesting an invite, and the Founders choose who gets in.

**Status:** proposal, not built. **Recommendation:** build the site as one well-made page first, together with an invite gate in the app, and launch the two at the same time. Estimated build: about 5 to 7 working days, plus Founder time to approve wording and artwork.

The app stays where it is, at app.askpip.garden. The beta is for gardeners in New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada.

## What the site is for

The site does four jobs, in this order of importance:

1. **Explain Ask Pip.** What it is and what it does today, in plain words, with real screens from the app.
2. **Bring gardeners into the beta.** One clear route: request an invite, get approved, open the app.
3. **Share the vision.** Where Ask Pip is heading, kept clearly apart from what works today.
4. **Let people get involved.** Join the beta, follow progress, give feedback and ideas, or partner and support.

It speaks to two groups. The first is home gardeners in the five beta countries who grow Hybrid Tea, Floribunda or Grandiflora roses. The second is people who may want to help: rose societies, garden clubs, nurseries and backers.

The site carries no rose-care advice of its own. Any advice it shows is a real screen from the app, so every statement still comes from an approved, published record with its sources.

## Joining the beta

Gardeners ask for an invite on the site, and a Founder approves each one. This keeps the numbers small and in your hands.

1. The gardener fills in a short form: name, email, country, which roses they grow, and whether they use an iPhone or an Android phone.
2. The request appears in the Garden Shed, and the Founders get an email.
3. A Founder approves it. The gardener gets an invite email with a link to the app.
4. The gardener opens the app and signs in with that same email address.
5. After their first session, Pip offers a short feedback form.

**What this needs in the app.** The app's sign-in screen appears to accept any email address today. For invites to mean anything, the app must let in only approved addresses. That is a small change, about 1 to 2 days, and it should go live before the site does.

**Gardeners the beta can't serve yet.** Someone who grows other roses or other plants is offered "follow progress" instead, so nobody is turned away with nothing.

**The Gemini rule.** An invite-only beta is not public use, so the free tier can stay for now. It is worth agreeing a number of beta gardeners at which you revisit that.

## Look and feel

The site should look like the same family as the app, with more room to breathe. The artwork to do that already exists in the `Graphics` folder.

- **Logo.** The approved round badge opens the page: Pip in the centre, "ASK PIP" above, and "Growing Understanding, Cultivating Confidence" below. The leaf-textured "Ask Pip" lettering, which the app uses as its title, is a supporting piece.
- **Pip.** The avatar in the green beanie, used large beside the opening line and smaller through the page. Four poses exist (front, both sides, behind), plus a short video, "Pip in the garden".
- **Colour.** The app's Leaf & Petal palette: deep leaf green, soft sage backgrounds and a rose-petal accent for buttons that matter.
- **Type.** Playfair Display for headings and Atkinson Hyperlegible for reading, as in the app. Large text and strong contrast throughout.
- **The app itself.** Real screens shown inside a phone outline, so people see the product and not a drawing of it.
- **Roses.** Your own photographs of real roses, so there are no stock images and no licensing questions.

**What is missing.** The approved logo is saved as `Graphics/Ask Pip Logo.png`. A cut-out of Pip exists, but only at a small size; the large poses sit on white and need cutting out for a coloured page. A wide image for link previews in messages and social posts does not exist yet. Rose photographs need choosing. I have looked at the approved logo, the lettering and the front-facing avatar; the other artwork I have only listed, not viewed.

Quality here mostly means restraint: one typeface pair, a few colours, generous spacing, fast loading and nothing that moves without a reason.

## How it would be built

Three stages, each useful on its own. The times are my estimates for the build, not counting the time you spend approving wording and artwork.

| Stage | What it delivers | Build time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The front door | The one-page site, the invite form, "follow progress" sign-up, privacy and terms pages, and the invite gate in the app | About 5 to 7 days |
| 2. Getting involved | The feedback and ideas form, the partner and support contact route, Marie's Story on its own page, and the home-screen install guide | About 2 to 3 days, after the home-screen app is built |
| 3. Keeping in touch | A short updates page, emails to people following progress, and store buttons if store apps are ever built | As needed |

**Where it lives.** A new `Site` folder in the same repository, built with the same tools as the app and published by Vercel as a second project at askpip.garden. The app is untouched apart from the invite gate.

**Where the forms go.** Into the existing Supabase project, as new tables for invite requests, followers and feedback. The Founders read and approve them in the Garden Shed.

**Cost.** No new hosting cost is expected at beta size. Invite and update emails can go through Resend, the email service the Garden Shed already uses. I have not checked whether its current plan covers the extra volume.
