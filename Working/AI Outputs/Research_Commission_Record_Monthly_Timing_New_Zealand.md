## 3. Background — the gap

Ask Pip records each rose's location (GPS, or town or city, region and country) and derives its hemisphere and current season. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for New Zealand, Australia or the United Kingdom, and the US months in approved ARCs are cut-offs for particular states, not a national pattern. Hemisphere alone cannot tell a mild garden from a cold one.

New Zealand has no single climate-zone system that gardeners commonly use. Sources often contrast the warmer north with the colder south, and frost-prone inland districts with mild coastal ones.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1 — Main winter pruning.** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band? What local signal do they tie it to (for example after the last hard frost, or when buds begin to swell)?
2. **Q2 — Feeding.** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
3. **Q3 — Late-season cut-off.** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
4. **Q4 — Planting and moving.** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
5. **Q5 — Winter clear-up.** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings, and, where sources describe a winter spray, when it is done? Product names, brands and rates are not collected as findings.
6. **Q6 — Climate bands.** How do New Zealand sources divide the country for rose timing (for example north and south of a named point, coastal and inland, frost-prone and frost-free districts, or by named region)? Is there an authoritative climate or frost-date source, such as NIWA, that a band could rest on?
7. **Q7 — Placing the gardener.** Which of these bands could a beginner place themselves in, using the region they already enter in Ask Pip (town or city, region, country) or their GPS location?
8. **Q8 — Year-to-year variation.** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band? Do they prefer local signals over fixed months?

## 5. Scope and Method

Established bush roses within the approved scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, provisionally Grandiflora), plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Whether Pip gives planting and moving advice at all is decided separately under `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`; this commission only gathers the months. Standard ROC method. Preferred sources: the New Zealand Rose Society and regional rose societies, NIWA and other New Zealand climate authorities, botanic gardens, regional councils, and New Zealand garden publications. Commercial New Zealand nurseries and garden centres allowed and labelled as commercial. NZ non-commercial rose sources are expected to be thin; where only commercial sources give a month, that is recorded as such and not disguised.

Every finding records which band it applies to. Months from one band are never applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are never converted to southern-hemisphere ones by shifting six months. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5) and carried into a Source Register (§12.6). Strictly what sources say; no ROC advice.

- The four markets are researched as separate commissions using the same questions, so the results can be compared and built into the app the same way.
