## 3. Background — the gap

Ask Pip records each rose's location (GPS, or town or city, region and country) and derives its hemisphere and current season. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom or Canada, and the US months in approved ARCs are cut-offs for particular states, not a national pattern. Hemisphere alone cannot tell a mild garden from a cold one.

US rose timing varies widely, from January pruning in the mildest states to April or later in the coldest. US guidance is commonly organised by USDA Plant Hardiness Zone and by state extension service.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1 — Main winter pruning.** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band? What local signal do they tie it to (for example after the last hard frost, or when buds begin to swell)?
2. **Q2 — Feeding.** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
3. **Q3 — Late-season cut-off.** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
4. **Q4 — Planting and moving.** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
5. **Q5 — Winter clear-up and protection.** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where sources describe a winter spray, when it is done; and where sources describe winter protection (for example mounding soil over the base), when it goes on and comes off? Product names, brands and rates are not collected as findings.
6. **Q6 — Climate bands.** How do US sources tie rose timing to USDA Plant Hardiness Zones, to local frost dates, or to state? Which zone bands are needed to cover where Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses are commonly grown, and what month windows do sources give for each band?
7. **Q7 — Placing the gardener.** Do US sources say gardeners can readily find their USDA zone (for example from plant labels or the USDA zone map), so that Ask Pip could ask for it? If not, what would a beginner need to be asked?
8. **Q8 — Year-to-year variation.** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band? Do they prefer local signals over fixed months?

## 5. Scope and Method

Established bush roses within the approved scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, provisionally Grandiflora), plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Whether Pip gives planting and moving advice at all is decided separately under `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`; this commission only gathers the months. Standard ROC method. Preferred sources: state extension services (at least one for each zone band covered), the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, the American Rose Society and local rose societies, and public botanic gardens. Commercial US rose growers allowed and labelled as commercial. Approved ARCs already carry some US month cut-offs from extension services (for example the Illinois, Penn State and Georgia fertiliser cut-offs in `ARC-BUSHROSE-BASICCARE-01`); these are re-used and cross-checked, not re-researched from scratch.

Every finding records which band it applies to. Months from one band are never applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are never converted to southern-hemisphere ones by shifting six months. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5) and carried into a Source Register (§12.6). Strictly what sources say; no ROC advice.
