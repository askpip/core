**Observation Name:** Damaged growth. This means a cane that is injured or diseased but not simply dead. Dead wood is its own observation (AF-1, **Moderate**). Thin or weak canes are a separate observation, not damage (AF-3, **Moderate**).

**Visual Criteria:**

| Possible cause | What to look for | Finding | Evidence Confidence | Photographable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canker (fungal) | Discoloured patches from light tan to dark purplish-brown; slightly sunken; edges that crack and fall away; the cane dying back above a patch that rings it; tiny black dots (fungal fruiting bodies); ring-shaped marks on larger stems | AF-4 | **High** | Yes, external signs |
| Frost or winter injury | Browned or blackened shoot tips; general cane discolouration | AF-5 | **Moderate** | The colour, yes. **Confirming it needs a cut into the pith** (below). |
| Physical damage | A visibly broken cane; worn or rubbed bark where canes touch | AF-6 | **Low** | Yes, only where visible |
| Hidden split or crack | Any visible sign (a bulge, a crack line, discolouration, or wilting beyond one point on the cane) means look closely at that spot on the plant | §6 default (U-1) | Not Applicable — approved default | Only the visible sign |
| Borer or pest | A new cane tip that suddenly wilts; an entry hole; slight swelling near the spot | AF-7 | **Low** | Partly |
| | **Caution:** a hole in the end of an old pruning cut is often a harmless hunting wasp's nest, not a pest damaging live wood | AF-7 | **Low** | — |
| | Sawdust is **not** used as a sign (not confirmed for roses) | §6 default (U-2) | Not Applicable — approved default | — |
| Sun damage (sunscald) | Treated only as a low-confidence possibility. It isn't confidently recognisable from a photo and relies on checking the plant. | AF-2; §6 default (U-7) | Moderate (AF-2, as a named cause); recognition: Not Applicable — approved default | No |

**Lookalikes, which are not damage:**

| Lookalike | Reading | Finding | Evidence Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insect galls (the red, mossy "Robin's pin-cushion"; spiny or mossy rose galls) | Generally harmless. Don't treat a gall alone as damage. A heavy, long-lasting infestation on stems can occasionally weaken or kill the part above it. | AF-9 | **Moderate** (heavy-infestation caveat: one source) |
| Red, purple or yellow spots on young canes | Early canker spots look like the normal colour of new canes in many roses. Don't treat this colour alone as canker. | AF-10 | **Low** |

**What the Photo Cannot Establish:** a photo can show outside signs (colour, shape, obvious splits, black dots, swelling). It can't show what's happening inside the cane. When a photo is unclear, the answer is to examine the plant itself, not to take a better photo (AF-8, **Low**, from general evidence). Frost damage in particular is only confirmed by cutting into the cane and checking the pith colour (AF-5).

**Deferral Trigger:** **frost or winter damage.** The full extent often isn't visible straight away. Sources advise waiting until at or near spring bud break before judging how far to cut, and a second pass may be needed later (AF-14, **Moderate**). Sources differ on how long to wait. The approved default favours the more conservative timing: wait until live growth is actually emerging (C-2). When a gardener confirms frost or winter damage, Pip offers **Decide later**, with a return point, as the recommended choice for that cane.

## 4. Wording Boundary Check (KIT OM §7.7)

- "Keep cutting a little further down" paraphrases AF-11/AF-12's progressive-cut rule without adding a threshold the sources don't give.
- "About 5–8 cm" is a unit conversion of AF-13's "2–3 inches", not a new figure.
- The borer "sealing" step is attributed to borer damage only, as AF-13 states; KIT has not extended it to other causes.
- No cause-specific diagnosis claim is made from a photo. The confidence level is always stated (C-3).

**Open decision: the frost-damage deferral and the dormancy gate.** The frost-damage deferral (wait until live growth is emerging, C-2) can pull against the dormancy gate (PKR-SGT-000001), which stops pruning once leaf-out is well underway. A gardener told to wait could find the dormancy window has closed by the time the damage shows. Two approved findings point different ways here, and KIT can't settle it. Options:

- (a) Keep both as they are. When the gardener comes back, frost-damaged canes are handled as "damaged growth found after bud break", and the dormancy gate decides whether that session can proceed.
- (b) Commission ROC to research whether cutting out damaged or dead wood after bud break is acceptable. Dead wood itself is already exempt from the recently-planted gate, but not from the dormancy gate.

## 6. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What these records are for:** they teach Pip to help a gardener spot canes that are injured or diseased (not simply dead), confirm each one on the plant, and decide what to do. The loop repeats until there are no more.

**What evidence they rest on:**
- Canker has the best-described signs: tan to purplish-brown sunken patches, cracking edges, tiny black dots (High).
- Frost damage shows as browned tips, but is only confirmed by cutting into the pith (Moderate). Broken or rubbed canes are clear when visible (Low). Borer signs are a suddenly wilting tip, an entry hole and swelling, but holes in old cuts are often harmless wasps (Low).
- Galls look alarming but are generally harmless (Moderate). Early canker spots can look like normal new-cane colour (Low).
- Once damage is confirmed: cut back to white or pale-green pith (High). Cut deeper or remove the whole cane depending on severity (Moderate). For suspected disease: cut well below, clean blades, bin the prunings (Moderate). For borers: seal the cut (Moderate).
- For frost damage: wait until the extent shows, and possibly cut again later (Moderate).

**Preserved limitations:** Pip can't see inside a cane. For anything unclear it sends the gardener to look closely at the plant, not to retake the photo, and states its confidence. "Slight" and "severe" aren't precisely defined; the rule is to keep cutting until the pith is healthy.
