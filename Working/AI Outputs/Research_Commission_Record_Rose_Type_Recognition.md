## 3. Background

The Architecture supports "an established bush rose" (§5.1), and `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` defines that as a conventional free-standing Hybrid Tea, Floribunda or Grandiflora rose grown from the base as a bush, excluding shrub roses (including English/Austin types), Old Garden Roses, species roses, climbers and ramblers, groundcover roses, miniature and miniflora roses, undefined patio roses, standards and weeping standards, and climbing sports of included cultivars.

The Architecture requires the journey to address "supported rose type" before pruning guidance proceeds (§5.4), and defers cultivar identification from appearance (§5.5). Three consequences follow, and the research shall be shaped by them:

1. **A label is the strongest evidence, and is often absent.** Beginners often have a plant tag, a nursery receipt or a cultivar name, and often do not. The research shall establish what classification information labels and nurseries commonly provide, how reliable it is, and where a gardener can verify a cultivar name.
2. **Where no label exists, the app needs recognisable features and its own comparison images.** Approved comparison material is required for every supported observation (Architecture §4.3). The research shall establish which features distinguish each type and how well a beginner can judge them, so the Founders can later source real photographs.
3. **The MVP is a dormant-season journey.** Features that rely on foliage or flowers are not visible then. The research shall distinguish features observable on a dormant plant from features that depend on remembered flowering or on other seasons.

The research shall serve a later Suitability Gate PKR (PKR Standard §5.4). Each finding shall be shaped so that it can support a stated gate question, an accepted or stopping answer, or an "unknown" outcome, each with its own Evidence Confidence Level.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3, folded into the eventual FRD's own §3 per Chapter 4).

**Q1 — Included classes.** How do recognised sources (with New Zealand sources first) describe Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora roses in bush form: typical habit, height, cane character, flower arrangement, and any features specific to a dormant plant? How do sources distinguish the three from each other, and how firm are those boundaries in practice?

**Q2 — Excluded types.** For each excluded type (shrub roses including English/Austin, hybrid musk and rugosa types; Old Garden Roses; species roses; climbers and ramblers, including climbing sports; groundcover roses; miniature and miniflora roses; patio roses; standard, half-standard and weeping-standard roses), what features do sources give for recognising it, and which features could a beginner reasonably judge on a plant in the garden?

**Q3 — Labels, names and marketing terms.** What class or type information do New Zealand and Australian nurseries and growers commonly print on tags, in catalogues and online, and what terms do they use ("bush rose", "patio", "compact", "shrub", "landscape", "garden rose")? How do those terms map to the classes in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`, and where is the mapping ambiguous? Where can a gardener check a cultivar's class (for example rose society directories or registries), and how reliable and accessible are those sources?

**Q4 — Boundary and confusable cases.** How do sources treat the cases most likely to cause a wrong qualification: compact Floribundas marketed as patio roses; Grandifloras against tall Hybrid Teas; hybrid musk, English and landscape shrub roses sold as bush roses; and climbing sports of included cultivars? The Founders reserved patio classification and Grandiflora pruning similarity for later work in `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` §10, and this commission takes up those two questions because they directly decide the gate's outcomes.

**Q5 — Observable features and photographs.** Which features can a beginner observe on a dormant, established rose to place it in or out of scope (for example height and spread, number and habit of canes, cane thickness and colour, prickle type, presence of a graft union or of a support or stake, a standard's bare trunk), and how reliable is each? What can and cannot be established from photographs (Architecture §4.1, §4.2)? Cross-refer to the photographic-limits findings in `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` rather than researching them again.

**Q6 — Uncertainty.** What do sources advise a gardener to do when the class is unknown or disputed (for example ask the nursery, a local rose society or an experienced grower), and in which situations do sources say the pruning approach is the same regardless of class? This question serves the gate's "unknown" outcome and shall not be resolved by assumption.

**Q7 — Comparison-image needs.** For each included class and for each excluded type most likely to be mistaken for an included one, which visible features, viewpoints, scale references and seasonal states would a comparison image need to show for a beginner to make the distinction, and what would each image be unable to establish? These findings shall inform a later sourcing brief; they shall not produce or source images.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The Bush Rose scope approved through `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`, taken as given.
- Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and Grandiflora roses in bush form.
- The excluded types listed in Q2, researched as lookalikes the gate must be able to recognise, not as subjects of care guidance.
- New Zealand practice and terminology first, then Australian, United Kingdom and United States sources, with regional differences noted where they occur.
- Recognition from labels and purchase records, and from features observable on an established dormant plant with a gardener's help.

### 5.2 Excluded

- Any change to the approved scope itself. A finding that the scope should change shall be reported as a recommendation for Founder consideration, not applied.
- Cultivar identification from appearance (Architecture §5.5).
- Pruning technique and care guidance for any rose type.
- Disease diagnosis, and the graft or own-root question already handled through `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`.
- Sourcing, licensing, generating or photographing comparison images.
- Drafting the final questionnaire wording, the Suitability Gate PKR, or any application change.

### 5.3 Known limitations

- Classification of individual cultivars varies between registries and regions, and practitioner sources vary in how directly they address beginner recognition.
- Dormancy limits the features available for observation, so some findings may rest on flowering-season material and shall be labelled as such.
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` should be checked for unverified candidate leads on rose-type recognition; any found may be used as search prompts only, not as evidence.

### 5.4 Dependencies and matters for separate work

- **Dependencies:** `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (scope, taken as given); `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`; `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`; `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` (photographic limits).
- **Separate work, not commissioned here, and to follow from an approved ARC:** a Suitability Gate PKR and questionnaire for supported rose type, built by the Knowledge Integration Technician (KIT); an image sourcing brief and field guide for the rose-type comparison images; and the application changes to enforce the gate. The application change is tracked in `Working/Project_Backlog.md`.
