## 3. Background

The Bush Rose V1 Architecture defines six supported observations (§5.2): dead versus living wood; damaged growth; crossing or rubbing stems; inward-growing stems; weak or congested growth; and the main framework to retain. Dead-versus-living wood, crossing/rubbing stems and inward-growing stems now have research submitted or approved. Damaged growth and weak or congested growth remain undrafted.

**Why damaged growth is commissioned on its own, not with weak or congested growth.** The two are easy to imagine overlapping — a weak cane can also be damaged, and vice versa — but that is exactly the kind of unverified assumption the crossing/rubbing-and-inward-growth commission tested and found unsupported by any source. Rather than repeat that pattern, this commission covers damaged growth alone. Where the research surfaces evidence bearing on weak or congested growth specifically, it is recorded as a lead for that commission (§5.2) and not assessed here.

**Why damaged growth needs its own recognition criteria, separate from dead wood.** `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` already governs distinguishing dead from living tissue (the green-cambium test and related indicators). Damaged growth is a different judgment: living wood that has been split, broken, cracked, cankered, frost-injured, pest-marked or otherwise physically compromised, but is not (or not yet) dead. A gardener needs to tell these apart — damaged-but-alive wood is treated differently from confirmed-dead wood — and the research shall keep that boundary explicit throughout.

Each Observation PKR needs its own diagnostic signals, each with its own Evidence Confidence Level (§5.1, §4.2 of the PKR Standard). A Decision Logic PKR's content requires its own properly evidenced Assessed Finding(s) — what a gardener is told to do cannot be inferred from what a photograph shows (§5.3). The commission is framed to produce both kinds of finding, each assessed on its own.

The recently-planted Suitability Gate (`PKR-SGT-000002`) and the dormancy gate (`PKR-SGT-000001`) are written to govern this observation, as they do the others.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3, folded into the eventual FRD's own §3 per Chapter 4 — no separate standalone Research Plan publication is required). They state what the Founders need answered, not expected answers.

**Q1 — Definition, purpose and boundary with dead wood.** How do recognised horticultural sources define "damaged" growth or canes on a bush rose, as distinct from dead wood and from ordinary weak or unproductive growth? What causes of damage do sources address (winter/frost injury, mechanical breakage, splitting, cracking, canker, borer or other pest damage, sunscald, and any others)? What reasons do sources give for addressing damaged growth at pruning?

**Q2 — Visible indicators.** What visible features do sources describe as indicating damaged growth, and do they differ by cause (for example, frost damage versus mechanical breakage versus canker)? Each distinct indicator is to be recorded separately, with its own supporting evidence and, where stated, the cause it signals.

**Q3 — What a photograph can and cannot establish.** Which indicators can be seen in a photograph, and which require examining or testing the physical cane (for example, flexing a cane to test for a hidden split, or scratching bark to check tissue beneath a suspicious area)? Cross-refer to `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` AF-34/AF-41 and `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01` AF-4 / `FRD-BUSHROSE-INWARDGROWTH-01` AF-3 (this project's existing general findings on photographic limits for structural judgments) as a starting point, rather than researching this question from nothing.

**Q4 — Lookalikes and confounders.** What might a beginner mistake for damage (for example, natural bark texture, thorns, leaf scars, or normal dormant-season colour change), or mistake damage for (dead wood, disease)? Can these be told apart from a photograph alone, or does confirmation require examining the physical rose?

**Q5 — Pruning choices.** Once a gardener has confirmed damaged growth on the physical rose, which of the four supported choices (Cut, Leave, Decide later, Get experienced local help) do sources support, and under what conditions — does this depend on the cause or severity of the damage, or on how much of the cane/bush is affected? Where sources instruct cutting, how far back do they instruct cutting (for example, back to healthy tissue only, or to the nearest healthy outward-facing bud, or to the ground)? **The general mechanics of making the cut itself are out of scope for this commission and are governed by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`; this commission's findings shall cross-refer to it rather than duplicate it.**

**Q6 — Deferral and borderline cases.** In what circumstances do sources advise waiting rather than deciding immediately — for example, suspected frost damage where the full extent will not be visible until growth resumes, or a cane that might recover? What timing guidance, if any, applies beyond the approved dormancy and recently-planted gates?

**Q7 — Safety.** What safety cautions specific to this task do sources evidence, beyond those already approved in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` — for example, tool sanitisation between cuts where canker or disease is suspected, or handling splintered/broken canes safely? Where existing approved safety content already covers a matter, the ROC shall cross-refer to it rather than research it again.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The established bush rose defined in Architecture §5.1 and, for taxonomic scope, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`.
- Dormant-season pruning, consistent with `PKR-SGT-000001` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.
- Assisted photo interpretation by a beginner gardener, with confirmation on the physical rose (Architecture §4.1–4.2).
- Findings on recognition and findings on pruning choices, kept as separate Assessed Findings.
- The distinction between damaged (living but compromised) and dead wood, kept explicit throughout.

### 5.2 Excluded

- Weak or congested growth — the other remaining undrafted observation. Where the ROC encounters evidence bearing on it, the finding is recorded as a lead for that future commission and not assessed here.
- Dead-versus-living wood recognition itself — already governed by `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`; this commission cross-refers to it for the living/dead boundary rather than re-researching it.
- The general mechanics of making a pruning cut — governed by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`.
- Disease diagnosis beyond what is needed to recognise a canker or similar damage as damage (full disease identification and treatment is out of scope per Architecture §5.5).
- Rose types outside approved scope, and pruning outside the dormant season.
- Sourcing, licensing or producing comparison photographs.

### 5.3 Known limitations

- As with every prior commission in this project, extension and society sources are practitioner guidance and vary in how much of a commission's specific sub-questions each addresses. The ROC shall record the extent to which this holds here.
- Q3 concerns what can be seen in a photograph versus what requires physically handling a cane (flexing, scratching bark). Published sources may address this only indirectly, and this project's existing general findings on photographic limits (Q3, above) give a starting point but not a complete answer for this specific observation.
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` should be checked for any unverified candidate leads on damaged growth; any found may be used as search prompts only, not as evidence.

### 5.4 Dependencies and matters for separate work

- **Dependencies:** `PKR-SGT-000002` (recently planted) and `PKR-SGT-000001` (dormancy) govern this observation. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` supplies the living/dead boundary and approved safety content that Q7 may cross-refer to. `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` supplies general cut mechanics.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** a Comparison Image PKR for this observation. No reference photograph exists anywhere in the KCS for damaged growth; sourcing one is procurement or photography work, not desk research.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** KIT builds of any PKR from this commission's outcome. KIT begins only after Founder approval and ARC creation.

## 6. Expected Deliverables

1. **A Research Plan**, folded into the FRD's own §3 per ROC Operations Manual Chapter 4. It shall not present findings.
2. **A Founder Review Dossier (FRD)** prepared under the FRDS, with the Evidence Confidence Level stated per Assessed Finding and never blended across the commission.
3. **Assessed Findings shaped for later PKR use.** Each distinct visible indicator from Q2 is its own Assessed Finding. The limits of photographic evidence (Q3) are stated explicitly, and the living/dead boundary is kept explicit throughout.
4. **A record of what was searched but not found**, with any absence of evidence stated as an absence, not as support for a conclusion.
5. **Recommendations for further research**, each given a stated ROC-recommended practical default rather than left as an open Founder decision point, per the pattern now established across this project's more recent dossiers.
