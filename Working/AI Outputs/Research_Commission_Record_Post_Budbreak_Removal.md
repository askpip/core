## 3. Background — the gap

Two approved findings pull in different directions:

- `ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` AF-14 (Moderate) and C-2: frost or winter damage often isn't fully visible straight away. Sources advise waiting until at or near spring bud break, and the approved default favours waiting until live growth actually emerges.
- `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01` AF-1 (High), implemented as PKR-SGT-000001: pruning belongs at or before bud swell. Once leaf-out is well underway, the gate doesn't pass.

A gardener told to wait for frost damage to show may therefore find the pruning window closed. `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` exempts dead wood from the recently-planted gate, but no approved finding says whether removing dead or damaged wood is acceptable after bud break or leaf-out. KIT can't resolve this by inference (KIT OM §9.3(b)).

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1.** Do credible sources treat removing dead, damaged or diseased wood after bud break or during active growth as acceptable, recommended, or to be avoided, for established bush roses (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, Grandiflora)? Does this differ from structural or whole-bush pruning after bud break?
2. **Q2.** Does the answer differ by cause: frost or winter damage, mechanical breakage, canker or disease, borer?
3. **Q3.** Is there a practical limit on timing (for example "any time of year", "before flowering", "not in heat or drought")? Do sources give reasons?
4. **Q4.** What should a beginner be told about how far to cut in that period, and does the healthy-pith check (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` AF-11) still apply unchanged?
5. **Q5.** Does anything here bear on the dormancy gate's scope? That is, should the gate govern only structural and whole-bush pruning rather than removal of dead or damaged wood? The ROC reports on this; it does not decide it.

## 5. Scope and Method

Established bush roses within the approved scope (`FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`; Architecture §5.1).
