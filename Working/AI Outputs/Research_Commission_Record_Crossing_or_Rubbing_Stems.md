## 3. Background

The Bush Rose V1 Architecture defines six supported observations (§5.2). Dead versus living wood is the only one with Founder-approved research behind it (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`). Crossing or rubbing stems and inward-growing stems are two of the five still unresearched, and `Working/Project_Backlog.md` lists them under Research Commissions — Not Yet Started.

The Founder's working expectation is that the two conditions will usually be found together: an inward-growing stem will often cross another stem on its path. This expectation is the Founder's and is not a research finding. The ROC shall test it against the evidence and shall not assume it (ROC Operations Manual §2.2). It is the reason both observations are commissioned together. The Architecture and the PKR Standard still treat them as two observations, each with its own Observation PKR (PKR Standard §3, §5.1), so the research shall keep them distinguishable.

Each Observation PKR needs its own diagnostic signals, each with its own Evidence Confidence Level (§5.1, §4.2). A Decision Logic PKR's content "requires its own properly evidenced Assessed Finding(s)" — what a gardener is told to do cannot be inferred from what a photograph shows (§5.3). The commission is therefore framed to produce both kinds of finding, each assessed on its own.

The recently-planted Suitability Gate (`PKR-SGT-000002`) is written to govern these observations, as it does the other four.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3). They state what the Founders need answered. They do not state expected answers. Each question applies to both observations unless stated otherwise.

**Q1 — Definition, purpose and relationship.** How do recognised horticultural sources define crossing stems, rubbing stems and inward-growing stems on a bush rose, and what reasons do they give for addressing each at pruning? Do sources treat these as one condition, two or three? Do they describe them as commonly occurring together, and if so, in what terms?

**Q2 — Visible indicators.** What visible features do sources describe as indicating each condition, including any visible sign of a rubbing wound? Each distinct indicator is to be recorded separately, with its own supporting evidence.

**Q3 — What a photograph can and cannot establish.** Which indicators can be seen in a photograph, and which require examining the physical rose? Does the evidence address whether stems that overlap in one view are in contact, whether the direction of a stem's growth can be judged from a photograph, and whether an additional viewpoint helps?

**Q4 — Lookalikes and confounders.** What conditions could a beginner mistake for each condition, or the reverse, and can they be told apart from appearance or a photograph alone?

**Q5 — Pruning choices and which stem to remove.** Once a gardener has confirmed the observation on the physical rose, which of the four supported choices (Cut, Leave, Decide later, Get experienced local help) do sources support, and under what conditions? What basis do sources give for choosing which stem to remove or retain, and where along that stem do they place the removal (for example, the whole stem to its point of origin, or a shortening)? The general mechanics of making a cut are excluded (§5.4).

**Q6 — Stems with more than one condition, and deferral.** How do sources treat a stem that is both inward-growing and crossing or rubbing, including which condition is assessed or acted on first? In what circumstances do sources advise against deciding at the time, and what timing or climate qualifications, if any, apply beyond the approved dormancy and recently-planted gates?

**Q7 — Safety.** What safety cautions specific to this task do sources evidence, beyond those already approved in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`? Where existing approved safety content already covers a matter, the ROC shall cross-refer to it rather than research it again.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The established bush rose defined in Architecture §5.1 and, for taxonomic scope, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (approved 9 September 2026).
- Dormant-season pruning, consistent with `PKR-SGT-000001` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.
- Assisted photo interpretation by a beginner gardener, with confirmation on the physical rose (Architecture §4.1–4.2).
- Findings on recognition and findings on pruning choices, kept as separate Assessed Findings, and findings on each of the two observations kept distinguishable.
- The stem-level removal decision: which stem, and where along it (Q5).

### 5.2 Excluded

- The other three supported observations. Where the ROC encounters evidence bearing on one of them, it shall record it as a lead for that future commission and shall not assess it here (compare ROC Operations Manual §8.4A). Weak or congested growth is likely to sit close to this commission's subject; the same rule applies.
- The general mechanics of making a pruning cut (§5.4).
- Rose types outside the approved scope, and pruning outside the dormant season.
- Cultivar identification, disease diagnosis, and any authorisation of a cut from an image alone (Architecture §5.5).
- Sourcing, licensing or producing comparison photographs (§5.4).

### 5.3 Known limitations

- The dead-wood commission found that extension sources are practitioner guidance and vary in how much of a commission's specific sub-questions each addresses. The same limitation is likely to apply here, and the ROC shall record the extent to which it does.
- Q3 and Q4 concern what can be seen in photographs. Published sources may address this only indirectly. The ROC shall record this as an evidence gap where it applies and shall not fill it by assumption.
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` holds a few unverified candidate leads on crossing or rubbing stems, taken from a superseded Drive document (for example, requesting a second photograph from a different angle to distinguish overlap from contact). It holds none on inward-growing stems. The ROC may use these leads as search prompts only. They are not evidence and shall not be cited as such (ROC Operations Manual §4.7A).

### 5.4 Dependencies and matters for separate work

- **Dependencies:** `PKR-SGT-000002` (recently planted) and `PKR-SGT-000001` (dormancy) govern these observations. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` supplies approved safety content (its AF-6) that Q7 may cross-refer to.
- **Proposed separate commission — the general mechanics of a pruning cut.** Every observation whose outcome is Cut ends in the same physical act, and the mechanics of that act (for example, cut angle, distance above a bud, which way the bud faces, tool condition) are common to all of them. No approved research in the Knowledge Curation System (KCS) covers this. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` addresses only where to cut in dead wood (its AF-7 and AF-8). Researching the mechanics once, as a topic of its own, avoids repeating them in each observation's commission, and lets each observation's Decision Logic link to a single record. The PKR Standard allows related PKRs to be linked (§8) but defines no record type for a shared technique topic, so the Knowledge Integration Technician (KIT) would refer that gap to the Founders (PKR Standard §3). This commission stays within Q5's stem-level decision, and the new commission would supply the how. The proposal is for Founder confirmation (§8, item 4).
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** a Comparison Image PKR for these observations. Per the Project Backlog, no reference photograph exists anywhere in the KCS, and sourcing one is procurement or photography work rather than desk research.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** KIT builds of any PKR from this commission's outcome. KIT begins only after Founder approval and Approved Research Compilation (ARC) creation.

## 6. Expected Deliverables

1. **A Research Plan** prepared under ROC Operations Manual Chapter 4, containing questions, information requirements, anticipated source types and known challenges. It shall not present findings (§4.7A).
2. **A Founder Review Dossier (FRD)** prepared under the FRDS, with the Evidence Confidence Level stated per Assessed Finding and never blended across the commission (FRDS §6.2; EAS).
3. **Assessed Findings shaped for later PKR use.** Each distinct visible indicator from Q2 is its own Assessed Finding, identifiable to the observation it belongs to. Findings on pruning choices (Q5–Q6) are kept separate from findings on recognition (Q2–Q4). The limits of photographic evidence (Q3) are stated explicitly.
4. **A finding, or a recorded absence, on the relationship between the two conditions (Q1, Q6).** Where the evidence supports a stated relationship, it is an Assessed Finding. Where it does not, and the ROC reasons that one is plausible, that reasoning is recorded separately as the ROC's own synthesis and carried forward as a Recommendation for Further Research (ROC Operations Manual §8.4A).
5. **A record of what was searched but not found**, with any absence of evidence stated as an absence and not as support for a conclusion (ROC Operations Manual §5.7).
6. **Recommendations for further research.**
