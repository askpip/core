## 3. Background

The Bush Rose V1 Architecture defines six supported observations (§5.2). Dead versus living wood is the only one with Founder-approved research behind it (`ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`). Crossing or rubbing stems and inward-growing stems are two of the five that were still unresearched when this commission was drafted; `Working/Project_Backlog.md` listed them under Research Commissions — Not Yet Started, and this commission's own hold has now been lifted (§1).

The Founder's working expectation is that the two conditions will usually be found together: an inward-growing stem will often cross another stem on its path. This expectation is the Founder's and is not a research finding. The ROC shall test it against the evidence and shall not assume it (ROC Operations Manual §2.2). It is the reason both observations are commissioned together. The Architecture and the PKR Standard still treat them as two observations, each with its own Observation PKR (PKR Standard §3, §5.1), so the research shall keep them distinguishable.

**Note added in Version 0.3.** A closely related question was tested during the pruning-framework commission (its R-1, closed in Revision 4): whether crossing, rubbing and inward-growing stems commonly occur *together on the same stem* across pruned bushes generally. That question was found to be a permanent limitation of the literature — an occurrence rate that only direct observation over time could establish, not something any source states — and was not resolved. It is a related but distinct question from the one this commission tests (whether, on an individual rose being examined, an inward-growing stem is found to be crossing another). This commission's own research is not pre-settled by that outcome and shall proceed as planned.

Each Observation PKR needs its own diagnostic signals, each with its own Evidence Confidence Level (§5.1, §4.2). A Decision Logic PKR's content "requires its own properly evidenced Assessed Finding(s)" — what a gardener is told to do cannot be inferred from what a photograph shows (§5.3). The commission is therefore framed to produce both kinds of finding, each assessed on its own.

The recently-planted Suitability Gate (`PKR-SGT-000002`) is written to govern these observations, as it does the other four.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3). They state what the Founders need answered. They do not state expected answers. Each question applies to both observations unless stated otherwise.

**Q1 — Definition, purpose and relationship.** How do recognised horticultural sources define crossing stems, rubbing stems and inward-growing stems on a bush rose, and what reasons do they give for addressing each at pruning? Do sources treat these as one condition, two or three? Do they describe them as commonly occurring together, and if so, in what terms?

**Q2 — Visible indicators.** What visible features do sources describe as indicating each condition, including any visible sign of a rubbing wound? Each distinct indicator is to be recorded separately, with its own supporting evidence.

**Q3 — What a photograph can and cannot establish.** Which indicators can be seen in a photograph, and which require examining the physical rose? Does the evidence address whether stems that overlap in one view are in contact, whether the direction of a stem's growth can be judged from a photograph, and whether an additional viewpoint helps? **Cross-refer to `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` AF-34 and AF-41 (general photo-versus-physical-examination findings, and a first breakdown of what a whole-plant photograph can and cannot support) as a starting point, rather than researching this question from nothing.**

**Q4 — Lookalikes and confounders.** What conditions could a beginner mistake for each condition, or the reverse, and can they be told apart from appearance or a photograph alone?

**Q5 — Pruning choices and which stem to remove.** Once a gardener has confirmed the observation on the physical rose, which of the four supported choices (Cut, Leave, Decide later, Get experienced local help) do sources support, and under what conditions? What basis do sources give for choosing which stem to remove or retain, and where along that stem do they place the removal (for example, the whole stem to its point of origin, or a shortening)? **The general mechanics of making the cut itself — distance above a bud, angle, tool selection, tool care, sealing — are out of scope for this commission and are governed by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` (its AF-21 to AF-30, AF-35, AF-38), pending that commission's own approval as `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`; this commission's findings shall cross-refer to it rather than duplicate it.** This commission does still need its own finding on the tie-break already available for one specific case — `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` AF-33 gives an explicit rule, from one source, for which of two crossing canes to remove — and shall test whether further sources corroborate, extend or qualify it for this commission's fuller purposes.

**Q6 — Stems with more than one condition, and deferral.** How do sources treat a stem that is both inward-growing and crossing or rubbing, including which condition is assessed or acted on first? In what circumstances do sources advise against deciding at the time, and what timing or climate qualifications, if any, apply beyond the approved dormancy and recently-planted gates? **Note:** `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` separately addresses a related but distinct interaction — a stem that is both a removal candidate and structurally useful to the framework — for which no source gives an answer and a ROC-recommended practical default was adopted (lean conservative, retain and reassess). That default does not answer this commission's own Q6 and is noted here only so the two are not conflated.

**Q7 — Safety.** What safety cautions specific to this task do sources evidence, beyond those already approved in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`? Where existing approved safety content already covers a matter, the ROC shall cross-refer to it rather than research it again.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The established bush rose defined in Architecture §5.1 and, for taxonomic scope, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (approved 9 September 2026).
- Dormant-season pruning, consistent with `PKR-SGT-000001` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.
- Assisted photo interpretation by a beginner gardener, with confirmation on the physical rose (Architecture §4.1–4.2).
- Findings on recognition and findings on pruning choices, kept as separate Assessed Findings, and findings on each of the two observations kept distinguishable.
- The stem-level removal decision: which stem, and where along it (Q5).

### 5.2 Excluded

- The other two undrafted supported observations (damaged growth; weak or congested growth). Where the ROC encounters evidence bearing on either, it shall record it as a lead for that future commission and shall not assess it here (compare ROC Operations Manual §8.4A). Weak or congested growth is likely to sit close to this commission's subject; the same rule applies.
- **The general mechanics of making a pruning cut.** Now covered by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` (§5.4) rather than proposed as a commission of its own; this commission cross-refers to it instead of researching it again.
- Rose types outside the approved scope, and pruning outside the dormant season.
- Cultivar identification, disease diagnosis, and any authorisation of a cut from an image alone (Architecture §5.5).
- Sourcing, licensing or producing comparison photographs (§5.4).

### 5.3 Known limitations

- The dead-wood commission found that extension sources are practitioner guidance and vary in how much of a commission's specific sub-questions each addresses. The pruning-framework commission found the same. The same limitation is likely to apply here, and the ROC shall record the extent to which it does.
- Q3 and Q4 concern what can be seen in photographs. Published sources may address this only indirectly. `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` AF-34 and AF-41 give a first, partial, ROC-assembled answer to a closely related general question, but neither claims completeness; this commission's own Q3 shall test and extend it for these two specific observations, not assume it is already answered. The ROC shall record any remaining gap as an evidence gap and shall not fill it by assumption.
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` holds a few unverified candidate leads on crossing or rubbing stems, taken from a superseded Drive document (for example, requesting a second photograph from a different angle to distinguish overlap from contact). It holds none on inward-growing stems. The ROC may use these leads as search prompts only. They are not evidence and shall not be cited as such (ROC Operations Manual §4.7A).

### 5.4 Dependencies and matters for separate work

- **Dependencies:** `PKR-SGT-000002` (recently planted) and `PKR-SGT-000001` (dormancy) govern these observations. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` supplies approved safety content (its AF-6) that Q7 may cross-refer to. **`FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`** (pending approval as `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`) supplies the general mechanics of making a cut (AF-21 to AF-30, AF-35, AF-38) and an explicit crossing-cane tie-break rule (AF-33) that Q5 cross-refers to, in place of the separate commission proposed in Version 0.2.
- **Resolved in Version 0.3 — no separate cut-mechanics commission needed.** Version 0.2 proposed researching the general mechanics of a pruning cut (cut angle, distance above a bud, bud direction, tool condition) as a commission of its own, reasoning that every observation whose outcome is Cut ends in the same physical act. `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` has since produced exactly that content, evidenced to Founder-review quality across 28 sources. Rather than commission it again, this commission's Decision Logic shall link to that ARC (once approved) as the shared technique reference. The PKR Standard's gap on a record type for a shared technique topic (§3, noted in Version 0.2) remains unresolved and is left for the Knowledge Integration Technician to raise when it builds these PKRs, since it no longer blocks this commission's own research.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** a Comparison Image PKR for these observations. Per the Project Backlog, no reference photograph exists anywhere in the KCS, and sourcing one is procurement or photography work rather than desk research.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** KIT builds of any PKR from this commission's outcome. KIT begins only after Founder approval and Approved Research Compilation (ARC) creation.

## 6. Expected Deliverables

1. **A Research Plan** prepared under ROC Operations Manual Chapter 4, containing questions, information requirements, anticipated source types and known challenges. It shall not present findings (§4.7A).
2. **A Founder Review Dossier (FRD)** prepared under the FRDS, with the Evidence Confidence Level stated per Assessed Finding and never blended across the commission (FRDS §6.2; EAS).
3. **Assessed Findings shaped for later PKR use.** Each distinct visible indicator from Q2 is its own Assessed Finding, identifiable to the observation it belongs to. Findings on pruning choices (Q5–Q6) are kept separate from findings on recognition (Q2–Q4). The limits of photographic evidence (Q3) are stated explicitly.
4. **A finding, or a recorded absence, on the relationship between the two conditions (Q1, Q6).** Where the evidence supports a stated relationship, it is an Assessed Finding. Where it does not, and the ROC reasons that one is plausible, that reasoning is recorded separately as the ROC's own synthesis and carried forward as a Recommendation for Further Research (ROC Operations Manual §8.4A).
5. **A record of what was searched but not found**, with any absence of evidence stated as an absence and not as support for a conclusion (ROC Operations Manual §5.7).
6. **Recommendations for further research.**
