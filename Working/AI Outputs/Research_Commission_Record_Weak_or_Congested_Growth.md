## 3. Background

The Bush Rose V1 Architecture defines six supported observations (§5.2). Five now have research submitted or approved: dead-versus-living wood, crossing/rubbing stems, inward-growing stems, damaged growth, and (functionally, via the pruning-framework commission) the main framework to retain. Weak or congested growth is the last to be commissioned.

**Boundary with already-researched observations.** `FRD-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01` (submitted) found that several sources treat "weak/thin/unproductive" growth as its own category, separate from damage — a stem can be thin and unproductive without being physically injured (its AF-3). That finding is the starting point for this commission, not a substitute for researching it properly: this commission shall establish weak growth's own recognition criteria and pruning treatment in full, rather than relying on the adjacent mentions already surfaced. Congestion (too many canes crowded together) is conceptually related to, but distinct from, crossing or rubbing stems (`FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`, submitted) — crossing/rubbing concerns two specific stems in contact, while congestion concerns overall cane density and the "open, airy centre" goal referenced throughout this project's prior dossiers. The research shall keep this boundary explicit and cross-refer to the existing dossiers rather than re-researching what they already cover.

**Why weak and congested are researched as one commission.** See the note under §1: the Architecture bundles them as a single named observation, unlike the earlier crossing/rubbing-and-inward-growth case where two separate Architecture items were combined on assumption. Findings on each are still kept distinguishable throughout (§5.1).

Each Observation PKR needs its own diagnostic signals, each with its own Evidence Confidence Level (§5.1, §4.2 of the PKR Standard). A Decision Logic PKR's content requires its own properly evidenced Assessed Finding(s) — what a gardener is told to do cannot be inferred from what a photograph shows (§5.3). The commission is framed to produce both kinds of finding, each assessed on its own.

The recently-planted Suitability Gate (`PKR-SGT-000002`) and the dormancy gate (`PKR-SGT-000001`) are written to govern this observation, as they do the others.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3, folded into the eventual FRD's own §3 per Chapter 4).

**Q1 — Definition, relationship and boundary.** How do recognised horticultural sources define "weak" growth (thin, spindly, unproductive canes) and "congested" growth (too many canes crowded together) on a bush rose? Are these treated as one condition or two? What causes are named for each — age of the plant, poor light/air circulation, too many basal breaks competing for resources, a general decline in vigour, or others? How do sources describe the boundary between weak growth and damaged growth (already researched), and between congestion and crossing/rubbing stems (already researched)?

**Q2 — Visible indicators.** What visible or measurable features do sources give for recognising weak growth (for example, a stated thickness threshold such as "thinner than a pencil," pale or sparse foliage, poor or absent bud/bloom production) and for recognising congestion (a stated cane-count guideline, visibly crowded/overlapping canes, a densely twiggy centre)? Record each distinct indicator separately, with its own source.

**Q3 — What a photograph can and cannot establish.** Can cane thickness be judged reliably from a photograph (for example, is a size reference needed)? Is overall bush density/congestion more or less assessable from a whole-plant photograph than a single-stem condition would be? Cross-refer to this project's existing general findings on photographic limits (`FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`, `FRD-BUSHROSE-STEMCROSSING-01`, `FRD-BUSHROSE-INWARDGROWTH-01`, `FRD-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01`) as a starting point rather than researching this from nothing.

**Q4 — Lookalikes and confounders.** What might a beginner mistake for weak growth (for example, a vigorous new basal cane that is simply young and not yet thick, versus a genuinely weak older cane) or for congestion (a densely-flowering, healthy bush versus a genuinely overcrowded one)? Can these be told apart from a photograph alone, or does confirmation require examining the physical rose?

**Q5 — Pruning choices.** Once a gardener has confirmed weak growth or congestion on the physical rose, what do sources instruct? For weak growth: is the weak cane removed entirely, and does this differ from the severity-dependent rule already found for damaged growth, or match the "always remove fully" rule found for crossing/rubbing and inward-growing stems? For congestion: do sources give a target number or density of canes to retain, and what basis (vigour, spacing, position) do they give for choosing which canes stay and which are thinned out? **The general mechanics of making the cut itself are out of scope for this commission and are governed by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`.**

**Q6 — Multiple conditions and deferral.** How do sources treat a cane that is both weak and part of a congested area? Is there a recommended order of operations (for example, address congestion first by thinning, then reassess remaining canes for weakness, or the reverse)? In what circumstances do sources advise waiting rather than deciding immediately?

**Q7 — Safety.** What safety cautions specific to this task do sources evidence, beyond those already approved in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` — for example, anything specific to working inside a dense, twiggy centre (scratches, reduced visibility of thorns, etc.)? Where existing approved safety content already covers a matter, the ROC shall cross-refer to it rather than research it again.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The established bush rose defined in Architecture §5.1 and, for taxonomic scope, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`.
- Dormant-season pruning, consistent with `PKR-SGT-000001` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.
- Assisted photo interpretation by a beginner gardener, with confirmation on the physical rose.
- Findings on weak growth and findings on congestion, kept distinguishable throughout, even though researched under one commission.
- The stem-level and whole-bush-level removal/thinning decisions.

### 5.2 Excluded

- Damaged growth, crossing/rubbing stems, and inward-growing stems — already researched; this commission cross-refers to them rather than re-researching the boundary conditions.
- The general mechanics of making a pruning cut — governed by `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04`.
- Disease diagnosis and cultivar identification (Architecture §5.5).
- Rose types outside approved scope, and pruning outside the dormant season.
- Sourcing, licensing or producing comparison photographs.

### 5.3 Known limitations

- As with every prior commission in this project, extension and society sources are practitioner guidance and vary in how much of a commission's specific sub-questions each addresses.
- Q3 concerns what can be judged from a photograph. This project's existing general findings on photographic limits give a starting point but not a complete answer for cane-thickness or whole-bush-density judgments specifically.
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` should be checked for any unverified candidate leads on weak or congested growth; any found may be used as search prompts only, not as evidence.

### 5.4 Dependencies and matters for separate work

- **Dependencies:** `PKR-SGT-000002` and `PKR-SGT-000001` govern this observation. `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01` supplies approved safety content. `FRD-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-04` supplies general cut mechanics and the "open, airy centre" goal this commission's congestion findings shall cross-refer to. `FRD-BUSHROSE-DAMAGEDGROWTH-01`'s AF-3 (weak growth as its own category) is the starting point for Q1.
- **Separate work, not commissioned here:** a Comparison Image PKR for this observation. **Separate work, not commissioned here:** KIT builds of any PKR from this commission's outcome.
