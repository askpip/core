## 3. Background — the gap

`ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-8 (Moderate) records that sources remove growth arising from below the bud union on grafted roses, and two say to tear it away at its origin. The same finding records that **"how a gardener recognises a sucker from a basal shoot of the rose itself is not established."** AF-10 (Low) treats new basal shoots as valuable, because they renew the framework. Guiding a beginner to remove suckers without a reliable way to recognise them risks removing a healthy new cane, so the Founders held AF-8 back from the MVP pending this research.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1.** What features do credible sources give for telling a rootstock sucker from a basal cane of the grafted variety? For example: where it emerges relative to the bud union, leaf or leaflet count and shape, thorn character, stem colour, growth habit. Which of these can a beginner observe on a **dormant** plant?
2. **Q2.** How reliable are those features? Do sources say they can mislead, for example with own-root roses, or with a bud union buried below the soil line (see `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01` AF-3)?
3. **Q3.** How is a confirmed sucker removed: torn away at its origin, cut, or dug down to the root? Why, and with what precautions?
4. **Q4.** What can and can't a photograph establish about a suspected sucker?
5. **Q5.** When should a beginner be advised to leave it, or to get experienced help?

## 5. Scope and Method

Grafted established bush roses within the approved scope (Architecture §5.1). Standard ROC method, with UK, US, Australian and New Zealand institutional sources where available. **Every online source must be recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5),** and the ARC must carry a Source Register (§12.6).

## 7. Interim Behaviour

Until this commission's findings are approved, Pip doesn't guide sucker removal. If a gardener asks about suckers, Pip suggests experienced local help.
