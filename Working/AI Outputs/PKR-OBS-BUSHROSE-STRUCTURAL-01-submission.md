## 8. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What these records are for:** they teach Pip to help a gardener find stems that cross or rub against each other, and stems growing into the middle of the bush, confirm each one on the real rose, and decide what to do. For each type, Pip keeps asking "can you see any more?" until the gardener says no.

**What evidence they rest on:**
- **Crossing or rubbing:** about sixteen sources, near-unanimous, say to remove a confirmed crossing or rubbing stem (High). Six say to remove it completely rather than shorten it (Moderate). Rubbing damages bark and lets disease in (Moderate). When choosing which of two to remove, the few sources with a rule say remove the weaker one (Low).
- **Inward-growing:** far thinner. Most sources just say to cut to an outward-facing bud so stems don't grow inward in future (Moderate). Only three name an existing inward stem as something to remove (Low), and one says to remove it completely (Moderate).
- **Photos:** for both, one photo can't reliably show whether stems actually touch or which way one really grows (Low, general evidence), so Pip asks for another angle and always has the gardener check the real rose.

**Preserved limitations:** nobody describes what a rubbing wound looks like, so the rubbing-vs-crossing distinction is your approved default. One respected Australian society argues against opening up the centre of the bush; that disagreement is kept, not hidden. A stem bending toward light could look like it's growing inward; that's a caution, not a sourced rule.

**Outstanding dependencies:** no comparison images yet. Per your direction, they don't block publication, and samples will be added later.

**Open decisions:** none. Leave was approved as drafted, 29 September 2026. **Known gap:** the dossiers behind these sources recorded no web addresses, so the new Source PKRs (000049–000068) have none. In the app, their attributions show publisher and title without a link until a web-address revision is approved.
