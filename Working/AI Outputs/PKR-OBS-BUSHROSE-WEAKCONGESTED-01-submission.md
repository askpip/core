The sources treat its two parts differently: weak growth is judged cane by cane, and congestion is judged for the whole bush (AF-1). So the record has two parts, taken in the order the sources use: **weak canes first, then congestion** (AF-14; U-6).

**Observation Name:** Weak or congested growth.

- **Weak** is judged one cane at a time: a thin cane that isn't strong enough to be worth keeping.
- **Congested** is judged for the whole bush: the base and middle are too crowded.

Sources keep the two distinct (AF-1, **Moderate**). Weak canes are living growth, a separate category from dead or damaged wood, but no source gives a dedicated test for "weak" (AF-3, **Moderate**). Congestion is a separate step from crossing or rubbing stems: it's about overall crowding, not two stems touching (AF-4, **Moderate**).

**Cautions:**

- A thin cane isn't necessarily weak. It may be young new growth. No source addresses this confusion directly. One source tells new wood from old by colour: thorns and bark are grey on old wood, and usually still brown or reddish on this season's stems (AF-11, **Low**). Where a thin cane has brown or reddish thorns, Pip suggests it may be this season's growth rather than a weak cane.
- As always, confirm on the actual rose before any cut (U-7).

**What the Photo Cannot Establish:**

- **Thickness:** without a size reference, cane thickness can't be judged reliably from a photo (AF-9, **Low**, from general photo-diagnosis guidance). Where a photo leaves thickness unclear, Pip asks for one with a coin or pencil held beside the cane (U-5 default).
- **Crowding:** overall crowding is somewhat easier to judge from a whole-bush photo than fine thickness is. But no single photo can rule out crowding hidden at the back or inside of the bush (AF-10, **Low**).

**Linked Decision Logic PKR:** PKR-DEC-000005.

**Confirmation Requirement:** Yes. The gardener checks each thin cane on the actual rose (ideally against a pencil) and checks the middle of the bush from more than one side.

## 6. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What these records are for:** they teach Pip to help a gardener remove thin, weak canes one at a time ("any more?"), and then, if the bush is still crowded, thin out whole canes until the middle is open. Pip keeps a sensible range of strong canes rather than a fixed number.

**What evidence they rest on:**
- Weak canes: removed completely at the base (High). Crowded bushes: thinned by removing whole canes, strongest kept, evenly spaced, open centre (High).
- "About pencil thickness" is the near-universal test (Moderate). How many canes to keep varies by source, roughly 3–7 (Moderate).
- Prune lightly if the plant is already weak (Moderate). Eye protection and gauntlets inside the bush (Moderate). In hot climates, leave some shade in the middle (Moderate).
- Photos need a coin or pencil for scale to judge thickness (Low). Crowding hidden at the back can't be seen in one photo (Low).

**Preserved limitations:** no number is used for "pencil thickness", because the two sources that give one disagree. A thin young cane may be mistaken for a weak one; there's only a thorn-colour hint for this (Low), and Pip says so.

**Source checks:** 40 sources checked on 29 September. 34 confirmed, 1 found by title only, 2 not confirmed and 3 not identifiable. Every affected finding rests on other confirmed sources. There's one real discrepancy (Orange County Rose Society, above).
