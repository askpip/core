# PKR Submission Package — Removal of Dead, Damaged or Diseased Wood After Bud Break (v1.0, Published)

**Published 29 September 2026.** Approved by Shaphan in chat, 29 September 2026 ("approve all"), under the standing condition that the records contain only content from already-approved ARCs. PKR-SGT-000001, PKR-DEC-000001 and PKR-DEC-000004 are now Published at v1.1; v1.0 of each is superseded. Built per the KIT Operations Manual from `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` v1.0 (approved by Shaphan, 29 September 2026). The package holds:

- **Revisions to published records:**
  - PKR-SGT-000001 (dormancy gate): v1.0 → v1.1
  - PKR-DEC-000001 (dead wood): v1.0 → v1.1
  - PKR-DEC-000004 (damaged growth): v1.0 → v1.1
- **Source PKRs:** in `PKR-SRC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-submission.md`.
  - New: PKR-SRC-000163 to 000180.
  - Reused, with new MIL references: 000001, 000002, 000003, 000004, 000007, 000013, 000021, 000022, 000065, 000069, 000073, 000080, 000087, 000090, 000095, 000131.

All of these are submitted together.

**Founder decisions applied (ARC §2):**

- **D2 (b):** the dormancy gate now governs only structural and whole-bush pruning. Wood confirmed dead, damaged or diseased may be removed after bud break, under conditions.
- **D3 (b):** PKR-DEC-000001 and PKR-DEC-000004 follow D2.
  - The frost-damage deferral stays, but when the gardener returns it leads to removal rather than a gate refusal.
  - The late-season caution is advice only, and diseased wood is exempt from it.
- **D4/D5:** the R-1 and R-2 defaults apply.

In the tables below, "AF-n" refers to `ARC-BUSHROSE-POSTBUDBREAK-01` unless another ARC is named.

### 2.1 Removal-only session (new)

When the gate doesn't pass for structural pruning, Pip may still guide removal of wood confirmed dead, damaged or diseased: the dead-wood observation (PKR-OBS-000001) and the damaged-growth observation (PKR-OBS-000004) only. Structural observations are not offered.

| Condition shown to the gardener | Finding | Evidence Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Your rose is already growing, so this is not the time for full pruning. Pruning hard now can cost you flowers. But you can still remove wood that is dead, damaged or diseased." | AF-3; AF-1 | **Moderate**; **Moderate** |
| Remove only the wood you've confirmed. Don't shorten or shape healthy canes today. | AF-3; R-1 default | **Moderate**; Not Applicable — approved default |
| Go gently and avoid damaging the new leaves and shoots. | AF-17 | **Low** |
| Cut back until the pith is white or pale green, exactly as at any other time. | AF-19 | **Moderate** |
| For anything that looks like canker or disease, the usual care applies: cut well below the affected part, clean your blades, and bin the prunings. | AF-20; R-2 default | **High**; Not Applicable — approved default |
| **Late in the season (advice only, never blocks):** "Sources advise against pruning late in the growing season, because it can encourage new growth that winter cold may damage. Diseased wood is the exception: remove it whenever you find it." Sources don't say whether dead (as distinct from diseased) wood is exempt. | AF-15; AF-16 | **High**; **Moderate** |

The one-third advisory (PKR-CGD-000003) and the "any more?" loop apply as in any session.

## 7. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What changes for the gardener:** before, if your rose had already started growing, Pip stopped and offered only care tips. Now it says: "It's too late for full pruning this season, but you can still remove dead, damaged or diseased wood." It then guides just those two observations, with four conditions:

- go gently;
- remove only confirmed wood, and don't shape healthy canes;
- cut back to white pith;
- use the usual canker care.

Late in the season it adds a gentle note that pruning then can risk winter damage, except for diseased wood. Frost-damaged canes that Pip told you to "come back to later" are now actually handled when you come back.

**What evidence it rests on:**

- The "prune at bud swell" window is about main pruning (High).
- Several sources say dead, damaged or diseased wood can go any time or as soon as seen (Moderate).
- Diseased wood should go promptly (Moderate), and cutting well below disease is standard (High).
- Heavy pruning in leaf costs flowers (Moderate).
- Late-season pruning is discouraged (High), with diseased wood exempt (Moderate).
- The two sources about a rose already in leaf both say "go gently" (Low).
