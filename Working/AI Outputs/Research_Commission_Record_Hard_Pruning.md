## 3. Background — the gap

Beginners commonly ask whether they can kill or harm a rose by pruning it too hard. No approved ARC answers this directly. `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` AF-40 (Low) and the published advisory `PKR-CGD-000003` carry a general woody-plant rule of removing no more than about a third of a bush's growth in one session; AF-40 itself records that this is not rose-specific and that one rose-specific source recommended a single hard renovation instead. AF-20 (Low) records staged renewal of about a third of old canes a year, and the §6 default stages heavier renovation over two to three years.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1.** How tolerant of hard pruning do reputable sources say an established bush rose (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, provisionally Grandiflora) is? Can pruning kill it?
2. **Q2.** What do sources mean by "hard", "moderate" and "light" pruning?
3. **Q3.** What real risks do sources name — for example old or weak plants, cutting below the graft, removing all canes, pruning at the wrong time, repeated severe pruning, stress, disease entry, grafted versus own-root?
4. **Q4.** What do sources say about renovating an old or neglected bush: hard renovation in one go, or staged over two to three years? Where do they agree or conflict with `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01` and `PKR-CGD-000003`?
5. **Q5.** What signs of recovery do sources describe, and what do they advise if a rose seems to have been pruned too hard?

## 5. Scope and Method

Established bush roses within the approved scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, provisionally Grandiflora). Newly planted roses are outside scope (governed by `ARC-BUSHROSE-RECENTPLANT-01`). Standard ROC method; priority market New Zealand, then Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States. Rose societies, extension services, the RHS and botanic gardens preferred; commercial sources allowed and labelled. Every online source recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5) and carried into a Source Register (§12.6). Strictly what sources say; no ROC advice.

- The question was raised as a common beginner question. The NZ Rose Society FAQ's statement that roses are forgiving was named in the commission as a known starting point; it is treated in the dossier only as one source among others.
