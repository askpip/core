**Published 29 September 2026.** Approved by Shaphan in chat, 29 September 2026, under the standing condition that the records contain only content from already-approved ARCs. §5 decisions: **1(c)** — 3–7 canes as the range, with 4–6 as typical, used everywhere; PKR-DEC-000005 was revised to match (v1.1). **2** — Grandifloras are treated exactly as Hybrid Teas, without the gentler-cutback nuance. **3** — sucker removal (AF-8) stays held; ROC is commissioned to research recognising suckers (`Research_Commission_Record_Rootstock_Suckers.md`). This is the sixth and last MVP observation (Architecture §5.2). It comes after the other five: once dead, damaged, crossing, inward and weak canes are dealt with, Pip helps the gardener identify the strong canes that form the bush's framework, keep them, and shorten them (AF-12, AF-13, High; §6 order-of-work default).

**Routed elsewhere, not in this package:**

- **Cut mechanics** (AF-21 to AF-31, AF-35, AF-38; conflicts C2, C3, C4, C8, C9, C10, C11) and the **one-third advisory** (AF-40) go to the Care Guidance PKR type, which is next. PKR-DEC-000006 refers to that guidance, and doesn't repeat it, when it says "shorten".
- **Categories of growth** (AF-1 to AF-6, AF-11, AF-33) are already covered by PKR-OBS/DEC-000001 to 000005.
- **Once-flowering roses** (AF-19) are outside scope. PKR-SGT-000003 stops them.

**Founder directions applied:** the Comparison Image gap is documented, and the loop continues until no canes are left to decide ("Any more canes to decide on?").

"AF-n" refers to `ARC-BUSHROSE-PRUNINGFRAMEWORK-01`, and "§6 default" means an approved practical default in that ARC.

**What the Photo Cannot Establish:** a photo can show the overall outline, canes crossing through the centre, and roughly how vigorous canes are by colour. How vigorous a cane really is (a bend test), what the pith looks like (only visible once cut), and the base and graft area (best seen close up) all need hands-on checking. A cane can look sound outside and still be compromised inside (AF-41, **Low**). General plant-diagnosis guidance also favours examining the plant over relying on a photo alone (AF-34, **Low**).

## 5. Decisions for the Founders

1. **How many canes to keep: two approved defaults disagree.** This ARC's C7 default says "four to six" (within the 3–8 range). `ARC-BUSHROSE-WEAKCONGESTED-01`'s C-2 default, already published in PKR-DEC-000005, says "roughly three to seven, fewer for a modest or young plant". Pip should say one thing. Options:
   - (a) Use **3–7** everywhere (already published).
   - (b) Use **4–6** everywhere, which means revising PKR-DEC-000005.
   - (c) Use 3–7 as the range and mention 4–6 as a typical middle.
2. **Grandiflora.** AF-37 (Moderate) says Grandifloras are pruned like Hybrid Teas. One commercial source suggests a gentler cutback. `ARC-BUSHROSE-ROSETYPE-01`'s R-4 default says to give no Grandiflora-specific guidance until stronger evidence exists. KIT proposes treating Grandifloras exactly as Hybrid Teas and leaving out the gentler-cutback nuance. That follows AF-37's main point and respects R-4.
   - Approve, or
   - include the nuance.
3. **Rootstock suckers (AF-8, Moderate).** Sources say to remove growth from below the graft. But the ARC records that how to tell a sucker from the rose's own new basal shoot "is not established", and new basal shoots are valuable (AF-10). KIT proposes **not** guiding sucker removal yet: Pip would risk a gardener removing a healthy new cane.
   - Hold it (Pip suggests experienced help if the gardener asks about suckers), or
   - commission ROC to research recognising suckers.

## 6. Founder Review Rendering (KIT OM Chapter 12)

**What these records are for:** after the problem canes are gone, Pip helps the gardener pick the strong canes to keep, spread evenly around an open centre, and then shorten them. Old grey canes come out in favour of younger ones.

**What evidence they rest on:**
- Open-centred shape (High). Order of work: remove first, then choose keepers, then shorten (High).
- Keep strong, generally younger canes (Moderate). Old wood looks grey and rough (Moderate).
- Keep a small number of canes, 3–8 across sources (Moderate). Shorten by about a third to a half, lower for Hybrid Teas (Moderate).
- Renew an old bush over several years (Low). A sickly, overgrown bush may be better replaced; Pip points to experienced help (Low).

**Preserved limitations:** no fixed height or cane age is given, because the sources disagree. A photo can't show a cane's inner health or the base properly, so the gardener checks by hand.

**Source checks:** 56 sources registered (28 from the dossier, plus the later-round sources, now with links). 52 confirmed, 2 found by title only (the sites blocked access), 1 not confirmed (a CSU Adams County table), and 1 not identified (a forum thread). Every affected finding rests on other sources.

**Decisions (29 Sep 2026):** 1(c), 2 (approved as proposed), 3 (research commissioned).
