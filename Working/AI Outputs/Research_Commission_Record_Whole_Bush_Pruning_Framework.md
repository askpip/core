## 3. Background

The Bush Rose V1 Architecture defines six supported observations (§5.2) and a journey in which Pip "may propose a plan from interpreted photographs, gardener-confirmed observations and approved rules" (§5.3). The approved journey states that Pip "creates a proposed pruning plan from Marie's confirmed observations and approved knowledge" (§6).

The knowledge defined so far is organised observation by observation. The PKR Standard describes a Decision Logic PKR as the rule for choices "for a given confirmed observation" (§3, §5.3). The Mother Information Library holds three Approved Research Compilations, on dead wood, dormancy and recent planting. None addresses how the observations combine on one plant, or how a cut is made.

The Founder's stated aim is that dead wood, rubbing, crossing and inward growth, and new and old growth, are considered together for the benefit of the bush as a whole. The Founder also expects that stems will often, but not always, fall into more than one category. That expectation is not a research finding, and the ROC shall not assume it (ROC Operations Manual §2.2). The Founder regards how a cut is made as a vital part of pruning.

The Pip Runtime Architecture requires Pip's guidance to come only from Founder-approved knowledge, applied deterministically (§3.1, §3.3). The PKR Standard requires that application code carry no plant-specific judgment (§9.1). Any whole-bush reasoning Pip uses must therefore first exist as evidenced, Founder-approved knowledge. This commission gathers the evidence that would allow the Founders to decide what such knowledge should contain. It does not design records, and it does not decide how the app works.

The approved dormancy, recently-planted and dead-wood findings apply within this commission as approved. The ROC shall not re-research them. Where a source appears to conflict with an approved finding, the ROC shall record the conflict and shall not treat it as overriding the approved finding.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

The ROC shall confirm and, where needed, subdivide these questions in its Research Plan (ROC Operations Manual §4.3). They state what the Founders need answered. They do not state expected answers.

**Q1 — Objectives.** What do recognised sources say pruning an established, dormant bush rose is meant to achieve, and how do they relate or rank those aims?

**Q2 — Categories of growth.** What categories of stem or growth do sources use when deciding what to keep or remove? For each, how do they describe it, including the age of wood (older and younger stems) and vigour? How do these categories correspond to the six supported observations, and which categories that sources use are not among the six?

**Q3 — Order of work.** Do sources describe an order in which categories of growth are dealt with? How consistent is that order between sources, and what reasons do they give for it?

**Q4 — Interactions.** How do sources treat a stem that fits more than one category, and how do they treat cases where one decision affects another? How do they resolve conflicts between categories, for example where a stem that could be removed also contributes to the plant's structure?

**Q5 — Structure and framework.** What overall structure do sources describe as the aim, and how do they say to identify the stems that carry it? What role do they give older and younger stems in keeping or renewing it?

**Q6 — Limits and restraint.** Do sources give limits on how much to remove at one time, or conditions that call for lighter pruning? Do they distinguish between the rose classes within the approved scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda and, provisionally, Grandiflora, per `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01`)?

**Q7 — Deferral and help.** In what circumstances do sources advise pausing, spreading work over more than one occasion, or seeking experienced help, when judged across the whole plant and not stem by stem?

**Q8 — What can be judged from photographs.** Which whole-plant judgements (for example overall shape and density) do sources say can be made from an overview photograph, and which require examining the physical rose?

**Q9 — Cut mechanics.** How do sources say a pruning cut should be made? This covers where the cut is placed in relation to a bud (distance, angle and the direction the bud faces), whether a stem is cut back to a bud, to a junction or to its base, the choice of tool for stems of different thickness, the sharpness and cleanliness of tools and how sources link them to the quality of the cut, and the treatment of cut ends and any remaining stub. Where do sources disagree, and what reasons do they give?

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Included

- The established bush rose defined in Architecture §5.1 and, for taxonomic scope, `FRD-BUSHROSE-SCOPE-01` (approved 9 September 2026).
- Dormant-season pruning of the whole plant, consistent with `PKR-SGT-000001` and `ARC-BUSHROSE-DORMANCY-01`.
- Findings at the level of the whole plant: objectives, categories of growth, order, interactions, structure, limits, deferral and photograph limits.
- Cut mechanics (Q9), including the placement of a cut on a stem and the tools used to make it.

### 5.2 Excluded

- Recognition of each individual observation in detail. The ROC records leads for the other commissions and shall not assess them here.
- Re-researching dead wood, dormancy or recent planting (§3), and the personal-protection content already approved in `ARC-BUSHROSE-DEADWOOD-01`.
- Rose types outside the approved scope, and pruning outside the dormant season.
- Any care beyond dormant pruning, including feeding, watering, pest or disease treatment, and planting.
- Cultivar identification, and any authorisation of a cut from an image alone (Architecture §5.5).
- Designing PKR record types, application behaviour or the pruning plan itself. Those follow Founder review of this commission's findings.
- Acquiring, requesting permission for or approving any image (§6, deliverable 4).

### 5.3 Known limitations

- Guidance on order, interaction, limits and cut mechanics is likely to appear mainly in practitioner publications, not in controlled studies, and sources may disagree. The ROC shall record disagreement and its likely causes (ROC Operations Manual §7.3).
- Some questions, especially Q4 and Q6, may have thin evidence or none. Where that is so, the ROC shall record an absence of evidence, and shall not fill it by assumption.
- Any rule of order or interaction that the ROC arrives at by its own reasoning, and that sources do not state, is not an Assessed Finding. It is recorded separately as the ROC's own synthesis and carried forward as a Recommendation for Further Research (ROC Operations Manual §8.4A; EAS §2.10).
- `Working/Project_Backlog.md` holds unverified leads on individual observations from a superseded Drive document. The ROC may use them as search prompts only, and shall not cite them as evidence.

### 5.4 Dependencies and follow-on work

- **Dependencies:** the three Approved Research Compilations (ARCs) in §1, and the recently-planted gate `PKR-SGT-000002`, which is written to govern the five observations not yet researched.
- **Follow-on work, not commissioned here:** the individual observation commissions; a Comparison Image PKR for each observation, which is procurement or photography work; and any revision of the PKR Standard or Architecture that this commission's findings lead the Founders to consider, including whether older and younger wood needs to be a supported observation in its own right, and how findings on cut mechanics are held and linked from each observation's Decision Logic.

## 6. Expected Deliverables

1. **A Research Plan** prepared under ROC Operations Manual Chapter 4, containing questions, information requirements, anticipated source types and known challenges. It shall not present findings (§4.7A).
2. **A Founder Review Dossier (FRD)** prepared under the FRDS, with the Evidence Confidence Level stated per Assessed Finding and never blended across the commission (FRDS §6.2; EAS). Each finding is stated at the level of the whole plant, or for Q9 at the level of the cut, and is identifiable to the question it answers. The ROC shall submit it as a single dossier, kept proportionate to the evidence (FRDS §4.3).
3. **A correspondence table** showing the categories of growth that sources use (Q2) against the six supported observations, including categories that correspond to none and observations that correspond to no source category. It reports what the sources say and makes no design decisions.
4. **An image-permissions register.** For every source the ROC relies on, it records whether the source carries photographs or illustrations, and what the source itself states about their reuse (a copyright notice, a licence, or a permissions contact), quoting the wording where it exists. Where the source states nothing, the register says "not stated". The register records what each source says and nothing more. It is not a legal assessment, and the ROC does not request permission, acquire or approve any image. It shows the Founders where matters stand for later sourcing of comparison images.
5. **A record of what was searched but not found**, with any absence of evidence stated as an absence and not as support for a conclusion (ROC Operations Manual §5.7).
6. **Recommendations for further research**, including any ROC synthesis routed there under §8.4A, and any lead for an individual observation commission.
