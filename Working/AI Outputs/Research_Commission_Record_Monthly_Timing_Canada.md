## 3. Background — the gap

Ask Pip records each rose's location (GPS, or town or city, region and country) and derives its hemisphere and current season. No approved ARC gives month-level timing for New Zealand, Australia, the United Kingdom or Canada, and the US months in approved ARCs are cut-offs for particular states, not a national pattern. Hemisphere alone cannot tell a mild garden from a cold one.

Canadian rose timing varies widely, from the mild south coast of British Columbia to the Prairies and the north. Canada has its own Plant Hardiness Zones (Natural Resources Canada), which differ from the USDA zones used in the United States, and Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses need winter protection across much of the country.

## 4. Commissioned Questions

1. **Q1 — Main winter pruning.** In which months do sources say to do the main pruning of an established Hybrid Tea or Floribunda in each climate band? What local signal do they tie it to (for example after the last hard frost, or when buds begin to swell)?
2. **Q2 — Feeding.** In which months do sources say to start feeding in spring, and when to give the last feed before winter, in each band?
3. **Q3 — Late-season cut-off.** When do sources say to stop deadheading and summer pruning before winter, in each band?
4. **Q4 — Planting and moving.** In which months do sources say to plant bare-root roses, plant potted roses, and move an established rose, in each band?
5. **Q5 — Winter clear-up and protection.** When do sources say to clear fallen leaves and prunings; where sources describe a winter spray, when it is done; and where sources describe winter protection (for example mounding soil over the base), when it goes on and comes off? Product names, brands and rates are not collected as findings.
6. **Q6 — Climate bands.** How do Canadian sources tie rose timing to Canada's Plant Hardiness Zones, to the USDA zones, to local frost dates, or to province? Which zone bands are needed to cover where Hybrid Tea and Floribunda roses are commonly grown, and what month windows do sources give for each band?
7. **Q7 — Placing the gardener.** Do Canadian sources say gardeners can readily find their hardiness zone, and whether it is the Canadian or the USDA one (for example from plant labels or the Natural Resources Canada map), so that Ask Pip could ask for it? If not, what would a beginner need to be asked?
8. **Q8 — Year-to-year variation.** How do sources handle a late or early season, or a garden near the edge of a band? Do they prefer local signals over fixed months?

## 5. Scope and Method

Established bush roses within the approved scope (Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, provisionally Grandiflora), plus planting new roses and moving established ones for Q4. Whether Pip gives planting and moving advice at all is decided separately under `FRD-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01`; this commission only gathers the months. Standard ROC method. Preferred sources: the Canadian Rose Society and local rose societies, provincial agriculture ministries, Natural Resources Canada (Plant Hardiness Zones), Landscape Ontario, and public botanic gardens (for example the Montreal Botanical Garden, the Royal Botanical Gardens in Ontario, UBC Botanical Garden and the University of Alberta Botanic Garden). Commercial Canadian rose growers allowed and labelled as commercial. Hardy shrub-rose series bred for Canada (for example Explorer and Parkland roses) are outside the approved bush-rose scope and are not researched, except where a source compares their timing with Hybrid Teas and Floribundas.

Every finding records which band it applies to. Months from one band are never applied to another, and northern-hemisphere months are never converted to southern-hemisphere ones by shifting six months. Every online source is recorded with its web address and access date (ROC OM §5.5) and carried into a Source Register (§12.6). Strictly what sources say; no ROC advice.
