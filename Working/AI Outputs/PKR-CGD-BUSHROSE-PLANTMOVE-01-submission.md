## 9. Wording Boundary Check (KIT Operations Manual §7.7)

- Each statement says the thing itself, in plain words a beginner can act on. None names a source.
- Where one source says something, the statement says "one source says". Kinds of source are named only where the kind is the point ("one garden centre", "two rose societies", "a UK rose nursery").
- "Bare-root", "dormant" and "bud union" are explained in the first statement of each answer that uses them, which every gardener is shown. They are not explained again in the statements beneath it. "Mycorrhizal fungi" is used as the source names it; no source explains it, so Pip does not.
- Ranges are given where sources differ (rule 3). Months appear only in statements for New Zealand and Australia, each with "Months are only a guide" (rule 6).
- Disagreements say that experts or sources disagree and what each side says. Gaps say "I couldn't find…" and point to a local rose society.
- No brand, no mixing amount and no product recipe appears. Gypsum, compost and mycorrhizal fungi are named as types, as the sources name them.
- Pip speaks in the first person. The text says "rose" or "roses", never "bush rose".
- Every statement says no more than its finding as corrected (`ARC-BUSHROSE-PLANTMOVE-01` §4).
