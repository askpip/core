## 1. What this package does

The six published Decision Logic PKRs (PKR-DEC-000001 to 000006) say *what* a gardener may choose. When the choice is Cut, they point here for *how*. Three records are built:

| PKR | Mode | Covers | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| PKR-CGD-000001 | Technique | Choosing, sharpening and cleaning pruning tools | Once, before the first cut of a session |
| PKR-CGD-000002 | Technique | Making the cut: removing a whole stem, or shortening one back to a bud; sealing larger cuts | Each time the gardener chooses Cut |
| PKR-CGD-000003 | Advisory | Not removing more than about a third of the bush in one session | Before the first cut, then after every third cut (Founder decision D2, confirmed 29 September 2026) |

None of the three adds, removes or changes a choice, passes or stops a gate, or diagnoses anything (PKR Standard v0.10 §5.7).

AF-31: 000109, 000123, 000124. AF-35: 000005, 000013, 000080, 000125, 000126. AF-38: 000001, 000059, 000102 |
| Founder Approval Date | 29 September 2026 |
| Related PKRs | Applies to the Cut choice of PKR-DEC-000001 to 000006; related to PKR-CGD-000001 and PKR-CGD-000003 |
| Preserved Uncertainty or Limitations | **Distance above the bud (C2):** most sources say about a quarter inch; a few say up to half an inch. Both are defensible answers to the same tradeoff (too close risks killing the bud; too far risks the stub dying back). All the quarter-inch sources are from the United States. **Slant direction (C3):** most sources say "away from the bud"; a few say "in the same direction as the bud", and it's unresolved whether that's the same technique. **Whether angle and bud direction matter at all (C11):** rose societies in South Australia and South Africa say they matter little, or recommend a straight cut; neither gives a reason. The approved default keeps the majority convention. **Sealing (C4, AF-30, AF-35):** most sources, including every UK and New Zealand source, don't mention it. Where rose sources recommend it, the reason is to keep cane-boring insects out of the soft pith, not to help the cut heal. General tree research finds that wound dressings don't help healing, which answers a different question. No trial of sealed against unsealed rose canes was found. **Stubs (AF-29):** five of the nine sources are about dead canes, so whether the rule applies equally to every kind of removal isn't directly addressed. **Two kinds of cut (AF-31):** only one of the three sources is rose-specific. **Outward bud (AF-21):** two sources allow an exception for a cane that already spreads sideways. |
| Evidence Confidence | See per-claim confidence |

- **Care Topic:** How to make the cut once the gardener has chosen Cut.
- **Guidance Mode:** Technique.
- **Presentation Points:** Part A, when the gardener chooses a Cut that removes a whole stem (for example, under PKR-DEC-000002 to 000005, or removing a non-framework cane under PKR-DEC-000006). Part B, when the gardener chooses a Cut that shortens a stem back to a bud (for example, shortening a kept cane under PKR-DEC-000006). Part C, with either, when the cut is roughly pencil-thickness or more. Which kind of cut a choice is comes from the Decision Logic record's own wording, not from this record. For dead wood, PKR-DEC-000001's own rule (cut back into living tissue) governs where the cut goes. Only Part A's "no stubs" item and Part C apply there.
- **Scope of Presentation:** As Applies To.

## 7. Founder Review Rendering (plain summary)

When the gardener chooses their first Cut of the session, Pip first explains tools:

- Use bypass hand pruners for thin canes, and loppers or a saw for anything over about three-quarters of an inch.
- Keep tools sharp and clean.
- Wipe blades with 70% alcohol between plants.

Pip shows the one-third advisory at the same moment, labelled as general and not rose-specific. It shows it again after every third cut.

With each cut, Pip explains how to make it:

- **Removing a stem:** cut it where it starts and leave no stub.
- **Shortening a stem:** cut about a quarter inch above an outward-facing bud, slanted about 45° away from the bud. Pip notes that a few Southern Hemisphere societies think the angle matters little.
- **Pencil-thick or larger cuts:** these can be sealed against cane borers. Pip explains this isn't about healing.

Each statement carries its own confidence and sources. Nothing here changes what the gardener may choose.
