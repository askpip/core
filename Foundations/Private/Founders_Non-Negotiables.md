## Introduction

Success has a way of testing convictions.

Many companies begin with clear values but gradually compromise them in pursuit of growth, convenience or profit.

This document exists to remind us that some principles are more valuable than success itself.

These commitments are not made because they are always easy.

They are made because we believe they are right.

Whenever we face difficult decisions, we will return to these principles before choosing a course of action.

## 1. We Will Honour God Above Profit

The purpose of this company is not merely to generate wealth.

Profit is necessary for sustainability and growth, but it must never become our master.

If we are ever forced to choose between faithfulness and financial gain, we choose faithfulness.

## 2. We Will Never Knowingly Compromise Truth

We will never knowingly exaggerate the capabilities of our products.

We will not manipulate evidence.

We will not knowingly provide advice that we believe to be false.

If we discover an error, we will correct it promptly and honestly.

Truth is more valuable than reputation.

## 3. We Will Protect User Trust

Every recommendation we make has the potential to influence real decisions affecting living plants.

Trust must therefore be earned continually.

We will not pretend certainty where certainty does not exist.

Where confidence is low, we will ask for more information rather than risk giving harmful advice.

## 4. We Will Respect the Privacy of Those We Serve

People entrust us with photographs, information and confidence.

We will treat that trust as a responsibility.

We will collect only information genuinely needed to provide our service.

We will seek to give users meaningful control over their information.

We will not knowingly misuse, exploit or sell personal information entrusted to us.

## 5. We Will Remember That AI Is a Tool

Artificial intelligence is a servant.

It is not our authority.

It may assist observation, organisation and communication.

Final responsibility for the integrity of this platform always rests with people.

We remain accountable for what we build.

## 6. We Will Separate Knowledge from Technology

Technology will continue to change.

Knowledge will continue to grow.

Our systems should always be designed so that improvements in one strengthen the other without creating unnecessary dependence.

## 7. We Will Build for Future Generations

Short-term success must never undermine long-term usefulness.

Every major decision should leave the platform stronger, clearer and more trustworthy than before.

## 8. We Will Welcome Correction

We recognise that we do not possess perfect knowledge.

We will listen carefully to gardeners, horticultural experts, researchers and thoughtful users.

When evidence demonstrates a better way, we will have the humility to improve.

## 9. We Will Treat Others with Fairness and Grace

Every customer, employee, contractor, supplier and partner deserves honesty, respect and kindness.

We will seek to resolve disagreements honourably.

We will pursue excellence without sacrificing compassion.

## 10. We Will Remain Good Stewards

If this company prospers, we will remember that success brings greater responsibility.

We desire to steward our time, abilities, finances and influence wisely.

We will seek opportunities to create genuine value and contribute positively to the communities we serve.

## 11. We Will Guard the Purpose of This Company

This platform exists to help people understand and care for the living world more wisely.

Every significant new feature, partnership, investment or opportunity should be tested against that purpose.

If something strengthens the mission, we will consider it.

If something weakens or distracts from the mission, we will decline it, regardless of how attractive it may appear.

## Decision Test

Before making any significant decision, we will ask:

Does this honour God?
Is it truthful?
Does it serve people well?
Does it protect user trust?
Does it reflect good stewardship?
Would we be comfortable explaining this decision publicly?
Would we still be proud of this decision ten years from now?
Would we make the same decision if money were not the deciding factor?

If we cannot answer these questions with integrity, we will pause, seek wisdom, and reconsider.

## Closing Commitment

We understand that we will not always make perfect decisions.

When we fail, we commit ourselves to humility, repentance where appropriate, correction, and renewed faithfulness.

May this document continually remind us that character is more valuable than reputation, integrity more valuable than profit, and faithful stewardship more valuable than success.

May the work of our hands always reflect the principles upon which it was founded.
