# **Chapter 1 — Meeting Sarah's Rose**

Marie had never planned to grow roses.

One afternoon, her sister arrived with a beautiful bush rose as a gift.

"I thought this would look lovely in your garden."

Together, they chose a sunny spot and planted it.

Over the following days, Marie found herself checking on the rose each time she walked through the garden.

The plant had quickly become more than just another shrub.

It reminded her of her sister.

Although she enjoyed gardening, she realised she knew very little about growing roses. She wanted to care for it properly but wasn't sure where to begin.

A friend suggested she try PIP.

"A gardening companion that helps you understand your plants."

Marie downloaded the app.

The splash screen appeared.

---

**PIP**

*Growing understanding. Cultivating confidence.*

**Begin**

---

Marie tapped **Begin**.

The Home screen opened.

---

**Home**

**How can I help you today?**

*Ask anything about your garden...*

🌱 Add a plant

🌹 Check on one of my plants

📖 Open my Garden Journal

📷 Identify a plant

---

Marie selected **Add a plant**.

PIP welcomed her with a simple message.

*"Let's get to know your new plant."*

It asked Marie to take a photograph of the whole rose.

She did.

Next, PIP asked:

*"Does your plant still have its nursery label attached?"*

Marie looked.

It did.

*"Great. Could you photograph both sides of the label? Nursery labels often contain the official cultivar name and other useful information about your plant."*

Marie photographed both sides.

PIP read the label.

It identified the official cultivar and added it to the Garden Journal, together with the information printed on the nursery label.

PIP then asked:

*"What would you like to call this rose in your garden?"*

Marie typed:

**Sarah's Rose**

PIP replied:

*"Perfect. I'll know this rose as Sarah's Rose while also remembering its official cultivar."*

It then asked one final optional question.

*"Would you like to record why this plant is special to you?"*

Marie wrote:

*"My sister gave me this rose at the beginning of spring. I'd like to look after it well and enjoy it for many years."*

Next, PIP asked Marie to take several photographs of Sarah's Rose to add to the Garden Journal—one from each side, one from the rear, and one looking down from above.

When Marie had uploaded the photographs, PIP added them to the Garden Journal and began analysing them.

A few moments later, PIP finished its analysis.

*"I've finished getting to know Sarah's Rose."*

*"This is an Iceberg Floribunda rose."*

**Confidence: High**

PIP then introduced the rose.

*"Your rose appears healthy and has settled in well after planting."*

*"Over the next few weeks, you should expect to see fresh shoots followed by flower buds."*

*"The most common things to watch for at this stage are aphids on new growth and black spot developing on the leaves."*

Finally, PIP added:

*"Sarah's Rose won't need pruning just yet. Bush roses are normally assessed for pruning during winter, while they are dormant."*

Then it asked:

*"Would you like me to remind you when it's time to assess Sarah's Rose for pruning?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

PIP replied:

*"Certainly. When pruning season approaches, I'll remind you, and we'll assess Sarah's Rose together."*

The Garden Journal was created.

It now contained:

- Sarah's chosen name

- Official cultivar name

- Plant photographs

- Nursery label photographs

- Personal story

- Date added

- Initial health assessment

- Future pruning reminder

Marie closed the app.

She hadn't simply added a plant.

She had learned what kind of rose she owned, what to expect over the coming months, what problems to watch for, and that PIP would continue helping her care for it in the future.



# **Chapter 2 — Something Didn't Look Right**

Several weeks passed.

Sarah's Rose was growing well.

Most mornings, Marie spent a few minutes wandering through her garden before leaving for work. She had started noticing little changes that she would probably have overlooked before downloading PIP.

One Saturday morning, something caught her attention.

Several leaves had small black spots.

Some leaves looked perfectly healthy.

Others didn't.

Marie remembered that when she first added Sarah's Rose, PIP had mentioned black spot as one of the things to watch for.

She walked back inside, picked up her phone and opened PIP.

The splash screen appeared briefly before the Home screen opened.

---

**Home**

**How can I help you today?**

*Ask anything about your garden...*

🌱 Add a plant

🌹 Check on one of my plants

📖 Open my Garden Journal

📷 Identify a plant

---

Rather than selecting a menu option, Marie tapped into the conversation box and typed:

*"I've noticed black spots on the leaves of Sarah's Rose."*

PIP immediately replied.

*"Let's have a look together."*

*"Could you take a few photographs of the affected leaves? If possible, include one photograph of the whole plant as well."*

Marie took the photographs.

Within a few moments, PIP had analysed them.

*"Thank you. I can see the spotting you're referring to. Before I explain what I think is happening, I'd like to ask a few questions."*

Marie answered them.

*"When did you first notice the spots?"*

**Today.**

*"Have you noticed them on many leaves or only a few?"*

**Only a few.**

*"Has there been much rain recently?"*

**Yes.**

PIP considered both the photographs and Marie's answers.

It then explained its assessment.

*"Based on what I can currently see, the spotting is most consistent with the early stages of black spot."*

**Confidence: Moderate**

PIP continued.

*"My confidence isn't higher because several different conditions can produce similar markings. At this stage, I can't be completely certain based on today's evidence alone."*

It highlighted the affected leaves in the photographs and explained the features that had influenced its assessment.

It also explained what evidence would increase—or decrease—its confidence over the coming days.

Rather than immediately recommending treatment, PIP explained what Marie should continue observing.

*"If these spots increase in number, begin to develop yellowing around them, or appear on new leaves, that will help confirm today's assessment."*

Marie now understood not only what PIP believed was happening, but also why.

Before finishing, PIP said:

*"A follow-up observation in three days would help us see how the spotting changes."*

*"Would you like me to remind you?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

PIP replied:

*"Done."*

*"In three days, I'll remind you to check Sarah's Rose. We'll compare the new photographs with today's observations so we can see what has changed before deciding whether any action is needed."*

Finally, PIP asked whether Marie would like today's observations added to Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal.

She selected **Yes**.

The Garden Journal now contained:

- Today's photographs

- Marie's observations

- The additional questions and answers

- PIP's assessment

- Confidence level

- Annotated photographs highlighting the affected leaves

- The scheduled follow-up reminder

Marie closed the app.

She felt reassured.

PIP hadn't rushed to a conclusion or suggested unnecessary treatment.

Instead, it had explained what it knew, what it didn't yet know, and how continuing to observe Sarah's Rose would lead to a better decision.

Marie felt more confident because she now understood both her rose and the reasoning behind PIP's advice.

A message from PIP appeared.

---

**Sarah's Rose is ready for another look.**

*Three days ago, we noticed early leaf spotting.*

*Let's compare today's observations with the previous ones and see how things are progressing.*

---

Marie tapped the reminder.

PIP opened directly to Sarah's Rose.

There was no splash screen.

There was no Home screen.

PIP already knew why Marie had returned.

The conversation simply continued.

---

**Welcome back, Marie.**

*Three days ago, we observed early leaf spotting on Sarah's Rose.*

*Today we'll compare those observations with what we can see now.*

*When you're ready, please take:*

- one photograph of the whole plant;

- one close-up photograph of the affected leaves; and

- one photograph of any new growth.

**Start Comparison**

---

Marie took the photographs.

Within a few moments, PIP displayed today's photographs alongside those taken three days earlier.

The affected areas were highlighted.

---

## **Comparison Summary**

**Changes since the previous observation**

✓ Two additional leaves now show spotting.

✓ Existing spots have increased slightly in size.

✓ New growth remains healthy.

✓ No significant leaf loss is visible.

---

PIP explained its findings.

*"Thank you, Marie."*

*"Compared with our previous observations, the pattern of change is now much more consistent with black spot."*

It continued.

*"My confidence has increased from Moderate to High because the disease has progressed in the way we would normally expect over the past three days."*

Marie now understood why PIP was more confident than it had been during the previous visit.

Because sufficient evidence had now been collected, PIP moved from observation to treatment.

---

## **Treatment Options**

PIP explained that there were several acceptable ways of managing black spot.

It presented each option together with:

- how it works;

- its advantages;

- its disadvantages; and

- the circumstances in which it may—or may not—be appropriate.

Marie spent a few minutes reading through the information.

She decided to:

- remove the affected leaves;

- clean up any infected leaves beneath the plant; and

- apply an organic fungicide.

PIP then asked:

*"Would you like me to record these as the treatment you've chosen for Sarah's Rose?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

PIP confirmed:

*"Thank you. I'll use this information when we review Sarah's Rose so I can better understand how the treatment is working."*

It then asked one final question.

*"Were you able to complete these treatments today?"*

Marie replied:

**Yes.**

PIP recorded both:

- the treatment Marie had chosen; and

- that it had been completed.

It then explained why this was important.

*"Knowing which treatment you've used helps me interpret future changes more accurately. Different treatments can produce different outcomes and timeframes."*

PIP then created a Care Plan.

---

## **Care Plan**

**Issue**

Black spot

**Treatment Selected**

- Remove affected leaves

- Remove fallen infected leaves

- Organic fungicide applied

**Treatment Status**

Completed

**Current Confidence**

High

**Objective**

Confirm that the disease has stopped progressing and that healthy new growth continues.

**Next Review**

Seven days

---

PIP asked:

*"Would you like me to remind you when it's time to review Sarah's Rose?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

PIP replied:

*"Certainly."*

*"Next week we'll compare Sarah's Rose with today's observations to see how it has responded to the treatment you've chosen. If necessary, we'll adjust our recommendations based on what we find."*

Before finishing, PIP asked whether today's interaction should be added to Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal.

Marie selected **Yes**.

The Garden Journal now recorded:

- the reminder visit;

- the comparison with previous observations;

- the updated confidence level;

- the treatment options presented;

- the treatment Marie had selected;

- confirmation that the treatment had been completed;

- creation of the Care Plan; and

- the scheduled review.

Marie closed the app.

She realised that PIP wasn't simply keeping a record of Sarah's Rose.

It was keeping track of what had happened, what decisions had been made, and what should happen next.

Rather than giving advice and disappearing, PIP was helping her work towards a successful outcome.

For the first time, Marie felt she had a knowledgeable gardening companion that genuinely cared about the long-term health of her rose.

Over the following two weeks, Marie completed the Care Plan with PIP's assistance.

Each review built upon the previous observations.

The spread of the disease gradually stopped, healthy new growth continued, and no further intervention was required.

PIP marked the Care Plan as **Completed** and recorded the outcome in Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal.

Marie could now look back over the entire event—from the first suspicious spots to the successful recovery—and understand not only what had happened, but why each decision had been made.

# **Chapter 4 — Understanding Before Pruning**

Several months passed.

Sarah's Rose recovered well from the earlier outbreak of black spot, and PIP closed the Care Plan after confirming that the disease had stopped progressing.

As spring turned to summer and then autumn, PIP quietly supported Marie as she cared for Sarah's Rose.

It reminded her when the rose entered new stages of growth, explained why regular deadheading encouraged repeat flowering, and showed her how to remove spent blooms correctly.

From time to time, Marie added photographs and observations to Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal, gradually building a richer history of the plant.

Most of these interactions were brief.

Some lasted only a few minutes.

Others simply reassured Marie that Sarah's Rose was healthy and developing as expected.

By the beginning of winter, PIP had built an evidence-based understanding of Sarah's Rose. It knew the cultivar, how vigorously it had grown, how it had responded to the earlier black spot infection, the treatments Marie had chosen, and the observations she had recorded throughout the season.

Marie had changed as well.

Instead of worrying whenever she noticed something different, she now looked more carefully at the plant herself. She recognised healthy new growth, understood why spent blooms should be removed, and had begun to notice how the framework of the rose slowly developed over time.

She no longer felt like someone who simply owned a rose.

She felt like someone who was learning to understand it.

One crisp June morning, Marie received another message from PIP.

---

**Sarah's Rose has now entered its dormant season.**

*Winter is the ideal time to understand your rose's structure before deciding what, if anything, should be pruned.*

*Would you like to assess Sarah's Rose together?*

---

Marie smiled.

When she had first installed PIP, the thought of pruning her rose had been intimidating. She had worried about removing the wrong branch or damaging the plant.

Now she felt curious rather than anxious.

She picked up her phone and walked out into the garden.

---

## **Understanding the Rose**

PIP began by explaining that pruning was not simply about removing branches.

It was about understanding how the rose grew and helping it develop into a healthier, more balanced plant.

Before recommending a single cut, PIP asked Marie to photograph Sarah's Rose from several different angles.

It explained that seeing the whole plant allowed it to understand the framework better and provide more reliable guidance.

Marie slowly walked around the rose, taking photographs from the front, both sides, the rear and above.

Once the photographs had been analysed, PIP presented an annotated image of Sarah's Rose.

The main framework canes were highlighted.

Basal shoots were identified.

Older canes were distinguished from newer growth where possible.

Areas that could not be assessed confidently were clearly marked.

Rather than hiding uncertainty, PIP explained it openly.

*"I can't clearly see the base of this cane from the current photographs."*

*"One additional photograph from this angle would help me assess it more accurately."*

Marie took the additional photograph.

The uncertainty disappeared.

---

## **Learning the Language of the Rose**

Instead of immediately discussing pruning, PIP introduced Marie to the important parts of her rose.

As she tapped on the highlighted structures, PIP explained:

- what each structure was;

- why it existed;

- how it contributed to the long-term health of the rose; and

- how it influenced future pruning decisions.

Marie discovered that some thick canes formed the permanent framework of the rose.

Others were vigorous new basal shoots that might become tomorrow's framework.

She learned that some smaller stems were unlikely to contribute significantly to the future structure of the plant.

For the first time, the rose began to make sense.

Instead of seeing a tangled collection of branches, Marie began to see an organised, living structure.

---

## **Building Confidence**

PIP explained that experienced gardeners did not begin pruning by asking,

*"What should I cut?"*

They began by asking,

*"What am I looking at?"*

Marie smiled.

Only a few months earlier, she would have wanted PIP simply to tell her where to cut.

Now she found herself studying the framework before thinking about pruning.

She realised that understanding the rose felt just as satisfying as caring for it.

PIP concluded the session.

*"Today we've identified the structure of Sarah's Rose and completed a structural assessment."*

*"At our next session, we'll use this understanding to develop a pruning plan together, explaining every recommended cut and the reason behind it."*

Marie saved the structural assessment to Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal.

As she walked back towards the house, she glanced once more at the rose.

For the first time, she wasn't seeing a confusing tangle of branches.

She could recognise the framework, identify the newer growth, and understand how the plant had developed over time.

She smiled.

She wasn't ready to prune because PIP had told her she was.

She was ready because she finally understood what she was looking at.



# **Chapter 5 — Making the First Cut**

The following Saturday morning, Marie made herself a coffee before heading into the garden.

She picked up her gloves, secateurs and phone.

Today felt different.

When she had first installed PIP, pruning had seemed intimidating. She had worried that every cut might be the wrong one.

Now she felt prepared.

Not because she had memorised instructions, but because she understood the structure of Sarah's Rose.

She opened PIP.

---

**Welcome back, Marie.**

*Last time we explored the structure of Sarah's Rose and prepared a structural assessment.*

*Today we'll use that assessment to create a pruning plan.*

*We'll review every recommended cut together before you begin.*

---

Marie selected **Begin Pruning**.

PIP displayed the annotated image of Sarah's Rose from the previous session.

One branch was highlighted.

---

## **Recommendation 1**

**Remove this dead cane.**

PIP explained:

*"This cane is no longer alive and cannot produce healthy growth or flowers. Removing it improves the health of the plant and creates space for healthy new growth."*

The highlighted branch was shown on both the annotated image and the original photograph.

Marie could see exactly which branch PIP was referring to.

She looked at the actual rose.

Then back at her phone.

For the first time, she recognised the branch without hesitation.

She made the cut.

PIP asked:

*"Was this cut completed?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

The pruning record was updated.

The next recommendation appeared.

---

## **Recommendation 2**

**Remove this inward-growing stem.**

Marie hesitated.

She tapped **Why?**

PIP explained:

*"This stem is growing towards the centre of the plant. As it develops, it is likely to reduce airflow and increase congestion within the framework. Removing it encourages a more open structure, improving both plant health and future flowering."*

Marie looked again.

This time she understood.

She completed the cut.

Over the next twenty minutes, Marie worked through the remaining recommendations.

Some cuts were straightforward.

Others prompted questions.

Whenever she was uncertain, she simply asked.

PIP answered each question in plain language, explaining the reasoning rather than simply repeating the recommendation.

Where more than one pruning approach was horticulturally acceptable, PIP explained the alternatives.

It never suggested there was only one correct answer.

As each cut was completed, Marie marked it as finished.

PIP updated the pruning record and adjusted the remaining plan accordingly.

---

## **Pruning Complete**

When the final recommendation had been completed, PIP congratulated Marie.

*"Well done."*

*"Sarah's Rose has now completed its winter pruning."*

*"Based on today's assessment, the rose now has a balanced framework with good spacing and strong potential for healthy spring growth."*

PIP then asked Marie to photograph the completed rose.

The new photographs were automatically displayed alongside those taken before pruning.

Marie could clearly see the difference.

The framework looked cleaner, more open and easier to understand.

PIP saved the complete pruning record to Sarah's Rose's Garden Journal.

The journal now contained:

- the structural assessment;

- the pruning plan;

- every completed pruning decision;

- before-and-after photographs;

- Marie's questions;

- PIP's explanations; and

- the completed pruning session.

Finally, PIP created a new Care Plan.

---

## **Care Plan**

**Issue**

Winter Pruning

**Status**

Completed

**Next Review**

Early Spring

**Objective**

Observe how Sarah's Rose responds to the pruning and confirm that healthy new growth develops as expected.

---

PIP explained:

*"Today's pruning is only the beginning."*

*"The real measure of successful pruning will be how Sarah's Rose responds in the months ahead. We'll review the new growth together in spring and continue learning from this season's results."*

Marie scheduled the follow-up.

Before putting her phone away, she looked once more at Sarah's Rose.

Only a few months earlier, she would have stood there wondering which branches to cut.

Today she understood why every cut had been made.

She realised that PIP had done something far more valuable than simply showing her where to prune.

It had taught her how to think like a gardener.

Part of her life.

She wondered just how much Sarah's Rose had changed over those months.

# **Chapter 7 — Asking Questions**

Spring had finally arrived.

Each morning, Sarah's Rose seemed just a little different.

The tiny buds Marie had watched swelling during late winter had unfolded into fresh green leaves, and vigorous new shoots were appearing throughout the rose.

Marie found herself looking forward to visiting the garden before leaving for work and again when she returned home.

It had become part of her daily routine.

One morning, she noticed something she hadn't expected.

Several of the newest shoots were a rich crimson red.

She bent down for a closer look.

The leaves looked healthy.

The stems appeared strong.

Nothing seemed damaged.

Still, she found herself wondering whether the unusual colour was normal.

Rather than searching the internet and sorting through hundreds of conflicting opinions, Marie opened PIP.

She selected **Sarah's Rose**.

The familiar greeting appeared.

---

**How can I help you today?**

---

She typed:

*"Why are the new shoots on Sarah's Rose red?"*

Within moments, PIP replied.

*"That's a great observation. Before I answer, I'd like to understand exactly what you're seeing."*

*"Are all of the new shoots red, or only the youngest ones?"*

Marie looked back at the rose.

**Only the youngest ones.**

PIP continued.

*"Do the leaves appear healthy, and are they opening normally?"*

Marie smiled.

**Yes.**

PIP asked one final question.

*"Could you please take a close-up photograph of one of the new shoots?"*

Marie took the photograph and uploaded it.

After analysing the image together with her answers, PIP responded.

*"Thank you. Based on your photograph and your observations, I'm highly confident that what you're seeing is normal spring growth."*

PIP explained that many bush roses produced reddish new shoots because the young leaves contained natural pigments that helped protect the tender growth while it developed.

As the leaves matured, chlorophyll would gradually become more dominant, and the foliage would turn green.

Marie looked back at Sarah's Rose.

What had seemed unusual only moments earlier suddenly made perfect sense.

She smiled.

Not because PIP had simply given her an answer.

But because she now understood what she was seeing.

Curious to learn more, she asked another question.

*"Do all bush roses produce red new shoots?"*

PIP replied.

*"Not all of them. Some cultivars produce deep red new growth, while others produce bronze, copper or light green shoots. The colour depends on the genetics of the individual cultivar and can also vary slightly with growing conditions."*

Marie nodded.

She was beginning to recognise that every rose had its own characteristics.

PIP continued.

*"Questions like yours help build a better understanding of Sarah's Rose over time."*

*"Would you like me to add today's question and explanation to the Garden Journal?"*

Marie selected **Yes**.

PIP recorded:

- Marie's question;

- the supporting photographs;

- the explanation provided;

- the confidence level; and

- the seasonal observation about Sarah's Rose's new growth.

Before finishing, PIP added:

*"The more we learn about Sarah's Rose together, the more complete its story becomes."*

Marie closed the app.

As she looked once more at the new shoots, she realised she had almost forgotten how often she used to search the internet whenever she noticed something unfamiliar in the garden.

Most searches had left her with dozens of conflicting answers and no real confidence about which one applied to her own plant.

Now the experience felt completely different.

Instead of searching for generic information, she was learning about **Sarah's Rose**.

Each question built upon everything PIP already knew about the plant.

Each answer became part of its continuing history.

Marie smiled.

She hadn't opened PIP because something was wrong.

She had opened it simply because she wanted to understand a little more about the plant growing in her own garden.

# **Chapter 8 — Growing Understanding**

Almost a year had passed since Sarah had arrived with a small potted bush rose and placed it into Marie's hands.

So much had changed.

The rose that had once been little more than a carefully labelled nursery plant had become a beautiful feature in Marie's garden.

The framework was balanced.

Healthy green foliage filled the plant.

New flowering stems carried beautiful blooms that gently swayed in the spring breeze.

Standing beside it, Marie found herself smiling.

She no longer saw a collection of branches and flowers.

She understood the story behind them.

Every cane had a purpose.

Every season had brought its own lessons.

Every photograph, every question, every pruning cut and every Care Plan had become part of Sarah's Rose's journey.

Later that afternoon, Sarah came to visit.

As they walked through the garden, Sarah stopped in front of the rose.

She looked at it for several moments before turning to Marie.

*"I can't believe that's the same rose."*

Marie laughed.

*"Neither can I sometimes."*

Sarah stepped closer.

*"It's beautiful."*

Marie smiled.

*"It really is."*

*"It's so open compared with when I bought it."*

Marie nodded.

*"I know why now."*

Sarah looked at her curiously.

*"You know why?"*

Marie smiled.

*"A year ago I would have said I had no idea."*

*"But now I understand what the framework is doing."*

She pointed to one of the main canes.

*"I know why this cane is important."*

Then she pointed to another.

*"I know why that one needed to be removed."*

She smiled.

*"I even know why new shoots appeared where they did."*

Sarah looked surprised.

*"You've become quite the gardener."*

Marie shook her head.

*"I'm still learning."*

*"But now I understand what I'm looking at."*

*"What changed?"*
