**Revision Note:** Version 0.5 retires the **Founder Executive Brief (FEB)** at Founder request, marking §8.2 retired rather than removing it. The FEB's stated purpose — efficient Founder review at volume, without requiring the complete research record to be examined every time — describes a need the platform does not yet have: with a single Founder reviewing every research commission directly and carefully, a separate summary document duplicates effort rather than saving it. The Founder Review Dossier (§8.1), prepared under the new Founder Review Dossier Standard, is now the sole Founder Review Documentation. The FEB may be reinstated by future Founder decision if review volume grows to require it. Version 0.4 retires **Approved Information Compilation (AIC)** and replaces it with **Approved Research Compilation (ARC)** as the name for the component defined at §7.3, at Founder request. Version 0.3 corrects three residual uses of "knowledge" in §6.2, §7.1 and §7.4 to "information" where each describes MIL content, completing the distinction Version 0.2 established: the MIL holds approved information, and "knowledge" or "operational intelligence" is reserved for the LIL/PKR side. Version 0.2 retires **Knowledge Asset (KA)** and replaces it with **Approved Information Compilation (AIC)** as the name for the governed record ROC creates and holds within the MIL (see §7.3). The change resolves two problems with the prior terminology: it no longer overlaps with the Founder Review Dossier (which names the pre-approval submission, not the archived record), and it no longer calls MIL content "knowledge" — the Mother Information Library Standard reserves that word for the LIL/PKR side, treating MIL content as approved information rather than operational intelligence.

## 1. Purpose

This Standard establishes the official names, identities and naming conventions used throughout the Plant Intelligence Platform (PIP).

Its purpose is to ensure that public brands, system components, roles, documents, Skills and knowledge assets are named clearly, consistently and without unnecessary repetition.

The Standard also preserves the distinction between:

* public-facing brands;
* platform components;
* internal systems;
* specialist roles;
* governed knowledge assets; and
* reusable intellectual property.

## 2. Scope

This Standard applies to:

* public brand names;
* product and application names;
* platform and system names;
* PIP CORE assets;
* Knowledge Curation System documents and components;
* PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System documents;
* role titles;
* Standards;
* Charters;
* Operations Manuals;
* Skills;
* Context documents;
* knowledge assets;
* filenames;
* acronyms; and
* other controlled terminology used within PIP.

This Standard governs naming and identity.

It does not govern:

* visual brand design;
* trademarks;
* legal ownership;
* marketing strategy;
* repository lifecycle procedures;
* internal document-creation procedures; or
* the operational responsibilities of individual systems or roles.

Those subjects shall be governed by their applicable approved documents.

## 3. Naming Principles

### 3.1 Clarity

A name shall make the identity or function of its subject understandable without unnecessary explanation.

Names shall distinguish public-facing products from internal systems and specialist roles.

### 3.2 Simplicity

Names shall remain as short and practical as their purpose permits.

System or brand prefixes shall not be repeated where the document’s location, metadata or established context already identifies its parent system.

### 3.3 Architectural Position

Names may identify a component’s position within PIP where that position is material to understanding its role.

Examples include:

* PIP CORE;
* PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System;
* Knowledge Curation System; and
* KCS Workspace.

### 3.4 Portability

The name of a reusable system, process, role or asset shall not be unnecessarily restricted to one application where its purpose is broader.

A component may operate as part of PIP while remaining capable of adaptation or reuse in another knowledge domain.

### 3.5 Consistency

Once a name has been approved, it shall be used consistently across:

* document titles;
* document content;
* metadata;
* filenames;
* diagrams;
* system interfaces; and
* related references.

A spelling or naming variation shall not be introduced without Founder approval.

### 3.6 No Unnecessary Branding

Not every internal document, role or asset requires a PIP prefix.

Branding shall be used where it establishes identity or ownership meaningfully. It shall not be added merely to make a title appear official.

## 4. Established Public Identities

### 4.1 Ask Pip

**Ask Pip** is the official public-facing brand and product name.

Ask Pip may identify:

* the gardener application;
* the public website;
* marketing channels;
* training and support services;
* public information;
* customer funnels; and
* other public-facing services approved by the Founders.

The official displayed spelling is:

**Ask Pip**

The domain and online identifier may appear without spaces:

`askpip.garden`

The compact domain form does not replace the official displayed brand spelling.

### 4.2 Pip

**Pip** is the official character, companion, narrator, educator and public face of Ask Pip.

Pip is a small, friendly, wise, honest and educated seed character.

Pip communicates platform knowledge, guides user interactions and may appear in:

* the Ask Pip application;
* voice and narration;
* educational material;
* marketing;
* books;
* games;
* video;
* social media; and
* other Founder-approved uses.

Pip has no defined gender. Gender is not relevant to Pip’s identity or function.

In normal prose, the character shall be written:

**Pip**

The stylised form **PIP** may be used in approved visual branding where it intentionally connects Pip with the Plant Intelligence Platform.

### 4.3 PIP

**PIP** stands for:

**Plant Intelligence Platform**

PIP is the complete parent platform.

It comprises the systems, technology, knowledge, governance, roles and public interfaces working together to deliver trusted plant intelligence.

PIP includes:

* Ask Pip;
* Pip;
* PIP CORE;
* the Knowledge Curation System;
* the PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System;
* platform software;
* knowledge libraries;
* image analysis;
* knowledge retrieval;
* plant and garden records;
* user interaction systems; and
* related operational processes.

PIP is not limited to the public application or to the technical software engine.

## 5. Established Internal Systems

### 5.1 PIP CORE

**PIP CORE** is the official name of the authoritative operational centre and repository of PIP.

CORE is a proper system name. It is not currently an acronym and shall not be given an invented expansion.

PIP CORE preserves and provides controlled access to PIP’s authoritative assets, including:

* governance;
* foundational documents;
* architecture;
* the PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System;
* Skills;
* software;
* Knowledge Curation System documentation;
* knowledge libraries;
* templates;
* operational records; and
* repository history.

PIP CORE may contain reusable governance, processes and operating systems while protecting libraries or other assets that are not authorised for reuse or distribution.

### 5.2 Knowledge Curation System

**KCS** stands for:

**Knowledge Curation System**

The KCS is the governed system through which knowledge is:

* researched;
* assessed;
* reviewed;
* approved;
* preserved;
* integrated; and
* published for operational use.

The KCS operates as a system within PIP but may also be adapted or used as a standalone knowledge-curation system.

Its name shall remain unprefixed unless a specific context requires its relationship to PIP to be stated expressly.

### 5.3 KCS Workspace

The **KCS Workspace** is the planned digital working environment through which Founders, authorised employees, contributors and agents may interact with the Knowledge Curation System.

The KCS Workspace may provide access to:

* research processes;
* Founder review;
* the Mother Information Library;
* the Live Intelligence Library;
* Research Origin Curator workflows;
* Knowledge Integration Technician workflows;
* system records; and
* authorised PIP CORE assets.

The KCS is the governed system.

The KCS Workspace is the environment through which authorised participants work with that system.

### 5.4 PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System

**PIP AI OS** stands for:

**PIP Artificial Intelligence Operating System**

The PIP AI OS governs artificial intelligence operating anywhere within PIP.

Its scope includes AI supporting:

* PIP CORE;
* the KCS;
* ROC;
* KIT;
* document creation and editing;
* repository operations;
* research;
* software development;
* application development; and
* other authorised PIP work.

The PIP AI OS is internal platform infrastructure. It is not part of the public Ask Pip brand.

## 6. Established KCS Roles

### 6.1 Research Origin Curator

**ROC** stands for:

**Research Origin Curator**

ROC is the specialist KCS role responsible for researching, assessing and organising trustworthy information, preparing Founder Review Documentation and creating and maintaining Approved Research Compilations (ARCs) within the Mother Information Library.

ROC does not approve knowledge.

The standard role name is:

**Research Origin Curator**

The standard shortened form is:

**ROC**

The role does not require PIP or KCS as a repeated title prefix.

### 6.2 Knowledge Integration Technician

**KIT** stands for:

**Knowledge Integration Technician**

KIT is the specialist KCS role responsible for converting Founder-approved information into structured operational intelligence, preparing PIP Knowledge Records and maintaining the Live Intelligence Library.

KIT does not create or approve knowledge.

The standard role name is:

**Knowledge Integration Technician**

The standard shortened form is:

**KIT**

The role does not require PIP or KCS as a repeated title prefix.

## 7. Established Knowledge Components

### 7.1 Mother Information Library

**MIL** stands for:

**Mother Information Library**

The MIL is the authoritative home of Founder-approved information, supporting evidence, traceability, metadata and history.

The MIL is maintained by ROC.

The previous expansion, **Master Information Library**, is replaced by **Mother Information Library** upon approval of this Standard.

### 7.2 Live Intelligence Library

**LIL** stands for:

**Live Intelligence Library**

The LIL contains Founder-approved structured operational intelligence prepared for rapid retrieval and use by PIP.

Its contents may include:

* operational knowledge;
* structured rules;
* relationships;
* retrieval directions;
* references to MIL knowledge; and
* other approved machine-usable structures.

The LIL is maintained by KIT.

### 7.3 Approved Research Compilation

**ARC** stands for:

**Approved Research Compilation**

An ARC is a governed body of Founder-approved information concerning a defined plant, subject or information scope. It is the primary record type held within the MIL — distinct from the Founder Review Dossier (FRD) from which it is built, which remains the historical pre-approval submission record rather than the archived asset itself (see §8.1).

ARCs are created and maintained by ROC, approved by the Founders and held within the MIL.

The scope and granularity of an ARC shall be determined by the applicable KCS governance rather than assumed from its name alone.

**Superseded terms:** this component has been named twice before. It was originally **Knowledge Asset (KA)**, retired as of Version 0.2 of this Standard because "Knowledge" wrongly implied MIL content was operational knowledge rather than approved information — the Mother Information Library Standard reserves "knowledge" and "operational intelligence" for content held within the Live Intelligence Library. It was then briefly **Approved Information Compilation (AIC)**, retired as of Version 0.4 of this Standard in favour of **Approved Research Compilation (ARC)**, at Founder request. Neither KA nor AIC shall be used in new or revised documents.

### 7.4 PIP Knowledge Record

**PKR** stands for:

**PIP Knowledge Record**

A PKR is a structured operational knowledge unit created and maintained by KIT from Founder-approved information held within the MIL.

Draft PKRs require Founder approval before publication to the LIL.

The PIP prefix remains part of the full title because the record is specifically prepared for operational use by PIP.

## 8. Established Research and Review Documents

The following document names shall remain unprefixed unless additional context is genuinely required.

### 8.1 Founder Review Dossier

**FRD** stands for:

**Founder Review Dossier**

The FRD is the complete supporting research record prepared for Founder review. It names the pre-approval submission specifically — once approved, the FRD is archived as a supporting record, and the resulting governed record is created and held in the MIL as an Approved Research Compilation (ARC, see §7.3), not as a renamed Dossier.

### 8.2 Founder Executive Brief (Retired)

**FEB** stood for:

**Founder Executive Brief**

The FEB is retired. It was the concise summary prepared to support efficient Founder review and decision-making, intended to work alongside the Founder Review Dossier (§8.1). It is retired because the review volume it was designed for does not yet exist: the Founder Review Dossier, prepared under the Founder Review Dossier Standard, is the sole Founder Review Documentation. The FEB may be reinstated by future Founder decision if review volume grows to require it.

### 8.3 Evidence Assessment

**EA** stands for:

**Evidence Assessment**

The EA records evidence confidence, rationale, limitations and remaining uncertainty.

### 8.4 Evidence Assessment Standard

**EAS** stands for:

**Evidence Assessment Standard**

The EAS governs how Evidence Assessments are completed and recorded.

These titles do not require PIP or KCS prefixes because their system relationship shall be established through repository location, metadata and related documents.

## 9. Document and Skill Titles

### 9.1 Functional Titles

Document titles shall identify the document’s function directly.

Examples include:

* ROC Operations Manual;
* KIT Charter;
* Mother Information Library Standard;
* Evidence Assessment Standard;
* Document Creation and Editing Skill; and
* CORE Integration Skill.

### 9.2 Use of System Prefixes

A system prefix shall be used where it materially distinguishes the subject.

Appropriate examples include:

* PIP System Identity and Naming Standard;
* PIP CORE Asset Lifecycle Standard;
* PIP AI Operations Manual; and
* KCS Framework.

Unnecessary examples include:

* PIP KCS ROC Operations Manual;
* PIP AI Document Creation and Editing Skill; and
* Ask Pip PIP Knowledge Integration Technician Charter.

### 9.3 Skill Names

Individual Skills within the PIP AI OS shall use concise functional names.

Their relationship to the PIP AI OS shall be established through:

* their repository location;
* metadata;
* the PIP AI Loading Guide; and
* related-document references.

The PIP or PIP AI prefix shall not be repeated in every Skill title or filename unless required to prevent genuine ambiguity.

## 10. Acronym Use

### 10.1 First Use

The first substantive use of an acronym within every standalone document shall be preceded by its full term, with the acronym placed immediately afterward in parentheses.

Examples:

* Plant Intelligence Platform (PIP);
* Knowledge Curation System (KCS);
* Mother Information Library (MIL);
* Live Intelligence Library (LIL);
* Research Origin Curator (ROC);
* PIP Knowledge Record (PKR);
* Founder Review Dossier (FRD); and
* Approved Research Compilation (ARC).

After definition, the acronym may be used alone throughout the remainder of the document.

### 10.2 Standalone Documents

Each standalone document shall define its own acronyms.

A document shall not assume that the reader has read another document in which an acronym was previously defined.

### 10.3 Titles and Metadata

A definition within the document title or metadata satisfies the first-use requirement when it includes both the full term and the acronym.

If only the acronym appears in the title or metadata, it shall still be defined at its first substantive use within the document body.

### 10.4 Plural Acronyms

A plural acronym may be introduced naturally.

Example:

> PIP Knowledge Records (PKRs)

### 10.5 Exceptions

The first-use requirement does not apply to an acronym appearing only within:

* a filename;
* a repository path;
* a URL;
* a domain name;
* a code identifier;
* a configuration value; or
* a file extension.

Common technical abbreviations shall still be defined where the intended reader may not reasonably understand them.

### 10.6 Unnecessary Acronyms

An acronym shall not be created where the shortened form is unlikely to be reused or where it would reduce clarity.

## 11. Filenames

Filenames shall be:

* concise;
* descriptive;
* consistent with the approved document title;
* understandable without unnecessary brand repetition; and
* suitable for reliable repository use.

Spaces within controlled Markdown filenames should normally be represented by underscores.

Examples include:

* `PIP_System_Identity_and_Naming_Standard.md`
* `PIP_CORE_Asset_Lifecycle_Standard.md`
* `ROC_Operations_Manual.md`
* `KIT_Charter.md`
* `Mother_Information_Library_Standard.md`
* `Document_Creation_and_Editing_Skill.md`

Established acronyms may be used in filenames where they improve clarity and remain understandable within the repository structure.

## 12. Metadata and System Relationships

Where necessary, document metadata shall establish relationships that are not repeated within the title.

Applicable metadata may include:

* Parent System;
* Repository;
* Document Type;
* Role;
* Custodian;
* Owner;
* Related Documents; and
* Authority.

Metadata shall be used to establish architectural context without making every document title unnecessarily long.

## 13. Governance

The Founders retain authority over:

* official names;
* brand spelling;
* acronym definitions;
* system relationships;
* role names;
* document-title conventions; and
* exceptions to this Standard.

An AI agent shall not rename an approved system, role, document or controlled asset without Founder authorisation.

Where an existing approved asset conflicts with this Standard, the conflict shall be identified and corrected through an authorised revision rather than silently changed.

## 14. Compliance

New or revised controlled documents shall comply with this Standard following its approval.

The Document Creation and Editing Skill shall apply and check this Standard when preparing Ask Pip and PIP documents.

Existing approved assets shall not be considered invalid solely because they predate this Standard.

They shall be aligned progressively when they are next revised or when the Founders authorise a coordinated naming update.
